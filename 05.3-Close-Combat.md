## Close Combat <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Close Combat occurs when characters fight at short distances using fists, improvised weapons, or hand-held weapons such as knives, swords, clubs, and axes.

Characters are normally considered within **Close Combat range when they are within 5 feet of one another**. Some weapons, such as spears and polearms, may allow attacks from greater distances as described by their weapon characteristics.

Close Combat uses the standard dice pool system. Players roll six-sided dice, with each result of 5 or 6 counting as one success.

### Close Combat Attacks

A character may make a Close Combat Attack during their Turn by spending **1 Action Point (AP)**.

The player assembles an Attack dice pool using an appropriate Attribute and Skill.

**Close Combat Attack Dice Pool = Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

Typical combinations include:

|Attack Method|Typical Dice Pool|
|---|---|
|Punching or kicking|Strength + Close Combat|
|Fighting with a sword or axe|Strength + Close Combat|
|Fighting with a knife|Dexterity + Close Combat|
|Using a precise martial arts technique|Dexterity + Close Combat|
|Grappling an opponent|Strength + Close Combat|
|Fighting with an improvised weapon|Strength or Dexterity + Close Combat|

The Game Master may approve another Attribute or Skill when the character's approach justifies it.

For example, Intelligence + Technology might apply when operating a specialized close-range weapon or device whose effectiveness depends on technical control.

However, the standard Close Combat Skill should remain the primary method of resolving ordinary melee attacks.

A different Skill should not be substituted merely because it provides a larger dice pool.

#### Resolving a Close Combat Attack

Follow these steps:

1. **Declare the Attack:** The player identifies the target and describes the intended attack.
2. **Build the Dice Pool:** Combine the appropriate Attribute, Skill, and modifiers.
3. **Apply Weapon Rules:** Include relevant weapon characteristics and special effects.
4. **Roll the Dice:** Each 5 or 6 counts as one success.
5. **Compare with Defense:** The Attack succeeds if the number of successes equals or exceeds the opponent's Defense Rating.
6. **Resolve Damage:** Apply the Damage and Wounds rules when the Attack succeeds.

An Attack that fails to meet the opponent's Defense Rating misses or fails to produce an effective hit.

##### Example: Sword Attack

A warrior attacks a guard with a sword.

The warrior has:

- Strength 3
- Close Combat 2
- A +1 die bonus from an applicable Edge

The warrior rolls **6 dice**.

The guard has Defense Rating 3.

The warrior rolls:

**1, 2, 4, 5, 5, 6**

The result contains 3 successes.

The Attack meets the guard's Defense Rating and hits.

Damage is resolved using the Damage and Wounds rules.

#### Push Your Luck in Close Combat

A player may Push Your Luck on a Close Combat Attack using the standard rules.

The player keeps all dice showing 1, 5, or 6 and rerolls dice showing 2, 3, or 4 once.

#### Multiple Close Combat Attacks

Characters may make multiple Close Combat Attacks during the same Turn by spending additional Action Points.

Each Attack after the first is considered a **Rushed Attack**.

Rushed Attacks suffer cumulative penalties.

|Attack During Turn|Modifier|
|---|---|
|First Attack|No penalty|
|Second Attack|–1 die|
|Third Attack|–2 dice|

The penalties apply regardless of whether the character attacks the same opponent or different opponents.

The standard minimum dice pool of two dice applies.

##### Example: Fighting Multiple Opponents

A warrior faces two opponents.

The warrior has a normal Close Combat Attack pool of 6 dice.

The warrior spends all 3 AP:

- First Attack against Opponent A: 6 dice.
- Second Attack against Opponent A: 5 dice.
- Third Attack against Opponent B: 4 dice.

Each Attack is resolved separately against its target's Defense Rating.

#### Firearms in Close Combat

Firearms are generally less effective when an opponent is within arm's reach.

A character using a firearm against a target within Close Combat range suffers a **–2 dice penalty**. This represents the difficulty of bringing a firearm to bear while an opponent can interfere with the weapon.

The penalty applies to firearms such as:

- Pistols.
- Revolvers.
- Rifles.
- Shotguns.
- Automatic weapons.

#### Weapon Range Modifiers

When a weapon's profile already specifies a –2 dice modifier at Close Combat range, use that modifier.

Do not apply a second –2 dice penalty for the same circumstance.

Other distinct modifiers, such as injuries, Conditions, or environmental penalties, may still apply.

##### Example: Pistol at Close Range

A character has a normal pistol Attack pool of 6 dice.

An enemy closes to within 5 feet.

The pistol's Close Combat range modifier is –2 dice.

The character rolls **4 dice**.

The character does not suffer an additional –2 dice penalty merely because the target is within Close Combat range.

### Grappling

A character may attempt to seize, hold, or restrain an opponent instead of inflicting damage.

Grappling uses the normal Close Combat Attack procedure.

A successful Grapple applies the **Grappled Condition** rather than causing Wounds.

#### Initiating a Grapple

**Cost: 1 AP**

To Grapple an opponent:

1. The attacker must be within Close Combat range.
2. The attacker declares a Grapple.
3. The player makes an unarmed Close Combat Attack, normally using Strength + Close Combat or Athletics.
4. The Attack is resolved against the target's Defense Rating.
5. If successful, the target gains the Grappled Condition.

A Grapple does not inflict damage unless a specific Edge, ability, or special rule states otherwise.

#### Grappled Condition

A Grappled character is physically restrained.

While Grappled:

- Speed becomes 0.
- The character cannot benefit from movement bonuses.
- The Condition ends if the grappler releases the character or becomes incapacitated.

The grappler must remain close enough to maintain physical control of the target.

#### Maintaining a Grapple

Once established, a Grapple normally continues without requiring the grappler to spend an Action Point each Turn.

However, the grappler must remain capable of maintaining the hold. The grappler may voluntarily release the target. The Grapple also ends if the grappler becomes incapacitated or otherwise loses the ability to maintain physical contact.

A grappler cannot move away from the target while continuing to hold them unless another rule permits the movement.

##### Escaping a Grapple

**Cost: 1 AP**

A Grappled character may attempt to escape during their Turn.

The player describes how the character attempts to break free.

Typical approaches include:

- Strength + Athletics to overpower the grappler.
- Dexterity + Close Combat to slip free.
- Dexterity + Finesse when escaping through agility or precise movement is appropriate.

The Game Master determines the relevant opposing rating. This can be the Opponent's Attack rating.

#### Escaping a Player Character's Grapple

When an Opponent attempts to escape a player character's hold, the player makes an appropriate Defensive Close Combat Check against the Opponent's Close Combat Attack rating.

On success, the player character maintains the Grapple.

On failure, the NPC escapes.

The Game Master does not roll dice for the NPC.

#### Character Versus Character

When two player characters contest a Grapple, both may roll their appropriate dice pools using the player-versus-player opposed-check rules.

The character with more successes wins the contest.

A tie preserves the existing Grapple.

#### Attacking While Grappled

A Grappled character may attempt actions that remain physically possible.

The Game Master determines whether an action can be performed while held.

For example, a character may be able to strike with a small knife but may be unable to swing a two-handed weapon effectively.

Any applicable penalties come from the Grappled Condition, the circumstances, or another specific rule.

Being Grappled does not automatically cause damage.

##### Example: Grappling a Guard

A character attempts to restrain a guard without injuring him.

The character has Strength 3 and Athletics 2.

The guard has Defense Rating 2.

The character rolls 5 dice and obtains 3 successes.

The Grapple succeeds.

The guard gains the Grappled Condition and cannot move away normally.

On the guard's round, he attempts to escape.

The player makes the appropriate defensive Close Combat Check against the guard's Close Combat Attack rating.

If the player succeeds, the guard remains Grappled.

If the player fails, the guard breaks free.

### Surprise Attacks

A character who approaches an opponent without being noticed may gain a significant advantage.

When a character successfully sneaks up on an unaware target, the attacker gains **+2 dice to the Close Combat Attack**.

This bonus represents the defender's inability to anticipate the initial strike.

#### Surprise Requirements

To receive the Surprise Attack bonus:

- The attacker must approach without being detected.
- The target must be unaware of the immediate attack.
- The attacker must be within the weapon's effective Close Combat range.

The Game Master determines whether the circumstances justify the bonus.

A successful Stealth Check may be required.

#### Surprise Attacks and Initiative

The Surprise Attack bonus is separate from the Initiative rules.

If the target is also surprised under those rules, the target cannot use Reactions against the opening attack. The +2 dice bonus does not automatically apply to every attack made during the opening Round.

It applies to attacks that genuinely catch the target unaware.

##### Example: Ambushing a Sentry

A Character Neo silently approaches a sentry from behind.

Neo successfully avoids detection and attacks with a knife.

Neo normally rolls 5 dice for the Attack.

Because the sentry is unaware, Neo receives +2 dice.

Neo rolls **7 dice** against the sentry's Defense Rating.

### Outnumbered

Numerical superiority can provide a substantial advantage in Close Combat.

When several combatants engage a smaller number of opponents, the larger group may surround, distract, and overwhelm the defenders.

The Outnumbered rule represents this advantage through additional Attack dice.

#### Determining Numerical Advantage

Count the combatants actively participating in the same Close Combat engagement.

Only combatants capable of threatening an opponent count toward numerical superiority.

A character who is incapacitated, unable to reach the fight, or otherwise unable to participate does not count.

A group gains the Outnumbered bonus when it has at least **twice as many active combatants** as the opposing group.

When this condition is met, calculate the bonus as follows:

| OutNumber Bonus |  =  | Number of Attackers<br>----------------------<br>Number of Defenders |  -  | 1   |
| :-------------: | :-: | :------------------------------------------------------------------: | :-: | --- |


Outnumber Bonus: When one side outnumbers another, divide the number of attackers by the number of defenders, rounding down to the nearest whole number, then subtract 1. 

The result is the Outnumber Bonus, with a minimum of +0. Each full multiple of attackers relative to defenders grants a +1 bonus beyond the first.

##### Outnumbered Examples

| Attackers | Defenders | Attack Bonus per Attacker |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------- |
| 2         | 1         | +1 die                    |
| 3         | 1         | +2 dice                   |
| 4         | 1         | +3 dice                   |
| 3         | 2         | No bonus                  |
| 4         | 2         | +1 dice                   |
| 6         | 2         | +2 dice                   |

#### Applying the Bonus

The Outnumbered bonus applies to Close Combat Attacks made by members of the larger group against opponents in that engagement.

It does not apply to ranged Attacks.

The bonus changes as combatants enter, leave, or become unable to participate in the engagement.

##### Example: Three Against One

Three adventurers surround a lone guard.

All three are within Close Combat range and capable of attacking.

The adventurers outnumber the guard 3 to 1.

Each adventurer receives **+2 dice** on Close Combat Attacks against the guard.

If one adventurer leaves the engagement, the remaining two still outnumber the guard 2 to 1.

Their bonus becomes **+1 die** each.

#### Maximum Bonus (Optional)

For campaigns that emphasize realistic combat and tighter dice-pool balance, the Game Master may limit the Outnumbered bonus to **+3 dice**.

This prevents unusually large groups from generating excessive bonuses.

The limit is optional and should be established before the campaign begins.

### Moving Out of Close Combat

Leaving an opponent’s Close Combat range may expose a character to an immediate attack.

A character moving out of Close Combat must make a Defense roll against the opponent’s Close Combat Attack rating −1.

Exception: No Defense roll is required if the character and their allies outnumber their opponents in that Close Combat at the start of the movement.

#### Moving Carefully

A character may prepare for danger before leaving Close Combat.

For example, the character may use **Assess: Anticipate Danger** to gain +1 die to their next Defense Roll.

##### Example: Breaking Away

A character fighting an enemy swordsman decides to retreat toward a nearby doorway.

The character spends 1 AP to Move out of Close Combat range and must make a Defense roll against the swordsman’s Close Combat Attack rating −1.

After resolving the attack, the character may continue moving if able.

### Game Master Guidance

Close Combat should be dangerous, dynamic, and responsive to positioning.

The Game Master should consider the following principles.

**Reward Tactical Positioning**

- Characters who surround opponents, attack from unexpected directions, or exploit the environment should receive appropriate advantages.

**Keep Grappling Simple**

- Grappling should provide a useful alternative to inflicting Wounds without becoming a complicated wrestling subsystem.
- Use the standard Close Combat rules whenever possible.

**Apply Numerical Superiority Carefully**

- Only combatants who can actively participate should count toward the Outnumbered bonus.
- The Game Master should consider narrow passages, obstacles, confined spaces, and other circumstances that prevent attackers from surrounding a defender.

**Distinguish Surprise from Positioning**

- An attack from behind does not automatically qualify as a Surprise Attack.
- The target must be genuinely unaware of the immediate threat.

**Encourage Alternatives to Damage**

- Characters may attempt to restrain opponents, force them to surrender, escape a dangerous engagement, or gain a tactical advantage.
- These options should remain meaningful alternatives to repeatedly attacking until every opponent is incapacitated.

**Close Combat should reward skill, positioning, timing, and teamwork while remaining consistent with the game's core dice pool system.**
