## Ranged Combat <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Ranged Combat occurs when a character attacks an opponent using a thrown or fired weapon.

Ranged weapons include bows, crossbows, pistols, rifles, shotguns, automatic weapons, thrown knives, spears, and other weapons capable of striking a target at a distance.

Ranged Combat uses the standard dice pool system. Each die showing a 5 or 6 counts as one success.

Players make Attack Rolls when their characters attack opponents. When opponents attack player characters, the players make the appropriate Defense Rolls.

### Ranged Attacks

A Ranged Attack normally costs **1 Action Point (AP)**.

The player assembles a dice pool using an appropriate Attribute and Skill, then applies any relevant modifiers.

**Ranged Attack Dice Pool = Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

The most common combination is:

**Dexterity + Ranged Combat**

Other combinations may apply when appropriate.

| Attack Method             | Typical Attribute and Skill                   |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Pistol, rifle, or shotgun | Dexterity + Ranged Combat                     |
| Bow or crossbow           | Dexterity + Ranged Combat                     |
| Light thrown weapon       | Dexterity + Ranged Combat                     |
| Heavy thrown weapon       | Strength + Ranged Combat                      |
| Specialized ranged device | Attribute and Skill appropriate to the device |

The Game Master may approve an alternative combination when the character's method justifies it.

For example, throwing a heavy spear may depend more on Strength than Dexterity.

An alternative combination should reflect how the character performs the attack rather than simply provide a larger dice pool.

#### Resolving a Ranged Attack

Follow these steps:

1. **Declare the Target:** Identify the opponent, object, or location being attacked.
2. **Determine Range:** Establish the distance to the target and identify the applicable range band.
3. **Build the Dice Pool:** Combine the appropriate Attribute, Skill, and weapon modifiers.
4. **Apply Other Modifiers:** Account for cover, target size, target location, Edges, Hindrances, Conditions, and other circumstances.
5. **Roll the Dice:** Each 5 or 6 counts as one success.
6. **Compare with Defense:** The Attack succeeds if the successes equal or exceed the opponent's Defense Rating.
7. **Resolve the Hit:** Apply the Damage and Wounds rules when the Attack succeeds.

An Attack that does not meet the required Defense Rating misses or fails to produce an effective hit.

##### Example: Rifle Attack

A character fires a rifle at a guard standing behind a low barricade.

The character has:

- Dexterity 3
- Ranged Combat 2
- A weapon range modifier of +0
- A –1 die penalty because the guard has Half Cover

The character rolls **4 dice**.

If the roll meets the guard's Defense Rating, the Attack hits.

The resulting damage is resolved using the rules for Damage and Wounds.

#### Range

Ranged weapons become more or less effective depending on the distance to the target.

The game uses five standard range bands.

|Range|Distance|
|---|---|
|Close|0–5 feet|
|Short|6–30 feet|
|Medium|31–120 feet|
|Long|121–300 feet|
|Extreme|301 feet or more|

These range bands provide a common reference for weapons and combat abilities.

Each ranged weapon has its own effective range and Attack modifiers. Consult the weapon's profile to determine which modifiers apply at the target's distance.

A weapon may be ineffective or unusable beyond its listed range.

##### Weapon Range Modifiers

Range modifiers reflect the weapon's characteristics.

- A pistol may be effective at Short range but difficult to use accurately at Long range.
- A rifle may remain effective at Medium or Long range.
- A thrown knife may be practical at Short range but ineffective at greater distances.

Use the modifier listed for the weapon and the target's range band.

Do not impose an additional general range penalty when the weapon profile already accounts for that distance.

##### Close Range Firearms

A firearm used against an opponent within Close range normally suffers the weapon's listed **–2 dice** Close range modifier.

This represents the difficulty of bringing a firearm to bear when an opponent can physically interfere.

The modifier is applied once.

Do not apply an additional –2 dice penalty merely because the attack occurs during Close Combat.

#### Target Modifiers

The position and protection of a target may make a ranged attack more difficult.

|Target Circumstance|Attack Modifier|
|---|---|
|Prone|–1 die|
|Half Cover|–1 die|
|Three-Quarter Cover|–2 dice|

These modifiers apply to the Attacker's dice pool.

##### Prone Targets

A Prone target presents a smaller profile to ranged attackers.

Ranged Attacks against a Prone target suffer **–1 die**. This modifier assumes the attacker is firing from a relatively similar elevation.

The Game Master may adjust or ignore the modifier when the circumstances differ significantly, such as an attacker firing almost directly downward.

##### Cover

Cover represents a physical obstruction between an attacker and a target.

Cover makes a target harder to hit.

###### Half Cover

A target with approximately half its body protected by a solid obstruction imposes **–1 die** on Ranged Attacks.

Examples include:

- A low stone wall.
- A sturdy table.
- A vehicle's engine compartment.
- A barricade protecting the lower body.

###### Three-Quarter Cover

A target with most of its body protected imposes **–2 dice** on Ranged Attacks.

Examples include:

- A narrow firing position.
- A substantial concrete barrier.
- A doorway exposing only part of the body.
- A defensive position with a small opening.

###### Full Cover

A target completely concealed behind an obstruction that blocks the attacker's line of fire cannot normally be directly attacked.

The attacker may need to:

- Change position.
- Destroy or penetrate the obstruction.
- Use an explosive or area-effect weapon.
- Wait until the target becomes exposed.

A target being unseen is not necessarily the same as being physically protected by cover.

The Game Master should distinguish concealment from an obstruction capable of stopping an attack.

##### Cover and Armor

Cover makes a target harder to hit.

Armor provides protection according to its own rules.

These effects are distinct and may apply together when appropriate.

#### Panic Fire

A character armed with a weapon capable of firing multiple rounds may rapidly discharge ammunition to improve the chance of hitting a target.

This is called **Panic Fire**.

Panic Fire represents an uncontrolled or hurried burst rather than carefully aimed fire.

##### Using Panic Fire

**Cost: 1 AP**

When declaring a Ranged Attack with an eligible weapon, the player may choose to use Panic Fire.

The character receives:

**+1 die to the Attack Roll.**

After the Attack is resolved, the weapon must be reloaded before it can fire again.

Reloading normally costs 1 AP.

##### Panic Fire Restrictions

Panic Fire requires a weapon capable of firing multiple rounds before reloading.

It cannot normally be used with:

- Single-shot firearms.
- Bows.
- Crossbows.
- Thrown weapons.
- Other weapons that fire only once before reloading.

The Game Master determines whether a particular weapon qualifies.

Panic Fire is resolved as a single Attack against a single target. It does not automatically strike multiple opponents.

###### Example: Panic Fire

A character sees an enemy rushing toward them and fires a rapid burst from a semiautomatic pistol.

The character normally rolls 5 dice.

Panic Fire grants +1 die, increasing the pool to **6 dice**.

After resolving the Attack, the pistol must be reloaded before the character can fire again.

#### Full Automatic Fire

Some firearms can fire controlled bursts or fully automatic streams of projectiles.

A character using Full Automatic Fire may make up to **three separate Attacks during a single Action**.

The character may attack one target multiple times or divide the attacks among several targets.

All targets must be within Short Range of one another.

##### Automatic Fire Penalties

|Attack|Attack Dice Penalty|
|---|---|
|First|–1 die|
|Second|–2 dice|
|Third|–3 dice|

These penalties apply to each Attack Roll.

They do not reduce the weapon's Weapon Dice.

Each Attack is resolved separately.

A successful Attack generates its own Damage Roll.

A character may stop after the first or second Attack.

##### Ammunition

Full Automatic Fire always requires reloading afterward.

###### Example: Automatic Fire

A character fires an assault rifle at a group of enemies.

The character has a normal Attack dice pool of 6 dice.

Using Full Automatic Fire, the character makes three attacks:

- First Attack: 5 dice.
- Second Attack: 4 dice.
- Third Attack: 3 dice.

Each successful Attack produces a separate Damage Roll.

The automatic-fire penalties affect accuracy, not the weapon's base damage.

#### Suppressive Fire

Automatic weapons may use sustained fire to force opponents to seek cover and limit their ability to attack effectively.

Suppressive Fire is intended to control an area rather than make a precise attack against a single target.

It is particularly useful for:

- Covering a retreat.
- Protecting advancing allies.
- Defending a narrow passage.
- Preventing opponents from attacking effectively.
- Creating an opportunity to move across exposed ground.

##### Using Suppressive Fire

**Cost: 2 AP**

A character using an automatic weapon declares a firing arc of approximately **30 degrees**.

The player makes a Ranged Combat Attack Roll at **Hard Difficulty (3 successes)**.

This is a single roll representing the effectiveness of the sustained fire.

###### Successful Suppression

On success, opponents exposed to the suppressive fire within the selected arc suffer:

**–1 to their Attack Rating during the next Round.**

The penalty represents opponents ducking, hesitating, or firing less effectively while under threat.

Suppression does not automatically inflict Wounds. It does not automatically prevent all movement or attacks. Opponents may still act, but the suppressive fire makes their attacks less effective.

###### Failed Suppression

On failure, the suppressive fire does not impose the Attack Rating penalty. 

The character still spends the Action Points and ammunition required.

##### Reloading

After using Suppressive Fire, the weapon must be reloaded before it can fire again.

Reloading normally costs 1 AP.

##### Suppressive Fire and Cover

The Game Master determines which opponents are meaningfully exposed to the firing arc.

An opponent completely protected by solid cover may not be directly threatened by the gunfire. An opponent behind partial cover may still be affected.

The Game Master should consider the weapon, terrain, available ammunition, and line of fire.

###### Example: Covering a Retreat

A soldier uses a machine gun to cover companions retreating across an open courtyard.

The soldier spends 2 AP on Suppressive Fire and selects a 30-degree arc covering several enemy positions.

The soldier rolls Dexterity + Ranged Combat against Hard Difficulty.

The roll produces 3 successes.

The suppression succeeds.

Exposed opponents within the arc suffer –1 to their Attack Ratings during the next Round.

The soldier must reload before firing the weapon again.

##### Characters Receiving Suppressive Fire

When a player character is affected by Suppressive Fire, the equivalent effect is **-1 die to the character's next Attack roll**.

The Game Master should apply the effect only to attacks meaningfully affected by the suppression.

#### Target Size

Most attacks are made against Normal-sized targets and receive no Size modifier.

Smaller targets are harder to hit, while larger targets are generally easier to strike.

Size modifiers apply to **Ranged Attack Rolls only**.

They represent the difficulty of hitting a target, not the difficulty of injuring or damaging it.

##### Target Size Modifiers

|Size|Ranged Attack Modifier|Typical Height|Examples|
|---|---|---|---|
|Tiny|–2 dice|Under 2 ft|Rat, raven, house cat, monkey, drone, small robot|
|Small|–1 die|2–4 ft|Child, medium dog, wolf, alien scout|
|Normal|None|4–8 ft|Human, zombie, large dog, average robot, motorcycle|
|Large|+1 die|8–15 ft|Horse, bear, elephant, Xenomorph, small vehicle|
|Giant|+2 dice|15–40 ft|Giant, Tyrannosaurus, tank, helicopter|
|Huge|+2 dice|Over 40 ft|Sailing ship, blue whale, passenger jet, spacecraft, colossal alien|

###### Huge Targets

Huge targets are so massive that additional size provides little benefit beyond making them easier to locate and strike.

For this reason, the maximum standard Size bonus is **+2 dice**.

When appropriate, the Game Master may treat individual sections of a Huge target as separate targets.

Examples include:

- A ship's bridge.
- A vehicle's engine.
- An aircraft's wing.
- A tank's turret.
- A spacecraft's exposed equipment.

The Game Master determines the effective Size of the specific component.

A component may be substantially smaller than the overall target.

###### Example: Attacking a Tank

A character fires a weapon at a tank.

Because the tank is a Giant target, the Attack receives **+2 dice**. However, the character decides to aim specifically at a small external sensor.

The Game Master determines that the sensor is a Small target. The character uses the sensor's effective Size modifier of **–1 die** instead of the tank's +2 dice bonus.

The character does not receive the benefit of the tank's overall size when aiming at a much smaller component.

#### Specifying a Target Location

Ranged Attacks normally aim at the target's center of mass.

A character may instead attempt to strike a particular body part or location.

Such attacks are called **Targeted Attacks** or **Called Shots**.

Targeted Attacks are more difficult but may produce specific consequences when they succeed.

##### Target Location Modifiers

|Location|Attack Modifier|Potential Benefit|
|---|---|---|
|Torso|None|Normal damage|
|Arm or Leg|–1 die|May restrict or disable the limb|
|Head|–2 dice|May cause Dazed or Stunned|

The player must declare the intended target location before rolling.

The modifier applies to the Attack Roll.

The target's Defense Rating and the normal weapon damage rules otherwise remain unchanged.

###### Torso

An Attack against the torso is treated as a normal Attack.

No additional modifier applies.

###### Arm or Leg

A character may target an opponent's arm or leg at **–1 die**.

A successful Attack may produce an appropriate effect, depending on the injury.

Examples include:

- Causing an opponent to drop a held object.
- Injuring an arm and restricting its use.
- Damaging a leg and reducing movement.
- Disabling a limb after a sufficiently severe injury.

The Game Master determines the consequences using the Wound rules and the circumstances.

###### Head

A character may target an opponent's head at **–2 dice**.

A successful head Attack may produce the **Dazed** or **Stunned** Condition.

The Game Master determines the appropriate effect based on:

- The severity of the injury.
- The weapon used.
- The target's nature and protection.
- Any applicable Wound or Condition rules.

A hit to the head does not automatically impose Stunned if the injury is too minor to justify that result.

###### Example: Disarming Shot

A character attempts to shoot at the arm of an opponent holding a pistol.

The Game Master treats the attack as an Arm Targeted Attack.

The player suffers –1 die.

If the Attack succeeds, the Game Master determines whether the resulting injury or impact is sufficient to force the opponent to drop the weapon.

If the character tries to shot the pistol, the modifier would be -2 dice because of the small size.

###### Example: Head Shot

A character aims at an enemy's head with a rifle.

The Attack suffers –2 dice.

If the Attack succeeds, damage is resolved normally.

The Game Master then determines whether the injury causes Dazed, Stunned, or another consequence consistent with the Wound and Condition rules.

#### Combining Ranged Attack Modifiers

Several modifiers may apply to the same Attack.

For example, a character may be firing at a Small target behind Half Cover at Long range.

Apply the modifiers in the following order:

1. Select the appropriate Attribute and Skill.
2. Apply the weapon's range modifier.
3. Apply the target's Cover modifier.
4. Apply the target's Size modifier.
5. Apply any Target Location modifier.
6. Apply relevant Edges, Hindrances, Conditions, and situational modifiers.
7. Apply any applicable Action bonus, such as Aim or Panic Fire.

Modifiers are cumulative when they represent different circumstances.

Do not apply multiple penalties for the same circumstance.

The normal minimum dice pool rule applies.

##### Example: Difficult Shot

A character fires a rifle at a Small opponent behind Half Cover.

The character's base Attack pool is 6 dice.

The target is at Medium range, where the rifle has no penalty.

Modifiers:

- Medium range: 0 dice.
- Small target: –1 die.
- Half Cover: –1 die.

The final Attack pool is **4 dice**.

If the character spends an Action to Aim before attacking, the Aim bonus increases the pool to **5 dice**.

### Game Master Guidance

Ranged Combat should emphasize distance, positioning, cover, weapon capabilities, and tactical choices.

**Make Cover Important**

Characters who use cover should gain meaningful protection.

Describe available barricades, buildings, vehicles, terrain, and other obstacles clearly.

**Distinguish Suppression from Damage**

Suppressive Fire is intended to influence enemy behavior and reduce the effectiveness of attacks.

It should not become a way to make numerous damaging attacks with a single roll.

**Use Weapon Profiles Consistently**

Different weapons have different effective ranges and capabilities.

The weapon profile should determine the applicable range modifier.

**Keep Targeted Attacks Reasonable**

Targeted Attacks can produce useful tactical effects, but their consequences should reflect the actual injury and circumstances.

A minor wound to an arm should not automatically have the same effect as a devastating injury.

**Preserve Player-Facing Combat**

Players roll to attack NPCs and to defend against NPC attacks.

The Game Master uses static opponent ratings rather than making separate NPC Attack Rolls.

**Reward Tactical Choices**

A character who takes time to Aim, moves to a better firing position, or uses an appropriate weapon should benefit from those decisions.

Likewise, poor positioning, exposed movement, and inappropriate weapons should create meaningful disadvantages.

**Ranged Combat should reward accuracy, positioning, and intelligent use of weapons while remaining fast and consistent with the game's core dice pool mechanics.**
