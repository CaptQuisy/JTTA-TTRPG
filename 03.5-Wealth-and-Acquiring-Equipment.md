## Wealth & Acquiring Equipment <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Every adventure requires resources.

Whether purchasing supplies in a pirate port, bribing a customs official, buying military equipment, or negotiating for advanced technology, characters must occasionally spend money, trade valuable goods, or rely upon the resources of an organization.

Rather than requiring players to track every coin or credit, _Journey Through the Ages_ uses a flexible Wealth system that can be adapted to any historical period or genre.

The names and relative values of currencies vary between settings, but the underlying equipment categories remain consistent.

Examples of currency include:

- Gold Crowns
- Pieces of Eight
- Dollars
- Euros
- Corporate Scrip
- Credits
- Imperial Marks

The currency changes. The rules do not.

The Game Master determines the economic conditions of the campaign, including prices, availability, wages, financial rewards, and the resources available to characters.

### Starting Wealth

Starting Wealth represents the equipment and personal possessions a character can reasonably acquire before the campaign begins.

Instead of assigning every character a fixed amount of currency, the Game Master selects a **Campaign Wealth Level** that determines how many items characters may choose during character creation.

This system allows a group of desperate survivors to begin with limited resources, while wealthy adventurers or military personnel may have access to substantially more equipment.

Starting Wealth determines initial equipment selections. It does not automatically represent a recurring income or a permanent purchasing allowance.

#### Cost Categories

Equipment is assigned a Cost Category representing its relative expense within the campaign setting.

|Cost|Description|Typical Examples|
|---|---|---|
|Free|Issued equipment or items with little or no market value|Common clothing, military-issued gear, basic tools|
|Trivial|Everyday purchases requiring little expense|Rope, lantern, ammunition, rations, flashlight|
|Common|Affordable purchases requiring modest savings|Sword, revolver, hunting rifle, first aid kit|
|Expensive|Significant purchases requiring planning or substantial savings|Ballistic armor, motorcycle, quality horse, precision rifle|
|Luxury|Exceptional purchases generally affordable only to wealthy individuals or organizations|Sports car, powered armor, advanced drones, light aircraft|
|Priceless|Items beyond normal commerce because of unique historical, technological, or cultural significance|Legendary relics, alien artifacts, famous works of art, unique prototypes|

Cost Categories are relative to the setting.

A firearm that is expensive and uncommon in one historical period may become an ordinary purchase in a later era. Likewise, a piece of advanced technology may be common in a futuristic civilization but priceless in a less developed society.

The Game Master may adjust an item's Cost Category when the campaign's economic conditions justify it.

**Cost and Availability are separate.** An item may be inexpensive but difficult to obtain, or expensive but readily available to someone with sufficient resources.

### Starting Wealth by Campaign

The Game Master chooses a starting Wealth level that reflects the campaign's circumstances.

| Campaign Style | Trivial   | Common | Expensive  | Luxury      |
| -------------- | --------- | ------ | ---------- | ----------- |
| Survival       | 10        | 2      | —          | —           |
| Standard       | Unlimited | 4      | 1          | —           |
| Prosperous     | Unlimited | 6      | 2          | —           |
| Military       | Unlimited | 4      | 2 (Issued) | As Assigned |
| Wealthy        | Unlimited | 8      | 3          | 1           |

These values represent the number of starting equipment selections available to each character, unless the Game Master establishes a shared equipment allowance for the group.

#### Survival Campaign

Characters begin with limited resources and must rely on ingenuity, scavenging, and careful planning.

Appropriate for:

- Post-apocalyptic survival
- Stranded expeditions
- Prison escapes
- Desperate fugitives
- Isolated frontier settlements

#### Standard Campaign

Characters begin with ordinary personal equipment and a limited selection of more valuable possessions.

Appropriate for most historical, modern, and adventure campaigns.

#### Prosperous Campaign

Characters have access to greater personal resources and may begin with more specialized or expensive equipment.

Appropriate for established professionals, successful adventurers, or well-funded expeditions.

#### Military Campaign

Characters receive equipment appropriate to their assignments and organizational responsibilities. The two Expensive selections listed as Issued represent organizational equipment rather than personal property. Luxury equipment is assigned by the Game Master according to the mission and organization.

Military characters may also receive additional mission-specific equipment when appropriate.

#### Wealthy Campaign

Characters possess substantial personal resources and may begin with exceptional equipment. Appropriate for wealthy patrons, aristocrats, corporate executives, or similarly privileged characters.

Wealth does not override technological, legal, or availability restrictions.

### Starting Equipment

Every starting character receives:

1. One set of clothing appropriate to the setting.
2. Basic personal possessions suitable for their Background.
3. The free signature item granted by their Background.
4. Equipment selections determined by the campaign's Starting Wealth table.

The Game Master may also provide equipment essential to the campaign's premise.

For example, a military unit may receive a transport vehicle, while the crew of a merchant spacecraft may begin with access to a shared vessel.

Such campaign-provided equipment is distinct from each character's personal starting selections.

#### Selecting Equipment

Players choose equipment from the available item listings using their starting Cost Category allowances. Each selected item normally consumes one selection from the corresponding Cost Category.

The Game Master determines reasonable quantities for items commonly purchased in groups, such as ammunition, rations, bandages, or ordinary supplies.

#### Unlimited Trivial Equipment

In most cases, the Starting Wealth table permits Unlimited Trivial equipment. This means characters may select reasonable quantities of ordinary, inexpensive personal supplies without counting individual selections. It does not grant unlimited stockpiles, cargo loads, or equipment intended for resale.

For example, a character may reasonably begin with rope, a lantern, spare ammunition, ordinary rations, and a backpack.

Beginning with thousands of rounds of ammunition or a warehouse of supplies requires Game Master approval. The Game Master may restrict Trivial equipment when scarcity is an important part of the campaign.

### Equipment Exchanges

Players may exchange higher-cost selections for additional lower-cost equipment.

The standard exchange rates are:

| Exchange              | Additional Selections |
| --------------------- | --------------------- |
| 1 Expensive selection | 2 Common selections   |
| 1 Common selection    | 2 Trivial selections  |

#### Example: Standard Starting Equipment

Sarah creates an investigator for a modern adventure using Standard Starting Wealth.

She receives:

- Unlimited reasonable Trivial equipment.
- Four Common equipment selections.
- One Expensive equipment selection.

Sarah selects a service pistol, first aid kit, binoculars, and portable radio as her Common items.

For her Expensive selection, she chooses a ballistic vest.

She also receives appropriate clothing, ordinary personal possessions, and her Background's free signature item.

The Game Master confirms that all selections are available and appropriate to the campaign.

### Issued Equipment

Some characters receive equipment through employers, military organizations, government agencies, patrons, or other institutions. Issued equipment does not normally count against personal Starting Wealth selections.

However, the organization determines what equipment is provided and under what circumstances it may be used.

A soldier may receive a service rifle and protective equipment, but this does not grant unrestricted access to every weapon in the military arsenal.

### Free Background Item

Every Background grants one signature item at no cost during character creation.

This item represents a possession, tool, or piece of equipment associated with the character's former profession, training, or social position.

The item is selected from the options listed for the Background or another suitable item approved by the Game Master.

The free Background item does not consume a Starting Wealth selection.

#### Background Item Restrictions

A free Background item must be reasonable for the character and setting.

For example, a Soldier may receive an ordinary service weapon appropriate to their military experience, but not an unrestricted selection of heavy weapons.

A Pilot may receive navigational instruments or a professional toolkit without automatically owning an aircraft.

Official credentials, badges, and similar items do not automatically grant current legal authority if the character no longer holds the relevant position.


### Lifestyle

Lifestyle describes a character's normal living conditions, social expectations, and access to ordinary conveniences.

A character's Background may influence their Lifestyle, but it does not automatically determine their starting equipment.

#### Lifestyle Categories

|Lifestyle|Description|
|---|---|
|Poor|Basic shelter and simple necessities|
|Comfortable|Reliable housing and ordinary conveniences|
|Wealthy|Fine accommodations and access to luxury goods|
|Affluent|Exceptional wealth, influence, and prestige|

Lifestyle primarily influences roleplaying and narrative opportunities. A character's Lifestyle may change during the campaign because of employment, investments, financial losses, patronage, or important story events.

The Game Master determines how these changes affect the character's access to resources.

### Purchasing Equipment

Characters may acquire additional equipment during play through ordinary purchases, trade, employment, rewards, or other opportunities. If an item is available and the character possesses sufficient resources, the purchase can normally be completed without rolling dice.

A Skill Check is appropriate only when obtaining the item presents meaningful uncertainty or consequences.

#### Determining Affordability

Cost Categories describe the relative expense of equipment. However, the standard Starting Wealth table does not establish a recurring purchasing allowance.

During play, the Game Master determines what a character can afford based on their established resources, income, possessions, rewards, and financial circumstances. Routine purchases appropriate to a character's resources may be handled narratively. Significant purchases should require a plausible source of funds or another meaningful expenditure.

For campaigns where exact financial accounting matters, the Game Master may assign numerical prices and track currency. The Cost Categories can still be used to indicate the relative expense of items.

### Availability

Not every item can be purchased everywhere.

Availability represents how easily equipment can be obtained in a particular setting or location.

#### Availability Categories

|Availability|Description|Typical Examples|
|---|---|---|
|Plentiful|Readily available almost anywhere|Food, rope, clothing, backpacks|
|Common|Available from most merchants or suppliers|Hunting rifles, tents, binoculars, first aid kits|
|Specialized|Requires a specialty retailer, skilled craftsman, or licensed dealer|Laboratory equipment, climbing gear, surgical instruments|
|Restricted|Requires permits, military authorization, or official approval|Assault rifles, explosives, military vehicles, encrypted communications|
|Illegal|Available only through criminal organizations or clandestine markets|Contraband weapons, forged documents, prohibited technology|
|Unique|One-of-a-kind items that cannot normally be purchased|Prototype technology, famous historical weapons, ancient relics|

Availability is always relative to the campaign's location, era, and laws. An item classified as Common in one society may be Restricted or unavailable in another.

#### Restricted Equipment

Some equipment requires legal authority, specialized access, or organizational approval. Characters may obtain restricted equipment through legitimate authorization or other methods appropriate to the campaign.

The Game Master determines whether an attempt is possible and what consequences may follow. Failure may attract attention from law enforcement, rival organizations, or other interested parties.

Even a successful acquisition may create obligations or future complications.

### Selling Equipment

Characters may sell unwanted equipment when suitable buyers are available. The price depends on the item's condition, demand, legality, and the circumstances of the sale.

#### Typical Resale Values

|Item Condition|Typical Resale Value|
|---|---|
|Poor|25% of market value|
|Standard|50% of market value|
|Superior|75% of market value|
|Masterwork|Negotiated individually|

These percentages are guidelines for campaigns using numerical prices.

For campaigns relying entirely on Cost Categories, the Game Master may instead describe the sale's financial significance and determine whether it meaningfully changes the character's available resources.

#### Selling Restrictions

Not every item can be sold freely.

Potential complications include:

- Lack of interested buyers
- Legal restrictions
- Stolen property
- Damaged or obsolete equipment
- Limited local demand
- Organizational ownership

Issued equipment cannot normally be sold as personal property without permission. Rare, historical, or culturally significant items may be worth substantially more to particular collectors.

### Barter

In many settings, goods and services may be more useful than currency.

Characters may exchange:

- Food
- Ammunition
- Fuel
- Medical supplies
- Livestock
- Information
- Precious metals
- Salvaged technology

The Game Master determines the relative value of traded goods based on local demand and circumstances. An item that is inexpensive in a prosperous city may become extremely valuable in an isolated settlement.

Barter is especially common in frontier, post-apocalyptic, and remote communities.

### Salvage & Loot

Equipment recovered during adventures may be kept, sold, repaired, dismantled, or traded. The Game Master determines the condition, value, and usability of recovered equipment.

Damaged items may require repairs before they can be used effectively.

Recovered technology may also provide valuable information or components even when it cannot be sold.

### Era

Era indicates the broad technological period in which an item is commonly manufactured or used. The Era classification helps the Game Master determine which equipment is appropriate for the campaign.

#### Era Categories

| Era        | Typical Period                         | Examples                                                     |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Primitive  | Prehistory to Bronze Age               | Stone axe, spear, hide armor                                 |
| Classical  | Iron Age through Renaissance           | Longsword, crossbow, plate armor                             |
| Industrial | Age of Sail through early 20th century | Flintlock pistol, steam engine, revolver, bolt-action rifle  |
| Modern     | Mid-20th century to near future        | Assault rifle, body armor, helicopter, laptop computer       |
| Advanced   | Science fiction technology             | Laser weapons, powered armor, AI assistants, fusion reactors |
| Alien      | Technology beyond human understanding  | Gravity manipulators, precursor artifacts, living starships  |

These categories are broad guidelines, not precise historical dates.

An item's appearance in an Era does not mean that every civilization or location within that period possesses it. The Game Master determines actual availability based on the campaign's date, location, and technological development.

#### Typical Campaign Eras

|Campaign|Common Equipment Eras|
|---|---|
|Pirates|Industrial|
|Wild West|Industrial|
|World War II|Industrial and Modern|
|Modern Espionage|Modern|
|Post-Apocalyptic|Industrial and Modern|
|Science Fiction|Modern, Advanced, and Alien|

Campaigns may include equipment from multiple Eras.

A post-apocalyptic setting might contain primitive tools alongside surviving modern firearms.

A science fiction society might use conventional weapons and vehicles alongside advanced technology.

Alien equipment should be introduced only when appropriate to the setting and story.

### Wealth Advancement

As characters gain experience, they may improve their financial circumstances.

|Wealth Improvement|XP Cost|
|---|---|
|Minor Increase|1 XP|
|Moderate Increase|2 XP|
|Major Increase|3 XP|

These improvements require Game Master approval. The standard Wealth rules do not assign a fixed numerical value to these increases.

Before permitting XP purchases, the Game Master should establish whether the improvement provides a one-time financial benefit, an ongoing source of income, or a lasting change in the character's resources.

Ordinary financial rewards, purchases, losses, and debts may also occur through play without requiring XP expenditures.

### Game Master Guidance

Wealth should support the story rather than dominate it.

Unless the campaign focuses on economics, survival, or resource management, avoid requiring players to account for every minor purchase.

Instead, use financial decisions to create meaningful choices. Can the group afford passage across the ocean? Should they spend their remaining resources on better armor or medical supplies? Can they obtain a restricted weapon without attracting unwanted attention? Is bribery worth the risk?

When adjudicating equipment and Wealth, consider four questions:

1. **Cost:** How expensive is the item relative to the setting?
2. **Availability:** Can the item reasonably be obtained here?
3. **Resources:** Does the character possess the funds, goods, authority, or connections necessary to acquire it?
4. **Consequences:** Will obtaining or using the item create meaningful obligations or risks?

The level of financial detail should match the campaign. A cinematic adventure may resolve routine purchases with little bookkeeping. A survival campaign may make every supply, fuel reserve, and valuable trade good important. Both approaches can use the same Cost and Availability categories.

**Financial choices are most interesting when they influence the adventure rather than slow it down.**
