## Hindrances <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

No hero is perfect.

Every character possesses flaws, limitations, fears, obligations, or personal challenges that influence how they approach the world. Hindrances represent disadvantages that create meaningful complications and opportunities for memorable roleplaying.

Some Hindrances are physical limitations. Others involve personality, reputation, finances, personal commitments, or circumstances beyond the character's control.

Hindrances should make characters more interesting without making them incapable of participating in an adventure.

Unlike Edges, which provide advantages, Hindrances create challenges that characters must overcome through determination, preparation, cooperation, and ingenuity.

### Choosing Hindrances

Selecting Hindrances is optional.

During character creation, a player may select up to **2 Hindrance levels**.

Each Hindrance level grants **1 Edge Point**, which may be spent according to purchase additional Edge Levels.

|Selection|Edge Points Gained|
|---|---|
|One Level 1 Hindrance|1|
|Two Level 1 Hindrances|2|
|One Level 2 Hindrance|2|

A character may not receive more than 2 Edge Points from Hindrances during character creation.

Each Hindrance has two levels:

**Level 1 — Minor:** A limitation or complication that creates occasional or moderate difficulties.

**Level 2 — Major:** A more significant limitation that can regularly affect the character's decisions, opportunities, or capabilities.

Level 2 replaces the corresponding Level 1 mechanical effects unless specifically stated otherwise. The penalties do not stack.

The Game Master must approve all Hindrances and may reject choices that would be inappropriate or insignificant in the campaign.

### Roleplaying Hindrances

Players are encouraged to portray their Hindrances consistently.

An Honest character may struggle with deception. A Pacifist may search for peaceful alternatives. An Overconfident character may underestimate danger.

However, a Hindrance should not force a player to act against their wishes without an established mechanical procedure.

The Game Master should present the consequences of a character's weaknesses while allowing the player to decide how the character responds.

Players and the Game Master should also agree on the portrayal of sensitive conditions or personal circumstances before play. A player may use an alternative fictional explanation for a mechanical Hindrance when appropriate.

### Hindrance Format

Each Hindrance includes:

**Name:** The name of the Hindrance.

**Level:** Level 1 or Level 2.

**Description:** The limitation, flaw, or complication represented.

**Effect:** The mechanical consequences during play.

### Categories of Hindrances

Hindrances are organized into four categories:

1. Physical Limitations
2. Mental & Emotional Challenges
3. Social & Financial Challenges
4. Miscellaneous

These categories are provided for reference and do not restrict character selection.

#### Physical Limitations

Physical Hindrances represent injuries, sensory limitations, reduced mobility, or conditions that affect physical capability.

Appropriate equipment, accommodations, or assistance may reduce their effects. The Game Master should determine whether such accommodations are available in the campaign's era.

##### Arm Injury

**Level 1:** One arm has reduced strength or mobility. Suffer –1 die on Finesse Checks, climbing checks, and grappling checks when the affected arm is needed.

**Level 2:** One arm is severely impaired or unusable. Suffer –2 dice on the affected checks. You cannot perform tasks requiring two functional hands without suitable equipment or assistance.

The Hindrance does not affect actions that do not require the impaired arm.

##### Blindness

**Level 1:** Your vision is significantly impaired. Suffer –1 die on Ranged Combat attacks and –2 dice on sight-dependent Perception Checks when the impairment is not adequately corrected.

**Level 2:** You are blind or have extremely limited functional vision. You cannot automatically identify visual details or targets solely through sight. Attacks against targets whose positions are known suffer –2 dice. You may locate nearby creatures through hearing, touch, or other available senses using appropriate checks.

Assistive equipment and the Blind Fighting Edge may modify these effects where applicable.

##### Clumsy

**Level 1 :** The character is physically awkward, accident-prone, and prone to mishaps, particularly when hurried, distracted, or under pressure. Whenever the character fails a Dexterity-based Skill Check, they may experience a minor mishap in addition to the normal consequences of failure. They might drop a carried object, knock something over, or stumble at an inconvenient moment. 

**Level 2:** The character is physically awkward, may stumble, drop objects, or have difficulty performing tasks requiring balance and precise movements. Whenever the character fails a Dexterity-based Skill Check or a Dexterity-based Attack or Defense Roll, they suffer an additional complication caused by their clumsiness. They might drop their weapon, fall Prone, or require an additional AP to complete an attempted action. 

On a Critical Failure, the mishap becomes more severe, potentially breaking equipment, losing an important item, or exposing the character to immediate danger.

##### Deafness

**Level 1:** Your hearing is reduced. Suffer –2 dice on Perception Checks that depend primarily on hearing.

**Level 2:** You cannot detect sounds through ordinary hearing. Hearing-dependent tasks cannot be completed without an alternative method, such as visual observation, vibration, assistive technology, or communication support.

Deafness does not impose a general penalty on unrelated actions or prevent communication through accessible methods.

##### Epilepsy 

**Level 1:** You experience occasional neurological episodes associated with agreed-upon triggers. When exposed to a significant trigger, make a Resilience/Acuity Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls for 10 minutes.

**Level 2:** Episodes may cause temporary incapacitation. When exposed to a significant trigger, make a Resilience/Acuity Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, become Unconscious for 1 round. At the end of each subsequent round, repeat the Saving Throw to recover. After recovery, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls for 30 minutes.

The player and GM should agree on the character's triggers before play. Episodes should not be introduced arbitrarily to punish a player.

##### Fragile Physique

**Level 1:** Suffer –1 die on all Resilience Saving Throws.

**Level 2:** Reduce your Minor Wound slots by 1, to a minimum of one. Suffer –2 dice on all Resilience Saving Throws.

This Hindrance does not change the Resilience Attribute itself.

##### Heavy Sleeper

**Level 1:** You are difficult to awaken. Suffer –1 die on Perception Checks to notice disturbances while sleeping. During the first 5 minutes after awakening, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks and Attack rolls.

**Level 2:** You are exceptionally difficult to awaken. Suffer –2 dice on Perception Checks to notice disturbances while sleeping. During the first 10 minutes after awakening, suffer –2 dice on Skill Checks and Attack rolls.

##### Leg Injury

**Level 1:** An injury or impairment reduces your mobility. Reduce your normal movement by 5 feet per Move action.

**Level 2:** Your mobility is significantly reduced. Your normal movement is halved, rounding down to the nearest 5 feet.

Appropriate mobility aids or equipment may reduce these restrictions with GM approval.

##### Low Pain Tolerance

**Level 1:** Once per combat, when you mark your second Minor Wound slot, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls until the end of your next turn.

**Level 2:** Once per combat, when you mark any Minor Wound slot, make a Resilience Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, gain the Stunned condition for 1 round. Whether the Saving Throw succeeds or fails, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls until all Minor Wounds have been healed.

##### Slow Reflexes

**Level 1:** During the first round of combat, suffer –1 die on Defense rolls made before your first turn.

**Level 2:** During the first round of combat, suffer –2 dice on Defense rolls made before your first turn. Also suffer –1 die on Dexterity Saving Throws that depend on sudden reactions.

This Hindrance does not change initiative or cause the Game Master to roll Defense for opponents.

#### Mental & Emotional Challenges

These Hindrances represent personality traits, persistent fears, habits, emotional difficulties, or behavioral tendencies.

They should create meaningful complications without allowing the GM to dictate the character's decisions.

##### Addiction

**Level 1:** Choose a specific dependency or recurring vice. When confronted with a significant temptation, make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes) to resist. On failure, suffer –1 die on Intelligence- and Acuity-based Skill Checks for the scene if you choose not to indulge.

**Level 2:** Your dependency creates substantial difficulties. Make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw when exposed to a significant temptation. On failure, suffer –2 dice on Skill Checks and Attack rolls for the scene if you choose not to indulge. Prolonged deprivation may create additional narrative complications agreed upon by the player and GM.

The specific triggers and effects should be established before play.

##### Arrogant

**Level 1:** Your pride makes cooperation difficult. Suffer –1 die on Influence Checks when dealing with peers or authority figures who perceive your arrogance.

**Level 2:** Your arrogance regularly undermines relationships. Suffer –2 dice on Influence Checks involving peers, authority figures, or allies who resent your behavior.

##### Compulsive Behavior

**Level 1:** Choose a recurring habit or ritual and pick a mental Attribute (Intelligence, Acuity, or Presence). When you suffer a Failure using that Attribute during a noncombat Skill Check, become Distracted for 1 minute while dealing with the compulsion.

**Level 2:** Your compulsion is more disruptive. Choose a recurring habit or ritual and pick a mental Attribute (Intelligence, Acuity, or Presence). When you suffer a Failure using that attribute during a Skill Check or Combat, become Distracted and feel compelled to perform the chosen behavior. At the end of each turn, you may make an Acuity Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes) to end the effect.

The behavior should be specific and agreed upon before play.

##### Cowardly

**Level 1:** You hesitate when danger becomes immediate. Suffer –1 die on your first Attack roll during each combat encounter.

**Level 2:** When directly threatened, make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, gain the Frightened condition for 1 round. This check is required only once per encounter.

##### Curious

**Level 1:** Unanswered mysteries distract you. When you deliberately abandon an accessible and significant mystery, suffer –1 die to any checks for that scene that don't pertain to pursuing the clues.

**Level 2:** You find it exceptionally difficult to leave mysteries unresolved. When you abandon an accessible and significant mystery, suffer –2 dice to any checks for that scene that don't pertain to pursuing the clues.

The player remains free to decide whether pursuing the mystery is worth the danger.

##### Depression

**Level 1:** During a period of significant emotional strain agreed upon by the player and GM, make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –1 die on noncombat Skill Checks for the scene.

**Level 2:** During a period of significant emotional strain, make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw. On failure, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls for the scene.

This Hindrance should not require a daily random roll or be used to remove a character's motivation or participation without the player's agreement.

##### Greedy

**Level 1:** You find valuable opportunities difficult to ignore. When refusing a significant and plausible financial opportunity, make an Acuity/Intelligence Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –1 die on your next Skill Check as the missed opportunity distracts you.

**Level 2:** Wealth and possessions strongly influence your judgment. When refusing a significant financial opportunity, make an Acuity/Intelligence Saving Throw. On failure, suffer –2 dice on your next Skill Check. You also suffer –1 die on Investigation Checks to recognize financial scams when personal profit is the principal temptation.

##### Obsession

**Level 1:** Choose a particular goal, person, mystery, or pursuit. When you deliberately abandon a meaningful opportunity to pursue your obsession, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks for the remainder of the scene.

**Level 2:** Your obsession dominates your attention. Under the same circumstances, suffer –2 dice on Skill Checks for the remainder of the scene.

The GM may present temptations and complications related to the obsession but does not take control of the character's decisions.

##### Overconfident

**Level 1:** You regularly underestimate danger. When attempting to withdraw from a confrontation you previously insisted on pursuing, make an Acuity/Intelligence Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –1 die on your next action because of hesitation.

**Level 2:** You find admitting defeat especially difficult. Under the same circumstances, suffer –2 dice on your next action if the Acuity/Intelligence Saving Throw fails.

The character may still retreat or surrender.

##### Pacifist

**Level 1:** You strongly prefer peaceful solutions. Suffer –1 die on Close Combat and Ranged Combat attacks intended to injure another person.

**Level 2:** You are deeply opposed to harming others. Suffer –2 dice on attacks intended to injure another person. You may defend yourself, escape, assist allies, or attempt nonviolent resolutions without this penalty.

##### Paranoia

**Level 1 :** The character is deeply suspicious of others, frequently questioning their motives and interpreting innocent actions as potential threats. When the character fails a Social Skill Check involving trust, cooperation, or negotiation, their suspicions create an additional complication. They might accuse someone of dishonesty, refuse reasonable assistance, or reveal information that causes an otherwise friendly person to become suspicious of them. 

**Level 2:** The character is deeply suspicious of others, struggle to trust strangers, allies, and authority figures, often reacting with hostility, secrecy, or excessive caution. Whenever the character fails a Social Skill Check involving another person's intentions, trust, or cooperation, their paranoia causes a significant complication. They might accuse an ally of betrayal, refuse to cooperate with a trusted companion, or interpret a harmless action as a deliberate threat. 

On a Critical Failure, the character may openly turn against an ally, sabotage a cooperative effort, or provoke a confrontation with someone who meant them no harm.

##### Phobia

**Level 1:** Choose a specific object, creature, place, or situation that provokes intense fear. While directly confronted with the source of your phobia, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls.

**Level 2:** When directly confronted with the source of your phobia, make an Acuity/Intelligence Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, gain the Frightened condition for 1 round. While the source remains present, suffer –1 die on Skill Checks, Attack rolls, and Defense rolls.

##### Self-Doubt

**Level 1:** When attempting a significant action under intense pressure, make a Presence/Acuity Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –1 die on the associated Skill Check.

**Level 2:** Under the same circumstances, make a Presence/Acuity Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –2 dice on the associated Skill Check or Attack roll.

The Saving Throw is made only once for a particular action.

##### Short Attention Span

**Level 1:** Suffer –1 die on Skill Checks requiring more than 10 minutes of sustained concentration.

**Level 2:** Suffer –2 dice on Skill Checks requiring more than 5 minutes of sustained concentration.

This penalty applies to continuous attention, not simply to activities that take a long time.

##### Unstable Temper 

**Level 1:** When you suffer a Failure during a tense social confrontation, make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –1 die on your next Influence Check as anger interferes with your judgment.

**Level 2:** When directly insulted or provoked during a significant confrontation, make an Acuity/Resilience Saving Throw at Normal Difficulty (2 successes). On failure, suffer –2 dice on Influence Checks for the remainder of the scene unless you withdraw from the confrontation or take a meaningful action to regain composure.

This Hindrance does not require the character to attack another person.

#### Social & Financial Challenges

These Hindrances represent disadvantages involving reputation, communication, financial resources, and relationships.

##### Honesty

**Level 1:** You strongly dislike deception. Suffer –1 die on Influence Checks when deliberately lying.

**Level 2:** You refuse to knowingly tell direct lies. If you deliberately violate this principle, suffer –2 dice on Influence Checks for the remainder of the scene because of guilt or hesitation.

You may remain silent, refuse to answer, or attempt to communicate without deception.

##### Inept Communicator

**Level 1:** You have difficulty expressing yourself clearly. Suffer –1 die on Influence and Performance Checks that depend on verbal communication.

**Level 2:** Your communication difficulties regularly cause misunderstandings. Suffer –2 dice on Influence and Performance Checks that depend on verbal communication.

##### Poverty/Debt

**Level 1:** You possess limited financial resources. Reduce your starting Wealth by one category, or to the minimum permitted by the Wealth rules. You may also have modest recurring debts or financial obligations.

**Level 2:** You have substantial debts or financial obligations. Reduce your starting Wealth by two categories, or to the minimum permitted by the Wealth rules. At least once per adventure, the GM may introduce a significant complication involving repayment, creditors, or financial pressure.

This Hindrance does not automatically remove essential campaign equipment provided by an employer, military organization, or patron.

##### Social Awkwardness

**Level 1:** You have difficulty interpreting social cues. Suffer –1 die on Influence Checks requiring tact and Perception Checks to interpret subtle social behavior.

**Level 2:** You regularly misinterpret or mishandle social situations. Suffer –2 dice on Influence Checks requiring tact and Perception Checks to interpret social behavior.

##### Untrustworthy Reputation

**Level 1:** Your reputation causes others to question your reliability. Suffer –1 die on Influence Checks involving people who know your reputation.

**Level 2:** Your reputation is widely known and seriously damages trust. Suffer –2 dice on Influence Checks involving people who know your reputation.

This penalty does not automatically apply to strangers who have never heard of you.

#### Miscellaneous

##### Unreliable Equipment

**Level 1:** Choose one important piece of equipment or a closely related set of equipment. When a Skill Check or Attack roll using that equipment results in a Failure, roll 1d6. On a result of 1, the equipment temporarily malfunctions and requires an appropriate action or repair to restore.

**Level 2:** Under the same circumstances, the equipment malfunctions on a 1–3. Restoring it requires an appropriate Crafting or Technology Check, depending on the equipment.

The Game Master determines the repair Difficulty based on the malfunction. Permanent destruction should result only from a separate, clearly established consequence.

##### Wanted

**Level 1:** A local authority, organization, or individual is actively seeking you. The GM may introduce complications involving identification, questioning, pursuit, or restricted access when the relevant pursuers could reasonably recognize or locate you.

**Level 2:** You are pursued by a powerful organization or dangerous adversary with substantial resources. The GM should introduce at least one meaningful complication related to the pursuit during each adventure in which the pursuers can reasonably reach you.

The player and GM should establish who is pursuing the character, why, and the geographic or institutional reach of the threat.

#### Other Potential Hindrances

The following are suggestions for additional Hindrances that may be developed with Game Master approval:

- Impulsive
- Stubborn
- Vengeful
- Enemy
- Infamous
- Obligation
- Outsider
- Dependents
- Oathbound
- Unlucky

These are concepts rather than complete rules. Each requires defined Level 1 and Level 2 effects before being used.

### Creating Custom Hindrances

The existing Hindrances cannot represent every possible weakness, obligation, or personal complication.

With Game Master approval, players may create custom Hindrances appropriate to their characters and the campaign.

A custom Hindrance should create meaningful disadvantages without making a character ineffective or removing the player's ability to participate.

#### Level 1 Hindrances

A Level 1 Hindrance represents a minor or moderate disadvantage.

Appropriate effects include:

- A –1 die penalty in specific circumstances.
- An occasional Saving Throw to resist a defined complication.
- Reduced access to equipment or resources.
- A recurring obligation or social difficulty.
- A modest limitation requiring preparation or assistance.

**Example — Poor Night Vision (Level 1):** Suffer –1 die on sight-dependent Perception Checks in darkness or poor lighting.

#### Level 2 Hindrances

A Level 2 Hindrance represents a more significant disadvantage.

Appropriate effects include:

- A –2 dice penalty in specific circumstances.
- A substantial limitation on particular activities.
- A more frequent or consequential complication.
- A significant financial, social, or personal obligation.
- A restriction that requires specialized equipment or assistance.

**Example — Poor Night Vision (Level 2):** Suffer –2 dice on sight-dependent Perception Checks in darkness or poor lighting.

#### Guidelines for Custom Hindrances

**Hindrances Should Matter.** A Hindrance should create meaningful complications during the campaign. Fear of space travel matters in a science fiction campaign but is unlikely to matter in a historical adventure with no space travel.

**Avoid Trivial Disadvantages.** Players should not gain Edge Points for limitations that are unlikely to affect their characters.

**Avoid Excessive Penalties.** A Hindrance should not prevent a character from participating in common activities unless the player and GM deliberately agree to that limitation.

**Preserve Player Agency.** Hindrances may create temptations, consequences, or penalties, but they should not routinely transfer control of a character's decisions to the GM.

**Use Existing Mechanics.** Prefer defined dice penalties, Saving Throws, Conditions, resource limitations, and narrative complications.

**Consider Accommodations.** Equipment, assistance, treatment, or other circumstances may reduce a Hindrance's practical impact. If a limitation is effectively eliminated throughout the campaign, the GM should reconsider whether it warrants Edge Points.

**Avoid Double Penalties.** A Hindrance should not impose an additional penalty when the same limitation is already fully represented by another rule, unless the description explicitly requires both effects.

**Require Game Master Approval.** The player and GM should agree on the Hindrance's triggers, effects, severity, and possible accommodations before play.

A custom Hindrance that proves substantially more or less restrictive than expected may be adjusted between sessions.

### Changing or Overcoming Hindrances

A character's circumstances may change during a campaign. Injuries may heal, debts may be repaid, reputations may improve, and personal weaknesses may become easier to manage.

When a Hindrance is permanently overcome, the player and GM should agree on an appropriate advancement or story-based resolution. Changes should reflect meaningful character development rather than arbitrary penalties.

**Hindrances are not punishments. They are opportunities to create memorable characters whose weaknesses matter as much as their strengths.**
