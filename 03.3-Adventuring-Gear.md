## Adventuring Gear <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Not every challenge can be solved with a weapon.

Adventuring Gear includes the tools, supplies, and specialized equipment characters rely upon to explore dangerous environments, overcome obstacles, investigate mysteries, and survive in the wilderness or hostile worlds.

A reliable rope may save a climber from a deadly fall. A medical kit may allow a physician to stabilize a wounded companion. A radio may permit an expedition to coordinate its movements, while a simple mirror may reveal an enemy waiting around a corner.

Rather than cataloging every possible object, _Journey Through the Ages_ organizes Adventuring Gear into broad categories based on its purpose.

The appearance and technology of individual items may change between eras, but the principles governing their use remain consistent.

### Using Adventuring Gear

Equipment supports a character's abilities rather than replacing them.

An item may provide one or more of the following benefits:

**Enable an Action:** Some tasks cannot reasonably be attempted without appropriate equipment.

**Remove a Limitation:** Equipment may eliminate a specific obstacle, such as darkness, insufficient reach, or a lack of communication.

**Improve an Action:** Specialized or exceptional equipment may provide a bonus to an appropriate Skill Check.

**Reduce Difficulty:** Suitable equipment may make a task easier by improving the conditions under which it is performed.

**Provide Information:** Observation, navigation, and diagnostic tools may reveal information that a character could not otherwise obtain.

**Protect Against Hazards:** Certain equipment provides protection against environmental dangers or other specific threats.

The Game Master determines which benefits apply based on the equipment's capabilities and the circumstances.

### Equipment and Skill Checks

When a character uses equipment to perform a challenging task, make a Skill Check using the appropriate Attribute and Skill.

Common examples include:

|Task|Typical Skill|
|---|---|
|Climbing with ropes and specialized gear|Athletics|
|Navigating unfamiliar wilderness|Survival|
|Treating an injury|Medicine|
|Repairing a mechanical device|Crafting|
|Diagnosing an electronic malfunction|Technology|
|Searching for hidden evidence|Investigation|
|Observing distant activity|Perception|
|Bypassing a mechanical lock|Finesse|
|Bypassing electronic security|Technology|

The Game Master may select a different Skill when the character's approach justifies it.

Routine use of familiar equipment does not normally require a roll.

A character using a flashlight to illuminate a dark room, for example, does not need to make a Skill Check merely to switch it on.

### Equipment Requirements

Some tasks require specialized equipment.

A character may be unable to perform complex surgery without medical instruments or repair a damaged engine without suitable tools.

When equipment is essential and no reasonable alternative exists, the task cannot be attempted. If the character possesses inadequate or improvised equipment, the Game Master may permit an attempt with an increased Difficulty or a dice penalty.

The Game Master should choose one appropriate disadvantage rather than automatically applying both.

### Equipment Categories

Most Adventuring Gear belongs to one of the following categories.

#### Climbing & Exploration

Climbing and exploration equipment helps characters traverse difficult terrain, cross obstacles, descend into dangerous locations, and explore unfamiliar environments.

##### Examples

- Rope
- Grappling hook
- Climbing harness
- Pitons
- Hammer
- Ascenders
- Portable ladder
- Survival line launcher

##### Using Climbing Equipment

Climbing equipment normally supports Athletics Checks.

Appropriate equipment may make a climb possible, reduce its Difficulty, or provide protection against a fall.

A rope and harness, for example, may prevent a character from falling a great distance even when a climbing attempt fails.

The Game Master should distinguish between equipment that makes climbing easier and equipment that provides protection when something goes wrong.

#### Illumination

Illumination equipment provides light in darkness or poor visibility.

##### Examples

- Torch
- Lantern
- Oil lamp
- Flashlight
- Headlamp
- Chemical light
- Portable floodlight

##### Using Illumination Equipment

Adequate illumination may eliminate penalties associated with darkness or allow characters to perform tasks requiring clear vision.

Different light sources have different limitations.

A torch requires fuel and produces an open flame. A flashlight depends on batteries or another power source. A floodlight may illuminate a large area but be difficult to conceal.

Light can also reveal a character's location.

The Game Master determines the illuminated area, duration, and visibility of each device based on its construction and the environment.

#### Navigation

Navigation equipment helps characters determine their location, establish direction, plan routes, and avoid becoming lost.

##### Examples

- Compass
- Sextant
- Maps
- Charts
- GPS receiver
- Star charts
- Planetary scanner

##### Using Navigation Equipment

Navigation commonly involves Survival, although Academics or Technology may be appropriate for specialized methods.

A compass can establish direction, while a map provides geographic information.

A sextant requires suitable observations and navigational knowledge. A GPS receiver may provide precise location information when its supporting systems are available.

Navigation equipment does not automatically guarantee accurate results.

Maps may be outdated, electronic systems may lose their signal, and unfamiliar terrain may contain unexpected hazards.

#### Medical Equipment

Medical equipment provides the instruments and supplies necessary to treat injuries, stabilize patients, and assist recovery.

##### Examples

- Bandages
- Trauma kit
- Surgical instruments
- First aid kit
- Field medic bag
- Auto-injector
- Nanomedical pack

##### Using Medical Equipment

Medical equipment normally supports Medicine Checks.

The equipment required depends on the injury and the treatment being attempted.

A basic first aid kit may be sufficient for treating minor injuries, while complex surgery may require specialized instruments and a suitable environment.

Advanced medical technology may provide additional capabilities appropriate to the setting.

However, equipment does not replace medical training.

The Game Master determines whether the available supplies are adequate for the proposed treatment.

Medical equipment does not automatically restore Wound Slots or remove Conditions unless a specific rule permits it.

#### Repair Tools

Repair equipment allows characters to maintain, diagnose, and restore damaged objects, machinery, and technological systems.

##### Examples

- Blacksmith tools
- Mechanic's toolkit
- Electronics kit
- Engineering tools
- Welding equipment
- Nanofabrication kit

##### Using Repair Tools

The appropriate Skill depends on the nature of the equipment.

**Crafting:** Generally applies to physical construction, fabrication, mechanical repair, and traditional trades.

**Technology:** Generally applies to electronic systems, computers, advanced technical equipment, and other specialized technological systems.

Some complex repairs may involve both Skills.

The Game Master determines whether a repair is possible, what equipment is required, and how much time the work takes.

##### Repair Limitations

Repair tools do not automatically restore a damaged item to perfect condition.

A repair may require replacement parts, raw materials, a suitable workspace, or specialized expertise.

Certain damage may be beyond the capabilities of a portable toolkit.

Vehicle repairs follow any additional procedures established in the vehicle and damage rules.

#### Observation Equipment

Observation equipment improves a character's ability to examine distant objects, detect activity, or gather information without approaching directly.

##### Examples

- Binoculars
- Spyglass
- Telescope
- Thermal optics
- Motion scanner
- Drone camera

##### Using Observation Equipment

Observation equipment normally supports Perception Checks.

Specialized equipment may allow a character to detect information unavailable to ordinary senses.

For example, binoculars may reveal details at a distance, while thermal optics may reveal heat signatures under suitable conditions.

However, every device has limitations. Fog, smoke, obstructions, interference, weather, and other environmental conditions may reduce its effectiveness.

The Game Master determines what information the equipment can reasonably provide.

#### Communication Equipment

Communication equipment allows characters to exchange information, coordinate actions, and maintain contact across distance.

##### Examples

- Messenger birds
- Signal flags
- Field telephone
- Radio
- Satellite communicator
- Secure communicator
- Quantum communicator

##### Using Communication Equipment

Communication equipment normally requires no Skill Check during routine operation.

A Skill Check may be necessary when:

- Establishing contact under difficult conditions
- Repairing a damaged communication device
- Identifying interference
- Intercepting or analyzing a signal
- Bypassing communication security
- Operating unfamiliar or highly specialized equipment

Technology is normally appropriate for electronic communication systems.

Other methods may rely on Academics, Survival, or another suitable Skill.

##### Communication Limitations

Communication devices vary in range, reliability, and security.

Possible limitations include:

- Distance
- Terrain and physical obstructions
- Weather
- Signal interference
- Power requirements
- Network availability
- Interception
- Encryption and authentication

The Game Master establishes these limitations according to the campaign setting.

A communication device does not automatically provide instantaneous or secure communication simply because it is technologically advanced.

#### Security Equipment

Security equipment allows characters to secure locations, bypass barriers, defeat locks, or interact with protective systems.

##### Examples

- Lockpicks
- Skeleton keys
- Electronic bypass kit
- Encryption tools
- Portable hacking device
- Demolition charges

##### Using Security Equipment

The Skill used depends on the security system and the character's approach.

|Task|Typical Skill|
|---|---|
|Picking a mechanical lock|Finesse|
|Examining a security mechanism|Investigation|
|Bypassing an electronic lock|Technology|
|Analyzing digital security|Technology|
|Disabling a mechanical trap|Finesse or Crafting|
|Disabling an electronic trap|Technology|
|Preparing a controlled demolition|Technology or Crafting, as appropriate|

The Game Master determines the required equipment and Difficulty.

Some systems cannot be bypassed without specialized tools, access credentials, or information.

##### Bypassing Versus Destroying

Opening a lock without damaging it is different from breaking down a door or destroying a security device.

The Game Master should use the rules appropriate to the chosen method.

Demolition charges and other destructive equipment may damage structures or equipment, but they also create noise, danger, and potentially serious consequences.

#### Survival Equipment

Survival equipment helps characters endure exposure, hunger, thirst, harsh weather, and other environmental dangers.

##### Examples

- Bedroll
- Tent
- Cooking gear
- Water purifier
- Fire starter
- Emergency rations
- Environmental suit

##### Using Survival Equipment

Survival equipment normally supports Survival Checks and other actions related to environmental hazards.

Appropriate supplies may reduce the Difficulty of finding shelter, preparing food, obtaining safe drinking water, or surviving extreme conditions.

Certain equipment may prevent exposure to a hazard entirely.

For example, a properly functioning environmental suit may allow a character to operate in conditions that would otherwise be immediately dangerous.

The equipment must be appropriate to the environment.

A cold-weather coat does not protect against vacuum, and an ordinary respirator may not provide breathable air in an oxygen-free atmosphere.

### Carrying Equipment

Characters may carry a reasonable amount of equipment appropriate to their Strength (STR) without requiring detailed weight calculations.

The standard rules use a simplified approach to carrying capacity.

The Game Master should consider an item's size, weight, bulk, and how it is carried.

#### Load Categories

|Load|Description|Typical Effect|
|---|---|---|
|Light|Ordinary personal gear and small items|No penalty|
|Bulky|Heavy equipment or awkward objects|May restrict movement or occupy both hands|
|Excessive|A load beyond what the character can comfortably carry|May require a Skill Check or impose penalties|

A character's Strength helps determine what can reasonably be carried.

##### Light Loads

Light loads include normal clothing, small tools, personal equipment, and ordinary supplies.

These items do not normally affect movement or Skill Checks.

##### Bulky Loads

Bulky loads include large packs, heavy containers, substantial equipment, or objects that are difficult to maneuver.

The Game Master may require the character to use both hands, move more slowly, or temporarily set down the item before performing certain actions. Movement is reduced by 10 feet per round.

##### Excessive Loads

An excessive load may require an Athletics Check or assistance from other characters.

**Recommended optional penalty:** A character carrying an excessive load suffers –1 die on Athletics and Dexterity-based Skill Checks and moves 15 feet less per Move Action.

The Game Master may prohibit carrying a load that is clearly beyond the character's physical capabilities.

These optional penalties should not be combined with additional arbitrary encumbrance penalties for the same load.

#### When to Track Encumbrance

Detailed carrying restrictions are most useful when they create meaningful choices.

Examples include:

- Escaping with heavy treasure
- Carrying an injured companion
- Transporting supplies across dangerous terrain
- Moving bulky equipment through confined spaces
- Choosing what to abandon during an emergency

If carrying capacity is not important to the adventure, the Game Master may ignore encumbrance.

### Equipment Quality

Individual items may vary in craftsmanship, durability, precision, and reliability.

Equipment Quality describes these differences.

|Quality|Description|
|---|---|
|Poor|Cheap, damaged, or unreliable equipment|
|Standard|Ordinary equipment suitable for its intended purpose|
|Superior|Well-crafted equipment built to professional standards|
|Masterwork|Exceptional equipment of outstanding quality or advanced design|

#### Poor Quality

Poor-quality equipment may be worn, damaged, unreliable, or poorly suited to its intended purpose.

**Recommended optional effect:** Suffer –1 die on Skill Checks when the item's poor condition materially interferes with the task.

A poorly made rope may also be more likely to fail under unusual strain, while damaged instruments may provide inaccurate information.

#### Standard Quality

Standard equipment functions normally and provides no additional modifier.

Most equipment encountered during play is Standard Quality.

#### Superior Quality

Superior equipment is well manufactured, carefully maintained, or specifically designed for professional use.

It may offer greater reliability, precision, durability, or ease of operation.

**Recommended optional effect:** Gain +1 die on Skill Checks when the equipment's superior quality directly contributes to the task.

A superior toolkit, for example, may improve a repair attempt.

#### Masterwork Quality

Masterwork equipment represents exceptional craftsmanship, precision, or technological sophistication.

Such equipment may possess capabilities beyond ordinary items of its type.

**Recommended optional effect:** Gain +2 dice on Skill Checks when the equipment's exceptional quality directly contributes to the task.

Masterwork equipment may also have a distinctive narrative benefit, such as exceptional durability or specialized functionality.

#### Applying Equipment Quality

Quality bonuses should be used selectively.

An item's quality does not automatically improve every action involving it.

For example, a Masterwork compass may be exceptionally accurate, but it does not grant +2 dice on unrelated Survival Checks.

When multiple items could provide equipment bonuses to the same check, use only the highest applicable equipment bonus.

Bonuses from Edges or other abilities follow their own rules. Equipment Quality does not alter the Weapon Quality or Armor rules in Sections 3.1 and 3.2 unless a specific rule permits it.

### Improvised Equipment

Characters frequently solve problems by using ordinary objects in unexpected ways.

A crowbar may force open a locked door. A blanket may smother a small fire. A mirror may reveal what lies around a corner.

Improvisation should be encouraged when the proposed action is reasonable.

#### Using Improvised Equipment

When a character improvises a tool:

1. Determine what the character is attempting.
2. Decide whether the improvised item could reasonably accomplish the task.
3. Select an appropriate Attribute and Skill.
4. Establish the Difficulty and any relevant limitations.
5. Resolve the action normally.

A well-chosen improvised tool may function as effectively as specialized equipment for a simple task.

For more demanding tasks, the Game Master may increase the Difficulty or impose a penalty.

**Recommended optional penalty:** Improvised equipment that is clearly inadequate but still usable imposes –1 die on the relevant Skill Check.

An improvised item cannot perform a task that its physical capabilities do not permit.

### Equipment Availability

Not every item exists in every campaign.

The Game Master determines which equipment is available based on the setting's technological development, location, economy, and circumstances.

|Setting|Typical Equipment|
|---|---|
|Historical|Torches, rope, horses, sailing tools|
|Modern|Radios, GPS receivers, drones, tactical equipment|
|Science Fiction|Smart tools, nanomedical kits, environmental suits, AI assistants|

These examples are illustrative rather than exhaustive.

A historical campaign set in the nineteenth century may have very different equipment from one set in the medieval period.

Likewise, a science fiction campaign may feature advanced computers without possessing faster-than-light communication or nanotechnology.

#### Availability and Wealth

An item may exist in the setting without being available for purchase.

Specialized equipment may be:

- Rare
- Expensive
- Restricted
- Illegal
- Controlled by an organization
- Unavailable in the current location

The character's Wealth determines what they can afford, while Availability determines what they can reasonably obtain.

See Section 3.5 for the rules governing Wealth and acquiring equipment.

#### Issued and Borrowed Equipment

Characters may receive equipment from employers, military organizations, patrons, or other sources.

Issued equipment is not necessarily the character's personal property.

Its use may be restricted by the organization providing it.

The Game Master determines the conditions of access, maintenance, replacement, and return.

### Game Master Guidance

Adventuring Gear should expand player creativity rather than restrict it.

The categories in this section are intentionally broad so that equipment from different historical periods can be introduced without requiring extensive new rules.

When introducing a new item, consider:

1. **Purpose:** What does the equipment allow the character to do?
2. **Requirements:** Does the item require training, supplies, power, or specialized knowledge?
3. **Limitations:** What circumstances prevent it from functioning effectively?
4. **Quality:** Is the item ordinary, unreliable, or exceptionally well made?
5. **Availability:** Can the character reasonably obtain it in the campaign?
6. **Mechanical Effect:** Does the item enable an action, remove a limitation, or justify a bonus?

Avoid assigning numerical bonuses to every piece of equipment.

Often, the most important benefit of an item is that it makes an action possible.

A telescope permits distant observation. A rope permits a safer descent. A medical kit provides the supplies needed to treat injuries.

Such capabilities are frequently more meaningful than an additional die.

When a player proposes a creative use for equipment, evaluate the idea according to the item's capabilities and the circumstances.

Reward preparation, ingenuity, and sensible problem-solving.

**The purpose of Adventuring Gear is to give characters more ways to overcome challenges, not to create unnecessary bookkeeping or restrict imaginative play.**
