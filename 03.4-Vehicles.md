## Vehicles

Vehicles allow characters to travel farther, faster, and often more safely than they could on foot.

Whether crossing the Caribbean aboard a brigantine, racing through city streets in an automobile, piloting a fighter aircraft, or commanding an interstellar spacecraft, vehicles play an important role in exploration, combat, and adventure.

Vehicles can also become central elements of a campaign. A ship may serve as the party's home, an automobile may be essential to an investigation, and a spacecraft may provide the means to travel between distant worlds.

_Journey Through the Ages_ uses a common set of Vehicle Profiles to represent transportation across different eras and technological settings.

These profiles describe a vehicle's capabilities without requiring separate movement and damage systems for every type of transportation.

### Vehicle Categories

Vehicles are organized into broad categories based on how they operate.

|Category|Examples|
|---|---|
|Mounts|Horse, camel, riding lizard, alien beast|
|Ground Vehicles|Wagon, automobile, motorcycle, truck, tank|
|Watercraft|Canoe, sailing ship, submarine, patrol boat|
|Aircraft|Balloon, airplane, helicopter, gunship|
|Spacecraft|Shuttle, freighter, fighter, capital ship|
Each category uses the same general vehicle rules.

However, the Game Master must account for differences in operating environment, technology, and physical capabilities.

A sailing ship, for example, cannot maneuver like a motorboat, and a spacecraft operating in vacuum does not necessarily behave like an aircraft.

The common Vehicle Profile provides a foundation for these differences without requiring every vehicle to follow identical movement restrictions.

### Vehicle Profiles

Every vehicle is described using a common set of statistics.

|Statistic|Description|
|---|---|
|Size|The vehicle's physical scale and general carrying capacity|
|Crew|The number of operators required for normal operation|
|Passengers|The additional occupants the vehicle can safely transport|
|Speed|The vehicle's relative movement capability|
|Handling|How easily the vehicle responds to its operator|
|Durability|The amount of damage the vehicle can withstand|
|Special Features|Capabilities such as amphibious travel, stealth systems, or atmospheric flight|
|Defense (Armor)|The vehicle's protection against physical attacks and other hazards|
|Close Combat Attack (Ram)|The vehicle's capability to strike or collide with another target|
|Ranged Attack|The attack rating used for mounted ranged weapons|
|Weapons|Mounted or integrated weapons, including damage, range, and ammunition requirements|
Additional statistics may be included when appropriate.

#### Size

Size describes a vehicle's overall physical scale.

It helps determine whether the vehicle can pass through restricted spaces, carry large objects, transport passengers, or interact with obstacles. Size may also influence how easily the vehicle can be targeted, as described in the applicable combat rules.

A large vehicle is not automatically more durable than a smaller one. Durability also depends on construction, armor, and design.

#### Crew

Crew indicates the number of operators normally required to control and operate the vehicle.

A motorcycle may require one operator, while a large sailing vessel or spacecraft may need several crew members.

A vehicle operating with fewer than its required crew may be unable to perform certain functions or may suffer penalties determined by the Game Master.

#### Passengers

Passengers indicates how many additional occupants a vehicle can safely transport beyond its operating crew.

The Game Master may permit additional passengers under exceptional circumstances, but overcrowding may restrict movement, access to equipment, or safe operation.

#### Special Features

Special Features describe capabilities that distinguish one vehicle from another.

Examples include:

- Amphibious operation
- Submersible operation
- Atmospheric flight
- Vacuum operation
- Stealth systems
- Advanced sensors
- Environmental protection
- Specialized cargo handling
- Integrated communications

Special Features should identify what the vehicle can actually accomplish rather than merely describe its appearance.

#### Speed Rating

A vehicle's **Speed Rating** represents its relative ability to travel quickly.

Speed Ratings range from 0 to 5.

|Rating|Speed Category|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|0|Stationary|Immobile vehicle or vehicle not moving|
|1|Slow|Camel, draft horse, cargo truck, armored vehicle, fishing boat, trawler, cargo plane, dirigible, cargo shuttle, transport spacecraft|
|2|Average|Riding horse, family sedan, police cruiser, sailboat, patrol boat, light aircraft, helicopter, civilian shuttle, transport spacecraft|
|3|Fast|Racehorse, sports car, motorcycle, speedboat, fighter aircraft, military shuttle, light interceptor|
|4|Very Fast|Formula race car, hydrofoil, racing yacht, supersonic jet, interceptor spacecraft, racing ship|
|5|Extreme|Teleporting speedster, high-speed train, experimental race vehicle, experimental hydrofoil, hypersonic aircraft, missile, military strike craft, courier vessel|
These examples are guidelines.

The Game Master may assign different Speed Ratings to particular models based on their performance.

For example, a heavily loaded transport spacecraft might have Speed 1, while a lighter or more powerful transport might have Speed 2.

##### Using Speed Ratings

Speed Ratings are primarily comparative.

They indicate which vehicles are generally faster when operating under comparable conditions. A higher Speed Rating does not automatically mean that a vehicle moves a fixed number of feet farther during every Action.

The procedures for movement, closing distance, escaping pursuit, and changing relative position are described in the Chase rules.

##### Comparing Different Vehicle Types

Speed Ratings should be interpreted within the vehicle's operating environment.

A Speed 3 automobile and a Speed 3 fighter aircraft are not assumed to travel at the same actual velocity. Their ratings represent relative performance within the scale of the activity.

When comparing very different forms of transportation, the Game Master should consider actual capabilities, terrain, and circumstances. For example, a sports car may be faster than a horse on an open highway but unable to follow it through a narrow mountain trail.

##### Speed and Conditions

A vehicle may be unable to use its full speed because of:

- Rough terrain
- Restricted waterways
- Poor weather
- Mechanical damage
- Heavy cargo
- Limited visibility
- Environmental hazards
- Congested surroundings

The Game Master determines whether these circumstances restrict the vehicle's movement or require an appropriate Skill Check.

#### Handling Rating

A vehicle's **Handling Rating** represents how easily it responds to its operator.

Handling Ratings range from 1 to 5.

| Rating | Handling    | Description                                                           | Examples                                                                                                                                     |
| ------ | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1      | Poor        | Cumbersome, slow to respond, and difficult to maneuver                | Pack mule, overloaded horse, loaded semi-truck, bus, large merchant ship, cargo barge, heavy cargo plane, bulk freighter, mining ship        |
| 2      | Fair        | Capable of ordinary maneuvers but limited during aggressive operation | Draft horse, pickup truck, SUV, fishing boat, workboat, passenger airliner, transport shuttle                                                |
| 3      | Good        | Responsive and reliable under normal operating conditions             | Riding horse, family sedan, police cruiser, modern sailboat, patrol boat, utility helicopter, light aircraft, civilian shuttle, patrol craft |
| 4      | Excellent   | Highly maneuverable and effective during demanding maneuvers          | Agile warhorse, sports car, rally car, racing yacht, fast attack boat, fighter aircraft, aerobatic plane, military interceptor, scout craft  |
| 5      | Exceptional | Capable of extreme maneuvers beyond most comparable vehicles          | Legendary trained mount, Formula race car, America's Cup racing yacht, advanced air-superiority fighter, high-performance starfighter        |
##### Using Handling Ratings

Handling matters when a character attempts to control a vehicle under difficult conditions.

Examples include:

- Making sharp turns at high speed
- Avoiding obstacles
- Maintaining control on hazardous terrain
- Maneuvering during combat
- Landing in difficult conditions
- Navigating confined spaces
- Recovering from a loss of control

The character normally makes an appropriate Vehicle Skill Check.

Handling contributes according to the applicable vehicle maneuver or Chase procedure. A Handling Rating does not automatically replace the operator's Skill.

A highly maneuverable vehicle remains difficult to control in inexperienced hands, while a skilled operator may achieve impressive results with an otherwise cumbersome vehicle.

##### Speed Versus Handling

Speed and Handling represent different capabilities.

A vehicle may be fast but difficult to control.

For example, a powerful cargo aircraft might travel quickly while remaining relatively cumbersome during tight maneuvers.

Likewise, a smaller utility vehicle may be highly maneuverable without possessing exceptional speed.

The Game Master should assign these ratings independently.

#### Vehicle Durability

Vehicles can be damaged by collisions, weapons fire, environmental hazards, mechanical failures, and other dangerous events.

Rather than using hit points, vehicles track damage through **Durability Slots**, similar to the Wound Slots used by characters. Each damaging event inflicts a Damage Level. When a vehicle suffers damage, mark one available Durability Slot at that level.

If all slots at that level are filled, the damage escalates to the next higher level.

##### Vehicle Durability Slots

Unlike character Wound Slots, the number of vehicle Durability Slots is determined by the Game Master.

The number of slots depends on the vehicle's:

- Size
- Construction
- Armor
- Structural strength
- Intended purpose
- General resistance to damage

A lightly built vehicle may have relatively few slots, while a heavily armored or exceptionally robust vehicle may have considerably more.

|Damage Level|Number of Slots|
|---|---|
|Minor|GM Determined|
|Serious|GM Determined|
|Critical|GM Determined|
|Destroyed|1|
Every standard vehicle has one Destroyed slot.

#### Vehicle Damage Levels

##### Minor Damage

Minor Damage represents superficial damage or limited mechanical wear.

Examples include:

- Dented body panels
- Cracked windshield
- Flat tire
- Damaged rigging
- Superficial hull scoring
- Sensor glitch
- Worn suspension

Minor Damage does not normally prevent operation.

However, repeated Minor Damage can eventually escalate into more serious structural or mechanical problems.

##### Serious Damage

Serious Damage represents damage that noticeably affects a vehicle's performance.

Examples include:

- Damaged engine
- Broken axle
- Damaged steering
- Cracked mast
- Ruptured fuel line
- Damaged wing
- Thruster malfunction
- Hull breach

A vehicle suffering Serious Damage may require repairs before normal operation can resume.

The Game Master determines which systems are affected based on the cause and location of the damage.

##### Critical Damage

Critical Damage represents major system failures that leave the vehicle barely operational.

Examples include:

- Engine fire
- Transmission failure
- Destroyed rudder
- Collapsed landing gear
- Reactor instability
- Life-support failure
- Catastrophic hull breach
- Multiple system failures

A critically damaged vehicle may be unable to perform certain maneuvers or may require immediate repairs to avoid further consequences.

Continued operation can be extremely dangerous.

##### Destroyed

A vehicle that suffers Destroyed-level damage is no longer operational.

Examples include:

- Automobile totaled
- Wagon shattered
- Sailing vessel sunk
- Aircraft crashed
- Spacecraft broken apart or drifting powerless
- Tank knocked out

Destroyed does not necessarily mean that every part of the vehicle has been physically annihilated. The vehicle may remain largely intact but be incapable of performing its intended function.

Occupants may survive depending on the nature of the damage and the circumstances.

#### Damage Escalation

Damage escalates when no available slot remains at the level inflicted.

Apply the following procedure:

1. Determine the Damage Level inflicted.
2. Check for an empty Durability Slot at that level.
3. If a slot is available, mark it.
4. If no slot is available, increase the damage by one level.
5. Continue until an empty slot is found.

Damage progresses in the following order:

**Minor → Serious → Critical → Destroyed**

If a vehicle has zero slots at a particular level, damage immediately escalates to the next level.

##### Example: Damage to a Family Car

A family car has:

- 3 Minor Damage slots
- 2 Serious Damage slots
- 1 Critical Damage slot
- 1 Destroyed slot

During a chase, the car suffers three Minor Damage results from collisions and weapons fire. All three Minor slots are now filled.

When the car suffers another Minor Damage result, it escalates to Serious Damage because no Minor slot remains available. The player marks one Serious Damage slot.

If the car continues taking damage, additional results may eventually fill its Serious and Critical slots.

A later damage result that reaches the Destroyed level renders the car inoperable.

##### Damage Beyond Destroyed

The standard Durability Table provides only one Destroyed slot.

If a vehicle already has its Destroyed slot filled, it is no longer operational. Any further damage is resolved according to the circumstances and the applicable combat or environmental rules. Additional damage may cause a wreck to burn, sink, break apart, or become more dangerous to its occupants.

The Game Master should not treat additional damage as requiring another nonexistent Durability Slot.

#### Vehicle Damage and Performance

Not every damaged vehicle behaves the same way.

A damaged wheel may affect steering, while a damaged engine may reduce speed. A ruptured hull may threaten the occupants of a watercraft or spacecraft.

When Serious or Critical Damage occurs, the Game Master should describe an appropriate consequence based on the vehicle and the source of the damage.

Possible consequences include:

- Reduced Speed
- Impaired Handling
- Loss of propulsion
- Disabled weapons
- Damaged communications
- Reduced visibility
- Compromised environmental protection
- Loss of control
- Increased risk of further damage

The precise mechanical effects should follow the applicable vehicle damage, repair, and combat rules.

Avoid automatically applying every possible penalty to a single damage result.

### Operating Vehicles

Routine vehicle operation does not normally require a Skill Check.

Characters trained to operate a vehicle can generally perform ordinary travel and maneuvers under favorable conditions without rolling dice.

A Vehicle Skill Check is appropriate when the outcome is uncertain and failure would have meaningful consequences.

#### Vehicle Skill Checks

Vehicle Checks may be required during:

- High-speed pursuits
- Hazardous weather
- Rough terrain
- Emergency maneuvers
- Vehicle combat
- Landing damaged aircraft
- Navigating asteroid fields
- Sailing through storms

The Game Master determines the appropriate Attribute, Skill, Difficulty, and applicable modifiers.

**Dexterity + Vehicle** is generally appropriate for immediate control, steering, piloting, and evasive maneuvers.

**Intelligence + Vehicle** may be appropriate for navigation, route planning, and other tasks emphasizing knowledge or calculation.

The Game Master may allow other Attribute combinations when justified by the situation.

##### Example: Emergency Landing

A pilot attempts to land a damaged aircraft during a storm.

The Game Master calls for a Dexterity + Vehicle Check.

The aircraft's Handling Rating and damage condition are considered under the applicable maneuver rules. A successful check allows the pilot to complete the landing to the extent established by the result.

Failure may result in a rough landing, additional damage, or another appropriate complication.

##### Example: Navigating a Dangerous Passage

A sailing vessel must pass through a narrow channel filled with submerged rocks.

The navigator may use Intelligence + Vehicle to identify a safe route.

The helmsman may then use Dexterity + Vehicle to maneuver through the passage.

These represent different tasks and need not always require separate checks.

The Game Master should call for additional rolls only when they introduce meaningful uncertainty.

#### Vehicle Weapons

Mounted weapons use the appropriate weapon statistics and Special Rules.

Examples include:

- Shipboard cannon
- Machine guns
- Tank guns
- Aircraft weapons
- Missile launchers
- Spacecraft weapons

A vehicle's weapons may have limitations based on ammunition, crew requirements, mounting position, and operating conditions.

Vehicle Combat and Chase rules govern the resolution of these attacks and maneuvers.

#### Vehicle Ownership

Vehicles may be acquired or made available to characters in several ways.

A vehicle may be:

- Personally owned
- Rented
- Borrowed
- Issued by an employer or military organization
- Captured during an adventure
- Shared by the entire group

Ownership and access are not the same.

A soldier may operate a military vehicle without owning it. A pilot may fly a rented aircraft, while a group of adventurers may jointly own a sailing vessel.

The Game Master determines who controls access to a vehicle and who is responsible for its upkeep, repairs, and replacement. In many campaigns, a vehicle serves as the party's primary means of transportation and may become one of its most valuable resources.

#### Vehicle Maintenance

Vehicles require maintenance to remain reliable.

Routine maintenance is normally assumed unless the campaign emphasizes resource management, logistics, or mechanical reliability.

Damage sustained during adventures may require repairs.

##### Repairing Vehicles

Repairs may require:

- Suitable tools
- Replacement parts
- A safe workspace
- Sufficient time
- Specialized knowledge
- Successful Skill Checks

The appropriate Skill depends on the work being performed.

**Crafting:** Mechanical repairs, structural work, and traditional fabrication.

**Technology:** Electronic systems, advanced machinery, sensors, computers, and other technical systems.

**Vehicle:** Operational diagnosis or adjustments requiring specialized knowledge of the vehicle's handling and performance.

The Game Master determines which Skill is most appropriate.

Repairing Durability Slots and restoring damaged vehicle systems follow the applicable repair rules.

##### Temporary Repairs

Some repairs may restore limited functionality without fully correcting the underlying damage.

Examples include:

- Patching a leaking hull
- Reconnecting damaged wiring
- Replacing a broken hose
- Securing damaged rigging
- Bypassing a malfunctioning sensor

The Game Master may allow temporary repairs during an adventure when the characters possess suitable equipment and sufficient time.

Temporary repairs should not automatically restore all lost Durability Slots unless the repair rules explicitly permit it.

#### Vehicle Customization

Many vehicles can be modified to better suit their operators.

Common modifications include:

- Improved engines
- Reinforced armor
- Cargo expansions
- Hidden compartments
- Advanced sensors
- Enhanced communications
- Specialized weapon mounts

Customization options depend on the campaign's technology and available resources.

##### Creating Vehicle Modifications

When a character proposes a modification, the Game Master should determine:

1. Whether the modification is technically possible.
2. What materials and equipment are required.
3. How much time the work takes.
4. Whether a Skill Check is necessary.
5. What mechanical benefit the modification provides.
6. Whether the modification introduces any limitations.

A larger engine may improve Speed but increase fuel consumption.

Additional armor may increase protection but reduce Handling or cargo capacity. Specialized sensors may improve detection but require power or trained operators. Vehicle modifications should involve meaningful trade-offs when appropriate.

The Game Master should avoid granting automatic increases to several vehicle statistics from a single inexpensive modification.

### Starting Vehicles

Some campaigns begin with characters having access to a vehicle.

The vehicle may belong to an individual, an organization, or the entire adventuring group.

|Campaign|Typical Starting Vehicle|
|---|---|
|Pirates|Small sailing vessel|
|Western|Horse or wagon|
|World War II|Military transport or jeep|
|Modern Action|Automobile or motorcycle|
|Science Fiction|Shuttlecraft or merchant freighter|
The Game Master determines which vehicles are appropriate for the campaign.

Starting vehicles should reflect the characters' circumstances, Backgrounds, available resources, and the intended adventures.

A group of ordinary investigators may begin with a modest automobile, while a military unit might receive access to armored transports or aircraft.

A science fiction crew might begin with a small freighter that serves as both transportation and a base of operations.

#### Starting Vehicle Considerations

Before play begins, establish:

- Who owns the vehicle
- Who may operate it
- Whether it requires a crew
- Who pays for fuel and maintenance
- What equipment and weapons it carries
- Whether the vehicle can be repaired or replaced
- What obligations accompany its use

These details help make the vehicle an integrated part of the campaign rather than simply another item of equipment.

### Game Master Guidance

Vehicle Profiles are designed to provide a consistent framework for transportation across different eras.

When creating a new vehicle:

1. Select its Vehicle Category.
2. Determine its Size, Crew, and Passenger capacity.
3. Assign Speed and Handling Ratings.
4. Establish its Durability Slots.
5. Determine its Defense and Attack statistics using the applicable combat rules.
6. Identify any mounted weapons.
7. Assign appropriate Special Features.
8. Establish its technological limitations and resource requirements.

The Game Master should consider the vehicle's intended role rather than relying on size or technological sophistication alone.

A small racing craft may have excellent Speed and Handling but limited Durability.

A heavily armored vehicle may withstand substantial punishment while remaining slow and difficult to maneuver.

A large merchant ship may provide tremendous cargo capacity but require an experienced crew and considerable maintenance.

Vehicles should create opportunities for exploration, tactical decisions, and memorable adventures.

**A vehicle is more than a means of transportation. In the right campaign, it can become a valuable resource, a source of danger, and an important part of the characters' story.**
