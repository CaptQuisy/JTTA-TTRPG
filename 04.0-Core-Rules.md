# Using Skills & Skill Checks

Characters in _Journey Through the Ages_ face challenges that test their abilities, training, judgment, and determination.

They may attempt to climb a dangerous cliff, persuade a suspicious guard, repair a damaged engine, treat an injured companion, or navigate a spacecraft through hazardous conditions.

When the outcome is uncertain and failure would have meaningful consequences, the Game Master may call for a **Skill Check**. Skill Checks form the foundation of the game's resolution system. The same basic procedure is used for exploration, investigation, social encounters, technical challenges, and many other actions.

The system uses pools of six-sided dice (d6). Each die showing a **5 or 6 counts as one success**. The number of successes determines whether the character accomplishes the task.

## When to Make a Skill Check

A Skill Check is appropriate when:

- The character is attempting something challenging.
- The outcome is uncertain.
- Failure would have meaningful consequences.

Not every action requires a roll. Routine tasks performed under normal circumstances generally succeed automatically.

For example, a trained mechanic does not need to make a Skill Check to perform ordinary maintenance with appropriate tools and sufficient time.

Likewise, an action that is clearly impossible does not require a roll. A character cannot leap across a hundred-foot canyon simply because the player wishes to attempt it.

The Game Master should call for Skill Checks when uncertainty creates tension, consequences, or meaningful choices.

## Building a Dice Pool

Most Skill Checks combine one Attribute and one Skill. The player adds the Attribute rating and Skill Level together, then applies any relevant bonuses or penalties.

**Dice Pool = Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

The final result determines how many six-sided dice the player rolls.

### Skill Check Procedure

1. **Describe the Action:** The player explains what the character is attempting and how.
2. **Choose the Skill:** The Game Master determines which Skill best represents the required training or experience.
3. **Choose the Attribute:** Select the Attribute that best represents the character's approach.
4. **Determine Difficulty:** The Game Master establishes the number of successes required.
5. **Apply Modifiers:** Add applicable bonuses and subtract penalties.
6. **Roll the Dice:** The player rolls the complete dice pool.
7. **Count Successes:** Each 5 or 6 counts as one success.
8. **Determine the Result:** Compare the successes rolled with the Difficulty.

If the character rolls at least as many successes as the Difficulty requires, the action succeeds. Otherwise, the action fails unless a special rule or optional result provides another outcome.

#### Example: Bypassing a Security Door

Sarah's character attempts to bypass an electronic security door. The Game Master determines that the task requires a Technology Check using Intelligence.

Sarah's character has:

|Source|Dice|
|---|---|
|Intelligence|3|
|Technology|2|
|Electronic Toolkit bonus|+1|
|**Total**|**6**|

The Game Master assigns a Hard Difficulty, requiring 3 successes.

Sarah rolls six dice:

**1, 2, 3, 5, 5, 6**

The two 5s and one 6 produce **3 successes**. Sarah meets the Difficulty and successfully bypasses the security door.

The equipment bonus in this example assumes the toolkit has a specific +1 die benefit approved by the Game Master. Ordinary equipment does not automatically grant additional dice.

## Difficulty Levels

Difficulty represents the number of successes required to complete a task.

The Game Master determines the Difficulty before the dice are rolled.

|Difficulty|Successes Required|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|Easy|1|Climb a fence, notice fresh footprints|
|Normal|2|Convince a guard, repair common equipment|
|Hard|3|Pick a quality lock, perform emergency surgery|
|Extreme|4|Disable advanced security, survive hazardous conditions|
|Legendary|5|Accomplish a feat worthy of stories|

These examples assume circumstances in which a roll is necessary.

A task that would ordinarily be Easy may not require a roll at all.

**Easy — 1 Success**

An Easy task presents a modest challenge.

A character with suitable abilities can usually complete it, although pressure or unfavorable circumstances may create uncertainty.

Examples include:

- Climbing a low wall.
- Following obvious tracks.
- Identifying a familiar symbol.
- Repairing a simple mechanism.

The GM may decide that Easy tasks automatically succeed to speed up gameplay.

**Normal — 2 Successes**

A Normal task requires reasonable competence or effort.

It represents the standard difficulty for many challenges encountered during an adventure.

Examples include:

- Negotiating with a reluctant merchant.
- Repairing common equipment under pressure.
- Tracking an animal through ordinary wilderness.
- Navigating unfamiliar terrain.

**Hard — 3 Successes**

A Hard task requires substantial skill, favorable preparation, or both.

Examples include:

- Picking a high-quality lock.
- Performing emergency surgery under difficult conditions.
- Piloting a vehicle through a dangerous obstacle.
- Finding carefully concealed evidence.

**Extreme — 4 Successes**

An Extreme task challenges even highly capable characters.

Examples include:

- Disabling sophisticated security under severe time pressure.
- Navigating through exceptionally hazardous conditions.
- Completing a delicate repair while equipment is failing.
- Performing a difficult maneuver under enemy fire.

**Legendary — 5 Successes**

A Legendary task represents an extraordinary accomplishment.

Such challenges should be rare and memorable.

Examples include:

- Accomplishing an exceptionally difficult feat under seemingly impossible conditions.
- Solving a highly complex technical problem with limited resources.
- Performing a maneuver beyond the capabilities of most trained professionals.

A Legendary task is not necessarily supernatural. It may simply demand exceptional skill, preparation, equipment, or circumstances.

### Adjusting Difficulty

The Game Master should establish Difficulty based on the task itself and the circumstances.

Factors may include:

- Environmental conditions.
- Time pressure.
- Complexity.
- Available information.
- Quality of preparation.
- Obstacles or interference.
- The consequences of failure.

The Difficulty should not increase merely because the character attempting the task is highly skilled.

A difficult lock remains difficult regardless of who attempts to open it.

A character's greater competence is represented by a larger dice pool.

### Difficulty Versus Dice Modifiers

Difficulty represents how challenging the task is.

Dice modifiers represent factors that affect the character's ability to perform it.

For example:

- A sophisticated lock may require a Hard Check.
- Damaged lockpicks may impose a dice penalty.
- A specialized tool may provide a bonus when its rules allow one.
- Working under poor lighting may impose an additional situational penalty.

The Game Master should avoid penalizing the same circumstance twice by both increasing Difficulty and reducing the dice pool unless separate effects clearly justify doing so.

## Choosing Skills and Attributes

Each Skill represents a character's training, knowledge, or experience.

Attributes represent natural abilities and personal characteristics.

Most Skills have a default Attribute, but the Game Master may approve a different combination when the character's approach makes it appropriate.

Players are encouraged to describe how their characters attempt a task.

### Common Skill Checks

|Action|Typical Dice Pool|
|---|---|
|Climbing a cliff|Strength + Athletics|
|Crossing a narrow ledge|Dexterity + Athletics|
|Tracking an animal|Acuity + Survival|
|Enduring a desert trek|Resilience + Survival|
|Convincing a diplomat|Presence + Influence|
|Researching historical records|Intelligence + Academics|
|Treating an injury|Intelligence + Medicine|
|Repairing an electronic generator controller|Intelligence + Technology|
|Repairing a mechanical generator component|Intelligence + Crafting|
|Firing a rifle|Dexterity + Ranged Combat|
|Fighting with a sword|Strength + Close Combat|
|Quietly approaching a guard|Dexterity + Stealth|
|Examining a crime scene|Intelligence + Investigation|
|Piloting through a dangerous maneuver|Dexterity + Vehicle|

These combinations are examples rather than an exhaustive list.

### Alternative Attribute Combinations

The same Skill may be used with different Attributes depending on the circumstances.

For example, Athletics might be combined with:

**Strength:** Climbing a cliff or lifting a heavy object.

**Dexterity:** Balancing on a narrow ledge or maneuvering through an obstacle course.

**Resilience:** Enduring a prolonged physical challenge when the Game Master determines that stamina is more important than strength or coordination.

Likewise, Influence might use:

**Presence:** Persuading an audience through confidence and charisma.

**Strength:** Intimidating someone through a credible display of physical power.

The Game Master determines whether an alternative combination is appropriate.

Changing the Attribute should reflect a genuinely different approach, not merely allow the player to use their highest rating for every task.

## Applying Modifiers

After choosing the Attribute and Skill, apply all relevant modifiers to the dice pool.

Modifiers may come from:

- Edges.
- Hindrances.
- Equipment.
- Conditions.
- Environmental hazards.
- Situational advantages or disadvantages.
- Other special rules.

Positive modifiers add dice.

Negative modifiers remove dice.

### Example: Difficult Climb

A character attempts to climb a wet stone wall.

The character has:

- Strength 3
- Athletics 2
- A situational penalty of –1 die (wet)

The final dice pool is:

**3 + 2 – 1 = 4 dice**

The player rolls four six-sided dice and compares the successes to the assigned Difficulty.

### Equipment Bonuses

Equipment may provide a bonus when its specific rules or the Game Master authorize one. However, ordinary equipment often provides a capability rather than an automatic dice bonus.

For example, climbing gear may make an ascent safer without increasing the dice pool.

A specialized toolkit may permit a repair that could not otherwise be attempted.


### Multiple Modifiers

Bonuses and penalties are combined when they represent distinct applicable effects.

The Game Master should avoid unnecessary modifiers for minor circumstances that do not materially affect the task.

### Minimum Dice Pool

A character always rolls a minimum of **two dice** when making a Skill Check. Apply all bonuses and penalties first. If the final dice pool is less than two dice, increase it to two dice.

### Example: Severe Penalties

A character has:

- Dexterity 2
- Stealth 1
- Heavy Armor penalty –2 dice
- Environmental penalty –2 dice

The calculated dice pool is:

**2 + 1 – 2 – 2 = –1 die**

Because the minimum dice pool is two dice, the player rolls **2 dice**.

This rule ensures that even a character facing severe disadvantages retains a chance of success when an attempt is possible.

The minimum dice pool does not permit actions that are physically impossible or require essential equipment or knowledge the character lacks.

## Rolling the Dice

Roll all dice in the pool simultaneously.

Each die is resolved independently.

|Die Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|1|No success; may contribute to a Critical Failure|
|2–4|No success|
|5–6|One success|

Count the number of dice showing 5 or 6.

### Example: Seven-Dice Pool

A character rolls seven dice:

**1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6**

The results include two 5s and one 6.

The character has **3 successes**.

Against a Hard Difficulty requiring 3 successes, the character succeeds.

Against an Extreme Difficulty requiring 4 successes, the character fails.

### Determining Success or Failure

After counting successes, compare the total with the assigned Difficulty.

**Success:** The number of successes equals or exceeds the Difficulty.

**Failure:** The number of successes is lower than the Difficulty.

#### Example: Investigating a Crime Scene

An investigator searches a room for hidden evidence.

The Game Master assigns a Normal Difficulty requiring 2 successes.

The investigator rolls 3 successes.

The investigation succeeds.

The Game Master reveals the information or evidence that the character was attempting to uncover.

#### Example: Failed Repair

A technician attempts to repair a damaged generator.

The task requires 3 successes.

The technician rolls 2 successes.

The attempt fails.

The Game Master determines the consequence based on the situation.

The repair may remain incomplete, valuable time may be lost, or the equipment may require another approach.

Failure does not automatically mean that the equipment suffers additional damage unless the circumstances or a specific rule justify that consequence.

## Variable Skill Check Results (Optional)

Most Skill Checks use a simple success-or-failure result.

However, the Game Master may use optional degrees of success or failure when the number of successes matters.

This is especially useful for investigations, negotiations, repairs, and other situations where outcomes can vary in quality.

Compare the successes rolled with the Difficulty.

### Degrees of Success

|Result|Margin|Suggested Outcome|
|---|---|---|
|Exceptional Success|2 or more successes above Difficulty|The character succeeds and gains a significant additional benefit|
|Additional Success|1 success above Difficulty|The character succeeds with a modest additional benefit|
|Success|Meets Difficulty|The character accomplishes the intended task|
|Partial Result|1 success below Difficulty|The character makes progress or succeeds with a meaningful limitation, if appropriate|
|Failure|2 or more successes below Difficulty|The character fails to accomplish the task|
|Critical Failure|Meets the Critical Failure conditions|Failure with an additional complication|

These degrees are an optional extension of the standard rules.

The Game Master should decide whether to use them before resolving a check.

### Additional Success

A character who exceeds the Difficulty may receive an additional benefit.

Possible benefits include:

- Completing the task more quickly.
- Avoiding unnecessary attention.
- Conserving resources.
- Obtaining additional information.
- Producing a more durable repair.
- Achieving a more favorable negotiated outcome.

The benefit should be appropriate to the task.

### Partial Result

When a character falls short by one success, the Game Master may allow partial progress or success with a limitation.

Examples include:

- Opening a lock but triggering an alarm.
- Repairing a machine temporarily rather than permanently.
- Persuading a guard to provide limited assistance.
- Finding some clues but missing important details.
- Completing a task while consuming additional time or supplies.

A Partial Result is not automatic. Some tasks have outcomes that cannot reasonably be divided.

For example, a character either clears a dangerous gap or does not.

The Game Master should use partial results only when they make sense for the action.

#### Example: Variable Repair Results

A mechanic attempts a Hard repair requiring 3 successes.

Depending on the roll:

- **5 successes:** The mechanic completes the repair and gains a significant additional benefit.
- **4 successes:** The mechanic completes the repair with a modest additional benefit.
- **3 successes:** The repair succeeds.
- **2 successes:** The mechanic may achieve a temporary repair or partial functionality.
- **1 success:** The repair fails.
- **Critical Failure:** The repair fails and creates an additional complication.

The Game Master determines the precise outcome based on the circumstances and the established repair rules.

## Untrained Attempts

Characters may attempt many tasks without specialized training.

The game assumes that Basic Skills represent abilities ordinary people can develop through everyday experience. 

All characters begin with Level 1 in Basic Skills during character creation. However, some tasks require specialized knowledge, professional training, or appropriate equipment.

Examples include:

- Performing major surgery.
- Programming advanced computer systems.
- Defusing military explosives.
- Piloting unfamiliar aircraft.
- Repairing highly specialized machinery.

The Game Master determines whether an untrained attempt is possible.

### Untrained Task Guidelines

**Ordinary Tasks:** Allow the character to attempt the task using the appropriate Skill and Attribute.

**Specialized Tasks:** Permit an attempt when the character has sufficient knowledge, guidance, or equipment to make success plausible.

**Tasks Requiring Essential Expertise:** The Game Master may rule that the character cannot attempt the task without the necessary training.

Increasing Difficulty is appropriate when a task remains possible but becomes more challenging.

It should not replace a genuine requirement for specialized expertise.

## Opposed Skill Checks

Sometimes a character's action is directly resisted by another character or opponent.

Examples include:

- Sneaking past a guard.
- Deceiving an investigator.
- Wrestling with an opponent.
- Competing for control of a vehicle.
- Attempting to bypass an actively monitored security system.

Players roll dice, while the Game Master normally uses static ratings for opponents.

### Player Versus Opponent

When a player character acts against an opponent, the player rolls the appropriate Skill Check against the opponent's relevant static rating.

This may be a Defense rating, Attack rating, or another established opponent rating, depending on the action.

The Game Master does not make a separate dice roll for the opponent.

#### Example: Sneaking Past a Guard

Jessica attempts to quietly bypass a security checkpoint.

The Game Master determines that the action requires Dexterity + Stealth against the guard's relevant detection rating.

Jessica has:

|Source|Dice|
|---|---|
|Dexterity|3|
|Stealth|2|
|Camouflage Clothing bonus|+1|
|**Total**|**6**|

Jessica rolls:

**1, 2, 3, 5, 5, 6**

She obtains **3 successes**.

If the guard's detection rating requires 3 successes, Jessica succeeds and slips past unnoticed.

### Opponent Ratings

The Game Master establishes opponent ratings using the appropriate rules for NPCs, creatures, combat, and other challenges.

Do not convert an opponent's Skill Level directly into a Difficulty without following the established opponent-rating procedure.

When no specific opponent rating exists, the Game Master may assign an appropriate Difficulty based on the opponent's capabilities and circumstances.

This is an adjudication rather than a separate opposed-roll system.

### Player Versus Player

When two player characters directly oppose one another, both players may roll their appropriate dice pools.

The character with more successes wins the contest. 

If both characters roll the same number of successes, neither gains the advantage. The current situation normally remains unchanged unless the circumstances or another rule require an immediate consequence.

#### Example: Contest of Strength

Two characters attempt to pull the same object away from one another.

Both roll Strength + Athletics.

One character rolls 3 successes.

The other rolls 2 successes.

The first character wins the contest.

If both roll 3 successes, neither gains control of the object.

## Game Master Guidance

Skill Checks should create meaningful uncertainty without interrupting the flow of the adventure.

The Game Master should focus on five principles.

**Roll Only When Necessary**

- Do not require Skill Checks for routine actions when failure would add nothing to the story.

**Choose Skills Based on the Action**

- Determine which Skill and Attribute best represent what the character is actually doing.
- Encourage players to describe their methods.

**Establish Difficulty Consistently**

- Use the standard Difficulty table as a common reference.
- Similar challenges under similar circumstances should require similar numbers of successes.

**Make Success and Failure Meaningful**

- A successful roll should accomplish the task established before the roll.
- A failed roll should create an appropriate consequence without automatically escalating into disaster.

**Keep the Rules Player-Facing**

- Players normally roll against established Difficulties and opponent ratings.
- The Game Master describes the situation, establishes the challenge, and determines the consequences.

**The Skill Check system should remain consistent, flexible, and quick to resolve, allowing the focus of play to remain on the characters' decisions and the consequences of their actions.**
