## Character Description

**Rules determine what your character can do. Your Character Description explains who they are.**

Attributes, Skills, Edges, and Hindrances establish a character's capabilities and limitations. A Character Description brings those elements together into a believable individual with a personality, history, relationships, and goals.

A memorable character does not require an elaborate biography. A few meaningful details are often enough to establish how they behave, what motivates them, and why they choose to face danger.

There is no single correct way to develop a character. Some players begin with a personality or personal goal, while others imagine an appearance, profession, or important event from the character's past.

Use the following sections as inspiration rather than a mandatory checklist.

The Character Description does not grant additional Skills, Edges, equipment, or other mechanical benefits unless a specific rule or the Game Master explicitly permits them.

### Physical Appearance

Describe how your character looks and how they present themselves to the world.

Consider details such as:

- Age, height, and build
- Hair and eye color
- Clothing and personal equipment
- Distinguishing features, scars, or tattoos
- Posture, expressions, and habitual movements
- Prosthetics, assistive devices, or cybernetic implants appropriate to the setting

A character's appearance may reflect their occupation, experiences, culture, or personal preferences.

A veteran soldier might wear practical clothing and carry well-maintained equipment. A wealthy socialite may favor fashionable attire, while a frontier explorer might possess clothing worn by years of travel.

Physical appearance can help other players visualize a character, but it does not determine their personality, abilities, or behavior.

### Personality

Personality describes how your character typically thinks, behaves, and responds to others.

Consider the following questions:

- Are they optimistic or cynical?
- Calm or impulsive?
- Friendly or reserved?
- Serious or humorous?
- Compassionate or ruthless?
- Trusting or suspicious?
- Patient or quick-tempered?

Choose traits that help establish a consistent personality.

Personality traits should guide roleplaying without becoming rigid restrictions. A normally cautious character may act recklessly to protect a friend, while a confident character may hesitate when confronted with an unfamiliar danger.

Such contradictions can make a character more believable.

**Example:** A former military officer is disciplined, practical, and protective of companions. Although usually calm under pressure, they become impatient when others ignore obvious dangers.

### Motivation

Motivation explains why your character undertakes adventures and continues when circumstances become dangerous.

A strong motivation provides direction for the character's decisions and helps the Game Master create meaningful challenges.

Common motivations include:

- Wealth or financial security
- Justice
- Revenge
- Knowledge
- Fame or recognition
- Duty
- Exploration
- Protecting loved ones
- Personal redemption
- Loyalty to a person, organization, or cause

Consider both what the character wants and why that goal matters.

A character seeking wealth might be motivated by greed, a desire for independence, or the need to support their family. These motivations may lead to very different decisions.

**Example:** A former detective investigates dangerous mysteries because an unsolved disappearance continues to haunt them.

#### Motivation and the Adventuring Group

Every character should have a believable reason to participate in the campaign and cooperate with the other characters.

Individual motivations need not be identical, but they should be compatible enough to support shared adventures.

A mercenary seeking payment, a scholar seeking knowledge, and a physician hoping to save lives may have different reasons for joining the same expedition.

Players should avoid creating characters whose primary motivations make cooperation impossible or repeatedly undermine the group's ability to participate in the adventure.

### Background Story

Your Background Story describes the important experiences that shaped your character before the campaign begins.

This narrative expands upon the mechanical Background selected in Section 2.2.

A character with the Criminal Background might have been a smuggler, burglar, confidence artist, or member of an organized crime syndicate. Their Background Story explains the specific circumstances of that life.

Consider these questions:

- Where did the character grow up?
- What was their family or community like?
- What occupation or training did they pursue?
- Who had the greatest influence on their life?
- What important event changed their circumstances?
- Why did they leave their former life?
- How did they become involved in the current adventure?

Focus on a few meaningful events rather than documenting every year of the character's life.

**Example:** Elena served as a military field medic during a devastating war. After leaving the service, she traveled between remote communities providing medical assistance. When former comrades began disappearing, she joined an investigation to discover what happened.

A Background Story may establish useful narrative connections, but it does not automatically provide additional Skill Levels, equipment, Wealth, or Edges.

### Relationships

Relationships connect your character to the world and provide opportunities for friendship, conflict, obligation, and personal growth.

Consider important people such as:

- Family members
- Friends
- Rivals
- Mentors
- Employers
- Romantic partners
- Former companions
- Professional associates
- Enemies

Not every relationship needs to be complicated or dramatic.

A trusted friend, respected teacher, estranged sibling, or former commanding officer can provide meaningful opportunities for future adventures.

For each important relationship, consider what the character wants from that person and what the person expects in return.

**Example:** A retired soldier maintains a close friendship with a former squadmate who now operates a transportation company. The friend occasionally provides information but expects assistance when trouble arises.

Relationships do not automatically grant the mechanical benefits of Edges such as _Well-Connected_, _Underworld Connections_, or _Bureaucratic Connections_. Those benefits require the appropriate Edge.

The Game Master may still use ordinary relationships as narrative opportunities when appropriate.

### Ideals and Beliefs

Ideals and beliefs represent the principles that guide your character's decisions.

They may arise from upbringing, education, faith, culture, personal experience, or a defining event.

Examples include:

- Justice must prevail.
- Knowledge should be shared.
- Family comes first.
- Freedom is worth any price.
- Strength earns respect.
- Never abandon a companion.
- Loyalty must be earned.
- The innocent deserve protection.

Beliefs become particularly interesting when they conflict with difficult circumstances.

A character who believes in honesty may face a situation where deception could save a life. Someone devoted to duty may discover that following orders would violate their conscience.

Such dilemmas provide opportunities for meaningful roleplaying.

Ideals should guide a character's choices without preventing them from changing their beliefs as the story develops.

### Fears and Weaknesses

Heroes are defined not only by their strengths but also by the challenges they struggle to overcome.

Consider:

- What frightens your character?
- What causes them to hesitate?
- What mistakes do they regret?
- What personal weakness do they recognize?
- What flaw do others notice that they refuse to acknowledge?

A character might fear failure, distrust authority, avoid emotional attachments, or struggle with pride.

These weaknesses can create dramatic situations without requiring mechanical penalties.

### Personality Flaws and Hindrances

Ordinary fears, habits, and personality flaws are narrative characteristics.

They do not impose dice penalties or grant Edge Points.

A **Hindrance**, as described in Section 2.5, represents a disadvantage with specific mechanical consequences.

For example, a character may dislike heights without possessing the Phobia Hindrance. They may express nervousness or reluctance while climbing, but no additional mechanical penalty applies solely because of that description.

If the player selects the Phobia Hindrance, its defined rules apply when the triggering circumstances occur.

Players should choose mechanical Hindrances only when they want those limitations to have a formal effect during play.

### Mannerisms

Mannerisms are small habits, gestures, and behaviors that help distinguish a character from others.

Examples include:

- Constantly adjusting glasses
- Speaking in military terminology
- Avoiding eye contact
- Whistling while working
- Keeping equipment perfectly organized
- Quoting historical figures
- Tapping a finger when nervous
- Examining unfamiliar objects with unusual curiosity

One or two distinctive mannerisms are usually enough.

They should help portray the character without becoming distracting or interfering with the group's enjoyment.

**Example:** A former naval officer habitually checks the time before making important decisions and refers to companions by their surnames.

### Personal Goals

Personal Goals represent what the character hopes to accomplish during the campaign.

Goals give players direction while providing the Game Master with material for future adventures.

Characters benefit from having both short-term and long-term goals.

#### Short-Term Goals

These are objectives that might reasonably be accomplished during one or several adventures.

Examples include:

- Earn enough money to purchase a vehicle.
- Rescue a missing friend.
- Learn a new Skill or acquire an Edge.
- Gain the trust of an important faction.
- Recover stolen equipment.
- Discover the identity of a mysterious informant.

#### Long-Term Goals

These are ambitions that may require many adventures or an entire campaign.

Examples include:

- Defeat a lifelong enemy.
- Build a successful business.
- Discover an ancient civilization.
- Become a renowned explorer.
- Restore a fallen kingdom.
- Expose a powerful criminal organization.
- Establish a permanent settlement on a distant world.

Goals should be ambitious enough to inspire action but flexible enough to evolve with the campaign.

A character may abandon an old ambition, discover a new purpose, or accomplish something they never originally imagined.

#### Personal Goals and Advancement

Personal Goals do not automatically grant Experience Points or Experience Levels.

Character advancement follows the rules in Section 2.8.

The Game Master may incorporate personal goals into adventures and use their completion as part of the campaign's normal milestone-based advancement.

### Character Voice

Character Voice describes how your character communicates with others.

Consider:

- Do they speak formally or casually?
- Are they quiet or talkative?
- Do they use slang, technical jargon, or regional expressions?
- Are they direct or diplomatic?
- Do they speak confidently or hesitate before answering?
- Do they use humor, sarcasm, or carefully chosen words?

A distinctive voice does not require a theatrical accent or elaborate performance.

A consistent choice of vocabulary, sentence structure, or speaking style can be enough to make the character recognizable.

**Example:** A veteran engineer speaks in short, practical sentences and frequently explains problems through mechanical comparisons.

Players should portray their characters in ways they find comfortable. Speaking in the first person, describing dialogue in the third person, and switching between the two are all valid approaches.

### Final Review

Before beginning play, review your character's description and consider the following questions:

1. Does the character have a clear reason to participate in the adventure?
2. Do they have a believable reason to cooperate with the other characters?
3. Are their personality, Background, Skills, Edges, and Hindrances reasonably consistent?
4. Do they possess recognizable strengths and weaknesses?
5. Do they have at least one meaningful personal goal?
6. Are their important relationships and motivations appropriate to the campaign?
7. Would you enjoy portraying this character over multiple adventures?

Not every question requires a detailed written answer. The purpose is to ensure that the character feels believable and fits the group's intended style of play.

### A Simple Character Description

A complete Character Description can be as brief as a few paragraphs.

**Example: Thomas Mercer — Former Soldier**

Thomas Mercer is a 35-year-old former infantry sergeant with a weathered appearance, short dark hair, and an old scar across his left cheek. He favors practical clothing and keeps his equipment meticulously maintained.

Thomas is disciplined, cautious, and loyal to his companions. He rarely speaks about his military service but becomes visibly uncomfortable when civilians are placed in danger.

After leaving the army, Thomas worked as a private security contractor. He now seeks to discover what happened to several former comrades who disappeared while investigating a smuggling operation.

His closest friend is a former military medic who occasionally assists him. Thomas believes that loyalty must be earned and that protecting innocent people is more important than following orders.

His immediate goal is to locate his missing comrades. His long-term ambition is to leave violence behind and establish a peaceful life.

Thomas speaks directly, rarely jokes with strangers, and habitually checks entrances and exits whenever he enters an unfamiliar building.

This description provides a clear personality, motivation, history, relationships, and goals without requiring an extensive biography.

### Character Development During Play

A Character Description is a starting point, not a permanent restriction.

Characters change through their experiences.

A cautious soldier may become a confident leader. A selfish criminal may discover loyalty. A sheltered scholar may become an accomplished explorer.

Victories, failures, friendships, injuries, and difficult choices should influence how characters see themselves and the world.

Players are encouraged to revise their Character Descriptions as the campaign progresses.

Changes in personality, relationships, or personal goals do not normally require Experience Points. However, changes affecting Skills, Edges, Hindrances, or other mechanical abilities must follow the applicable rules.

**The most memorable characters are not necessarily the strongest or most skilled. They are the ones whose choices, relationships, strengths, and weaknesses make their stories worth telling.**
