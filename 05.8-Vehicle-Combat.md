## Vehicle Combat

Vehicle Combat covers attacks involving cars, trucks, armored vehicles, boats, ships, aircraft, spacecraft, and other vehicles.

Vehicle combat follows the same basic principles as character combat. However, the effectiveness of a vehicle in combat depends on both its capabilities and the skills of its operator.

A vehicle may attack another vehicle, a character, a structure, or another appropriate target.

### Vehicle Combat Profiles

Vehicles use several characteristics to resolve combat.

| Rating                      | Purpose                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Close Combat Attack Rating  | Represents the vehicle's effectiveness when ramming, sideswiping, crushing, or colliding with another target |
| Ranged Weapon Attack Rating | Represents the accuracy of mounted weapons and targeting systems                                             |
| Defense Rating              | Represents the vehicle's ability to avoid or resist successful attacks                                       |
| Durability                  | Represents the vehicle's ability to withstand damage                                                         |

A vehicle's combat ratings represent its inherent capabilities. When operated by a player character, these ratings are combined with the appropriate Skill to determine the dice pool.

### Vehicle Combat Dice Pools

When a player character operates a vehicle or its weapons, use the vehicle's appropriate combat rating as the base dice pool, then add the operator's relevant Skill and any applicable modifiers.

|Combat Action|Dice Pool|
|---|---|
|Ranged Attack|Vehicle Ranged Attack Rating + Gunner's Ranged Combat Skill + Modifiers|
|Close Combat Attack|Vehicle Close Combat Attack Rating + Driver/Pilot's Vehicle Skill + Modifiers|
|Defense|Vehicle Defense Rating + Driver/Pilot's Vehicle Skill + Modifiers|

**Vehicle combat ratings replace Attributes when constructing these dice pools.** Do not add the character's Attribute separately.

Modifiers may reflect range, visibility, equipment, environmental conditions, vehicle damage, Edges, or other circumstances.

The Game Master determines which modifiers apply.

### Vehicle Close Combat Attack Ratings

A vehicle's Attack Rating represents its effectiveness in delivering attacks, based on its construction, maneuverability, and technology level.

|Attack Rating|Description|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|1|Minimal offensive capability|Bicycle, canoe, horse-drawn cart, ultralight aircraft, escape pod|
|2|Light offensive capability|Passenger car, pickup truck, speedboat, early fighter aircraft, small shuttle|
|3|Moderate offensive capability|Heavy truck, armored car, patrol boat, WWII fighter aircraft, armed transport spacecraft|
|4|Military-grade offensive capability|Armored personnel carrier, modern attack helicopter, destroyer, military gunship, armed spacecraft|
|5|Heavy military offensive capability|Main battle tank, battleship, modern combat aircraft, heavily armed military spacecraft|
|6|Exceptional offensive capability|Super-heavy tank, specialized siege vehicle, dreadnought, advanced space warship, capital ship|

Game Master Guidance: Attack Ratings reflect the vehicle's offensive capabilities rather than its ability to withstand damage. A vehicle's Attack Rating may differ significantly from its Defense Rating.

| -------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1              | Fragile or poorly protected    | Bicycle, canoe, ultralight, escape pod                                     |
| 2              | Light construction             | Passenger car, speedboat, civilian aircraft, small shuttle                 |

| 6              | Exceptional protection         | Super-heavy tank, fortified warship, capital ship                          |

The Game Master assigns Defense Ratings based primarily on the vehicle's construction and protective capabilities.

A vehicle's **Defense Rating** represents its inherent ability to avoid or resist successful attacks.

It reflects factors such as:

- Physical construction and protection.
- Armor and defensive systems.
- Structural characteristics.
- General resistance to attacks.
### Vehicle Close Combat Attacks

Vehicles may attack through direct physical contact, including:

- Ramming another vehicle.
- Sideswiping an opponent.
- Crushing a smaller vehicle.
- Forcing another vehicle off course.
- Deliberately colliding with an enemy vessel or spacecraft.

A vehicle's **Close Combat Attack Rating** represents its inherent effectiveness when attempting these maneuvers.

When a player character attempts a Close Combat attack with a vehicle, combine its Close Combat Attack Rating with the driver's or pilot's Vehicle Skill.

**Close Combat Attack Dice Pool = Close Combat Attack Rating + Vehicle Skill +/- Modifiers**

Compare the successes against the target's Defense Rating.

On success, the vehicle makes contact and the resulting collision or impact is resolved.

The Game Master may apply modifiers for speed, Handling, relative size, terrain, or other circumstances.
#### Example: Ramming an Enemy Truck

A player character drives an armored vehicle with a Close Combat Attack Rating of 4.

The driver has Vehicle Skill 3.

The target is an enemy truck with Defense Rating 3.

**Close Combat Attack Dice Pool = 4 + 3 = 7 dice**

The player rolls 7 dice.

If the roll produces at least 3 successes, the armored vehicle successfully rams the truck.

The Game Master then resolves collision damage based on the vehicles' relative size, speed, construction, and Durability.

### Vehicle Ranged Attacks

Vehicle-mounted weapons use a **Ranged Attack Rating** representing their inherent accuracy and targeting capabilities.

This rating reflects factors such as:

- Weapon accuracy and stabilization.
- Targeting and fire-control systems.
- Weapon mounts and firing mechanisms.
- Vehicle stability.
- Other equipment affecting accuracy.

When a player character operates a mounted weapon, combine its Ranged Attack Rating with the gunner's Ranged Combat Skill.

**Ranged Attack Dice Pool = Ranged Attack Rating + Ranged Combat Skill + Modifiers**

The gunner rolls the resulting dice pool against the target's Defense Rating.

If the number of successes equals or exceeds the target's Defense Rating, the attack hits.

Resolve damage separately using the appropriate weapon damage and vehicle Durability rules.

##### Vehicle Weapon Attack Rating

Vehicle-mounted weapons use five general accuracy categories.

|Ranged Attack Rating|Accuracy|Description|
|---|---|---|
|2|Poor|Crude, improvised, damaged, or extremely difficult to aim|
|3|Fair|Unguided or inherently difficult weapon|
|4|Good|Standard military weapon system|
|5|Excellent|Guided or advanced targeting system|
|6|Exceptional|Extremely sophisticated precision targeting|

These ratings represent the capabilities of the weapon system, independent of the gunner's skill.

A weapon's Ranged Attack Rating represents its ability to hit a target, not its destructive power.

For example, a machine gun and a tank cannon might have similar Attack Ratings, even though the cannon inflicts considerably greater damage.

**Attack determines whether the weapon hits. Damage determines the consequences of the hit.**

#### Applying Range Modifiers

Range modifiers reflect the weapon's effective engagement distance, targeting limitations, and accuracy at different ranges.

- +1: Favorable engagement range.
- 0: Normal engagement range.
- –1: Difficult engagement range.
- –2: Very difficult engagement range.
- —: Outside the weapon's effective range.

Special: Bombs use the appropriate range and targeting rules for the delivery method. The Game Master determines modifiers based on altitude, speed, target size, and available targeting equipment.
#### Range by Vehicle Type

Vehicle combat often occurs at distances much greater than ordinary character combat. The appropriate distances depend on the type of vehicle and the environment.

- A car chase may involve vehicles separated by a few hundred feet.
- Aircraft may engage targets several miles away.
- Spacecraft may exchange fire across hundreds of miles.

Use the following ranges as general guidelines.
##### Vehicle Combat Range Bands

| Range Band | Ground Vehicles      | Watercraft           | Aircraft             | Spacecraft        |
| ---------- | -------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------- |
| Close      | Up to 100 ft         | Up to 300 ft         | Up to 1,500 ft       | Up to ½ mile      |
| Short      | Over 100–300 ft      | Over 300–1,500 ft    | Over 1,500 ft–1 mile | Over ½–5 miles    |
| Medium     | Over 300–1,500 ft    | Over 1,500 ft–1 mile | Over 1–5 miles       | Over 5–50 miles   |
| Long       | Over 1,500 ft–1 mile | Over 1–5 miles       | Over 5–30 miles      | Over 50–500 miles |
| Extreme    | Over 1 mile          | Over 5 miles         | Over 30 miles        | Over 500 miles    |
These distances are approximate.

The Game Master should use the category that best represents the technology and circumstances of the encounter.

For example, a machine gun mounted on a truck normally uses Ground Vehicle ranges, while a similar weapon mounted on an aircraft may operate at much greater distances.
##### Example: Tank Cannon at Long Range

A player character operates a tank cannon with Attack Rating 5 and has Ranged Combat Skill 3.

The target is at Long Range (2000 ft), which has a modifier of 0.

Attack Dice Pool = 5 + 3 + 0 = 8 dice

If the same target were at Close Range, the –1 modifier would reduce the Attack dice pool to 7 dice.
##### Game Master Guidance

These modifiers are suggested values for typical weapon systems, not universal restrictions. Advanced targeting equipment, specialized ammunition, or unusual circumstances may justify adjustments.

The range bands should be interpreted using the appropriate vehicle type. For example, Long Range for ground vehicles represents a much shorter distance than Long Range for spacecraft.

### Determining Damage to Opponents

A successful Attack Roll determines whether the attack hits, while a separate Damage Roll determines the severity of the resulting damage.
#### Ranged Weapons Damage Dice Pool

When a ranged attack hits a vehicle, the attacker makes a Damage Roll using the Weapon's Damage Dice plus any additional successes from the Attack Roll.

**Damage Dice Pool = Weapon Damage Dice + Additional Attack Successes**

Additional Attack Successes are the number of successes exceeding the target's Defense Rating.

**Additional Attack Successes = Attack Successes − Defense Rating**

A successful attack that exactly equals the target's Defense Rating receives no additional Damage Dice.
#### Collision Damage Dice Pool

When an attacker collides with a vehicle, the attacker makes a Damage Roll using the Vehicle's Attack Rating plus any additional successes from the Attack Roll.

**Damage Dice Pool = Vehicle Attack Rating + Additional Attack Successes**

Additional Attack Successes are the number of successes exceeding the target's Defense Rating.

**Additional Attack Successes = Attack Successes − Defense Rating**

A successful attack that exactly equals the target's Defense Rating receives no additional Damage Dice.

The attacking Vehicle may also suffer some Damage based on the severity of the attack.
#### Resolving Damage

1. **Determine the Hit:** The Attack Roll must equal or exceed the target's Defense Rating.
2. **Calculate Additional Successes:** Count the Attack successes exceeding the target's Defense Rating.
3. **Build the Damage Pool:** Add those additional successes to the vehicle/weapon's Attack Rating/Damage Dice.
4. **Roll Damage:** Roll the resulting dice pool, counting each 5 or 6 as one success.
5. **Determine Damage Severity:** Use the total Damage successes to determine the level of damage inflicted.
6. **Apply the Damage:** Record the damage against the vehicle's available Durability slots, applying any applicable damage effects.
7. **Apply Collision Damage (if applicable):** Determine if the Attacking Vehicle sustains any Damage.

**Push Your Luck cannot be used on Damage Rolls.** Damage dice are rolled only once, without rerolls from Push Your Luck.

#### Vehicle Damage Severity

Vehicles use the same damage severity levels as characters.

| Damage <br>Successes | Damage <br>Level |
| -------------------- | ---------------- |
| 0                    | No Damage        |
| 1                    | Minor            |
| 2                    | Serious          |
| 3                    | Critical         |
| 4 or more            | Destroyed        |

The resulting damage is recorded against the vehicle's available Durability slots.

If all Durability slots of the indicated severity are occupied, the Damage escalates according to the standard rules.

Vehicle Damage capacity depends on the vehicle's size, construction, and Durability, as established by the Game Master.

### Character Vehicle Defense

When a player character controls a vehicle being attacked, combine the vehicle's Defense Rating with the driver's or pilot's Vehicle Skill.

**Defense Dice Pool = Vehicle Defense Rating + Vehicle Skill +/- Modifiers**

The player rolls this dice pool against the attacking Opponent vehicle's appropriate Attack Rating.

If the Defense Roll produces successes equal to or greater than the Attack Rating, the attack is avoided or otherwise fails to inflict a successful hit.

If the Defense Roll fails, the attack hits and damage is resolved normally.

Defense may represent evasive maneuvering, defensive systems, or the vehicle's inherent protection.
#### Failed Defense and Damage Severity

When the player's Defense successes are fewer than the opponent's Attack Rating, the character suffers Damage.

The severity of the Damage depends on the number of successes missing.

**Defense Shortfall = Opponent Attack Rating – Defense Successes**

Each Defense success reduces the effective Attack Rating by one.

The remaining value determines the Damage severity.
#### Defense Shortfall Table

| Missing <br>Successes | Damage <br>Severity |
| --------------------- | ------------------- |
| 0 or fewer            | No Damage           |
| 1                     | Minor               |
| 2                     | Serious             |
| 3                     | Critical            |
| 4 or more             | Destroyed           |
A failed Defense normally inflicts damage and fills one Durability slot. The shortfall determines how severe that Damage is.

If the Vehicle has no available slot for that Durability level apply the normal escalation rules.

#### Example: Evading a Missile

A player character pilots a fighter aircraft with Defense Rating 3 and has Vehicle Skill 4..

An enemy aircraft fires a guided missile with Ranged Attack Rating 4.

**Defense Dice Pool = 3 + 4 = 7 dice**

The player rolls 7 dice against Attack Rating 4.

If the roll produces at least 4 successes, the missile misses.

Otherwise, the missile hits and damage is resolved using the vehicle damage rules.

#### Example: Failed Defense by 2 Successes

If instead the player only rolled and rerolled 2 Successes on the Defense check.

Defense Shortfall = Opponent Attack Rating − Defense Successes

\[ 4-2=2 \]

The Defense Roll fails by 2 successes. According to the Defense Shortfall Table, the fighter aircraft suffers Serious Damage.

The aircraft fills one Serious Durability slot.

The aircraft remains airborne but is now more difficult to control. If no Serious Durability slot is available, the damage escalates according to the normal Vehicle Durability rules.

### Collision Damage to the Attacking Vehicle

When a vehicle successfully rams another vehicle, the attacking vehicle may also sustain damage from the collision.

First, resolve the Close Combat Attack and determine the **Damage Severity inflicted on the target** using the normal vehicle Damage rules. The target's Damage Severity remains unchanged.

Next, compare the Defense Ratings of both vehicles to determine the Damage Severity suffered by the attacker.

The attacking vehicle receives a **one-level damage advantage**, representing its ability to prepare for and control the collision.
#### Determining Attacker Collision Damage

An Attacker with a higher Defense rating is less likely to take damage from ramming the opponent. 

**Attacker Damage Severity = Target Damage Severity − 1 Level  − (Attacker Defense − Target Defense)**

| Attacker's Defense <br>Compared to Target | Attacker's Damage <br>Severity |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Higher by 3+                              | Reduce by 4 levels             |
| Higher by 2                               | Reduce by 3 levels             |
| Higher by 1                               | Reduce by 2 levels             |
| Equal                                     | Reduce by 1 level              |
| Lower by 1                                | Same severity as target        |
| Lower by 2                                | Increase by 1 level            |
| Lower by 3                                | Increase by 2 levels           |
| Lower by 4+                               | Increase by 3 levels           |

The attacker records the resulting Damage using the vehicle's Durability slots.

**No additional Damage Roll is required.** The original target's damage is never modified.

#### Examples: Critical Damage to the Target

**Example 1 — Tank Rams an Armored Truck**

A tank with Defense Rating 5 rams an armored truck with Defense Rating 3, inflicting Critical Damage.

The tank's Defense is 2 higher than the truck's, reducing its own damage by 3 levels.

**Attacker Severity = 3 − 1 − (5 − 3) = 0**

The tank suffers **No Damage**.

**Example 2 — Armored Vehicle Rams a Truck**

An armored vehicle with Defense Rating 4 rams a heavy truck with Defense Rating 3, inflicting Critical Damage.

The attacker's Defense is 1 higher, reducing its own damage by 2 levels.

**Attacker Severity = 3 − 1 − (4 − 3) = 1**

The armored vehicle suffers **Minor Damage**.

**Example 3 — Two Similar Vehicles Collide**

A heavy truck with Defense Rating 3 rams another heavy truck with Defense Rating 3, inflicting Critical Damage.

Both vehicles have equal Defense Ratings, so the attacker receives its one-level damage advantage.

**Attacker Severity = 3 − 1 − (3 − 3) = 2**

The attacking truck suffers **Serious Damage**.

**Example 4 — Passenger Car Rams a Heavy Truck**

A passenger car with Defense Rating 2 rams a heavy truck with Defense Rating 3, inflicting Critical Damage.

The car's Defense is 1 lower, canceling its one-level damage advantage.

**Attacker Severity = 3 − 1 − (2 − 3) = 3**

The passenger car suffers **Critical Damage**.

**Example 5 — Motorcycle Rams a Heavy Truck**

A motorcycle with Defense Rating 1 rams a heavy truck with Defense Rating 3, inflicting Critical Damage.

The motorcycle's Defense is 2 lower, increasing its damage by one level after applying the attacker's advantage.

**Attacker Severity = 3 − 1 − (1 − 3) = 4**

The motorcycle is **Destroyed**. 
### Vehicle Damage Effects

Vehicle Damage represent structural damage, damaged equipment, impaired mobility, or other consequences appropriate to the vehicle and attack.

The Game Master determines the specific effects using the vehicle's damage severity and the circumstances of the attack.

Minor Damage might represent superficial damage or a damaged component, while Critical Damage could disable a major system or leave the vehicle barely operational.

Destroyed represents catastrophic damage that may render the vehicle inoperable, destroyed, or beyond immediate repair.

Use the established Vehicle Durability rules to determine how much damage a vehicle can withstand.

### Vehicle Crew and Actions

Vehicle combat may involve multiple crew members performing different tasks.

Typical crew positions include:

- **Driver/Pilot:** Controls movement, maneuvering, Close Combat attacks, and Defense.
- **Gunner:** Operates mounted weapons and makes Ranged Attacks.
- **Commander:** Coordinates crew actions and directs combat operations.
- **Navigator:** Assists with positioning, navigation, and route planning.
- **Engineer:** Operates or repairs vehicle systems.
- **Support Crew:** Performs other specialized duties.

In vehicle combat, each character may only perform 1 Action a turn.

When multiple characters operate the same vehicle, use the Skill of the character performing the action.

For example, a tank gunner uses Ranged Combat when firing the cannon, while the driver uses Vehicle when attempting to ram another vehicle or defend against an attack.

When a single character performs multiple crew roles, that character must manage the applicable actions.
### Game Master Guidance

**Use Vehicle Ratings Instead of Attributes**

Vehicle combat dice pools begin with the appropriate vehicle combat rating, not the operator's Attribute. Add the operator's relevant Skill to represent their experience and training.

**Separate Accuracy from Damage**

A highly accurate weapon is not necessarily more destructive. Use Attack Ratings to determine hits and damage rules to determine their consequences.

**Consider Vehicle Scale**

Collisions involving cars, warships, and spacecraft produce different consequences. Consider relative size, speed, construction, and Durability.

**Reward Skilled Operators**

A skilled gunner, driver, or pilot can substantially improve a vehicle's effectiveness. Apply the appropriate Skill and any relevant Edges or situational modifiers.

**Keep Players Involved**

Players make Attack Rolls when operating vehicle weapons or initiating collisions. They make Defense Rolls when their vehicles are attacked. NPC vehicles use fixed ratings rather than dice rolls.

**Preserve the Consequences of Damage**

Vehicle combat remains dangerous. Damage may impair movement, maneuverability, weapons, defensive systems, or crew members.

**Vehicle Combat follows the core player-facing dice pool system, combining a vehicle's inherent capabilities with the skills of the characters operating it.**
