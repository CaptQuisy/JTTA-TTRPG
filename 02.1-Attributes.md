## Attributes <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Attributes represent a character's natural physical, mental, and social capabilities. They describe innate potential rather than learned expertise and form the foundation of every character.

Attributes contribute to Skill Checks, Combat, Saving Throws, and other actions whenever the applicable rules require an Attribute-based dice pool.

Unlike Skills, Attributes rarely change over the course of a campaign. Characters become more capable through experience, training, Edges, equipment, and special abilities, while their natural aptitude generally remains constant.

Every character possesses six Attributes:

|Attribute|Abbreviation|Description|
|---|---|---|
|Strength|STR|Physical power and muscular capability|
|Dexterity|DEX|Coordination, agility, balance, and reflexes|
|Resilience|RES|Endurance, toughness, and resistance to physical hardship|
|Intelligence|INT|Reasoning, knowledge, memory, and problem-solving|
|Acuity|ACT|Perception, awareness, intuition, and mental alertness|
|Presence|PRS|Confidence, personality, leadership, and social influence|

Each Attribute is rated from **1 to 4**, with **2 representing average human capability**.

|Rating|Capability|
|---|---|
|**1 — Below Average**|Noticeably weaker or less capable than an average person in this area|
|**2 — Average**|Normal human capability|
|**3 — Above Average**|Clearly capable, with strong natural aptitude|
|**4 — Exceptional**|Outstanding natural ability approaching the limits of ordinary human potential|

An Attribute rating does not automatically determine success. Training, circumstances, equipment, and the Difficulty of the task also influence the outcome.

### Attribute Descriptions

#### Strength (STR)

Strength measures physical power, muscular endurance, and the ability to exert force.

Strength is commonly used for:

- Close Combat attacks relying on physical power
- Lifting, carrying, pushing, or pulling heavy objects
- Climbing and swimming through physical effort
- Breaking or forcing objects
- Athletic feats requiring power 

**Commonly Associated Skills:** Athletics, Close Combat

Strength determines how much force a character can exert, but it does not automatically represent endurance or resistance to injury. Those capabilities are primarily governed by Resilience.

#### Dexterity (DEX)

Dexterity measures coordination, balance, agility, reflexes, and fine motor control.

Dexterity is commonly used for:

- Ranged Combat attacks
- Dodging or avoiding physical attacks
- Stealth and careful movement
- Acrobatics and maintaining balance
- Delicate manual tasks
- Operating vehicles requiring precise control

**Commonly Associated Skills:** Finesse, Ranged Combat, Stealth, Vehicle

Dexterity represents how precisely and quickly a character moves rather than how much physical force they can exert.

#### Resilience (RES)

Resilience measures endurance, physical toughness, and resistance to injury, fatigue, disease, and environmental hazards.

Resilience is commonly used for:

- Resisting poison and disease
- Surviving harsh environments
- Enduring physical hardship
- Recovering from injury
- Resisting exhaustion
- Withstanding prolonged exertion

Resilience also determines a character's **Minor Wound slots**, representing their ability to endure injuries before suffering more serious consequences.

Resilience measures the body's ability to withstand hardship rather than the character's ability to avoid danger.

#### Intelligence (INT)

Intelligence measures reasoning, memory, education, and problem-solving ability.

Intelligence is commonly used for:

- Academic knowledge
- Scientific reasoning
- Medical diagnosis and treatment
- Technical expertise
- Investigation through analysis
- Planning and solving complex problems

**Commonly Associated Skills:** Academics, Investigation, Medicine, Technology

Intelligence represents a character's ability to understand, remember, and apply information. It does not necessarily indicate awareness of immediate surroundings, which is primarily governed by Acuity.

#### Acuity (ACT)

Acuity measures perception, awareness, intuition, and mental alertness.

Acuity is commonly used for:

- Detecting hidden threats
- Noticing subtle environmental details
- Reading body language
- Tracking and recognizing signs
- Maintaining situational awareness
- Recognizing deception or suspicious behavior
- Resisting certain effects that target awareness or mental focus

**Commonly Associated Skills:** Perception, Survival

Acuity represents the ability to notice and interpret what is happening rather than the ability to reason through complex information.

Depending on the nature of a threat, Acuity may contribute to Mental Saving Throws. It is not automatically used for every mental effect.

#### Presence (PRS)

Presence measures confidence, leadership, force of personality, and social influence.

Presence is commonly used for:

- Persuasion and negotiation
- Leadership and command
- Intimidation
- Public speaking
- Performance
- Inspiring or influencing others
- Maintaining composure during social confrontation

**Commonly Associated Skills:** Influence, Performance

Presence represents the strength of a character's personality and ability to affect others through words, behavior, and demeanor.

A high Presence does not guarantee that others will cooperate. Their motivations, loyalties, attitudes, and Social Defense may still make them difficult to influence.

### Determining Attribute Ratings

During character creation, choose one of the following methods to determine Attribute ratings.

The Game Master should establish which method is used before characters are created.

#### Option 1: Point Buy (Recommended)

Distribute **13 points** among the six Attributes.

The following restrictions apply:

- Every Attribute must begin at a minimum rating of 1.
- No Attribute may begin higher than 4.
- All 13 points must be assigned.
- Attribute ratings must be whole numbers.

This method provides balanced characters while allowing players to customize their strengths and weaknesses.

**Example:**

A player creates a former military scout who is physically capable, observant, and comfortable working independently.

|Attribute|Rating|
|---|---|
|Strength|2|
|Dexterity|3|
|Resilience|2|
|Intelligence|2|
|Acuity|3|
|Presence|1|
|**Total**|**13**|

The character possesses above-average Dexterity and Acuity but is less naturally confident in social situations.

**Heroic Campaigns:** For more cinematic or heroic adventures, the Game Master may increase the allocation to **14 points**. The normal minimum of 1 and maximum of 4 still apply.

#### Option 2: Random Generation

Players who prefer an element of chance may generate their Attributes randomly.

1. Roll one six-sided die (1d6) seven times.
2. Convert each die result into an Attribute rating using the table below. 
3. Discard the lowest converted rating. 
4. Assign the remaining six ratings among the six Attributes in any order, unless the Game Master requires assignment in the order rolled.

|1d6 Roll|Attribute Rating|
|---|---|
|1|1|
|2–3|2|
|4–5|3|
|6|4|

**Example:**

A player rolls seven dice with the following results:

**6, 5, 4, 3, 3, 2, 1**

These convert to Attribute ratings of:

**4, 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 1**

The player discards the lowest rating (1), leaving:

**4, 3, 3, 2, 2, 2**

The player assigns these ratings to the six Attributes in any order.

Randomly generated characters may have higher or lower total Attribute ratings than characters created using the point-buy method. This variation is an intentional feature of random generation.

The Game Master should use the same generation method for all starting characters when a consistent level of natural ability is desired.

### Improving Attributes

Attributes represent a character's innate potential and are not normally increased through experience.

Instead, characters become more capable by:

- Improving Skills
- Acquiring or upgrading Edges
- Overcoming or compensating for Hindrances
- Gaining specialized training
- Obtaining superior equipment
- Developing new abilities permitted by the campaign

Some settings may introduce extraordinary abilities, such as Psion or Technomage powers. These are optional campaign elements and do not automatically increase Attribute ratings unless their specific rules state otherwise.

This approach keeps character advancement focused on experience, training, and accomplishment rather than continually increasing natural ability.
