## Explosives

Some weapons affect an entire area rather than a single target.

Examples include:

- Grenades
- Explosive charges
- Rocket launchers
- Artillery shells
- Mines
- Energy explosions

Area attacks generally target a location rather than a single opponent.

### Explosive Power Levels

| Power Level | Classification | Examples                                                           | Primary Blast Radius |
| ----------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| 3           | Minor          | Firecracker, small pyrotechnic charge                              | 5 ft                 |
| 4           | Small          | Large firework, small improvised explosive                         | 10 ft                |
| 5           | Moderate       | Small explosive charge, compact explosive device                   | 15 ft                |
| 6           | Powerful       | Fragmentation grenade, standard demolition charge, claymore        | 20 ft                |
| 7           | Extreme        | Land mine, Large demolition charge                                 | 30 ft                |
| 8           | Devastating    | Large demolition charge, Anti-tank mine, major vehicle explosion   | 50 ft                |
| 9           | Massive        | Large bomb, ammunition depot explosion, major industrial explosion | 100 ft               |
| 10          | Catastrophic   | Catastrophic power plant explosion, enormous industrial disaster   | 300+ ft              |

The Game Master may adjust the Difficulty based on the circumstances, including available cover, confinement, warning, and opportunities to escape.

### Blast Falloff (Optional)

For campaigns emphasizing realism, explosive attacks become less effective farther from the point of detonation.

For every **10 feet beyond the center of the blast**, reduce the weapons Damage Dice by 1.

Continue reducing Damage Dice until they reach zero.

This optional rule affects the effectiveness of the explosion at increasing distances.

### Secondary Hazards

Explosions may create additional dangers, including fire, smoke, flying debris, collapsing structures, and hazardous materials.

These effects may require separate Saving Throws or impose Conditions when appropriate.

### Ranged Explosives

Ranged Explosives are weapons designed to deliver an explosive charge to a distant target. They range from improvised weapons, such as dynamite tied to an arrow, hand-thrown grenades, and shoulder-fired bazookas. It can also include sophisticated guided missiles like anti-tank missiles, remote controlled drones, and man-portable anti-aircraft weapons. 

These weapons may use direct fire, ballistic trajectories, or guidance systems to strike personnel, vehicles, aircraft, and fortified positions. Unlike conventional firearms, Ranged Explosives typically inflict damage across a blast area, potentially affecting multiple targets. 

Their effectiveness depends on the weapon's range, explosive power, accuracy, and guidance capabilities. Ranged Explosives use the normal Attack Roll rules to determine whether they strike their intended target, while their Explosive Power Level determines Damage Dice, and Blast Radius.

#### Resolving Ranged Explosive Attacks Against Opponents

1. Select a point within the weapon's effective range.
2. Build the appropriate Attack dice pool.
3. Apply Range and other Attack modifiers.
4. Make the Attack Roll.
5. Compare the Attack successes against the Physical Defense of each opponent within the affected area.
6. Resolve Damage separately for each opponent successfully struck.

The Attack Roll determines which opponents are affected.

For each successfully struck opponent, calculate extra Attack successes against that opponent's Physical Defense, and make a Damage Roll.

Different targets may therefore suffer different Wound severities from the same explosion.

##### Grenade Example:

A character throws a grenade at three enemy soldiers. Two soldiers are standing together 40 feet away, while a third soldier is positioned 10 feet farther from the intended point of impact.

The character aims the grenade at the two closest soldiers.

**Step 1: Build the Attack Dice Pool**

The character has Dexterity 2 and Athletics 3. The target is at Medium range (0 dice modifier), but darkness imposes a –1 die penalty.

| Source       | Dice  |
| ------------ | ----- |
| Dexterity    | 2     |
| Athletics    | 3     |
| Medium Range | 0     |
| Darkness     | –1    |
| **Total**    | **4** |
**Step 2: Make the Attack Roll**

The player rolls 4 dice and chooses to Push Your Luck. After rerolling the eligible dice, the final result produces **3 successes**.

All three soldiers have Physical Defense 2.

The grenade successfully strikes the targeted area, generating one extra Attack success.

**3 Attack successes − 2 Physical Defense = 1 extra success**

**Step 3: Determine Damage to the Two Closest Soldiers**

The two soldiers nearest the explosion are within the grenade's primary blast area.

The grenade has 6 Damage Dice.

| Source               | Dice  |
| -------------------- | ----- |
| Grenade Damage Dice  | +6    |
| Extra Attack Success | +1    |
| **Total**            | **7** |
The player rolls 7 Damage dice for each soldier.

The roll for the first soldier is **4 Successes**. A **Lethal Wound**.

The roll for the second soldier is **3 successes**. A **Critical Wound**.

**Step 4: Determine Damage to the Third Soldier**

The third soldier is 10 feet farther from the center of the explosion.

Using the optional Blast Falloff rule, the explosion loses 1 Damage die for every 10 feet beyond the primary blast area.

| Source                  | Dice  |
| ----------------------- | ----- |
| Grenade Weapon Dice     | +6    |
| Extra Attack Success    | +1    |
| Blast Falloff (10 feet) | –1    |
| **Total**               | **6** |
The player rolls 6 Damage dice and obtains **2 successes**.

The third soldier suffers a **Serious Wound**.

**Result:** On the soldiers nearest the blast, the grenade inflicts a Lethal Wounds on one, and a Critical Wound on the other. The soldier 10 feet away receives a serious Wound. The Attack Roll determines whether the grenade hits, while the Damage Rolls determine each soldier's injuries. The optional Blast Falloff rule reduces the Damage Dice pool for targets farther from the explosion.

### Planted Explosives

Planted explosives are charges, bombs, mines, and other explosive devices positioned in advance and detonated when a specified condition occurs.

Unlike thrown grenades, planted explosives do not normally require an Attack Roll when triggered. The explosive is already positioned, and its effects are resolved as an environmental hazard.

Planted explosives may be activated by a timer, remote command, contact, proximity, or another appropriate triggering mechanism.

#### Opponents Triggering a Placed Explosive

Once properly prepared, a planted explosive detonates when its triggering condition is met.

No Attack Roll is required.

Every opponent within the affected area subtracts their Physical Defense rating from the number of Explosive's Power Level. This is the number of Damage Dice rolled to determine Wounds.

**Damage Dice = Explosive Power Level - Opponent's Physical Defense**

### Characters and Explosions

If characters are attacked with area of effect weapons or are exposed to an explosion. Every character within the affected area must attempt an appropriate Saving Throw.

**Typical Saving Throw: Dexterity + Athletics** 

The Difficulty is determined by the explosive's Damage Level.

#### Resolving the Explosion

Each character within the blast area makes a Saving Throw. Typically Dexterity + Athletics. Compare the number of successes against the Power Level.

**Successful Saving Throw:** The character avoids the primary blast and suffers no Wound, unless the explosive specifies an unavoidable secondary effect.

**Failed Saving Throw:** The character is struck by the explosion and takes Damage equal to the Defense Shortfall minus any applicable Armor and Blast Falloff.

**Defense Shortfall = Explosive Power level - Defense Successes - Armor - Blast Falloff**

Each affected character resolves damage separately.

##### Example: Two Soldiers Caught in a Land Mine Explosion

Two soldiers are crossing an abandoned battlefield when the first soldier accidentally steps on a concealed land mine. The second soldier is following 10 feet behind him.

The mine has an **Explosive Power Level of 7 (Extreme)**. Because the mine is already planted, no Attack Roll is required.

Both soldiers wear Light Armor, providing 1 point of protection. Each must make a **Dexterity + Athletics Saving Throw** against the mine's Power Level of 7.

**Step 1: First Soldier — Direct Explosion**

The first soldier is standing directly over the mine and has:

|Source|Dice|
|---|---|
|Dexterity|3|
|Athletics|2|
|**Total**|**5**|
The player rolls **1, 2, 4, 5, 6**, producing 2 successes.

The player chooses to **Push Your Luck**, keeping the dice showing 1, 5, and 6, and rerolling the dice showing 2 and 4.

The rerolled dice produce **3 and 5**, adding 1 success.

The final result is **3 successes**, insufficient to overcome the mine's Power Level of 7.

**Step 2: Calculate the First Soldier's Defense Shortfall**

**Defense Shortfall = Explosive Power Level − Saving Throw Successes − Armor − Blast Falloff**

|Source|Value|
|---|---|
|Explosive Power Level|7|
|Saving Throw Successes|−3|
|Light Armor|−1|
|Blast Falloff (0 feet)|0|
|**Final Defense Shortfall**|**3**|
A Defense Shortfall of 3 inflicts a **Critical Wound**.

The first soldier marks one Critical Wound slot and is automatically **knocked Prone** by the explosion.

**Step 3: Second Soldier — 10 Feet from the Explosion**

The second soldier is standing 10 feet from the mine. The optional Blast Falloff rule reduces the explosion's effectiveness by 1 for every 10 feet from the blast center.

The second soldier has:

| Source    | Dice  |
| --------- | ----- |
| Dexterity | 2     |
| Athletics | 3     |
| **Total** | **5** |
The player rolls **1, 2, 3, 5,6**, producing 2 successes.

The player chooses to **Push Your Luck**, keeping the dice showing 1 and 5, and rerolling the dice showing 2 and 3.

The rerolled dice produce **4 and 6**, adding 1 success.

The final result is **3 successes**, insufficient to overcome the mine's Power Level of 7.

**Step 4: Calculate the Second Soldier's Defense Shortfall**

| Source                      | Value |
| --------------------------- | ----- |
| Explosive Power Level       | 7     |
| Saving Throw Successes      | −3    |
| Light Armor                 | −1    |
| Blast Falloff (10 feet)     | −1    |
| **Final Defense Shortfall** | **2** |
A Defense Shortfall of 3 inflicts a **Serious Wound**.

The second soldier marks one Serious Wound slot and is automatically **knocked Prone** by the explosion.

**Step 5: Secondary Effect — Knockdown**

The Game Master determines that characters suffering Serious or Critical Wounds from this explosion are automatically knocked Prone.

Because both soldiers suffered these types of Wounds, both are knocked to the ground. **No additional Saving Throw is required.**

The Knockdown effect does not inflict an additional Wound. It represents the physical force of the explosion and the severity of the soldiers' injuries.

**Result**

| Soldier        | Distance | Initial Save | Final Save After Push | Armor | Blast Falloff | Wound    | Secondary Effect |
| -------------- | -------- | ------------ | --------------------- | ----- | ------------- | -------- | ---------------- |
| First Soldier  | 0 ft     | 2            | 3                     | 1     | 0             | Critical | Knocked Prone    |
| Second Soldier | 10 ft    | 2            | 3                     | 1     | −1            | Serious  | Knocked Prone    |
### Detecting a Planted Explosive

Characters searching for a planted explosive may attempt:

- **Acuity + Perception** to notice suspicious details.
- **Intelligence + Investigation** to identify signs of concealment or tampering.
- **Intelligence + Technology** to recognize electronic or mechanical components.

The Game Master determines the Difficulty based on concealment, circumstances, and preparation quality.

A concealed explosive should not automatically be discovered simply because a character enters the area.

### Awareness and Cover

Characters who notice an explosive before detonation may be able to seek cover, move away, or warn others.

The Game Master may grant additional Saving Throw dice for substantial cover or advance warning. Characters caught completely unaware may receive a penalty when there is no reasonable opportunity to react.

An explosion should not automatically become more powerful simply because its victims are unaware. Instead, surprise affects their ability to avoid its effects.

### Disabling an Explosive

A character attempting to disable a discovered explosive makes an **Intelligence + Technology** or **Dexterity + Technology Skill Check**

The Difficulty depends on the device's complexity and condition.

Success disables the explosive or prevents its intended activation.

Failure leaves the device active.

A Critical Failure may create a dangerous complication, including an unintended activation when appropriate.

### Placing an Explosive

A character placing an explosive makes an **Intelligence + Technology Skill Check**.

The Difficulty depends on the complexity of the device and the circumstances.

| Difficulty    | Example                                                              |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Easy (1)      | Place a prepared explosive in an accessible location                 |
| Normal (2)    | Conceal and prepare a charge under ordinary conditions               |
| Hard (3)      | Prepare a charge under pressure or in a difficult environment        |
| Extreme (4)   | Prepare a complex device under dangerous conditions                  |
| Legendary (5) | Prepare an exceptionally complex or improvised explosive arrangement |
**Success:** The explosive is placed and prepared as intended.

**Additional Successes:** For each success beyond the required Difficulty, the character may gain one preparation benefit, subject to Game Master approval. Benefits include improving concealment, ensuring reliable triggering, or increasing the difficulty of detecting or disabling the device.

**Failure:** The explosive is not properly prepared. The Game Master may allow another attempt with additional time or require the character to correct the problem.

**Critical Failure:** The device develops a serious fault or creates an immediate complication. Accidental detonation should occur only when the circumstances justify that danger.
