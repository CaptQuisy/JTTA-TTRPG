## Initiative <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Initiative determines which combatants act first when combat begins.

_Journey Through the Ages_ uses a simplified Initiative system that emphasizes player decisions and keeps combat moving quickly.

Under normal circumstances, **player characters act first**, followed by their opponents.

The players decide the order in which their characters act. No Initiative roll is required.

Surprise, ambushes, and encounters initiated by opponents may change this order.

### Initiative

Combat is divided into Rounds, each representing approximately six seconds.

A normal combat Round follows this sequence:

1. **Player Phase:** Each player character takes a Turn in an order chosen by the players.
2. **Opponent Phase:** Opponents take their Turns in an order determined by the Game Master.
3. **End of Round:** Resolve any effects that occur at the end of the Round, then begin the next Round.

During the Player Phase, the players may decide who acts first based on their immediate plans.

For example, a character may move into position before another character attacks, or a specialist may disable a security system before the rest of the group advances.

Each character normally takes one Turn per Round.

The Game Master determines the order in which opponents act during the Opponent Phase.

If the players cannot agree on their order, the Game Master may establish one to keep the encounter moving.

#### Example: Initiative

Three adventurers confront a group of armed smugglers.

Neither side is surprised, and the smugglers have not initiated combat. The adventurers act first.

The players decide that:

1. The soldier moves behind cover and opens fire.
2. The technician attempts to disable the warehouse lights.
3. The investigator moves toward an exit.

After the three characters complete their Turns, the smugglers act.

The Round ends when all combatants have had their opportunity to act.

### Combat Initiated by Opponents

Player characters do not always control when a fight begins.

When opponents clearly initiate combat before the characters can act, the Game Master may allow the opponents to take the first phase of the encounter.

Examples include:

- Guards suddenly opening fire.
- An enemy attacking during a tense confrontation.
- A creature lunging from concealment.
- A hostile vehicle beginning an attack before the characters respond.

The Game Master should distinguish between an opponent initiating combat and a successful ambush.

**Opponent Initiative:** The opponents act first, but the characters are aware of the immediate danger and may use permitted Reactions.

**Surprise:** One or more characters are caught unaware and suffer the additional restrictions described under Surprise.

Each of the following rounds follow the same order.

The Game Master may preserve a different phase order when an encounter-specific rule requires it.

#### Example: Opponents Act First

The characters are negotiating with an armed criminal. Without warning, the criminal draws a pistol and fires.

The Game Master determines that the criminal has initiated combat. The criminal's side acts first. Characters who are aware of the threat may use appropriate Reactions if they have available Action Points.

The player characters then take their Turns.

Subsequent Rounds follow the same sequence.

### Reactions Before the First Turn

A character not surprised may use an appropriate Reaction before taking their first Turn.

Each Reaction costs the normal number of Action Points.

Action Points spent before the character's first Turn reduce the number available during that Turn.

#### Example: Reacting Before Acting

An enemy initiates combat and fires at a character.

The character begins with 3 AP and spends 1 AP on the Defend Reaction.

The character has 2 AP remaining.

When the character's Turn begins, those 2 AP are available for Actions.

At the end of the Turn, the character's AP refresh to 3.

### Optional Initiative Rule

The Game Master may use an optional Initiative roll to introduce uncertainty into the opening moments of combat.

This rule determines **how many Action Points a character receives for their first Turn**.

It does not determine the order in which characters act.

Each player rolls:

**2d6 + Dexterity Attribute**

Compare the result with the following table.

|Initiative Result|First-Turn Action Points|
|---|---|
|6 or less|1 AP|
|7–10|2 AP|
|11 or more|3 AP|

This represents how quickly a character reacts when combat begins.

A low result indicates hesitation, confusion, or a slow response.

A high result indicates readiness, quick reflexes, and decisive action.

#### Optional Initiative Procedure

When using this rule:

1. Each player rolls 2d6 and adds their Dexterity Attribute.
2. Consult the Initiative table.
3. Set the character's available AP to the resulting number.
4. Begin combat using the normal phase order, unless opponents initiate combat or Surprise applies.
5. Each character spends their available AP on Actions and permitted Reactions.
6. At the end of the character's first Turn, restore their AP to 3.

The Initiative result affects only the character's first Turn.

All subsequent Turns use the normal Action Point rules.

#### Example: Optional Initiative

Three characters roll Initiative.

|Character|Initiative Roll|First-Turn AP|
|---|---|---|
|Soldier|12|3|
|Technician|9|2|
|Investigator|5|1|

The players may still choose the order in which the characters act.

The Soldier begins with 3 AP, the Technician with 2 AP, and the Investigator with 1 AP.

After each character completes their first Turn, their Action Points refresh to 3.

Beginning with their second Turns, all three characters use the normal AP allowance.

### Surprise

Not every combat begins with both sides prepared.

When one side successfully ambushes another, the surprised combatants begin the encounter at a significant disadvantage.

Surprise represents an attack that occurs before the affected characters recognize the immediate threat.

The Game Master determines whether Surprise applies based on the circumstances leading into combat.

Factors may include:

- Successful Stealth.
- Concealment or darkness.
- A sudden betrayal.
- An unexpected attack from a hidden position.
- A distraction that prevents the target from noticing danger.
- An attack from an unexpected direction.

Surprise should follow naturally from the situation.

An opponent does not automatically gain Surprise merely by declaring an attack first.

#### Determining Surprise

The Game Master determines which characters are surprised.

When appropriate, the Game Master may call for an Acuity + Perception Check against the ambusher's relevant Stealth or detection Difficulty.

A character who detects the danger in time is not surprised.

Different characters may have different results.

For example, one character may notice a concealed attacker while another remains unaware.

#### Effects of Surprise

A surprised character cannot react to the opening attack or hazard that catches them unaware.

If the opening event causes damage, resolve the required Damage Checks before the surprised character takes any Reaction or Action.

The surprised character must suffer the consequences of that opening event before responding. Surprise does not automatically cause damage. A Damage Check is made only when an actual attack or damaging event requires one.

After the opening event is resolved, the character may take their first Turn when their phase occurs, using any Action Points available under the normal or optional Initiative rules.

A surprised character cannot retroactively Defend, Interpose, or otherwise react to the event that caused the Surprise.

Once the surprised character completes their first Turn, the Surprise restriction ends. The character acts normally for the remainder of the encounter.

##### Example: Ambush in the Jungle

A patrol moves through dense jungle without noticing concealed attackers.

The attackers open fire.

The Game Master determines that two patrol members are surprised, while a third noticed movement in the undergrowth. The surprised characters cannot use Reactions against the opening gunfire.

They resolve the resulting attacks and any required Damage Checks.

The third patrol member is not surprised and may use an appropriate Reaction.

After the opening attack, the surviving patrol members take their Turns using their available Action Points.

Once each surprised character completes their first Turn, they are no longer surprised.

### Game Master Guidance

Initiative should establish the opening circumstances of combat without requiring unnecessary bookkeeping.

**Use Standard Initiative for Most Encounters**

- Allowing player characters to act first and choose their order keeps combat fast and encourages cooperation.

**Reserve Opponent Initiative for Clear Circumstances**

- Opponents should act first when they genuinely seize the initiative, not simply because the Game Master wants to increase difficulty.

**Distinguish Initiative from Surprise**

- Acting first does not automatically mean the opposing side is surprised.
- Surprise requires the target to be genuinely unaware of the immediate threat.

**Preserve Reaction Opportunities**

- Characters who recognize danger may use available Action Points to react before their first Turns.
- Surprised characters cannot react to the opening event that catches them unaware.

**Initiative should determine who has the advantage at the beginning of combat while allowing players to make meaningful decisions about how their characters respond.**
