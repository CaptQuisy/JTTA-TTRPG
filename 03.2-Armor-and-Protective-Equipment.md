## Armor & Protective Equipment

Armor protects characters from injury by reducing their vulnerability to attacks.

Throughout history, people have developed protective equipment ranging from padded clothing and metal armor to ballistic vests, advanced composites, and powered combat suits.

Although the materials and technology change between eras, _Journey Through the Ages_ uses a consistent system for determining the protection armor provides.

Armor is divided into three categories: **Light, Medium, and Heavy**.

Each category represents a balance between protection, mobility, and concealment.

The Game Master determines which types of armor are available in the campaign and whether their construction is suitable against particular threats.

### Armor Profiles

Armor provides a bonus to Physical Defense when it can reasonably protect against an incoming attack.

Heavier armor provides greater protection but may restrict movement, stealth, and agility.

| Armor Category | Defense Bonus | Movement | Stealth | Agility                                                            |
| -------------- | ------------- | -------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| None           | —             | Normal   | Normal  | Normal                                                             |
| Light Armor    | +1 die        | Normal   | Normal  | Normal                                                             |
| Medium Armor   | +2 dice       | –5 feet  | –1 die  | No penalty                                                         |
| Heavy Armor    | +3 dice       | –10 feet | –2 dice | –1 die on Dexterity-based Skill Checks and Dexterity Saving Throws |
#### Defense Bonus

Armor adds the listed number of dice to the character's Physical Defense roll against attacks that the armor can reasonably resist.

For example, a character wearing Medium Armor gains +2 dice on qualifying Defense rolls.

Armor does not normally modify Attack rolls, Skill Checks, or Saving Throws unless a specific rule states otherwise.

#### Movement Penalties

Armor movement penalties reduce the distance a character can travel with each Move Action.

Under the standard movement rules, a character normally moves 30 feet with one Move Action.

|Armor|Movement per Move Action|
|---|---|
|None|30 feet|
|Light Armor|30 feet|
|Medium Armor|25 feet|
|Heavy Armor|20 feet|
These values assume the character has no other movement modifiers.

Apply any additional movement restrictions from injuries, difficult terrain, or other effects according to their respective rules.

#### Stealth Penalties

Medium and Heavy Armor may make stealth more difficult because of noise, weight, restricted movement, or bulky equipment.

Apply the listed penalty to Stealth Skill Checks while the armor is worn.

The penalty applies whether the character is attempting to move quietly, remain concealed, or avoid detection through physical movement.

#### Agility Penalties

Heavy Armor restricts flexibility and rapid movement.

A character wearing Heavy Armor suffers –1 die on Dexterity-based Skill Checks and Dexterity Saving Throws.

This penalty applies to actions such as balancing, tumbling, delicate physical manipulation, and avoiding sudden hazards when Dexterity is the relevant Attribute.

It does not reduce the character's Dexterity Attribute or Dexterity-based Attack rolls unless another rule specifically imposes such a penalty.

### Wearing Armor

A character may wear only one suit of armor at a time.

Armor bonuses do not stack with bonuses from another suit of worn armor unless a specific rule states otherwise.

Wearing multiple layers of protective clothing does not automatically grant additional Defense dice.

The Game Master may describe a suit of armor as consisting of several layers or components, but the entire suit normally uses a single Armor Profile.

#### Armor and Physical Defense

Armor may protect against:

- Close Combat weapons
- Ranged weapons
- Natural weapons
- Unarmed attacks
- Other physical attacks appropriate to the armor's construction

The Armor Defense bonus applies only when the armor can reasonably resist the attack.

Some hazards bypass ordinary armor entirely, including:

- Drowning or suffocation
- Poisonous gases entering an unsealed suit
- Psychic or mental attacks
- Extreme temperatures beyond the armor's protective capabilities
- Grappling techniques that restrain movement without needing to penetrate the armor

Other attacks may partially or completely bypass armor because of specialized weapon properties.

See Section 3.1 for Armor Piercing weapons.

#### Armor and Technology

Armor protection depends not only on its category but also on its construction.

A leather jerkin, padded gambeson, and ballistic vest might all use the Light Armor profile, but they are not necessarily effective against the same threats.

For example:

- A padded gambeson may protect against cuts and impacts.
- A ballistic vest may protect against certain firearms but provide limited coverage against other attacks.
- A sealed environmental suit may protect against atmospheric hazards but offer little protection against weapons.

The Game Master determines whether a particular armor type provides its full Defense bonus, reduced protection, or no protection against a specific attack.

These distinctions should be established consistently for the campaign rather than decided arbitrarily during combat.

#### Light Armor

Light Armor emphasizes mobility and concealment rather than maximum protection.

It offers modest protection without significantly restricting movement or physical coordination.

**Defense Bonus:** +1 die

**Movement:** Normal

**Stealth:** No penalty

**Agility:** No penalty

##### Examples

- Leather jerkin
- Padded gambeson
- Reinforced clothing
- Ballistic vest
- Concealable tactical armor
- Reinforced jumpsuit

Light Armor is appropriate for characters who expect danger but need to remain mobile.

It is especially useful for scouts, investigators, travelers, and characters who rely on stealth.

##### Concealment

Light Armor can often be concealed beneath ordinary clothing, depending on its construction.

A concealable ballistic vest might be hidden beneath a jacket, while a bulky padded garment may be more noticeable.

The Game Master determines whether the armor is visible and whether an observer has a reasonable opportunity to discover it.

#### Medium Armor

Medium Armor provides substantial protection while retaining enough mobility for active combat.

It is commonly worn by soldiers, SWAT teams, guards, and other characters who expect direct confrontation.

**Defense Bonus:** +2 dice

**Movement:** –5 feet per Move Action

**Stealth:** –1 die

**Agility:** No penalty

##### Examples

- Chainmail
- Scale armor
- Riot armor
- SWAT protective equipment
- Composite combat armor
- Military tactical armor

Medium Armor offers a useful balance between protection and mobility.

However, its weight, bulk, and construction make quiet movement more difficult.

##### Concealment

Medium Armor cannot normally be concealed beneath ordinary clothing.

Loose cloaks, coats, or other bulky garments may disguise portions of the armor, but careful observation will usually reveal its presence.

#### Heavy Armor

Heavy Armor provides the greatest protection available under the standard Armor Profiles.

Its substantial weight and bulk restrict movement, stealth, and physical flexibility.

**Defense Bonus:** +3 dice

**Movement:** –10 feet per Move Action

**Stealth:** –2 dice

**Agility:** –1 die on Dexterity-based Skill Checks and Dexterity Saving Throws

##### Examples

- Full plate armor
- Bomb-disposal suit
- Heavy combat armor
- Powered infantry armor
- Assault exoskeleton

Heavy Armor is generally worn by characters who prioritize protection over mobility.

It may be particularly useful during direct assaults, defensive operations, or situations involving sustained enemy fire.

##### Concealment

Heavy Armor is highly visible and cannot normally be concealed.

Its appearance may also attract attention, restrict access to certain locations, or create social and legal complications.

#### Powered Armor

Some futuristic settings feature powered armor or combat exoskeletons.

These systems may use the Heavy Armor profile for their protective characteristics.

However, powered assistance does not automatically eliminate the standard movement, Stealth, or Agility penalties.

Any additional capabilities—such as enhanced Strength, improved movement, integrated weapons, environmental sealing, or life-support systems—must be defined separately.

The Game Master may modify the standard penalties if the powered armor's design specifically compensates for them.

#### Shields

Shields provide additional protection by allowing a character to block or deflect incoming attacks.

They may be constructed from wood, metal, ballistic composites, or advanced energy-projecting technology.

##### Examples

- Wooden shield
- Kite shield
- Metal combat shield
- Riot shield
- Ballistic shield
- Energy shield projector

##### Shield Defense

A shield provides **+1 die on Physical Defense rolls** against attacks originating from the direction the shield protects.

This bonus is separate from worn armor and may be combined with the Armor Defense bonus when both apply.

A character wearing Medium Armor and carrying an appropriate shield may therefore receive +3 Defense dice: +2 from armor and +1 from the shield.

##### Shield Limitations

The shield bonus applies only when the character can reasonably use the shield against the incoming attack.

The Game Master considers:

- The direction of the attack
- Whether the character is holding or actively using the shield
- The size and construction of the shield
- Whether the attack can be blocked or deflected
- Whether the character is surprised, restrained, or otherwise unable to position the shield

A shield does not automatically protect against attacks from behind or from directions outside its effective coverage.

##### Shields and Area Attacks

Shields may provide protection against explosions or other area attacks when their size and construction make such protection reasonable.

For example, a large ballistic shield might protect against fragments from an explosion occurring directly in front of the character.

A small wooden shield might provide little protection against a large blast or intense heat.

The Game Master determines whether the shield's Defense bonus applies.

#### Helmets

Helmets protect the head against impacts, falling objects, fragments, and certain weapon attacks.

They may also provide additional protection against environmental hazards.

##### Examples

- Steel helmet
- Great helm
- Combat helmet
- Tactical helmet
- Powered armor helmet

##### Helmet Protection

When a Saving Throw specifically involves avoiding or resisting injury to the head, the Game Master may grant **+1 die** if the helmet is appropriate to the hazard.

Examples include:

- Falling debris striking the head
- Head injuries caused by a collision
- Certain concussive impacts
- Hazards involving flying fragments

This bonus is conditional rather than universal.

A helmet does not provide +1 die on every Saving Throw or every Defense roll.

##### Helmet Limitations

Some helmets may restrict hearing, peripheral vision, or communication.

An enclosed helmet may impose a penalty on relevant Perception Checks when its construction significantly interferes with the character's senses.

The Game Master determines whether such penalties apply.

Modern helmets with integrated communications or enhanced sensors may reduce or eliminate these limitations.

##### Helmets and Armor

A helmet is normally considered part of a complete suit of armor when appropriate to the equipment.

Wearing a helmet does not automatically increase the Armor Defense bonus beyond the value listed in the Armor Table.

Its separate +1 die benefit applies only to qualifying Saving Throws.

### Armor and Special Attacks

Not every attack interacts with armor in the same manner.

The following guidelines help determine when armor applies.

|Attack or Hazard|Typical Armor Interaction|
|---|---|
|Sword, knife, or club|Armor normally applies if suitable against the weapon|
|Firearm|Armor applies if designed or sufficiently protective against the projectile|
|Unarmed strike|Armor normally applies|
|Grappling or restraint|Armor may not apply|
|Armor Piercing attack|Reduce the applicable armor protection|
|Poison gas|Ordinary unsealed armor provides no protection|
|Drowning|Ordinary armor provides no protection|
|Fire or extreme heat|Requires appropriate heat-resistant protection|
|Psychic or mental attack|Physical armor normally provides no protection|
|Falling debris|Armor or a helmet may provide protection, depending on the hazard|
These examples are guidelines rather than additional universal modifiers.

The Game Master should apply the established equipment properties and relevant hazard rules consistently.

### Opponent Armor

The Game Master does not roll Defense dice for opponents.

Instead, opponents possess a static Physical Defense rating.

An opponent's Physical Defense should already include the protection provided by worn armor, natural armor, or other defensive abilities.

#### Converting Armor to Static Defense

When creating an opponent, the Game Master should account for its Armor Profile when determining its Physical Defense.

The exact numerical conversion must follow the standard rules for constructing opponent Defense ratings.

Do not add the Armor Defense dice directly to an opponent's static Defense number unless the opponent-creation rules explicitly require that conversion.

#### Armor Piercing Against Opponents

When an attack has Armor Piercing, reduce only the portion of the opponent's Physical Defense attributable to armor, using the applicable Armor Piercing rules.

The opponent's other defensive abilities remain unchanged.

If the opponent's Defense rating does not separately identify its armor contribution, the Game Master should establish that contribution before resolving Armor Piercing attacks.

### Customizing Armor

Armor Profiles are intentionally broad.

The same profile can represent different protective equipment across many historical periods and fictional settings.

Changing an armor's appearance does not automatically change its statistics.

The Game Master may approve modifications that reflect meaningful technological or functional differences.

Examples include:

- Improved ballistic resistance
- Environmental sealing
- Integrated communication systems
- Enhanced mobility
- Reduced weight
- Improved concealment
- Specialized protection against heat, radiation, or chemicals
- Powered assistance

Such modifications should have clearly defined effects.

A suit of armor should not receive additional Defense dice merely because it is described as technologically advanced.

Advanced armor may instead provide specialized protection, reduced penalties, or capabilities unavailable to earlier equipment.

### Game Master Guidance

When assigning an Armor Profile to new equipment:

1. Determine the armor's level of physical protection.
2. Select Light, Medium, or Heavy Armor.
3. Apply the profile's Defense bonus and movement penalties.
4. Identify which attacks or hazards the armor can reasonably resist.
5. Determine whether it can be concealed.
6. Assign any special capabilities or limitations.

Armor should provide meaningful protection without making characters invulnerable.

The Game Master should consider the campaign's technology, the weapons commonly encountered, and the desired level of realism.

**Armor helps characters survive dangerous encounters, but its weight, construction, and limitations remain important considerations when preparing for adventure.**
