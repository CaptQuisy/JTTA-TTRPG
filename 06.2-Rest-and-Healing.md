# Rest & Healing

Adventuring is dangerous, and even the hardiest characters eventually suffer injuries. Whether recovering from a sword wound, gunshot, broken bone, or other serious injury, characters need time and sometimes medical care to heal.

How quickly characters recover depends on the tone of the campaign.

_Journey Through the Ages_ provides two approaches to healing:

- **Epic Heroism:** Characters recover quickly, allowing adventures to focus on action, danger, and dramatic encounters.
- **Gritty Realism:** Injuries require more time, medical attention, and careful recovery. Severe wounds may leave lasting consequences.

The Game Master should choose one healing model before the campaign begins and apply it consistently.

Both models use the Wound severities and Injury Tracker described in Chapter 6, _Damage & Wounds_.

## Epic Heroism

Epic Heroism is designed for cinematic adventures in which characters recover quickly between dangerous encounters.

Injuries remain meaningful during combat, but extended periods of medical recovery rarely interrupt the story.

### Types of Rest

Characters who survive their injuries recover through rest.

A **Short Rest** requires at least 1 hour of rest without strenuous activity.

A **Full Day of Rest** requires approximately 24 hours devoted primarily to recovery, including normal sleep and limited physical activity.

### Epic Heroism Recovery

| Rest Period         | Recovery                                            |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Short Rest (1 hour) | Removes all Minor Wounds                            |
| Full Day of Rest    | Reduces every remaining Wound by one severity level |

A Wound that is reduced in severity moves to the corresponding lower-severity slot on the Injury Tracker.

For example:

- Lethal becomes Critical.
- Critical becomes Serious.
- Serious becomes Minor.
- Minor is removed by a Short Rest.

The character must have an available slot at the new severity. If there is no available slot, the Wound remains at its current severity until a suitable slot becomes available.

A character cannot reduce a Wound below Minor.

Minor Wounds are removed after the required Short Rest.

#### Example: Recovering from a Lethal Wound

A character survives a Lethal Wound and is stabilized.

The character rests for several days.

|Recovery Period|Wound Status|
|---|---|
|Initial Injury|Lethal|
|After Day 1|Critical|
|After Day 2|Serious|
|After Day 3|Minor|
|After a Short Rest|Fully Healed|

The character must still receive any immediate stabilization required by Chapter 6.

Natural recovery does not replace stabilization for a character who is actively Dying.

### Medical Treatment

Medical treatment can accelerate recovery.

A character providing treatment makes an **Intelligence + Medicine Skill Check**. 

The Difficulty depends on the severity of the Wound being treated.

|Wound Severity|Treatment Difficulty|
|---|---|
|Serious|Normal (2 successes)|
|Critical|Hard (3 successes)|
|Lethal|Hard (3 successes)|

Minor Wounds do not normally require a Medicine Check in Epic Heroism.

#### Successful Treatment

A successful treatment reduces the selected Wound by one severity level.

Additional successes beyond the required Difficulty may reduce the Wound by additional severity levels.

**Each additional success reduces the Wound by one additional severity level.**

For example, treating a Critical Wound requires 3 successes.

If the healer obtains:

|Medicine Successes|Result|
|---|---|
|0–2|No reduction|
|3|Critical → Serious|
|4|Critical → Minor|
|5 or more|Critical → Fully Healed|

A single treatment cannot reduce a Wound below fully healed.

### Treatment Frequency

Only **one medical treatment attempt per Wound per day** is permitted.

A failed attempt cannot be repeated on the same Wound until the next day.

A different healer cannot bypass this limit.

Treatment does not automatically remove Conditions that require separate care.

#### Example: Treating a Serious Wound

A physician treats a character who has suffered a Serious Wound.

The physician rolls Intelligence + Medicine against Normal Difficulty (2 successes).

The roll produces 3 successes.

The first 2 successes meet the required Difficulty, reducing the Wound from Serious to Minor.

The additional success reduces the remaining Minor Wound to fully healed.

The treatment succeeds, and the Wound is removed from the Injury Tracker.

## Gritty Realism

Gritty Realism is intended for campaigns in which injuries have greater consequences.

Recovery requires time, appropriate care, and sometimes medical facilities.

Even when a character survives combat, serious injuries may limit their ability to continue adventuring.

### Minor Wounds

Minor Wounds represent bruises, cuts, scrapes, and other relatively manageable injuries.

A character may recover from Minor Wounds through rest.

|Rest Period|Recovery|
|---|---|
|Short Rest (1 hour)|Removes one Minor Wound, maximum once per day|
|Full Day of Uninterrupted Rest|Removes all Minor Wounds|

The character chooses which Minor Wound is removed after a Short Rest, if individual injuries are being tracked.

A character cannot repeatedly take Short Rests to remove multiple Minor Wounds during the same day.

A Full Day of uninterrupted rest removes all remaining Minor Wounds.

#### Example: Minor Wound Recovery

A character has three Minor Wounds.

After resting for 1 hour, the character removes one Minor Wound.

The character still has two Minor Wounds.

Additional Short Rests that day do not remove more Wounds.

If the character instead completes a Full Day of uninterrupted rest, all remaining Minor Wounds are removed.

### Serious Wounds

Serious Wounds represent substantial injuries that interfere with a character's activities.

Examples include:

- Deep cuts.
- Significant bruising or blunt-force trauma.
- Moderate burns.
- Painful sprains.
- Non-disabling fractures.
- Gunshot wounds that do not immediately threaten life.

A Serious Wound normally requires **2 days of rest** to heal.

Successful medical treatment reduces the recovery time to **1 day**.

#### Treating a Serious Wound

**Skill Check:** Intelligence + Medicine

**Difficulty:** Normal (2 successes)

|Treatment Result|Recovery Time|
|---|---|
|No successful treatment|2 days|
|Successful treatment|1 day|

The recovery time assumes the character receives adequate rest and avoids activities that aggravate the injury.

If the character resumes strenuous activity before recovery is complete, the Game Master may delay healing or require additional treatment.

##### Example: Treating a Gunshot Wound

A character suffers a Serious gunshot wound to the shoulder.

Without successful treatment, the Wound heals after 2 days of rest.

A physician makes a Normal Intelligence + Medicine Check and succeeds.

The recovery time is reduced to 1 day.

The character must still rest for the required period.

### Critical Wounds

Critical Wounds represent severe injuries that may disable a limb, cause substantial blood loss, or otherwise threaten the character's ability to function.

Examples include:

- Major fractures.
- Severe burns.
- Deep penetrating wounds.
- Extensive tissue damage.
- Serious internal injuries.
- Injuries causing significant loss of mobility.

A Critical Wound normally requires **3 days of rest and successful medical treatment**.

#### Treating a Critical Wound

**Skill Check:** Intelligence + Medicine

**Difficulty:** Hard (3 successes)

Successful treatment:

- Supports recovery within the normal 3-day period.
- Reduces the likelihood of permanent injury.
- May improve the quality of recovery.
- May prevent additional complications.

##### Example: Critical Leg Injury

A character suffers a Critical Wound to the leg. The injury causes the Disabled Leg Condition.

The character receives treatment from a physician. The physician succeeds on a Hard Intelligence + Medicine Check. The character requires 3 days of rest to recover from the Wound.

If the character receives inadequate treatment or attempts strenuous activity during recovery, the Game Master may introduce additional complications.

### Lethal Wounds

Lethal Wounds are immediately life-threatening.

Examples include:

- Severe gunshot wounds.
- Major internal bleeding.
- Extensive burns.
- Severe head trauma.
- Massive crushing injuries.
- Other potentially fatal trauma.

A character who suffers a Lethal Wound gains the **Dying Condition**, as described in Chapter 6.

#### Requirements for Recovery

Recovering from a Lethal Wound requires:

1. Immediate stabilization or survival options.
2. At least **5 days of recovery**.
3. Successful daily medical treatment.
4. Appropriate medical supplies and care.

#### Daily Medical Treatment

**Skill Check:** Intelligence + Medicine

**Difficulty:** Hard (3 successes)

The character requires one medical treatment check for each day of recovery.

A successful check represents adequate treatment and continued progress.

A failed check represents a day in which treatment does not adequately support recovery.

#### Failed Treatment

A single failed daily treatment check does not automatically kill the character.

Instead, the Game Master determines an appropriate complication.

Possible consequences include:

- The recovery period is extended.
- The character develops an additional Condition.
- The Wound worsens.
- The character requires more advanced medical care.
- The character returns to the Dying Condition.

Repeated failures or the complete absence of medical care may lead to death.

#### Additional Successes

Additional successes beyond the required Difficulty may:

- Reduce the likelihood of permanent complications.
- Improve recovery quality.
- Shorten the recovery period when the Game Master permits it.

However, the character must still complete the minimum **5-day recovery period**.

##### Example: Recovery from a Lethal Wound

A soldier suffers a Lethal Wound and is stabilized.

The soldier is transported to a hospital. A physician makes a Hard Intelligence + Medicine Check each day. The first four days of treatment succeed. 

On the fifth day, the physician fails the check. The Game Master determines that the soldier needs an additional day of recovery.

The physician succeeds on the sixth day. The soldier completes recovery from the Lethal Wound. However, the Game Master may determine that the injury has left a lasting impairment.

## Medical Facilities

The quality of available medical equipment and facilities affects treatment.

Improvised field care is more difficult than treatment in a properly equipped clinic or hospital.

Apply the following modifiers to Medicine Skill Checks.

### Medical Facility Modifiers

|Medical Facility|Medicine Modifier|
|---|---|
|Improvised or Poor Supplies|–1 die|
|Standard Supplies or Ordinary Field Care|No modifier|
|Adequate Clinic or Aid Station|+1 die|
|Advanced Hospital or Medical Center|+2 dice|

These modifiers represent access to appropriate equipment, sanitation, medication, diagnostic tools, and other medical resources.

The Game Master may adjust them to reflect the campaign's technology.

#### Primitive and Historical Settings

In settings with limited medical knowledge, even the best available facilities may struggle to treat certain injuries.

A well-equipped historical hospital may provide good care by the standards of its era without possessing modern antibiotics, imaging equipment, or advanced surgery.

#### Modern and Advanced Settings

Modern hospitals may provide substantial benefits for treating severe injuries. Advanced or science-fiction medical facilities may have specialized equipment that improves treatment.

The Game Master may allow exceptional technology to reduce recovery times or prevent lasting complications. Such benefits should be established through equipment or setting rules rather than assumed.

##### Example: Field Treatment

A physician attempts to treat a Critical Wound using improvised supplies in an abandoned building.

The physician's normal Intelligence + Medicine dice pool is 6 dice.

Improvised supplies impose –1 die.

The physician rolls **5 dice** against Hard Difficulty (3 successes).

If the same treatment were performed in an Advanced Hospital, the physician would instead receive +2 dice, increasing the pool to **8 dice**.

## Rest and Recovery

Healing requires appropriate rest.

A character who continues strenuous activity while injured may delay recovery.

### Short Rest

A Short Rest lasts approximately **1 hour**.

The character may:

- Sit or lie down.
- Eat and drink.
- Perform light conversation.
- Receive basic medical attention.
- Perform other activities that do not interfere with recovery.

The character should not engage in combat, heavy physical exertion, or strenuous travel during a Short Rest.

### Full Day of Rest

A Full Day of Rest represents approximately **24 hours** devoted primarily to recovery.

The character may perform basic personal activities but should avoid strenuous exertion.

The Game Master determines whether interruptions are significant enough to prevent recovery.

### Interrupted Recovery

If a character engages in combat or strenuous activity during a recovery period, the Game Master may:

- Delay the recovery.
- Require additional rest.
- Call for an appropriate Saving Throw.
- Introduce a complication if the injury is aggravated.

These consequences should reflect the severity of the injury and the campaign's selected healing model.

## Wounds and Conditions

Wounds and Conditions represent related but distinct effects.

A Wound represents physical injury.

A Condition represents a specific mechanical or narrative consequence.

Examples include:

- Bleeding.
- Burning.
- Dazed.
- Disabled.
- Slowed.
- Stunned.
- Unconscious.
- Dying.

### Removing Conditions

A Condition ends according to its own rules, medical treatment, recovery, or another applicable effect.

Healing a Wound does not automatically remove every Condition associated with it. Likewise, removing a Condition does not necessarily heal the underlying Wound.

For example, a character may stop Bleeding while still suffering a Serious Wound.

A character with a Disabled Leg may require additional recovery before the limb functions normally.

## Long-Term Recovery

Some injuries leave lasting effects even after the immediate Wound has healed.

These consequences are particularly appropriate in Gritty Realism campaigns.

Examples include:

- Permanent scars.
- Chronic pain.
- Reduced mobility.
- Loss of function in an injured limb.
- Lasting weakness.
- Other physical impairments.

The Game Master may assign an appropriate Hindrance when a character survives an especially severe injury.

The Hindrance should reflect the nature of the injury.

#### Examples of Lasting Hindrances

|Injury|Possible Hindrance|
|---|---|
|Severe arm injury|Arm Injury|
|Severe leg injury|Leg Injury|
|Major lasting physical trauma|Fragile Physique|
|Permanent neurological impairment|Appropriate custom Hindrance|
|Lasting mobility restriction|Leg Injury or appropriate custom Hindrance|

Permanent Hindrances should not be imposed for every Serious or Critical Wound. They are most appropriate when an injury has produced a significant and lasting consequence.

The Game Master should explain the likely consequences of severe injuries before they become permanent.

## Game Master Guidance

**Choose the Healing Model Before Play**

- Epic Heroism and Gritty Realism create different campaign experiences.
- Epic Heroism supports frequent action and rapid recovery.
- Gritty Realism makes injury management, medical care, and downtime more important.
- Avoid switching between models merely to increase or reduce the difficulty of a particular encounter.

**Keep Recovery Consistent**

- Apply the selected recovery times and medical Difficulty values consistently.
- Players should be able to anticipate the general consequences of injury.

**Make Medical Skills Valuable**

- Medicine should provide meaningful benefits.
- Characters who invest in medical training, equipment, and facilities should improve the group's chances of surviving serious injuries.

**Consider Available Technology**

- Medical capabilities vary significantly across historical eras.
- A Civil War battlefield surgeon, a modern trauma physician, and a science-fiction medical specialist may all possess the Medicine Skill but have very different equipment and treatment options.

**Avoid Arbitrary Permanent Injuries**

- Lasting Hindrances should arise from significant injuries and established campaign expectations.
- They should not be imposed simply because a character failed an ordinary Medicine Check.

**Preserve the Importance of Rest**

- Recovery should create meaningful decisions about whether to continue an adventure or withdraw to safety.
- Injured characters may need to decide between pressing forward and risking additional complications.

**Rest & Healing should support the campaign's tone while keeping medical treatment, injury recovery, and lasting consequences consistent with the game's Wound system.**
