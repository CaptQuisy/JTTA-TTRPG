## Vehicle Combat

Vehicle Combat covers attacks involving cars, trucks, armored vehicles, boats, ships, aircraft, spacecraft, and other vehicles.

Vehicle combat follows the same basic principles as character combat, but vehicles introduce additional considerations, including mounted weapons, targeting systems, maneuverability, armor, size, and durability.

A vehicle may attack another vehicle, a character, a structure, or another appropriate target.

The Game Master determines the applicable rules based on the type of vehicle, weapon, and circumstances.

### Vehicle Combat Ratings

Vehicles use several characteristics to resolve combat.

|Rating|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Ranged Attack Rating|Represents the effectiveness of mounted weapons and targeting systems|
|Close Combat Attack Rating|Represents the effectiveness of ramming, sideswiping, crushing, and collisions|
|Defense Rating|Represents the vehicle's protection against successful attacks|
|Handling|Represents maneuverability and ease of control|
|Durability|Represents the vehicle's ability to withstand damage|

These characteristics represent different aspects of vehicle performance.

A vehicle may have excellent targeting equipment but poor maneuverability. Likewise, a heavily armored vehicle may be difficult to damage despite having limited speed or handling.

The Game Master should consider each characteristic separately.

### Vehicle Ranged Attacks

Vehicles equipped with mounted weapons have a **Ranged Attack Rating**.

This rating represents the combined effectiveness of:

- Weapon mounts and stabilization.
- Targeting systems.
- Fire-control equipment.
- Weapon accuracy.
- Vehicle stability.
- Other factors affecting the ability to engage a target.

The Ranged Attack Rating belongs to the vehicle or weapon system rather than the operator.

A skilled operator, specialized crew, Edge, or appropriate equipment may improve the effectiveness of the attack.

### Vehicle Weapon Accuracy

Vehicle-mounted weapons use five general accuracy categories.

|Ranged Attack Rating|Accuracy|Description|
|---|---|---|
|2|Poor|Crude, improvised, damaged, or extremely difficult to aim|
|3|Fair|Unguided or inherently difficult weapon|
|4|Good|Standard military weapon system|
|5|Excellent|Guided or advanced targeting system|
|6|Exceptional|Extremely sophisticated precision targeting|

These ratings provide guidelines for assigning values to additional vehicle weapons.

The Game Master may adjust a weapon's rating to reflect the quality and capabilities of a particular installation.

#### Accuracy and Destructive Power

A weapon's Ranged Attack Rating represents its ability to hit a target.

It does not directly represent the weapon's destructive power.

For example, a machine gun and a tank cannon might have the same Attack Rating even though the cannon is capable of inflicting much greater damage.

**Attack Rating answers: Can the weapon hit the target?**

**Weapon damage answers: What happens when the weapon hits?**

Use the appropriate weapon damage and Durability rules to determine the effects of a successful attack.

#### Sample Vehicle Weapons

The following examples provide representative Ranged Attack Ratings and typical engagement ranges.

|Weapon Type|Attack Rating|Typical Range|Description|
|---|---|---|---|
|Machine Gun|3|Medium|Rapid-fire weapon commonly mounted on military vehicles, boats, and aircraft|
|Heavy Machine Gun|3|Medium–Long|Powerful automatic weapon effective against personnel, light vehicles, and exposed equipment|
|Autocannon|4|Long|Rapid-firing cannon used against vehicles, aircraft, and hardened targets|
|Cannon / Tank Gun|5|Long|Heavy direct-fire weapon designed primarily to destroy armored vehicles and fortifications|
|Rocket Launcher|3|Medium–Long|Fires unguided rockets against vehicles, structures, or groups of targets|
|Guided Missile|4|Long–Extreme|Guided weapon designed to engage distant vehicles, aircraft, ships, or spacecraft|
|Naval Gun|6|Long–Extreme|Large-caliber weapon mounted aboard warships for engaging vessels or shore targets|
|Bomb|4|Special|Dropped or released against a location or target below an aircraft or spacecraft|
|Laser|3|Long|Directed-energy weapon typically found in advanced or science-fiction settings|
|Railgun|4|Extreme|High-velocity projectile weapon used primarily by advanced military vehicles or spacecraft|
|Torpedo|5|Long|Self-propelled guided weapon designed for underwater or space combat|
These values represent typical weapon systems, not every possible example of the weapon.

A crude naval gun may be less accurate than the example above, while an advanced laser with sophisticated targeting equipment may be considerably more effective.

The Game Master may adjust ratings to suit the technology and setting.

#### Resolving Vehicle Ranged Attacks

Vehicle-mounted weapons use the standard player-facing combat system.

The exact procedure depends on who is operating the weapon.

### Player Character Attacking an NPC Vehicle

When a player character fires a mounted weapon at an NPC-controlled vehicle:

1. The player declares the target and weapon.
2. The Game Master identifies the vehicle's Defense Rating.
3. The player builds an appropriate Attack dice pool.
4. The weapon system's Ranged Attack Rating provides the base attack capability.
5. Applicable operator, equipment, range, and situational modifiers are added or subtracted.
6. The player rolls the Attack dice.
7. If successes equal or exceed the target's Defense Rating, the attack hits.
8. Damage is resolved using the applicable vehicle damage rules.

For this procedure, the weapon's Ranged Attack Rating is treated as the base number of Attack dice.

The Game Master determines any additional dice provided by the operator's capabilities or other circumstances.

#### Example: Tank Cannon

A player character operates a tank cannon with Ranged Attack Rating 5.

The target is an enemy armored vehicle with Defense Rating 4.

The weapon provides a base Attack pool of 5 dice.

The Game Master applies any relevant range, crew, equipment, or environmental modifiers.

The player rolls the final pool.

If the roll produces at least 4 successes, the cannon hits.

The resulting damage is resolved separately.

### NPC Vehicle Attacking a Player-Controlled Vehicle

When an NPC-controlled vehicle attacks a player-controlled vehicle, the Game Master does not roll an Attack.

Instead:

1. The Game Master identifies the attacking weapon and its Ranged Attack Rating.
2. Applicable modifiers adjust that rating.
3. The player describes the defensive response.
4. The player makes an appropriate Defense Roll.
5. Compare Defense successes with the modified Attack Rating.
6. Resolve the attack and damage according to the vehicle combat and damage rules.

The player's Defense Roll may represent evasive maneuvering, defensive systems, or other appropriate actions.

The Game Master determines the applicable Attribute, Skill, and modifiers.

#### Example: Evading a Missile

A hostile aircraft fires a guided missile at a player-controlled aircraft.

The missile has Ranged Attack Rating 4.

The pilot attempts to evade the missile using Dexterity + Vehicle, modified by the aircraft's Handling and other relevant circumstances.

The player rolls the Defense dice pool against Attack Rating 4.

If the Defense succeeds, the missile fails to hit.

If the Defense fails, the missile hits and the applicable vehicle damage rules determine the consequences.

### Range by Vehicle Type

Vehicle combat often occurs at distances much greater than ordinary character combat. The appropriate distances depend on the type of vehicle and the environment.

A car chase may involve vehicles separated by a few hundred feet.

Aircraft may engage targets several miles away.

Spacecraft may exchange fire across hundreds of miles.

Use the following ranges as general guidelines.

#### Vehicle Combat Range Bands

|Range Band|Ground Vehicles|Watercraft|Aircraft|Spacecraft|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Close|Up to 100 ft|Up to 300 ft|Up to 1,500 ft|Up to ½ mile|
|Short|Over 100–300 ft|Over 300–1,500 ft|Over 1,500 ft–1 mile|Over ½–5 miles|
|Medium|Over 300–1,500 ft|Over 1,500 ft–1 mile|Over 1–5 miles|Over 5–50 miles|
|Long|Over 1,500 ft–1 mile|Over 1–5 miles|Over 5–30 miles|Over 50–500 miles|
|Extreme|Over 1 mile|Over 5 miles|Over 30 miles|Over 500 miles|
These distances are approximate.

The Game Master should use the category that best represents the technology and circumstances of the encounter.

For example, a machine gun mounted on a truck normally uses Ground Vehicle ranges, while a similar weapon mounted on an aircraft may operate at much greater distances.

#### Weapon Range Limitations

Vehicle range bands establish the scale of the encounter.

They do not automatically extend the capabilities of a weapon. A weapon must still be capable of reaching the target.

For example, a vehicle-mounted machine gun may be unable to engage a target at Extreme range even when the vehicle's sensors can detect it.

The Game Master should consider the weapon's listed effective range and the available targeting systems.

#### Close Range

Close Range represents the distance at which vehicles may attempt direct physical interaction.

Possible actions include:

- Ramming.
- Sideswiping.
- Boarding.
- Grappling.
- Docking.
- Crushing or Tackling.

Being within Close Range does not mean the vehicles are already in physical contact.

The vehicles must still maneuver into an appropriate position before a collision, boarding action, or similar interaction can occur.

### Vehicle Close Combat Attacks

Vehicles may attack through direct physical contact.

Examples include:

- A truck ramming another vehicle.
- A tank crushing a smaller car.
- A boat sideswiping another vessel.
- A spacecraft colliding with an enemy ship.
- A large vehicle attempting to force a smaller vehicle off course.

Vehicles have a separate **Close Combat Attack Rating** representing their effectiveness in making such attacks.

#### Resolving Vehicle Collisions

A vehicle collision has two distinct parts:

**1. Determining whether the attack succeeds.**

Use the vehicle's Close Combat Attack Rating and the appropriate attack or defense procedure.

**2. Determining the resulting damage.**

Use the vehicles' Durability, relative size, speed, and other circumstances to determine the effects of the collision.

A successful collision does not automatically inflict the same damage on every target.

A large armored vehicle striking a small civilian vehicle may cause devastating damage to the smaller vehicle while suffering relatively little damage itself.

Conversely, a motorcycle attempting to ram an armored truck places the motorcycle and its rider at considerable risk.

#### Player-Initiated Collision

When a player character attempts to ram an opponent:

1. The player declares the intended collision.
2. The Game Master determines whether the target is within an appropriate position and range.
3. The player makes the relevant vehicle attack roll.
4. Compare the result with the target's Defense Rating.
5. On success, resolve the collision damage.

The Game Master may apply modifiers for speed, Handling, vehicle size, terrain, or other relevant circumstances.

#### Defending Against a Collision

When an NPC vehicle attempts to ram a player-controlled vehicle, the player makes an appropriate Defense Roll.

The player may attempt to:

- Swerve away.
- Brake.
- Accelerate.
- Maneuver around the attacker.
- Use an obstacle or terrain feature to avoid contact.

A typical Defense combination is:

**Dexterity + Vehicle**

The Game Master may approve another combination when appropriate.

On a successful Defense, the vehicle avoids the collision.

On a failed Defense, the collision occurs and damage is resolved.

#### Collision Damage

Collision damage depends primarily on:

- Vehicle Durability.
- Relative size and mass.
- Relative speed.
- Type of impact.
- Armor and structural protection.
- Environmental circumstances.

The Game Master uses the vehicle damage and Durability rules to determine the consequences.

##### Example: Motorcycle Versus Armored Truck

A motorcycle attempts to ram an armored truck.

The rider successfully maneuvers into the truck.

The collision occurs, but the truck is considerably larger and more durable.

The Game Master determines that the motorcycle is likely to suffer much greater damage than the truck.

The outcome is resolved using the established vehicle damage rules.

### Vehicle Defense

A vehicle's **Defense Rating** represents its ability to avoid or withstand attacks.

It reflects the vehicle's:

- Physical construction.
- Armor.
- Structural protection.
- General resistance to damage.
- Defensive characteristics.

Higher Defense Ratings make vehicles more difficult to damage successfully.

#### Vehicle Defense Ratings

|Defense Rating|General Description|Ground Vehicles|Watercraft|Aircraft|Spacecraft|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|1|Fragile or poorly protected|Bicycle, motorcycle, open wagon|Canoe, small sailboat|Ultralight|Escape pod, small satellite|
|2|Light construction with little protection|Passenger car, pickup truck, van|Speedboat, fishing boat|Light civilian aircraft|Civilian shuttle, small transport|
|3|Rugged construction or moderate protection|Heavy truck, armored SUV, military utility vehicle|Patrol boat, large sailing ship|Transport aircraft, military helicopter|Cargo ship, patrol craft|
|4|Heavily constructed or armored|Armored personnel carrier, armored car|Corvette, destroyer|Ground-attack aircraft, armored helicopter|Military transport, frigate|
|5|Heavy military protection|Main battle tank, heavy armored vehicle|Cruiser, battleship|Heavily protected military aircraft|Destroyer, armored warship|
|6|Exceptional protection designed to withstand major attacks|Super-heavy tank, fortified mobile platform|Heavily armored battleship|Specialized futuristic armored aircraft|Battleship, capital ship|
The Game Master assigns a vehicle's Defense Rating based primarily on its construction and protection.

Size alone should not determine Defense.

A large civilian cargo ship may be easier to damage than a much smaller armored warship.

#### Defense, Handling, and Durability

These characteristics serve different purposes.

**Defense Rating:** Determines how difficult it is to land an effective damaging hit.

**Handling:** Represents the vehicle's maneuverability and ability to respond to the operator's controls.

**Durability:** Determines how much damage the vehicle can withstand before suffering serious damage or destruction.

A vehicle may have high Handling but low Durability, or high Durability but poor Handling.

The Game Master should not treat these characteristics as interchangeable.

### Vehicle Crew and Actions

Vehicle combat often involves several characters working together.

Typical roles include:

- Driver or pilot.
- Gunner.
- Commander.
- Navigator.
- Engineer.
- Support crew.

Characters use the normal Action Point system when performing actions during vehicle combat.

The Game Master determines which actions are possible from a particular crew position. A character operating a vehicle may need to divide their attention between maneuvering and operating weapons.

Larger vehicles may have separate crew members responsible for movement, targeting, and other tasks.

Use the Vehicle Skill rules and normal combat Actions when determining the cost and resolution of crew activities.

### Game Master Guidance

Vehicle combat should emphasize the characteristics that make different vehicles distinct.

**Separate Accuracy from Damage**

- A highly accurate weapon is not necessarily more destructive.
- Use Attack Ratings to determine whether attacks succeed and damage rules to determine their consequences.

**Consider Vehicle Scale**

- A collision involving two cars is different from a collision involving warships or spacecraft.
- The Game Master should consider relative size, speed, and Durability.

**Use Appropriate Range Bands**

- Choose range bands based on the vehicles and environment.
- Do not apply Ground Vehicle distances to spacecraft merely because both are equipped with similar weapons.

**Reward Skilled Operators**

- A skilled driver, pilot, or gunner should be able to improve a vehicle's effectiveness.
- Use appropriate Skill Checks, Edges, and situational modifiers.

**Keep Players Involved**

- When player characters operate weapons, they make Attack Rolls.
- When opponents attack player-controlled vehicles, players make Defense Rolls.
- The Game Master uses established opponent ratings rather than rolling dice.

**Preserve the Consequences of Damage**

- Vehicle combat should remain dangerous.
- Damage may impair a vehicle's ability to move, maneuver, attack, or protect its occupants.
- Apply the appropriate vehicle damage and Durability rules.

**Vehicle Combat should remain consistent with the core dice pool system while emphasizing the differences between personal combat and battles involving vehicles.**
