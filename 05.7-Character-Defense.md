## Character Defense <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

When opponents attack player characters, the players make Defense Rolls to determine whether their characters avoid the attack or suffer an injury.

The Game Master does not normally roll dice for opponents. Instead, each opponent has an **Attack Rating** representing the effectiveness of its attack. The player rolls a Defense dice pool against that rating.

A successful Defense avoids the attack. A failed Defense results in a Wound, with the severity determined by how many successes the character falls short.

This system allows players to remain actively involved when their characters are threatened and emphasizes the consequences of dangerous encounters.

### Opponent Attack Ratings

Opponents normally have two types of Attack Ratings:

- **Close Combat Attack Rating:** Used for punches, kicks, melee weapons, grappling, and other attacks at close range.
- **Ranged Attack Rating:** Used for firearms, bows, thrown weapons, and other attacks made at a distance.

An opponent's Attack Rating determines how difficult the attack is to avoid. Higher ratings represent more effective or dangerous attacks.

#### Attack Rating Table

|Attack Rating|Difficulty|Description|
|---|---|---|
|2|Easy|Unskilled or poorly executed attack|
|3|Normal|Competent attack by a trained opponent|
|4|Hard|Dangerous attack by a skilled opponent|
|5|Extreme|Highly effective attack by an expert opponent|
|6+|Exceptional|Extremely dangerous attack or unusual threat|

The Game Master may assign Attack Ratings outside these examples when the circumstances justify them. An opponent may have different ratings for different attacks.

For example, a trained soldier might have a higher Ranged Attack Rating than Close Combat Attack Rating.

Attack Ratings may also be modified by equipment, abilities, circumstances, or other rules.

### Defense Dice Pool

When a character is attacked, the player describes how the character attempts to avoid or resist the attack.

The Game Master determines the appropriate Attributes and Skills.

**Defense Dice Pool = Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

In some circumstances, the Game Master may permit two Attributes instead of an Attribute and Skill.

The selected combination should represent the character's actual defensive response.

#### Common Defense Combinations

|Threat|Typical Defense Dice Pool|
|---|---|
|Opponent attacks with fists, clubs, or swords|Strength or Dexterity + Close Combat|
|Opponent fires a ranged weapon|Dexterity + Athletics|
|Opponent attempts to grapple or choke the character|Strength + Athletics|
|Opponent throws a table, chair, or similar object|Strength or Dexterity + Athletics|
|Opponents attempt to surround or overwhelm the character|Dexterity + Perception|

These combinations are guidelines rather than an exhaustive list.

#### Resolving a Defense Roll

When an opponent attacks, follow these steps.

1. **Declare the Attack:** The Game Master identifies the attacker, target, and type of attack.
2. **Describe the Defense:** The player explains how the character attempts to avoid or resist the attack.
3. **Build the Defense Pool:** Determine the appropriate Attributes, Skills, and modifiers.
4. **Apply Protection:** Add applicable Armor dice and other defensive bonuses.
5. **Roll the Dice:** Each die showing 5 or 6 counts as one success.
6. **Compare Successes:** Compare the number of Defense successes with the opponent's Attack Rating.
7. **Resolve the Result:** A successful Defense avoids the attack. A failed Defense causes a Wound based on the number of missing successes.

#### Successful Defense

If the player rolls successes **equal to or greater than the opponent's Attack Rating**, the character successfully defends. The attack does not inflict a Wound.

The defense may represent:

- Dodging out of the way.
- Blocking or parrying.
- Ducking behind cover.
- Deflecting a blow with armor.
- Breaking an opponent's grip.
- Avoiding a dangerous projectile.

The Game Master describes the result according to the chosen defensive method.

##### Example: Successful Defense

A guard attacks a character with a sword.

The guard has Close Combat Attack Rating 3.

The character rolls Strength + Close Combat and obtains 3 successes.

The Defense succeeds.

The character parries the sword strike and suffers no Wound.

#### Failed Defense and Wound Severity

When the player's Defense successes are fewer than the opponent's Attack Rating, the character suffers a Wound.

The severity of the Wound depends on the number of successes missing.

**Defense Shortfall = Opponent Attack Rating – Defense Successes**

Each Defense success reduces the effective Attack Rating by one.

The remaining value determines the Wound severity.

#### Defense Shortfall Table

|Missing Successes|Wound Severity|
|---|---|
|0 or fewer|No Wound|
|1|Minor|
|2|Serious|
|3|Critical|
|4 or more|Lethal|

A failed Defense normally inflicts **one Wound**, not one separate Wound for each missing success.

The shortfall determines how severe that Wound is.

The Wound is recorded on the character's Injury Tracker.

If the character has no available slot for that Wound severity, apply the normal Wound escalation rules.

##### Example: Serious Wound

An opponent has Attack Rating 4.

The player rolls 2 Defense successes.

The shortfall is:

**4 – 2 = 2**

The character suffers a Serious Wound.

### Armor and Protection

Armor improves a character's ability to survive attacks by adding dice to the Defense Roll.

The Armor bonus depends on the type of protection worn.

|Armor|Defense Bonus|
|---|---|
|No Armor|None|
|Light Armor|+1 die|
|Medium Armor|+2 dice|
|Heavy Armor|+3 dice|

Armor bonuses apply to the Defense dice pool before the roll.

Armor may represent the attack being deflected, absorbed, or otherwise prevented from causing an effective injury.

The Game Master may reduce or ignore an Armor bonus when the attack is specifically capable of bypassing that protection, as described in the Armor and weapon rules.

#### Example: Armor Preventing Injury

A character has a base Defense pool of 4 dice.

The character wears Medium Armor, providing +2 dice.

The final Defense pool is **6 dice**.

The opponent has Attack Rating 3.

The player rolls 3 successes.

The character successfully defends and suffers no Wound.

### Ranged Defense

When an opponent makes a Ranged Attack, the character normally defends using:

**Dexterity + Athletics**

This represents moving, ducking, dodging, or using nearby protection to avoid the attack.

Cover, distance, Armor, and other circumstances may modify the Defense pool.

#### Cover and Distance

The Game Master applies appropriate modifiers based on the circumstances.

Cover may improve a character's ability to avoid injury.

Distance may affect the effectiveness of the incoming attack.

The relevant weapon and combat rules determine the appropriate modifier.

Likewise, avoid counting the same Cover benefit twice.

### Close Combat Defense

When an opponent makes a Close Combat Attack, the character normally defends using:

**Strength or Dexterity + Close Combat**

The player describes the defensive method.

Examples include:

- Blocking with a weapon.
- Parrying a sword strike.
- Evading a punch.
- Deflecting an opponent's club.
- Using body positioning to avoid a grapple.

The Game Master may allow other combinations when appropriate.

### Environmental and Situational Modifiers to Defense

When a character defends against an Opponent's attack, environmental and situational factors such as **range, weather, lighting, elevation, cover, and visibility** may affect the outcome.

**An Opponent's Attack Rating is never modified by these conditions.** Instead, apply any positive or negative modifiers directly to the character's **Defense Dice Pool**.

Conditions that make the Opponent's attack more difficult add Defense dice, while conditions that give the Opponent an advantage remove Defense dice.

Examples include:

- **Range:** Attacks at greater distances may grant additional Defense dice.
- **Weather:** Heavy rain, wind, or fog may increase Defense dice by making attacks less accurate.
- **Lighting:** Darkness or poor visibility may increase Defense dice when the attacker cannot clearly see the target.
- **Elevation:** An Opponent attacking from an advantageous elevated position may reduce the character's Defense dice.
- **Cover:** Walls, trees, barricades, and other obstacles may provide additional Defense dice.
- **Other Conditions:** Movement, concealment, injuries, and tactical advantages may add or remove Defense dice as appropriate.

Use the normal modifier values established elsewhere in the combat rules.

**Example: Defense in Poor Conditions**

A German soldier with a **Ranged Attack Rating of 4** fires at an American paratrooper during a rainy night. The German soldier is positioned on higher ground, giving him an advantage over the paratrooper below.

The paratrooper has a normal **Defense Dice Pool of 6 dice**.

|Modifier|Defense Dice|
|---|---|
|Normal Defense Dice Pool|6|
|Low light|+1|
|Heavy rain|+1|
|Opponent on higher ground|−1|
|**Final Defense Dice Pool**|**7**|

The paratrooper rolls **7 Defense dice** (6 + 1 + 1 − 1) and must obtain at least **4 successes** to avoid being hit.

The German soldier's Attack Rating remains 4 regardless of the environmental conditions.

**Important:** All modifiers are applied to the player's Defense Dice Pool, never to the Opponent's Attack Rating. Apply each modifier only once, and avoid counting overlapping environmental effects twice.

### Grappling and Restraining Attacks

When an opponent attempts to grapple, choke, or restrain a character, the player may use an appropriate physical defense.

A typical combination is:

**Strength + Athletics**

A successful Defense prevents the opponent from establishing the hold.

On a failed Defense, apply the consequences of the attack according to the Grappling and Condition rules.

A grappling attack that is specifically declared as non-damaging does not automatically inflict a Wound merely because the Defense failed.

Instead, the failed Defense establishes the Grappled Condition.

### Other Modifiers

A Defense dice pool may also be modified by:

- Edges and Hindrances.
- Environmental conditions.
- Technology.
- Mysticism.
- Special equipment.
- Other situational effects.

Modifiers are cumulative when they represent separate circumstances.

Do not apply the same protection or penalty more than once.

### Defensive Reactions

A character may spend **1 AP** on the Defend Reaction when targeted by an Attack.

Defend grants **+1 die** to the Defense Roll against that Attack.

The Reaction must be declared before the Defense Roll is resolved.

Other applicable defensive abilities may provide additional benefits.

### Push Your Luck on Defense

Players may Push Your Luck when making Defense Rolls.

### Critical Failure

A Critical Failure occurs when the final roll contains:

- At least three dice showing 1.
- More 1s than successes.

The Game Master determines an appropriate complication.

Possible complications include:

- Losing balance.
- Dropping a weapon.
- Falling into a disadvantageous position.
- Becoming exposed to another threat.
- Damaging equipment.
- Becoming entangled or restrained.

The complication should reflect the circumstances.

A Critical Failure does not automatically increase Wound severity unless another rule or the specific situation justifies that consequence.

### Example: Suki and the Gangster

Sarah's character, Suki, is attacked by a gangster armed with a shotgun at Short range.

Suki is not an experienced fighter and has limited options for escaping the attack.

The gangster has:

**Ranged Attack Rating: 4**

Suki's Defense dice pool is:

|Source|Dice|
|---|---|
|Dexterity 2|2|
|Athletics 2|2|
|Leather Jacket (Light Armor)|+1|
|Range Modifier|0|
|Other Modifiers|0|
|**Total Defense Pool**|**5**|

Sarah rolls 5 dice and chooses to Push Your Luck.

After the reroll, Suki has **2 successes**.

The gangster's Attack Rating is 4.

The Defense shortfall is:

**4 – 2 = 2**

According to the Defense Shortfall Table, Suki suffers a **Serious Wound**.

The Game Master describes the shotgun blast striking Suki and records the injury on her Injury Tracker.

Any additional effects are determined using the Damage, Injury, and Condition rules.

### Defense Against Multiple Attacks

A character may be targeted by more than one Attack during a Round.

Each Attack is resolved separately.

The player makes a Defense Roll against each Attack using the appropriate dice pool and modifiers.

There is no automatic cumulative penalty for making multiple Defense Rolls unless a specific rule imposes one.

However, defensive Reactions such as Defend are limited by available Action Points and the Reaction rules.

#### Example: Multiple Opponents

Two opponents attack a character during the same Round.

The first opponent has Attack Rating 3.

The second opponent has Attack Rating 4.

The player makes a separate Defense Roll against each attack.

If these are Close Combat attacks and the opponent's outnumber the characters, they add +1 to each of their attacks.

The character may use the Defend Reaction against these attacks if the Reaction is available and sufficient Action Points remain.

### Game Master Guidance

Character Defense is one of the central mechanics of the combat system.

The following principles help maintain consistency.

**Use Appropriate Attack Ratings**

- Attack Ratings should reflect the opponent's effectiveness and the circumstances of the attack.
- Avoid assigning extremely high ratings without considering their potential to cause severe injuries.

**Keep Defense Player-Facing**

- Players should make the rolls that determine whether their characters avoid incoming attacks.
- The Game Master establishes the opponent's Attack Rating and describes the consequences.

**Describe Defense Narratively**

- A successful Defense may represent a dodge, parry, block, near miss, or attack absorbed by protection.
- A failed Defense should describe the resulting injury in a way that reflects the weapon and circumstances.

**Apply Wound Severity Consistently**

- Use the difference between Attack Rating and Defense successes to determine the Wound severity.
- Do not inflict multiple Wounds from one failed Defense unless a specific attack explicitly requires it.

**Encourage Defensive Choices**

- Characters may improve their chances through Armor, Cover, positioning, defensive Reactions, Edges, and other abilities.
- Players should understand that surviving combat often depends on preparation and tactical decisions.

**Maintain the Danger of Combat**

- Even capable characters can suffer serious injuries when facing effective opponents.
- The Wound system should make violence consequential without requiring complicated damage calculations.

**Character Defense places the outcome of incoming attacks in the players' hands while keeping combat fast, dangerous, and consistent with the game's core dice pool system.**
