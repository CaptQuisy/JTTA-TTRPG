## Weapons

Weapons are among the most common tools characters use to defend themselves, overcome enemies, and survive dangerous encounters.

From primitive clubs and swords to revolvers, automatic firearms, railguns, and energy weapons, the weapons available to characters depend upon the historical period and technology of the campaign.

Rather than providing statistics for every weapon ever created, _Journey Through the Ages_ uses a flexible system of **Weapon Profiles**. Each profile represents a broad category of weapons with similar combat characteristics.

For example, a medieval longsword and a futuristic plasma blade may use the same basic Weapon Profile, even though their appearance, construction, and technology differ considerably.

This approach allows Game Masters to create equipment appropriate to any era without introducing unnecessary complexity.

### Weapon Profiles

Every weapon is described by several characteristics.

#### Weapon Role

The Weapon Role identifies the general category of the weapon and how it functions during combat.

Examples include Light Close Combat, Heavy Close Combat, Sidearm, Long Arm, and Support Weapon.

Weapons within the same role generally share similar combat characteristics.

#### Weapon Dice

**A weapon's Damage Dice represent the additional dice a weapon contributes to the Damage Roll after a successful Attack.**

Damage Dice are **not added to the Attack Roll**.

A weapon with 1 Damage Die adds 1 die to the Damage Roll.

A weapon with 3 Damage Dice adds 3 dice to the Damage Roll.

Weapons with more Damage Dice generally have greater potential to inflict serious injuries.

The number of Damage Dice reflects the weapon's intended role, striking power, and destructive capability.

#### Range Effectiveness

Every weapon performs differently depending on the distance to its target.

Range modifiers adjust the **Attack Roll**, not the Damage Roll.

#### Special Rules

Certain weapons possess additional characteristics, including ammunition requirements, automatic fire, armor penetration, area effects, and special Conditions.

These rules are described later in this section.

##### Weapon Notes

**1. Light and Long Close Combat Weapons:** Certain weapons, such as knives, hatchets, and spears, may be thrown. Their listed range modifiers apply when used in this manner. The Game Master determines whether a particular weapon is suitable for throwing.

**2. Sidearms:** Pistols represent compact, usually one-handed firearms. Smaller calibers generally use fewer Weapon Dice, while larger calibers use more. For example, a small-caliber pistol may have 1 Weapon Die, while a powerful revolver may have 3.

**3. Long Arms:** Rifles represent muskets, hunting rifles, military rifles, longbows, gauss rifles, and similar weapons. Their Weapon Dice vary according to caliber, power, and intended role.

**4. Automatic Weapons:** Assault rifles, submachine guns, and similar weapons may fire individual shots or automatic bursts. See Full Automatic Fire.

**5. Support Weapons:** Machine guns and other support weapons are particularly effective at controlling areas and suppressing enemy movement. See Suppressive Fire.

**6. Ranged Explosives:** Grenades, rockets, and similar weapons affect an area rather than a single target. See Area Effects.

### Weapon Special Rules

Some weapons possess special characteristics that modify how they are used.

Unless specifically stated otherwise, these rules modify the Attack Roll, Damage Roll, or effects of an attack as described.

#### Ammunition & Reloading

To keep combat fast, weapons capable of holding multiple rounds or projectiles do not normally require players to track every shot.

Instead, ammunition becomes important during dramatic moments.

A weapon must be reloaded when:

- The Attack Roll contains more 1s than successes.
- The character uses Panic Fire.
- The character uses Full Automatic Fire.
- The character uses Suppressive Fire.
- The weapon's description specifically requires reloading.

The ammunition requirement is determined from the final Attack Roll, including any Push Your Luck reroll.

#### Reload Action

Reloading requires **1 Action Point**, unless modified by an Edge, special equipment, or another ability.

Single-shot weapons, such as muskets, bows, crossbows, and similar weapons, must be reloaded after each attack.

The Game Master may require detailed ammunition tracking when scarcity is an important feature of the campaign.

#### Armor Piercing

Some weapons or ammunition are designed to overcome armor protection.

Armor Piercing reduces the Armor bonus included in the target's Defense dice pool.

The Game Master may assign an Armor Piercing rating from 1 to 3.

| Armor Piercing | Effect                               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 1              | Ignore 1 die of Armor Defense bonus  |
| 2              | Ignore 2 dice of Armor Defense bonus |
| 3              | Ignore 3 dice of Armor Defense bonus |
Armor Piercing cannot reduce the Armor Defense bonus below zero.

For example, a character wearing Heavy Armor receives +3 Defense dice.

An attack with Armor Piercing 2 reduces that bonus to +1 Defense die.

Armor Piercing does not directly increase weapon's Damage Dice or Damage successes.

The Game Master may restrict Armor Piercing ammunition according to the campaign's technology and availability.

### Special Weapon Effects

Some weapons inflict Conditions or other effects in addition to ordinary Wounds.

These effects may result from poison, fire, electricity, concussive force, or specialized ammunition.

| Condition | Example Weapon                    | Effect                                                |
| --------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Burning   | Flamethrower, incendiary grenade  | Target catches fire and suffers the Burning Condition |
| Bleeding  | Combat knife, sword               | Target continues losing blood until treated           |
| Poisoned  | Toxic ammunition, venomous weapon | Target may become Poisoned                            |
| Stunned   | Taser, stun baton                 | Target may become Stunned                             |
| Prone     | Heavy impact weapon               | Target may be knocked Prone                           |
| Deafened  | Flashbang grenade                 | Target temporarily loses hearing                      |
| Blinded   | Flashbang, blinding device        | Target temporarily loses sight                        |
The weapon description determines whether the effect occurs automatically after a successful hit or requires a separate Saving Throw.

When a Saving Throw is required, the weapon should specify:

- The Attribute and Skill combination.
- The Difficulty.
- The effect of success or failure.
- The duration of the Condition.

Unless specifically stated otherwise, a Special Weapon Effect does not change the weapon's listed Weapon Dice.

See Chapter 6, _Conditions_, for complete Condition rules.

### Improvised Weapons

Nearly any suitable object can become a weapon in desperate circumstances.

Examples include:

- Chairs.
- Pipes.
- Wrenches.
- Bottles.
- Crowbars.
- Shovels.
- Rocks.
- Tools.

The Game Master should compare the object with the closest Weapon Profile.

#### Improvised Weapon Examples

|Object|Suggested Weapon Profile|
|---|---|
|Bottle|Light Close Combat|
|Crowbar|Light Close Combat|
|Fire Axe|Heavy Close Combat|
|Kitchen Knife|Light Close Combat|
|Pool Cue|Long Close Combat|
An improvised weapon uses the Damage Dice of its assigned profile.

If the object is awkward, poorly balanced, or unsuitable for combat, the Game Master may apply a **–1 die Attack penalty**.

This penalty affects the Attack Roll, not the Damage Roll.

Improvised weapons may break after repeated use when dramatically appropriate.

### Oversized Weapons 

A character attempting to wield a weapon too large, heavy, or awkward for effective use suffers **–2 dice**.

Examples include:

- A small character attempting to use a massive sword.
- A character attempting to fire a heavy weapon without proper support.
- A combatant using equipment designed for a much larger creature.
- A character struggling to control a weapon whose weight exceeds their practical capabilities.

The Game Master determines whether the weapon is too large for the character to handle based on Strength, physical size, weapon design, and circumstances.

Some weapons may be impossible to use without specialized equipment or assistance.

### Weapon Quality

Weapons vary in craftsmanship, condition, and reliability.

The Game Master may assign one of the following quality levels.

|Quality|Effect|
|---|---|
|Poor|–1 die on Attack Rolls involving the weapon|
|Standard|No modifier|
|Superior|+1 die on Attack Rolls involving the weapon|
|Masterwork|+1 die on Attack Rolls and ignore the first weapon malfunction each session|
Weapon quality modifies the Attack Roll.

It does not change weapon's Damage Dice unless the weapon's description specifically provides a separate damage modification.

For example, a Superior sword and a Standard sword may both have 2 Damage Dice.

The Superior sword grants +1 Attack die, increasing the chance of hitting and potentially producing extra Attack successes.

The sword's Damage Dice remain unchanged.

Weapon quality should be used sparingly to distinguish particularly valuable, well-made, or legendary equipment.

### Weapon Customization

Players are encouraged to describe their weapons in ways appropriate to the campaign.

Two weapons with identical game statistics may have completely different appearances and technologies.

Examples include:

- A medieval longsword and a futuristic plasma blade.
- A revolver and a laser pistol.
- A hunting rifle and a gauss rifle.
- A spear and an electrified shock lance.

Changing a weapon's appearance does not automatically change its statistics.

A Game Master may approve mechanical differences when justified by the weapon's construction or technology.

Possible modifications include:

- Different Damage Dice.
- Improved or reduced Range effectiveness.
- Armor Piercing.
- Additional Conditions.
- Different ammunition requirements.
- Special firing modes.
- Unique equipment restrictions.

Such modifications should be balanced against existing Weapon Profiles.

### Damaged Weapons

A weapon that has been bent, cracked, misaligned, poorly maintained, or otherwise damaged suffers **–1 die** to Attack Rolls.

The penalty remains until the weapon is repaired or replaced.

A weapon that is severely damaged may become unusable.

The Game Master determines whether a repair requires a Skill Check, specialized tools, or replacement parts.

### Game Master Guidance

Weapon Profiles are intentionally broad.

Rather than assigning unique statistics to hundreds of similar weapons, use the existing categories as a starting point.

When designing a weapon, consider three separate questions.

**How Accurate Is the Weapon?**

- Accuracy is represented through the Attack Roll.
- Range modifiers, weapon quality, handling characteristics, and other circumstances may increase or decrease the Attack dice pool.

**How Much Damage Can the Weapon Inflict?**

- Damage potential is represented by the weapon's Damage Dice.
- Damage Dice are added to the Damage Roll after a successful Attack.
- A weapon with more Damage Dice has greater potential to inflict serious injuries, but does not automatically become easier to hit with.

**Does the Weapon Have Special Effects?**

- Some weapons possess characteristics beyond ordinary damage.
- Examples include armor penetration, poison, fire, electricity, explosive effects, or automatic fire.
- These effects should be described separately from Attack modifiers and Damage Dice.
