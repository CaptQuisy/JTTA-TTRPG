## Equipment Listings <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

The following tables provide equipment commonly available to characters across the historical, modern, and futuristic settings supported by _Journey Through the Ages_.

These listings are intended as a flexible reference rather than an exhaustive catalog. They include representative weapons, armor, tools, personal supplies, professional equipment, and vehicles.

Each item is described using three classifications:

- **Cost:** The item's relative expense.
- **Availability:** How easily the item can normally be acquired.
- **Era:** The broad technological period in which the item is commonly available.

These classifications provide a starting point for equipment selection and acquisition.

The Game Master may adjust individual entries to reflect the campaign's historical period, location, economy, laws, or technological development.

An item may be inexpensive but difficult to obtain, or expensive but readily available to characters with sufficient resources.

Equipment requiring specialized training, ammunition, fuel, power, or supporting infrastructure remains subject to those requirements.

#### Weapon Selection

When selecting a weapon, the Game Master should assign the appropriate Weapon Profile.

For example:

- A dagger normally uses the Light Close Combat profile.
- A longsword normally uses the Heavy Close Combat profile.
- A spear normally uses the Long Close Combat profile.
- A revolver normally uses the Sidearm profile.
- A bolt-action rifle normally uses the Long Arm profile.
- An assault rifle normally uses the Automatic Weapon profile.

The weapon's exact statistics may vary depending on its design, quality, and technological characteristics.

A weapon's Cost Category does not determine its Weapon Dice.

Likewise, an advanced weapon does not automatically inflict greater damage than a comparable weapon from an earlier era.

The Game Master should use the weapon's intended function and capabilities when assigning statistics.

#### Historical and Legal Considerations

The listed Era identifies a broad default classification rather than a precise invention date.

For example, firearms of different designs developed over several centuries. The Game Master should use the campaign's actual historical period when determining which weapons exist.

Weapon Availability may also vary by jurisdiction.

A weapon classified as Common in the table may be Restricted or Illegal in a particular location.

#### Armor Selection

Each suit of armor should be assigned a Light, Medium, or Heavy Armor Profile. Shields and helmets use their own equipment rules.

The Game Master determines which attacks and hazards the armor can reasonably protect against.

For example, a ballistic vest and a leather jerkin may provide similar general protection under the abstract Armor Profile system, but they are not necessarily effective against the same threats.

Advanced equipment may possess additional capabilities such as environmental sealing, powered movement, integrated sensors, or specialized defensive systems. These capabilities must be defined separately when they affect game mechanics.

### Adding New Equipment

The Game Master may introduce additional equipment whenever the campaign requires it.

Most new items can be created without adding new rules.

**Step 1: Identify the Item's Purpose**

- Determine what the item does and how characters are likely to use it.

**Step 2: Assign a Cost Category**

- Compare the item with similar equipment already listed.
- Consider the item's materials, complexity, rarity, and expected expense.

**Step 3: Determine Availability**

- Decide whether the item is Plentiful, Common, Specialized, Restricted, Illegal, or Unique.
- Consider the campaign's location, laws, and technological development.

**Step 4: Assign an Era**

- Select the broad technological period in which the item belongs.
- If the item exists across multiple periods, use the earliest broadly appropriate classification or Any when suitable.

**Step 5: Establish Mechanical Effects**

- If the item is a weapon, armor, or vehicle, use the corresponding equipment rules.

For other items, determine whether the equipment:

- Enables an action.
- Removes a limitation.
- Supports a Skill Check.
- Provides specialized information.
- Protects against a particular hazard.

Only assign numerical bonuses when the item has a clearly defined mechanical benefit.

**Step 6: Define Limitations**

Consider whether the item requires ammunition, power, fuel, supplies, maintenance, specialized training, or supporting infrastructure. These limitations should reflect the equipment's function and the campaign's intended level of realism.
