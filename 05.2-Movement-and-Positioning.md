## Movement and Positioning <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Movement allows characters to maneuver around the battlefield, reach objectives, avoid danger, and engage or disengage from opponents. During combat, movement is measured in feet and requires Action Points (AP).

A character's position can affect their ability to attack, defend, use cover, and interact with their surroundings.

### Movement Basics

A character may spend **1 AP to Move up to 30 feet** during their Turn. Movement may be combined with other Actions, provided the character has sufficient Action Points remaining.

A character may perform multiple Move Actions during the same Turn.

|Action|AP Cost|Distance|
|---|---|---|
|Move|1|Up to 30 feet|
|Double Move|2|Up to 60 feet|
|Triple Move|3|Up to 90 feet|

Characters do not need to move the full distance allowed. Any unused movement is lost when the Move Action ends.

**Example: Moving and Attacking**

A soldier begins his Turn behind a stone wall. He spends 1 AP to Move 20 feet toward an enemy position, 1 AP to fire his rifle, and 1 AP to Move another 30 feet into cover.

The soldier has spent all 3 AP for the Turn.

### Movement Rates

A character's standard movement rate assumes they are moving on foot across reasonably clear terrain.

Certain circumstances may reduce movement speed.

|Movement Type|Distance per AP|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|Normal Movement|30 feet|Standard movement|
|Cautious Movement|15 feet|Moving carefully through hazardous or uncertain terrain|
|Difficult Terrain|15 feet|Mud, rubble, dense vegetation, or uneven ground|
|Crawling|10 feet|Moving while Prone|
|Swimming|15 feet|Swimming under ordinary conditions|
|Climbing|15 feet|Climbing a ladder, rope, or suitable surface|

The Game Master may adjust these distances based on environmental conditions, equipment, injuries, or other circumstances.

Characters wearing armor or affected by Conditions that reduce movement must apply those penalties to their movement distance.

### Difficult Terrain

Difficult Terrain includes surfaces or obstacles that make movement slower or more demanding.

Examples include:

- Deep mud, snow, or loose sand.
- Dense vegetation or thick undergrowth.
- Rubble, debris, or unstable ground.
- Shallow water or slippery surfaces.
- Crowded spaces or areas filled with obstacles.

Movement through Difficult Terrain is reduced to **15 feet per AP**.

If only part of a character's movement crosses Difficult Terrain, each foot traveled through that terrain counts as 2 feet of movement.

**Example: Crossing Difficult Terrain**

A character moves toward a ruined building. The first 10 feet cross open ground, followed by 10 feet of rubble.

The open ground costs 10 feet of movement, while the rubble costs 20 feet. The character uses their full 30 feet of movement to travel 20 feet across the battlefield.

The Game Master may require a Dexterity + Athletics Skill Check when the terrain presents a significant risk of falling, becoming trapped, or suffering injury.

### Obstacles and Climbing

Characters may encounter obstacles such as fences, walls, trenches, windows, and other barriers.

Minor obstacles that can be crossed without significant effort may be included as part of normal movement.

More challenging obstacles may require an Athletics Skill Check.

|Obstacle|Suggested Difficulty|
|---|---|
|Low fence or small barrier|Easy (1)|
|High fence or difficult jump|Normal (2)|
|Climbing a rough wall|Hard (3)|
|Climbing a smooth or unstable surface|Extreme (4)|
|Exceptional physical obstacle|Legendary (5)|

**Success:** The character crosses the obstacle and continues moving, subject to the remaining movement distance.

**Failure:** The character cannot complete the movement and may need to attempt the obstacle again.

**Critical Failure:** The character may fall, become Prone, lose equipment, or suffer another appropriate complication.

The Game Master determines whether an obstacle requires additional Action Points based on its size and complexity.

### Movement While Prone

A character who is Prone may crawl up to **10 feet per AP**.

Standing from Prone requires a **Recover Action (1 AP)** and automatically succeeds if the character is physically capable of standing.

A character who remains Prone is subject to the normal combat modifiers associated with that Condition.

### Entering Close Combat

A character enters Close Combat when they move within an opponent's Close Combat range.

Entering Close Combat does not normally require an additional Action Point beyond the Move Action.

Once engaged, characters may attack using appropriate Close Combat weapons or other permitted combat Actions.

Characters may move around an opponent while remaining within Close Combat range, provided their movement does not take them outside the opponent's reach.

### Moving Out of Close Combat

Leaving an opponent's Close Combat range may expose a character to an immediate attack.

When a character moves out of an opponent's Close Combat range, they must make a **Defense Roll against the opponent's Close Combat Attack Rating −1**.

This Defense Roll represents the character's attempt to withdraw safely while avoiding the opponent's attack.

- **Successful Defense:** The character avoids the attack and continues moving.
- **Failed Defense:** The character suffers a Wound based on the Defense Shortfall. If still capable of moving, they may continue their movement.
- **Critical Failure:** The Game Master may introduce an additional complication, such as the character falling Prone or losing their footing.

The Defense Roll does not require the opponent to spend an Action Point.

**Outnumbering Exception:** A character does not need to make this Defense Roll if, at the start of their movement, the character and their allies involved in that particular Close Combat outnumber their opponents.

When withdrawing from multiple opponents, the Game Master determines which opponents are positioned to threaten the character's movement. Each threatening opponent may require a separate Defense Roll.

#### Example: Breaking Away

A character is fighting an enemy swordsman and wants to retreat toward a nearby doorway.

The character spends 1 AP to Move out of Close Combat range.

The swordsman threatens the retreat, requiring the character to make a Defense Roll against the swordsman's Close Combat Attack Rating −1.

The character successfully defends and continues moving toward the doorway.

If the character had been fighting alongside two allies against a single swordsman, no Defense Roll would have been required because their side outnumbered the opponent.

### Moving Around Allies and Opponents

Characters may normally move through spaces occupied by friendly characters, provided there is sufficient room. Moving through an opponent's occupied space is not normally permitted unless the character successfully maneuvers past them.

The Game Master may require a Dexterity + Athletics or Dexterity + Finesse Skill Check to move through an opponent's position.

The Difficulty depends on the opponent's size, positioning, and ability to block the character's movement.

### Movement and Cover

Characters may use movement to reach or leave protective positions. Moving behind a wall, vehicle, doorway, or other obstacle may provide Cover against incoming attacks. Cover benefits apply when the character is appropriately positioned behind the obstacle.

A character who leaves Cover loses its protection whenever the obstacle no longer shields them from an attack.

The Game Master determines whether Cover applies based on the positions of the attacker, defender, and intervening obstacles.

### Movement Outside Combat

Outside combat, movement generally does not require Action Points. Characters may walk, run, climb, swim, or travel at a pace appropriate to the circumstances.

The Game Master calls for Skill Checks only when movement involves meaningful difficulty, danger, or uncertainty.

Extended pursuits involving characters, animals, or vehicles use the rules in the **Chases** chapter rather than normal combat movement.

## Optional Rule: Range Band Movement

For faster, narrative-focused combat, the Game Master may use **Range Band Movement** instead of tracking movement in feet.

Characters use the existing weapon Range Bands to determine their relative positions. Moving from one Range Band to an adjacent band requires **0, 1 , 2, or 3 Action Point (AP)**.

| Range Band | Distance (Feet) | Movement Cost          |
| ---------- | --------------- | ---------------------- |
| Close      | 0–5 ft          | 0 AP to enter or leave |
| Short      | 6–30 ft         | 1 AP to enter or leave |
| Medium     | 31–100 ft       | 1 AP to enter or leave |
| Long       | 101–300 ft      | 2 AP to enter or leave |
| Extreme    | 301–1,000 ft    | 3 AP to enter or leave |

**Movement Rules**

- **Difficult Terrain:** Moving through difficult terrain costs an additional 1 AP per Range Band.
- **Obstacles:** Significant obstacles may require an appropriate Skill Check before movement can continue.
- **Close Combat:** Entering Close Combat requires moving into the Close Range Band. Leaving an engaged opponent's reach follows the normal Moving Out of Close Combat rules.
- **Positioning:** Characters within the same Range Band may interact, seek cover, or maneuver as appropriate to the situation.

**Relative Distances**

Range Bands represent the distance between characters or groups rather than fixed positions on a battlefield. A character's Range Band may differ depending on which opponent or location is being considered.

The Game Master determines starting Range Bands based on the environment and circumstances.

### Example: Advancing Under Fire

A soldier begins combat at Long Range from an enemy position.

During his Turn, he spends:

- **2 AP** to Move from Long to Medium Range.
- **1 AP** to Move from Medium to Short Range.
- **0 AP** to Move from Short to Close Range.

The soldier finishes his Turn at Close Range, having spent all 3 AP.

### Using Range Band Movement

Range Band Movement is particularly useful for theater-of-the-mind combat, large battlefields, and encounters where exact distances are less important than positioning.

The Game Master may switch between measured movement and Range Band Movement depending on the needs of the adventure.
