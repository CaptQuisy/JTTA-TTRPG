## Push Your Luck

Heroes are remembered for the moments when they risk everything to achieve the impossible.

When facing a difficult challenge, a character may choose to **Push Your Luck**, attempting to improve a dice roll at the risk of making the situation worse.

A character may Push Your Luck after making a:

- Skill Check
- Attack Roll
- Defense Roll
- Saving Throw

A character may Push Your Luck **once per roll**.

The player makes this decision after seeing the initial dice results but before the Game Master applies the outcome and its consequences.

Pushing represents determination, desperation, instinct, or extraordinary effort. It may turn failure into success, but it can also turn a promising result into a costly mistake.

### Pushing Your Luck

After rolling the dice, the player may choose to Push Your Luck if at least one die shows a 2, 3, or 4.

Follow these steps:

1. **Keep Successes:** Set aside every die showing a 5 or 6. These successes are retained.
2. **Keep 1s:** Set aside every die showing a 1. These dice cannot be rerolled.
3. **Reroll 2s, 3s, and 4s:** Reroll every die showing a 2, 3, or 4 once.
4. **Count New Successes:** Each new 5 or 6 adds one success to the original total.
5. **Check for Overextension:** If the reroll produces no new successes, remove one original success, if available.
6. **Check for Critical Failure:** Evaluate the final dice results using the Critical Failure rules.
7. **Resolve the Action:** Compare the final successes with the required Difficulty or opposing rating, then apply any appropriate consequences.

All eligible dice must be rerolled. The player cannot select only some of the dice showing 2, 3, or 4. The rerolled dice become part of the original roll.

A character cannot Push Your Luck a second time on the same roll.

### Dice Results

|Original Die|When Pushing|
|---|---|
|1|Keep; cannot reroll|
|2|Reroll once|
|3|Reroll once|
|4|Reroll once|
|5|Keep; counts as 1 success|
|6|Keep; counts as 1 success|
A die rerolled through Push Your Luck cannot be rerolled again through another Push Your Luck attempt.

Other abilities that permit rerolls follow their specific rules.

### When to Push Your Luck

Pushing Your Luck is always optional.

Characters commonly Push Their Luck when:

- One additional success could change the outcome.
- Failure would place an ally in danger.
- There may be no second opportunity.
- Time is running out.
- The potential reward outweighs the risk.
- Additional successes may provide a meaningful benefit.

A character may Push Your Luck even when the initial roll already succeeds.

However, doing so is dangerous. If the reroll produces no additional successes, Overextension may reduce the final result below the required Difficulty.

### When Pushing Is Not Possible

A character cannot Push Your Luck if no dice show 2, 3, or 4.

A character also cannot Push Your Luck after the Game Master has already applied the consequences of the roll.

#### Example: A Desperate Leap

Captain Mara attempts to leap between two moving trains.

The Game Master calls for a **Dexterity + Athletics** Skill Check requiring 3 successes (Hard Difficulty).

Mara rolls seven dice.

**Initial Roll:** 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 2, 1

She has 2 successes, one short of the required total.

Mara chooses to Push Her Luck.

She keeps:

**6, 5, 1**

She rerolls:

**4, 3, 2, 2**

The reroll produces:

**2, 3, 4, 6**

Mara gains one additional success.

Her final result is:

- Original successes: 2
- New successes: 1
- Total successes: 3
- Required successes: 3

**Result: Success.**

Mara successfully leaps between the trains.

Because the reroll produced a new success, Overextension does not occur.

### Overextension

Pushing beyond one's limits can cause a character to lose focus, overcommit, or make a costly mistake.

If a Push Your Luck reroll produces **no additional successes**, the character has Overextended.

When Overextension occurs:

1. Remove one success from the original roll, if any.
2. Count the final number of successes.
3. Check for Critical Failure.
4. Resolve the action using the reduced success total.

If the original roll contained no successes, there is no success to remove.

Overextension does not automatically cause a Critical Failure. The final dice must still satisfy the Critical Failure conditions.

#### Example: Overextending

A character attempts a Normal Skill Check requiring 2 successes.

The player rolls:

**5, 5, 3, 2, 1**

The initial roll has 2 successes and would succeed.

The player chooses to Push Your Luck in the hope of gaining an additional benefit.

The dice showing 3 and 2 are rerolled.

**Reroll:** 2, 4

No new successes are gained.

The character Overextends and loses one original success.

The final result is:

- Original successes: 2
- Overextension penalty: –1 success
- Final successes: 1
- Required successes: 2

**Result: Failure.**

The character's attempt to improve an already successful roll has turned success into failure.

### Critical Failures

A **Critical Failure** occurs when both of the following conditions are met:

1. The final dice contain **at least three 1s**.
2. The number of 1s is **greater than the final number of successes**.

Both conditions must be satisfied.

A Critical Failure can occur on an ordinary roll or after Push Your Luck.

### Determining Critical Failure

For an ordinary roll, count the 1s and successes after the dice are rolled.

For a pushed roll, count the 1s and successes after completing the reroll and applying any Overextension penalty.

Only the final dice are considered.

A die showing 1 on the original roll remains a 1.

A rerolled die that produces a 1 also counts toward the final total.

#### Critical Failure Examples

| Final 1s | Final Successes | Critical Failure?               |
| -------- | --------------- | ------------------------------- |
| 2        | 0               | No — fewer than three 1s        |
| 3        | 0               | Yes                             |
| 3        | 2               | Yes                             |
| 3        | 3               | No — 1s do not exceed successes |
| 4        | 2               | Yes                             |
| 4        | 4               | No — 1s do not exceed successes |
### Consequences of Critical Failure

A Critical Failure represents an additional complication beyond the ordinary outcome of the action.

The Game Master determines a consequence appropriate to the circumstances.

Possible complications include:

- Dropping or damaging equipment.
- Running out of ammunition.
- Triggering an alarm.
- Falling prone.
- Damaging important machinery.
- Alerting nearby enemies.
- Losing valuable time.
- Becoming exposed to danger.
- Creating a new obstacle.

The Game Master should choose consequences that follow naturally from the attempted action.

A Critical Failure does not automatically require the most severe imaginable consequence.

### Success and Critical Failure

The number of successes still determines whether the action meets its required Difficulty.

If a roll satisfies the Critical Failure conditions but also meets the required Difficulty, the character accomplishes the action while suffering an additional complication.

For example, a character might successfully force open a door but damage the locking mechanism, preventing it from being closed again.

This preserves the original principle that a Critical Failure introduces a complication rather than automatically replacing the normal result.

#### Example: Overextension and Critical Failure

Later, Captain Mara attempts to force open a damaged airlock.

The Game Master assigns a Hard Difficulty requiring 3 successes.

Mara rolls seven dice.

**Initial Roll:** 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6

She has:

- 2 successes
- 2 dice showing 1
- 3 dice eligible for reroll

Mara chooses to Push Her Luck.

She rerolls the dice showing 2, 3, and 4.

**Reroll:** 1, 2, 4

The reroll produces no additional successes.

Mara has Overextended.

She loses one original success.

Her final result contains:

- 1 success
- 3 dice showing 1
- Required successes: 3

The attempt fails because Mara has only one success.

It is also a Critical Failure because:

- There are at least three 1s.
- The number of 1s exceeds the number of successes.

**Result: Critical Failure.**

The airlock remains sealed, and Mara's excessive force damages the emergency release mechanism. The group must now find another way inside.

### Critical Success (Optional)

The Game Master may use the optional Critical Success rule to reward extraordinary rolls.

A **Critical Success** occurs when:

1. The character succeeds at a Skill Check or Attack Roll.
2. There are at least three 6s.
3. More than half of the dice in the roll show **6**.

A Critical Success may occur on an ordinary roll or after Push Your Luck.

When evaluating a pushed roll, use the final dice results.

The total number of dice in the roll does not change when some dice are rerolled.

#### Critical Success Requirements

| Dice Rolled | Sixes Required |
| ----------- | -------------- |
| 3           | 3              |
| 4           | 3              |
| 5           | 3              |
| 6           | 4              |
| 7           | 4              |
| 8           | 5              |
| 9           | 5              |
#### Describing a Critical Success

When a character achieves a Critical Success, the player may describe an especially impressive or favorable outcome.

The description should remain consistent with the action attempted.

Examples include:

- Completing a repair with exceptional precision.
- Making a difficult shot in a particularly impressive manner.
- Finding an important additional clue.
- Completing a task unusually quickly.
- Creating an unexpected tactical advantage.

The Game Master may modify the description to preserve the established situation, game balance, and narrative consistency.

A Critical Success does not automatically grant additional damage, extra Actions, or other numerical benefits unless a specific rule provides them.

##### Example: An Exceptional Shot

A character fires at an enemy using a six-dice Attack Roll.

The result is:

**6, 6, 6, 6, 3, 1**

The character rolls four 6s, more than half the dice.

If the Attack Roll succeeds, the character achieves a Critical Success.

The player might describe the shot as striking the enemy's weapon or forcing the enemy into an unfavorable position.

The Game Master determines whether the proposed effect is appropriate and whether it requires any additional mechanical resolution.

### Game Master Guidance

Push Your Luck exists to create dramatic choices, not to punish players for taking risks.

The player should understand the potential consequences before deciding whether to push.

A failed attempt should produce consequences appropriate to the action and circumstances.

**Ordinary Failure:** The character does not achieve the required result.

**Overextension:** The character gains no new successes from pushing and loses one original success, if available.

**Critical Failure:** The final dice meet the specific Critical Failure conditions, introducing an additional complication.

These results may overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A character may Overextend without suffering a Critical Failure. Likewise, a Critical Failure may occur without Push Your Luck.

The purpose of the mechanic is to let players decide when an additional chance is worth the danger.

**Push Your Luck should make important rolls more exciting by giving players meaningful control over the risks their characters take.**
