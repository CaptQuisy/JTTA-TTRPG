## Character Advancement <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Every adventure changes a character. Battles fought, mysteries solved, disasters survived, and hard-earned victories contribute to a character's growing knowledge, confidence, and experience. As characters overcome challenges and accomplish meaningful objectives, they advance in **Experience Level**, gaining opportunities to improve their abilities.

_Journey Through the Ages_ is primarily a skill-based roleplaying system. A character's capabilities are determined by their Attributes, Skills, Edges, equipment, and circumstances rather than by a rigid class progression.

Experience Levels provide a structured way to manage character development while allowing players to choose how their characters improve.

Unlike class-based advancement systems, gaining an Experience Level does not automatically increase Attributes, Wound Slots, combat abilities, or other characteristics. Instead, characters receive Experience Points (XP) that they may spend on improvements of their choice.

This approach allows characters of the same Experience Level to develop very different capabilities.

### Experience Levels

Characters normally begin play at **Experience Level 1**. As they complete adventures and overcome meaningful challenges, they may advance through Level 9.

Experience Levels represent a character's overall experience and opportunities for growth. They are not a direct measure of combat ability or personal power. 

Each time a character advances to a new Experience Level, they receive **3 Experience Points (XP)**. These points may be spent immediately or saved for future improvements.

A Level 5 scholar may possess extensive academic expertise but little combat training, while a Level 5 soldier may be highly effective in battle but have limited scholarly knowledge. Both characters have experienced comparable opportunities for advancement, but they have invested their XP differently.

### Awarding Experience Levels

The Game Master determines when characters advance.

Advancement should reflect meaningful accomplishments rather than simply the number of enemies defeated.

Appropriate milestones may include:

- Completing an important mission.
- Solving a significant mystery.
- Surviving a dangerous expedition.
- Defeating a major adversary.
- Negotiating a lasting agreement.
- Achieving an important personal or group objective.
- Overcoming a challenge that substantially changes the campaign.

Characters should generally advance together, allowing the group to develop at a similar pace.

The Game Master may adjust advancement to reflect the campaign's intended tone and duration.

A fast-paced pulp adventure might advance characters frequently, while a gritty historical or military campaign may emphasize slower development.

### Recommended Advancement Pace

The following table suggests how many meaningful game sessions characters should spend at each Experience Level.

|Experience Level|Recommended Sessions at Level|
|---|---|
|1|1|
|2|1|
|3|2|
|4|2|
|5|2|
|6|3|
|7|3|
|8|3|
|9|4|

The listed number represents the recommended time spent at that level before the next advancement milestone.

For example, characters beginning at Level 1 normally advance to Level 2 after one meaningful session. They may then advance to Level 3 after another meaningful session.

The Level 9 recommendation provides a suggested pacing interval for high-level adventures or optional advancement beyond the standard progression. It does not automatically establish a Level 10.

These numbers are guidelines rather than requirements.

A particularly significant accomplishment may justify earlier advancement, while a campaign involving extended investigations, travel, or complex political developments may progress more slowly.

The Game Master should award levels when advancement feels earned and appropriate to the story.

### Experience Points

Each time a character gains an Experience Level beyond their starting Level 1, they receive **3 XP**.

Experience Points represent the benefits of training, practice, study, professional experience, and lessons learned during adventures.

XP may be spent on:

1. Improving Skills.
2. Purchasing new Edges.
3. Improving existing Edges.
4. Increasing Wealth, if the optional Wealth advancement rules are used.

XP may be spent when a character gains a level, or during downtime if the Game Master prefers advancement to occur between adventures.

### Standard XP Progression

The following table shows the total XP earned through normal Experience Level advancement.

It assumes that characters begin at Level 1 without an additional advancement XP award.

|Experience Level|XP Earned at This Level|Total Advancement XP Earned|
|---|---|---|
|1|—|0|
|2|3|3|
|3|3|6|
|4|3|9|
|5|3|12|
|6|3|15|
|7|3|18|
|8|3|21|
|9|3|24|

This table represents total XP earned, not the XP a character currently has available to spend.

Previously spent XP remains invested in the character's improvements.

### Saving Experience Points

Characters are not required to spend all their XP immediately.

Unspent XP may be saved and combined with XP earned at later Experience Levels. This allows players to work toward expensive improvements rather than purchasing less desirable abilities simply because they have available points.

**Example:** A character advances to Level 3 and receives 3 XP. They spend 1 XP improving a Skill to Level 2 and save the remaining 2 XP.

When the character reaches Level 4, they receive another 3 XP, bringing their available total to 5 XP. They may now purchase a new Level 1 Edge for 2 XP and upgrade it to Level 2 for an additional 3 XP, provided the Edge is eligible and all prerequisites are satisfied.

### Spending Experience Points

As a reward for surviving or achieving goals, players can increase their character's Skills, Edges, and Wealth.  

#### Improving Skills

Skills represent training, education, and practical experience.

Characters may spend XP to improve their existing Skills, becoming more capable as their experience grows.

Unlike character creation, the cost of improvement increases at higher Skill Levels.

|New Skill Level|XP Cost|
|---|---|
|Level 2|1 XP|
|Level 3|2 XP|
|Level 4|2 XP|
|Level 5|3 XP|

Each XP cost purchases one additional Skill Level.

Skill Levels must be purchased sequentially. A character cannot skip intermediate levels.

**Example: Improving a Skill**

Sarah has Investigation Level 2 and wishes to increase it to Level 4.

She must purchase Level 3 for 2 XP and then Level 4 for another 2 XP.

The total cost is 4 XP.

She cannot purchase Level 4 directly without first purchasing Level 3.

**Example: Mastering a Skill**

Thomas has Ranged Combat Level 3.

Improving to Level 4 costs 2 XP.

Improving from Level 4 to Level 5 costs another 3 XP.

The total cost to improve from Level 3 to Level 5 is 5 XP.

#### Maximum Skill Level

Under the standard rules, no Skill may exceed **Level 5**.

A character who has reached Level 5 represents exceptional expertise in that field. Further improvement may come from appropriate Edges, specialized equipment, or favorable circumstances rather than increasing the Skill rating beyond its normal maximum.

The Game Master may permit higher ratings only if a specific campaign rule explicitly allows them.

#### Learning New Skills

All 15 Basic Skills begin at Level 1 during character creation.

Consequently, characters improve existing Basic Skills rather than purchasing entirely new ones.

Some campaigns may introduce optional or specialized Skills that characters do not automatically possess. When a character learns such a Skill, purchasing Level 1 costs **1 XP**, unless the Skill's description specifies otherwise. The character may then improve that Skill using the normal XP costs.

Optional Skills must be approved by the Game Master and should not duplicate existing Basic Skills unnecessarily.

#### Training and Experience

Skill improvements should reflect a character's activities, training, or experiences.

A character who regularly pilots aircraft may reasonably improve Vehicle. A character who spends several adventures investigating mysteries may improve Investigation.

The Game Master may require an explanation for a major improvement, particularly when the character has had little opportunity to practice the Skill. Appropriate explanations include formal training, independent study, instruction from a mentor, or significant practical experience.

Training requirements should support the story without unnecessarily preventing characters from spending earned XP.

### Purchasing Edges

Edges represent exceptional talents, specialized training, unusual experiences, and other advantages that distinguish characters from one another.

Characters may purchase new Edges or improve existing Edges by spending XP.

|Edge Improvement|XP Cost|
|---|---|
|Purchase Level 1 Edge|2 XP|
|Upgrade to Level 2 Edge|3 XP|

A character must possess Level 1 before purchasing Level 2.

Unless the Edge states otherwise, Level 2 replaces the equivalent Level 1 numerical benefits rather than stacking with them.

#### Example: Purchasing an Edge

Elena wants to acquire the Expert Locksmith Edge.

She spends 2 XP to purchase Level 1.

Later, she spends 3 additional XP to upgrade the Edge to Level 2.

The total cost is 5 XP.

#### Edge Availability

Edges may have restrictions on when they can be acquired.

**Character Creation Only:** These Edges must be selected during character creation and cannot normally be purchased for the first time through advancement.

However, a character who selected Level 1 during character creation may improve that Edge to Level 2 through advancement.

**Character Creation or Advancement:** These Edges may be acquired during character creation or purchased later using XP.

Characters must meet all prerequisites before acquiring or improving an Edge.

The Game Master may also require appropriate training, mentorship, or significant story events for certain Edges.

#### Example: Improving a Starting Edge

Marcus selected the Lucky Edge at Level 1 during character creation.

Although Lucky is a Character Creation Only Edge, Marcus may spend 3 XP during advancement to improve it to Level 2.

A character who did not select Lucky during character creation cannot normally purchase it later.

### Increasing Wealth (Optional)

As characters gain experience, they may acquire greater financial resources through employment, investments, business ventures, recovered valuables, or rewards earned during adventures.

For campaigns that prefer abstract financial advancement, the Game Master may allow characters to spend XP to represent meaningful improvements in their financial circumstances.

|Wealth Improvement|XP Cost|
|---|---|
|Minor Increase|1 XP|
|Moderate Increase|2 XP|
|Major Increase|3 XP|

The Game Master determines the financial effect of each improvement using the campaign's Wealth rules.

The scale of an increase depends on the setting.

In a pirate adventure, a financial improvement might represent valuable cargo, shares in a trading venture, or recovered treasure.

In a modern campaign, it might represent a promotion, investment income, or a successful business.

In a science fiction campaign, it might represent corporate shares, mining rights, or income from interplanetary commerce.

#### Wealth Advancement Guidelines

**Wealth improvements require Game Master approval.** Players should provide a plausible explanation for the financial increase.

XP expenditures do not automatically grant access to equipment restricted by law, technology, or campaign circumstances.

The Game Master should establish whether a Minor, Moderate, or Major increase represents a temporary financial benefit, a permanent increase in Wealth, or an increase in an existing Wealth rating.

Once the campaign adopts an interpretation, it should apply consistently to all characters.

Characters may also gain or lose Wealth through ordinary adventures without spending XP.

The optional XP system is intended to represent lasting financial development rather than replace normal rewards, purchases, or economic consequences.

### Introducing New Characters

Experience Levels provide a convenient method for introducing new or replacement characters into an ongoing campaign.

A new character may begin at the group's current Experience Level rather than starting at Level 1.

This allows the new character to participate effectively without requiring the group to repeat earlier adventures.

#### Creating an Experienced Character

To create a character above Level 1:

1. Complete normal character creation, including Attributes, Background, starting Skills, Edges, and Hindrances.
2. Determine the Experience Level at which the character enters the campaign.
3. Grant the advancement XP normally earned by reaching that level.
4. Spend the XP using the advancement rules.
5. Select equipment and determine Wealth appropriate to the campaign and character's experience.

A character entering at Level 5, for example, normally receives **12 advancement XP** in addition to their starting character-creation benefits.

These points may be spent on Skills, Edges, and optional Wealth advancement.

The Game Master may adjust the starting XP or equipment if the established party has received additional rewards, special training, or other benefits outside normal advancement.

#### Example: Joining an Existing Party

A group of adventurers has reached Experience Level 6.

A new player creates a former naval officer who will join the party.

The character begins with the normal Attribute allocation, Background Skill Points, general Skill Points, and starting Edge benefits.

Because Level 6 characters have earned 15 advancement XP, the new character receives 15 XP to spend on improvements. The player might use those points to improve Vehicle, Ranged Combat, and Perception, and to purchase appropriate Edges.

The result is an experienced character capable of contributing alongside the established party.

### Advancement Beyond Level 9

Experience Level 9 represents the highest standard level described by these rules.

Characters who reach Level 9 may continue adventuring, developing relationships, accumulating resources, and accomplishing personal goals. The Game Master may allow additional advancement beyond Level 9, but such advancement is optional.

Any such extension should preserve the normal maximum Skill Level of 5 unless the campaign explicitly adopts different limits.

Advancement beyond Level 9 should not automatically increase Attributes or other characteristics that are normally fixed.

### Advancement Is a Choice

There is no single correct way to develop a character.

One investigator may focus on improving Investigation and Perception, becoming an exceptional detective. Another may invest in Edges that provide specialized talents and unusual advantages. A former soldier might improve combat training, while a merchant might invest in Influence, Vehicle, and financial opportunities.

Two characters at the same Experience Level may possess very different capabilities because of the choices they make.

Players are encouraged to select improvements that reflect their characters' experiences, ambitions, and changing circumstances.

**Experience Levels measure opportunities for growth. The choices players make with those opportunities determine the characters they become.**
