## Wounds

Adventuring is dangerous. Characters may suffer cuts, burns, broken bones, gunshot wounds, and other injuries as they confront the hazards of their world.

Rather than tracking Hit Points, _Journey Through the Ages_ uses a **Wound Slot system**. Injuries are recorded according to their severity, allowing characters to withstand a limited number of wounds before additional injuries become increasingly dangerous.

A character's capacity to endure injury depends partly on their Resilience (RES), while the severity and consequences of individual wounds depend on the circumstances of combat and other hazards.

This system supports both cinematic adventures and more realistic campaigns in which injuries can have lasting consequences.

### Character Wound Slots

Every character has four Wound Levels:

- **Minor:** Relatively minor injuries that can accumulate.
- **Serious:** Significant injuries that may impair physical capabilities.
- **Critical:** Life-threatening injuries that may disable or incapacitate a character.
- **Lethal:** Potentially fatal injuries requiring immediate attention.

Each Wound Level contains a limited number of slots.

| Wound Level | Number of Slots           |
| ----------- | ------------------------- |
| Minor       | Equal to Resilience (RES) |
| Serious     | 2                         |
| Critical    | 1                         |
| Lethal      | 1                         |
During character creation, record these slots on the character sheet or Injury Tracker.

**Example:** A character with RES 3 has three Minor Wound slots, two Serious Wound slots, two Critical Wound slots, and one Lethal Wound slot.

Some Hindrances or special rules may modify a character's available Wound Slots. Apply those modifications when determining the character's final Wound capacity.

### Recording Wounds

Whenever a character suffers an injury, determine its Wound Level using the applicable damage rules.

Mark one empty Wound Slot at that level. A marked slot represents an injury that has not yet healed.

Wound Slots remain marked until the character recovers through rest, medical treatment, or another applicable healing effect.

### Wound Escalation

When a character suffers a wound and all slots at that Wound Level are already filled, the new wound increases to the next higher Wound Level.

If the next level is also full, continue increasing the wound's severity until an empty slot is available.

Wounds escalate in the following order:

**Minor → Serious → Critical → Lethal**

An existing wound does not move to a higher level merely because its slots are full. Instead, the **new wound** is recorded at the first higher level with an available slot.

#### Example: Minor Wound Escalation

Sarah has RES 3, giving her the following Wound Slots:

| Wound Level | Slots |
| ----------- | ----- |
| Minor       | 3     |
| Serious     | 2     |
| Critical    | 1     |
| Lethal      | 1     |
During a firefight, Sarah suffers four Minor Wounds.

The first three fill her Minor Wound slots.

When Sarah suffers the fourth Minor Wound, all Minor slots are already filled. The new wound therefore escalates to a Serious Wound.

Sarah now has three Minor Wounds and one Serious Wound.

#### Example: Escalation Through Multiple Levels

Later, Sarah has three Minor Wounds and two Serious Wounds.

She suffers another Minor Wound.

Because all Minor slots are filled, the new wound escalates to Serious. However, both Serious slots are also filled.

The new wound therefore escalates again and fills one Critical Wound slot.

This represents the cumulative effects of repeated injuries, even when the latest injury would ordinarily be minor.

If the Lethal slot is already occupied, the consequences of further wounds must be resolved under the rules established for Lethal injuries and the Dying Condition in Chapter 6.

### Wound Levels

#### Minor Wounds

Minor Wounds represent bruises, scrapes, shallow cuts, sprains, and other injuries that are painful but not immediately life-threatening.

A character can usually continue acting while suffering Minor Wounds, although accumulating injuries can eventually produce more serious consequences through Wound Escalation.

**Examples:** Black eye, shallow knife cut, bruised ribs, twisted ankle, minor burn.

#### Serious Wounds

Serious Wounds represent significant injuries that may interfere with a character's ability to fight, move, or perform demanding tasks.

These injuries often require medical attention and may produce additional Conditions depending on their location and nature.

**Examples:** Deep laceration, broken arm, gunshot wound through the shoulder, severe burn, cracked ribs.

#### Critical Wounds

Critical Wounds represent severe, potentially life-threatening injuries.

A character suffering a Critical Wound may be seriously impaired, disabled, or incapacitated. Prompt medical treatment may be necessary to prevent further complications.

**Examples:** Collapsed lung, shattered leg, major internal bleeding, severe head trauma, multiple gunshot wounds.

#### Lethal Wounds

A Lethal Wound represents a potentially fatal injury.

A character who suffers a Lethal Wound immediately gains the **Dying Condition**, as described in Chapter 6.

Without immediate medical attention or another applicable survival procedure, the character may die.

**Examples:** Bullet through the heart, severed major artery, crushed chest, catastrophic trauma, massive blood loss.

### Injury Descriptions

For every Serious or Critical Wound, record a brief description of the injury, including its location and any important circumstances.

Examples include:

- Serious Wound — Broken left arm
- Serious Wound — Deep sword cut across the chest
- Critical Wound — Gunshot wound to the abdomen
- Critical Wound — Severe head trauma
- Critical Wound — Shattered right leg

These descriptions help the Game Master determine appropriate injury-related Conditions, treatment requirements, and narrative consequences.

An injury's description does not independently establish a mechanical penalty. Apply Conditions and other effects according to the relevant rules in Chapter 6.

Lethal Wounds should also be described when doing so helps establish the nature of the injury and the circumstances of treatment.

### Healing Wounds

Wound Slots are not permanently lost when marked.

As characters recover, wounds are removed or reduced according to the healing rules.

The Game Master selects the campaign's healing model:

**Epic Heroism:** Characters recover relatively quickly, supporting fast-paced adventures and cinematic action.

**Gritty Realism:** Injuries require more time and may demand medical treatment or produce lasting complications.

Both approaches use the same Wound Slot system.

See Chapter 7, _Rest & Healing_, for complete recovery procedures.

### Wounds and Character Development

Serious injuries may become important parts of a character's story.

A broken leg might delay an expedition. A gunshot wound may leave a permanent scar. A near-fatal injury could change a character's outlook or motivation.

In particularly severe cases, lasting injuries may be represented by an appropriate Hindrance or other consequence, as described in the applicable injury and recovery rules.

The Game Master should distinguish temporary injury effects from permanent character changes and discuss significant lasting consequences with the affected player.

**Wounds represent more than lost endurance. They are the physical consequences of danger, violence, and the difficult choices characters make during their adventures.**
