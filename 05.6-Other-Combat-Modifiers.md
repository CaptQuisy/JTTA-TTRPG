## Other Combat Modifiers

Combat is influenced by more than a character's Attributes, Skills, and equipment.

Weapon handling, positioning, movement, lighting, and environmental conditions can provide advantages or impose penalties.

The following modifiers apply to combat situations not already covered by the standard Attack and Defense rules.

Unless otherwise specified, modifiers adjust the number of dice rolled. The Game Master determines which modifiers apply based on the circumstances.

### Off-Hand Attacks

A character who attacks using their **off-hand** suffers a **–2 dice penalty**.

The off-hand is the hand the character does not normally use for precise or practiced tasks.

This penalty represents reduced coordination and control.

|Circumstance|Attack Modifier|
|---|---|
|Attack with dominant hand|No penalty|
|Attack with off-hand|–2 dice|
The penalty applies to Close Combat and Ranged Combat attacks when the weapon is being used with the off-hand.

The Game Master may waive the penalty when an Edge or special ability explicitly allows the character to fight effectively with either hand.

#### Example: Off-Hand Knife Attack

A character normally rolls 6 dice when attacking with a knife.

The character's dominant hand is occupied, so they attack with the knife in their off-hand.

The Attack suffers –2 dice.

The character rolls **4 dice**.

### Two-Weapon Fighting

A character wielding a weapon in each hand may use both weapons together in a coordinated attack.

**Cost: 1 Action Point (AP)**

Two-Weapon Fighting is resolved as **one Attack**.

The character receives **+1 die** to the Attack Roll.

|Circumstance|Attack Modifier|
|---|---|
|Two-Weapon Fighting|+1 die|
The two weapons are treated as contributing to a single coordinated attack rather than making two independent attacks.

#### Two-Weapon Fighting Requirements

To use Two-Weapon Fighting:

- The character must have a usable weapon in each hand.
- Both weapons must be suitable for one-handed use.
- The character must be capable of wielding both weapons.
- Both weapons must be able to reach the intended target.

The normal off-hand penalty does not apply to the combined Two-Weapon Fighting Attack. Its effect is represented by the single +1 die bonus.

However, a character who makes a separate attack using only the off-hand weapon suffers the normal –2 dice penalty.

#### Resolving Damage

A successful Two-Weapon Fighting Attack counts as **one hit**, not two.

Use the damage characteristics of one of the weapons, chosen before the Attack Roll. The second weapon contributes to the +1 die Attack bonus.

The character does not inflict damage twice merely because two weapons were used.

#### Multiple Two-Weapon Attacks

A character may perform multiple Two-Weapon Fighting Attacks during a Turn if sufficient Action Points remain.

Each Attack after the first suffers the normal Rushed Attack penalty.

##### Example: Sword and Dagger

A character fights with a sword in one hand and a dagger in the other.

The character normally rolls 6 dice for a Close Combat Attack.

Two-Weapon Fighting grants +1 die.

The character rolls **7 dice**.

If successful, the Attack is resolved as one hit using the selected weapon's damage characteristics.

### Weapon Handling

A weapon's condition, quality, and suitability affect how effectively it can be used.

|Weapon Condition|Attack Modifier|
|---|---|
|Improvised Weapon|–1 die|
|Weapon Larger Than Character Can Handle|–2 dice|
|Masterwork Weapon|+1 die|
|Damaged Weapon|–1 die|
### Position and Mobility

Combatants can gain advantages through movement and positioning.

|Circumstance|Modifier|
|---|---|
|Higher Ground|+1 Attack die|
|Charging in Close Combat|+1 Attack die; –1 Defense die until next Turn|
|Difficult Terrain|–1 die to Athletics or movement-related Skill Checks|
#### Higher Ground

A character attacking from a meaningful elevation advantage receives **+1 die**.

Examples include:

- Fighting from the top of a staircase.
- Attacking an opponent from an elevated platform.
- Standing on a raised defensive position.
- Striking an opponent positioned significantly below the attacker.

The Game Master determines whether the elevation provides a genuine advantage.

A slight difference in height does not automatically qualify. Higher Ground may apply to Close Combat or Ranged Combat when the position meaningfully improves the attack.

The bonus does not automatically apply when elevation creates a disadvantage, such as firing through an obstructed angle.

##### Example: Fighting on a Staircase

A warrior stands several steps above an advancing opponent.

The higher position provides a useful advantage.

The Game Master grants **+1 die** to the warrior's Close Combat Attack.

### Charging

A character may charge an opponent by moving aggressively into Close Combat.

A Charge uses the normal movement and Attack rules. The character must move meaningfully toward the target before attacking.

A successful Charge is not dependent on the Attack hitting; the bonus represents the momentum and commitment of the approach.

#### Charging Modifiers

- **+1 die** to the Close Combat Attack.
- **–1 die** to Defense until the beginning of the character's next Turn.

The Defense penalty represents the character's commitment to an aggressive attack, leaving them temporarily exposed.

A Charge requires a Move Action and a Close Combat Attack, normally costing **2 AP total**.

The character must have enough room to build momentum. A character already standing directly beside an opponent cannot normally claim a Charge bonus merely by attacking.

##### Example: Charging an Enemy

A soldier sees an enemy 20 feet away.

The soldier spends:

- 1 AP to Move toward the enemy.
- 1 AP to make a Close Combat Attack.

The Charge grants +1 die to the Attack.

However, the soldier suffers –1 die to Defense until the beginning of their next Turn.

The soldier has 1 AP remaining.

### Visibility

Poor visibility can make it difficult to locate, identify, and accurately attack a target.

Lighting, darkness, smoke, fog, and other obstructions may impose penalties.

|Visibility Condition|Attack Modifier|
|---|---|
|Dim Light|–1 die|
|Darkness without aid|–3 dice|
|Target Heavily Obscured|–2 dice|
|Smoke or Fog|–1 to –3 dice|
Visibility penalties apply when the attacker depends on sight to locate or strike the target.

The Game Master determines whether the condition meaningfully affects the attack.

#### Dim Light

Dim Light imposes **–1 die** on sight-dependent attacks.

Examples include:

- Twilight.
- A poorly lit hallway.
- Weak candlelight.
- A moonlit outdoor scene.

A character with appropriate lighting equipment or vision capabilities may avoid the penalty.

#### Darkness

Attacking in darkness without an appropriate light source or visual aid imposes **–3 dice**.

This represents the difficulty of identifying and accurately striking a target that cannot be clearly seen.

Darkness does not automatically make every attack impossible. A character may still attack a target whose approximate location is known. However, if the character has no reasonable way to determine the target's location, the Game Master may rule that a direct attack cannot be attempted.

#### Heavily Obscured Targets

A target that is difficult to see because of foliage, dust, debris, visual interference, or another obstruction imposes **–2 dice**.

Heavy obscurity represents difficulty seeing the target, not necessarily physical protection. A target may be obscured without having Cover.

For example, thick foliage may conceal a target while providing little protection against gunfire.

The Game Master should distinguish between visibility penalties and Cover modifiers.

#### Smoke and Fog

Smoke and fog impose penalties according to their density.

|Condition|Attack Modifier|
|---|---|
|Light Smoke or Fog|–1 die|
|Moderate Smoke or Fog|–2 dice|
|Heavy Smoke or Fog|–3 dice|
The Game Master determines the appropriate penalty.

Visibility may change during combat as smoke spreads, fog thickens, or environmental conditions shift.

#### Avoiding Duplicate Visibility Penalties

Visibility penalties should not automatically stack when they describe the same obstruction.

For example, a target concealed by thick fog is already difficult to see.

The Game Master should normally use the appropriate Smoke or Fog penalty rather than applying additional penalties for Heavy Obscurity caused by that same fog.

When several distinct conditions affect visibility, the Game Master may apply additional penalties if justified. However, the same visual difficulty should not be counted more than once.

##### Example: Shooting Through Fog

A character fires at an opponent through moderate fog.

The fog imposes –2 dice.

If the target is additionally behind a solid barricade, the appropriate Cover modifier may also apply because the barricade represents a separate obstacle.

### Applying Multiple Combat Modifiers

Several modifiers may affect the same Attack or Defense Roll.

The Game Master should apply modifiers consistently while avoiding duplicate penalties.

#### General Procedure

1. Determine the appropriate Attribute and Skill.
2. Apply weapon-specific modifiers.
3. Apply relevant positioning and movement modifiers.
4. Apply visibility and environmental modifiers.
5. Apply relevant Edges, Hindrances, and Conditions.
6. Apply any Action or Reaction bonuses.
7. Roll the resulting dice pool.

Modifiers may combine when they represent genuinely different circumstances.

For example, a damaged weapon and poor lighting are separate problems and may both affect an Attack.

However, two descriptions of the same obstacle should not impose duplicate penalties.

The standard minimum dice pool of **2 dice** applies unless a specific rule states that the attempted action is impossible.

##### Example: Fighting in Poor Conditions

A character attacks an enemy with a damaged sword in dim light.

The character's normal Close Combat Attack pool is 6 dice.

The modifiers are:

- Damaged Weapon: –1 die.
- Dim Light: –1 die.

The final dice pool is **4 dice**.

If the character also has Higher Ground, the +1 die bonus increases the pool to **5 dice**.

### Game Master Guidance

Combat modifiers should reflect meaningful circumstances rather than create unnecessary bookkeeping.

**Apply Modifiers Only When Relevant**

- A circumstance should affect the dice pool only when it meaningfully changes the character's ability to perform the action.

**Avoid Double-Counting**

- Do not apply multiple penalties for the same obstruction, weapon defect, or tactical disadvantage.

**Reward Tactical Positioning**

- Characters should benefit from taking Higher Ground, choosing appropriate weapons, and improving visibility.

**Make Environmental Conditions Matter**

- Darkness, smoke, terrain, and damaged equipment can change how combatants approach an encounter.
- These factors should create interesting decisions rather than merely punish players.

**Combat modifiers should make circumstances matter while keeping combat fast, consistent, and easy to resolve.**
