# Combat

Combat represents those moments when negotiation has failed and the outcome must be decided through action.

Whether exchanging sword blows, trading gunfire, or battling terrifying creatures, combat uses the same core dice pool system found throughout _Journey Through the Ages_.

Players assemble dice pools, roll six-sided dice, and count each **5 or 6 as one success**.

Combat introduces additional rules for Actions, Reactions, movement, and tactical positioning, allowing characters to make meaningful decisions under pressure.

The combat system supports both fast narrative encounters and tactical battles using maps and miniatures.

## Theater-of-the-Mind or Tactical Combat

Before combat begins, the Game Master should decide how the battlefield will be represented.

### Theater-of-the-Mind

Theater-of-the-Mind combat relies on descriptive narration rather than precise positioning.

Characters move through the scene, with the Game Master determining distances, terrain, obstacles, and lines of sight through the narrative.

This approach emphasizes fast resolution and dramatic action.

The Game Master should describe the environment clearly enough for players to understand their options and make informed decisions.

### Tactical Combat

Tactical combat uses maps, miniatures, or tokens to represent the battlefield.

Positioning, movement, cover, range, and terrain are tracked more precisely, allowing greater emphasis on tactical decisions.

Both approaches use the same combat mechanics.

The difference is how the battlefield is represented, not how attacks and defenses are resolved.

Groups may combine these approaches, using narrative descriptions for straightforward encounters and maps when precise positioning becomes important.

## Combat Rounds

Combat is divided into **Rounds**.

Each Round represents approximately **6 seconds** of action, during which every combatant has an opportunity to act.

Ten Rounds represent approximately one minute.

Although characters and opponents take their Turns in sequence, their activities are understood to occur within the same short period of time.

The order in which combatants act is determined by the Initiative rules in Section 5.1.

### Combat Sequence

A combat encounter normally follows this procedure:

1. **Establish the Situation:** The Game Master describes the battlefield, participants, and immediate circumstances.
2. **Determine Initiative:** Establish the order in which combatants take their Turns.
3. **Resolve Turns:** Each combatant acts according to the Initiative order.
4. **Resolve Reactions:** Characters may respond to appropriate triggers outside their own Turns by spending available Action Points.
5. **Begin the Next Round:** After every combatant has had an opportunity to act, a new Round begins.
6. **End Combat:** Structured combat ends when the immediate conflict is resolved.

When opponents attack player characters, the players make the appropriate Defense Rolls against the opponents' Attack Ratings.

## Action Points

Every character has **3 Action Points (AP)**.

Action Points represent the character's capacity to act and react during combat.

Most Actions cost **1 AP**, although some abilities or activities may require more.

A character may spend Action Points to:

- Move.
- Attack.
- Assess the situation.
- Reload a weapon.
- Aim.
- Interact with complex objects.
- Perform other activities permitted by the Game Master.

Characters may perform Actions in any order during their Turns.

A character may also repeat the same Action when sufficient Action Points remain, unless a specific rule states otherwise.

### Spending and Refreshing Action Points

Spent Action Points remain unavailable until the end of the character's Turn.

**At the end of each Turn, the character's Action Points refresh to their maximum of 3.**

Unused Action Points do not accumulate beyond this maximum.

The refreshed Action Points are immediately available for Reactions before the character's next Turn.

Characters begin combat with their full 3 Action Points available, including for Reactions that occur before their first Turn.

### Actions

An Action represents a meaningful activity performed during a character's Turn.

Unless otherwise specified, each Action costs **1 AP**.

#### Move

**Cost: 1 AP**

A character may move up to **30 feet** across normal, unobstructed ground. This distance represents the standard movement of an unimpeded human character.

A character may use multiple Move Actions during the same Turn. Each additional Move Action provides another movement allowance.

For example, a character who spends two Action Points on movement may travel up to 60 feet across normal terrain.

##### Difficult Terrain

Difficult Terrain reduces movement speed by half. A character moving through Difficult Terrain may travel up to **15 feet per Move Action** instead of 30 feet.

Examples include:

- Thick undergrowth.
- Deep mud.
- Rubble.
- Snow.
- Uneven or unstable ground.
- Slippery ice.
- Climbable surfaces such as trees, cliffs, or building exteriors.

The Game Master determines whether a surface or obstacle qualifies as Difficult Terrain. Particularly dangerous movement may also require an appropriate Skill Check.

If movement crosses both normal and Difficult Terrain, the Game Master may calculate movement proportionally or make a reasonable ruling based on the circumstances.

#### Attack

**Cost: 1 AP**

An Attack is an action intended to harm an opponent or another target.

Examples include:

- Striking with a sword.
- Punching an enemy.
- Firing a pistol.
- Shooting a rifle.
- Throwing a grenade.
- Using a weapon mounted on a vehicle.

The player assembles the appropriate Attack dice pool and resolves the Attack using the combat rules.

Typical Attack combinations include:

**Strength + Close Combat:** Fighting with melee weapons or unarmed attacks.

**Dexterity + Ranged Combat:** Firing ranged weapons.

Other combinations may apply when a specific weapon, ability, or situation requires them.

Range modifiers, cover, size, armor, and special weapon rules are determined using the applicable combat and equipment sections.

##### Rushed Attacks

A character may spend additional Action Points to make multiple Attacks during the same Turn.

Each Attack after the first is considered a **Rushed Attack**. Rushed Attacks suffer cumulative dice penalties.

|Attack During Turn|Dice Modifier|
|---|---|
|First Attack|No penalty|
|Second Attack|–1 die|
|Third Attack|–2 dice|
The penalties apply to the corresponding Attack Rolls.

The number of Attacks made during a Turn determines the penalty, regardless of whether the Attacks target the same opponent.

The Rushed Attack count resets when the character begins a new Turn.

##### Example: Multiple Attacks

A character has a normal Attack dice pool of 6 dice.

The character spends all 3 Action Points making Attacks.

- First Attack: 6 dice.
- Second Attack: 5 dice.
- Third Attack: 4 dice.

Each Attack is resolved separately.

#### Assess

**Cost: 1 AP**

A character may study the battlefield, observe an opponent, or identify a useful opportunity.

The character makes an **Acuity + Perception Skill Check** at Normal Difficulty, requiring 2 successes.

On success, choose one of the following benefits.

##### Ask a Question

The character may ask the Game Master one question about an enemy, environmental hazard, immediate threat, or another relevant aspect of the current situation.

Examples include:

- Does the enemy appear injured or weakened?
- Is the opponent preparing to attack?
- Where is the safest route through the battlefield?
- Is there an obvious weakness in the enemy's position?
- What nearby object could provide useful cover?

The Game Master provides information the character could reasonably discover through observation.

Assess does not reveal information that cannot be perceived or reasonably inferred.

##### Create an Opening

The character identifies a weakness in an opponent's defenses.

The next Attack against that opponent receives **+1 die**.

This bonus must be used before the end of the assessing character's next Turn.

Only one Create an Opening bonus may apply to a single Attack.

##### Anticipate Danger

The character studies an immediate threat and prepares to respond.

The character receives **+1 die to their next Defense Roll**.

The bonus expires if unused by the end of the character's next Turn.

This bonus may be combined with the Defend Reaction.

##### Failed Assessment

If the Assess Check fails, the character gains no selected benefit.

The Action Point remains spent.

A Critical Failure may produce an appropriate complication, such as misunderstanding an opponent's intentions or overlooking an immediate danger.

#### Reload

**Cost: 1 AP**

A character reloads ammunition into a weapon.

Examples include:

- Loading a bolt into a crossbow.
- Loading a musket.
- Replacing a pistol magazine.
- Reloading a rifle.
- Preparing another shot for a single-shot weapon.

Unless a weapon's specific rules state otherwise, reloading costs 1 AP.

Some weapons may require additional time, special equipment, or multiple Actions.

A weapon cannot be fired when its ammunition requirements have not been satisfied.

See Section 3.1 for ammunition and weapon-specific Reload rules.

#### Aim

**Cost: 1 AP**

A character takes additional time to carefully align a ranged weapon with a target.

Aiming grants **+1 die to the character's next ranged Attack Roll**.

The bonus applies only to the next ranged Attack made by that character.

The Aim bonus expires if unused by the end of the character's next Turn.

A character may Aim more than once, but repeated Aim Actions do not increase the bonus beyond +1 die unless a specific ability permits it.

##### Example: Careful Shot

A character has a ranged Attack dice pool of 5 dice.

The character spends:

- 1 AP to Aim.
- 1 AP to Attack.

The Attack receives +1 die, increasing the pool to 6 dice.

The character has 1 AP remaining.

#### Interact with Complex Objects

**Cost: Usually 1 AP**

A character may spend an Action interacting with an object or device that requires meaningful attention or effort.

Examples include:

- Operating a complicated control panel.
- Opening a heavy mechanical door.
- Activating specialized machinery.
- Manipulating a security device.
- Retrieving equipment from a difficult-to-access container.
- Performing a brief emergency repair.

The Game Master may require an appropriate Skill Check when the outcome is uncertain.

More complicated interactions may require additional Action Points or several Turns.

The required time should reflect the task's complexity and the circumstances.

#### Recover 

A character may spend **1 Action Point (AP)** during their Turn to remove or reduce a negative Condition affecting them.

Some Conditions can be removed automatically, while others require an appropriate Skill Check or Saving Throw against a Difficulty determined by the Game Master.

- **Success:** Remove the Condition or reduce its severity.
- **Failure:** The Condition remains in effect.
- **Critical Failure:** The Condition remains, and the Game Master may introduce an additional complication.

|Condition|Recovery Method|
|---|---|
|Prone|Automatically stand, if physically able|
|Stunned or Disoriented|RES + Athletics|
|Frightened|RES + Survival|
|Grappled or Restrained|STR + Athletics or DEX + Finesse|
|Blinded (temporary obstruction)|DEX + Athletics|
|Burning|DEX + Athletics|
|Bleeding|DEX + Medicine|

**Bleeding:** A character may attempt to stop their own bleeding by applying pressure, binding the wound, or using available medical supplies. Success removes the Bleeding Condition but does not heal existing Wounds.

**Restrictions:** Recover cannot heal Wounds or remove Conditions requiring extensive treatment or outside assistance. A character may attempt Recover only once per Condition each Turn.

##### Example: Bleeding During Combat

A soldier suffers a Serious Wound and gains the Bleeding Condition during an enemy's Turn.

When his Turn begins, the soldier has 3 AP available. He spends 1 AP to attempt Recover, making a Dexterity + Medicine Skill Check against Normal Difficulty (2).

The soldier rolls 5 dice and obtains 2 successes.

He successfully stops the bleeding and removes the Bleeding Condition.

The soldier has 2 AP remaining for other Actions. Because the Bleeding Condition was removed before the end of his Turn, he suffers no additional Wound.

Had he failed, he would have suffered a Minor Wound from Bleeding at the end of his Turn.

#### Stabilizing Another Character (2 AP)

A character may spend **2 Action Points (AP)** to stabilize an adjacent wounded character who is Bleeding or Dying.

The assisting character makes an **Intelligence + Medicine Skill Check**. The Difficulty depends on the severity of the wounded character's condition.

|Condition|Difficulty|Result of Success|
|---|---|---|
|Bleeding|Normal (2)|Removes the Bleeding Condition.|
|Dying|Hard (3)|Stabilizes the character and removes the Dying Condition.|

**Success:** The wounded character is stabilized and no longer suffers the ongoing effects of the treated Condition. Existing Wounds remain and must be healed normally.

**Failure:** The Condition remains. The assisting character may attempt another Restore Action if sufficient AP remain.

**Critical Failure:** The treatment fails, and the Game Master may introduce a complication, such as aggravating the injury or exhausting available medical supplies.

**Restrictions:** The assisting character must be able to reach the wounded character and have the means to provide appropriate treatment. The Game Master may adjust the Difficulty based on available equipment, environmental conditions, or the nature of the injuries.

### Reactions

A **Reaction** is an Action performed outside the character's own Turn in response to a specific event.

Reactions allow characters to defend themselves, protect allies, exploit openings, and assist others.

Each Reaction costs **1 AP** from the character's current Action Point pool.

Because Action Points refresh at the end of the character's Turn, spending AP on Reactions before that Turn reduces the number available when the Turn begins.

Reactions must be declared when their triggering circumstances occur. A character cannot normally wait until the outcome has been resolved before declaring a Reaction.

#### Defend

**Cost: 1 AP**

**Trigger:** The character becomes the target of an Attack.

The character takes immediate defensive action, such as dodging, ducking, bracing, or making better use of nearby cover.

The character gains **+1 die to the Defense Roll against that Attack**.

This bonus applies to one Attack only.

Defend may be combined with the bonus from Assess: Anticipate Danger.

Defend does not automatically move the character to a new position or grant protection from physical cover that is not actually available.

##### Example: Defensive Reaction

A character is targeted by an enemy's ranged Attack.

The character spends 1 AP to Defend.

The player adds +1 die to the Defense dice pool for that Attack.

After resolving the Attack, the Defend bonus ends.

#### Interpose

**Cost: 1 AP**

**Trigger:** An ally within 10 feet becomes the target of an Attack.

The character moves to protect the ally and becomes the new target of the Attack. The character enters the ally's space and pushes the ally into an adjacent unoccupied space.

The Interpose Reaction must be declared after the Attack is announced but before it is resolved. The character making the Interpose Reaction becomes the defender and resolves the Attack normally.

The Game Master may prohibit Interpose when physical circumstances make the movement impossible.

Examples include:

- The ally is behind an impassable barrier.
- There is insufficient space to exchange positions.
- The reacting character is restrained or unable to move.
- The Attack cannot reasonably be intercepted.

Interpose does not automatically negate the Attack.

It changes who must defend against it.

#### Opportunity Attack

**Cost: 1 AP**

**Trigger:** An opponent creates an immediate opening by moving away from Close Combat or entering a valid ranged firing opportunity.

The character makes an immediate Attack against the triggering opponent.

The Opportunity Attack suffers **–1 die**.

##### Close Combat Opportunity

An opponent moving away from an engaged character may provoke an Opportunity Attack.

The reacting character must be able to reach the opponent with the chosen Close Combat weapon.

##### Ranged Opportunity

An opponent who moves into the character's line of sight may provoke a ranged Opportunity Attack when the character has a ready weapon and a valid firing opportunity.

The target must be within the weapon's effective range.

The Game Master determines whether the character can reasonably react before the opponent reaches safety.

##### Opportunity Attack Restrictions

An Opportunity Attack:

- Costs 1 AP.
- Suffers –1 die.
- Requires an appropriate weapon or attack method.
- Must have a valid target.

An Opportunity Attack does not count toward the Rushed Attack penalties applied to Attacks made during the character's own Turn.

#### Help

**Cost: 1 AP**

**Trigger:** An ally attempts a Skill Check, Attack Roll, Defense Roll, or another action that the character can reasonably assist.

The character provides immediate assistance.

The assisting player describes how the character helps.

Examples include:

- Distracting an opponent.
- Calling out a warning.
- Helping an ally maintain balance.
- Providing instructions.
- Supporting an ally's physical effort.
- Coordinating a combined maneuver.

The Game Master determines whether the proposed assistance is possible and assigns a bonus of **+1 to +3 dice**.

##### Help Bonus Guidelines

|Assistance|Bonus|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Minor|+1 die|Calling out a warning or providing simple assistance|
|Significant|+2 dice|Providing direct, meaningful support|
|Exceptional|+3 dice|Coordinated assistance that substantially improves the attempt|
Only **one Help Reaction** may benefit any single roll.

The Game Master may require an appropriate Skill Check when the assistance is itself difficult or uncertain.

If the assisting character cannot reasonably influence the outcome, no Help bonus is granted.

##### Example: Helping an Ally

A character attempts to force open a heavy security door.

An ally spends 1 AP to Help by applying additional force.

The Game Master determines that the assistance is significant and grants +2 dice.

The acting character adds the bonus to the Skill Check.

### Free Actions

Characters may perform **one Free Action per Round**.

A Free Action represents a very brief activity that requires little effort or attention.

Free Actions do not cost Action Points.

Examples include:

- Speaking a short phrase.
- Dropping an item.
- Dropping prone.
- Drawing a readily accessible weapon.
- Opening an unlocked door.
- Pressing a nearby button.
- Making a quick, simple gesture.
- Performing another minor interaction approved by the Game Master.

The Game Master determines whether an activity qualifies as a Free Action.

An action requiring concentration, substantial movement, or significant manipulation should instead require an Action Point.

#### Communication During Combat

Characters may exchange brief warnings, commands, or statements during combat.

Long conversations, complex negotiations, or detailed planning may require additional time.

The Game Master should permit reasonable communication without allowing characters to conduct lengthy discussions during a six-second Round.

### Example: Actions and Reactions

A character begins combat with 3 AP.

Before the character's first Turn, an enemy surprises the character and attacks first.

The character spends 1 AP to Defend, leaving 2 AP.

When the character's Turn begins, only 2 AP remain available.

The character spends:

- 1 AP to Move.
- 1 AP to Attack.

At the end of the Turn, the character's Action Points refresh to 3.

Those points are now available for Reactions before the next Turn.

## Turn Sequence

Each character's Turn follows the same basic sequence.

### 1. Start of Turn

The character begins the Turn with whatever Action Points remain after any Reactions performed since the previous Turn.

Action Points do not automatically refresh at the start of the Turn.

### 2. Spend Action Points

The character spends available Action Points in any permitted order.

Possible activities include:

- Attacking.
- Moving.
- Aiming.
- Assessing.
- Reloading.
- Interacting with objects.
- Activating powers.
- Performing other appropriate Actions.

The character may repeat an Action when the rules permit it and sufficient Action Points remain.

### 3. End of Turn

Resolve any effects that occur at the end of the character's Turn.

Examples include:

- Bleeding.
- Burning.
- Poison.
- Ongoing power effects.
- Other Conditions with start-of-turn effects.

Then restore the character's Action Points to the maximum of **3 AP**.

These refreshed points are available for Reactions before the character's next Turn.

Unused Action Points do not carry over as additional points beyond the maximum.

### 4. Continue Initiative

The next combatant acts according to the established Initiative order.

When all combatants have had an opportunity to act, the Round ends and the next Round begins.

Conditions and other effects that expire at the end of a Round are resolved according to their specific rules.

### 5. Opponents Attack

Opponents act according to the established Initiative order, following the same basic Turn Sequence as player characters.

When an opponent attacks a player character, the Game Master declares the target and type of attack. The player then makes an appropriate **Defense Roll** against the opponent's Attack Rating.

The Defense Roll represents the character's attempt to dodge, block, parry, or otherwise avoid the attack. The Game Master applies any appropriate modifiers for cover, armor, visibility, or other circumstances.

Once the opponent finishes its Actions, play continues to the next combatant in Initiative order.

### Ending Combat

Combat ends when the immediate conflict has been resolved or when structured Rounds are no longer necessary.

This may occur because:

- All opponents have been defeated.
- One side retreats or escapes.
- Both sides agree to negotiate.
- The characters achieve their objective.
- The opponents surrender.
- The combatants are no longer able or willing to continue fighting.
- The Game Master determines that structured combat is no longer necessary.

Once combat ends, normal narrative play resumes.

Conditions, injuries, ammunition expenditure, and other consequences remain in effect according to their respective rules.

## Game Master Guidance

Combat should reward teamwork, tactical positioning, creative problem-solving, and meaningful decisions.

Although defeating opponents may be an objective, characters should also consider:

- Seeking cover.
- Coordinating Attacks.
- Protecting vulnerable allies.
- Exploiting environmental hazards.
- Identifying enemy weaknesses.
- Conserving ammunition.
- Retreating from unfavorable positions.
- Completing mission objectives without eliminating every opponent.

 **Keep Action Costs Consistent**

- Most meaningful activities cost 1 AP.
- Actions requiring greater effort may cost additional points, but the Game Master should establish these costs consistently.

**Describe the Battlefield Clearly**

- Players should understand the distances, cover, obstacles, hazards, and opportunities relevant to their decisions.
- This is particularly important in Theater-of-the-Mind combat.

**Encourage Reactions**

- Reactions are a central part of the combat system.
- Players should be reminded that Action Points refreshed at the end of their Turns are available for defensive and supportive actions before their next Turns.

**Preserve Meaningful Risks**

- Characters should be able to take decisive actions, but doing so may leave them with fewer opportunities to react.
- A character who spends Action Points on Reactions before their Turn begins has fewer points available for Actions during that Turn.

**Resolve Opponents Consistently**

- The game uses player-facing dice rolls.
- Opponents act according to Initiative, while players make the relevant Defense Rolls against opponent Attack Ratings.
- The Game Master determines opponents' intentions and describes the consequences of their actions.

**Keep Combat Moving**

- Use clear descriptions, consistent rulings, and the established Action Point structure to resolve encounters efficiently.

==**Combat should be dangerous, dynamic, and tactical without becoming unnecessarily complicated. The Action Point system gives characters meaningful choices between attacking, moving, defending, and supporting their allies.**==
