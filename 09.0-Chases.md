# Chases

Chases are fast-paced encounters in which one group attempts to escape while another tries to catch them.

Whether characters are fleeing through crowded city streets, galloping across open country, racing automobiles along a highway, pursuing a ship through dangerous waters, or maneuvering spacecraft around an asteroid field, the same basic rules apply.

During a chase, everyone is already moving as quickly as circumstances reasonably allow. What matters is how participants use speed, skill, terrain, concealment, and clever tactics to gain an advantage.

The Chase system uses a common set of actions for every form of movement. The rules remain consistent while the narrative changes to reflect the setting.

## Beginning a Chase

A chase begins when one party attempts to escape and another attempts to pursue.

The Game Master establishes:

1. **The Fleeing Party:** The characters or opponents attempting to escape.
2. **The Pursuing Party:** The characters or opponents attempting to catch them.
3. **Starting Range:** The initial distance between the parties.
4. **Movement Type:** On foot, riding animals, automobiles, boats, aircraft, spacecraft, or another appropriate form.
5. **Environment:** Streets, wilderness, waterways, airspace, orbital debris, or other terrain. 
6. **Opponent Strategy:** The general approach taken by the opposing party.

The fleeing party initiates the chase by choosing its first action. The pursuing party responds.

Each Chase Round normally produces one primary check that determines whether the distance between the parties increases or decreases.

The chase continues until one party escapes, the pursuers catch the prey, or the circumstances bring the pursuit to another conclusion.

## Chase Ranges

Every chase uses six Range Bands representing the relative distance between the **Pursuer** and the **Prey**.

Range Bands are abstract. Their actual distances depend on the movement type, speed, visibility, terrain, and scale of the encounter.

### Range Descriptions

|Range|Description|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|**Engaged**|Close enough to attempt physical capture, grappling, ramming, or boarding. The pursuit phase ends.|Wrestling on foot, horses side by side, vehicles touching, ships alongside, spacecraft docking|
|**Close**|A short distance apart. Short-range attacks and immediate interception are possible.|Across a street, one car length, one boat length, close formation flight|
|**Short**|Comfortable engagement distance. Many weapons can be used effectively.|Down the block, several car lengths, across a harbor, visual pursuit|
|**Medium**|The target is becoming difficult to engage.|Across a field, several hundred yards, distant aircraft, sensor contact|
|**Long**|The target is barely visible or detectable.|Blocks away, distant vessel, radar contact, long-range sensors|
|**Extreme**|The fleeing party has escaped. The chase ends.|Vanishing beyond the horizon, disappearing into crowds, leaving sensor range|
### Range by Movement Type

|Range|Foot|Riding Animal|Automobile|Boat|Aircraft|Spacecraft|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Engaged|Grappling|Horses side by side|Vehicles touching|Boarding distance|Wingtip distance|Docking distance|
|Close|Across the street|One horse length|One car length|Alongside|Tight formation|Close pursuit|
|Short|Down the block|Across a field|Several car lengths|Across a harbor|Dogfighting distance|Visual range|
|Medium|Across a park|One hill away|Several hundred yards|Offshore|Several miles|Combat sensor range|
|Long|Barely visible|Distant rider|Highway ahead|Near horizon|Radar contact|Long-range sensors|
|Extreme|Lost in a crowd|Tiny silhouette|Disappearing from sight|Beyond horizon|Distant radar echo|Beyond useful pursuit sensors|
These are narrative examples, not fixed measurements.

### Changing Range

A successful chase action normally changes the distance by **one Range Band**.

For example:

**Short → Medium:** The fleeing party gains distance.

**Short → Close:** The pursuing party closes distance.

Range cannot increase beyond Extreme or decrease beyond Engaged.

#### Engaged

When Range reaches Engaged, the active pursuit ends because the pursuers have caught up with the fleeing party.

However, catching up does not automatically mean capturing the prey.

A pursuer may still need to grapple, tackle, ram, board, or otherwise prevent escape.

Resolve these actions using the appropriate combat, Skill Check, or vehicle rules.

#### Extreme

When Range reaches Extreme, the fleeing party escapes.

The chase ends unless the pursuers have an independent means of tracking or reacquiring the prey.

Such a situation becomes a new pursuit or search encounter rather than continuing the same chase indefinitely.

## Chase Round Sequence

Each Chase Round represents a brief exchange of movement, maneuvers, and counter-maneuvers.

Use the following sequence.

### Step 1: Declare the Fleeing Action

The fleeing party chooses one primary strategy:

- **Run:** Attempt to gain distance through speed.
- **Hide:** Attempt to disappear using concealment or misdirection.
- **Evade:** Use unpredictable movement to avoid interception.

### Step 2: Determine the Pursuer's Response

The pursuing party responds with an appropriate strategy:

- **Chase:** Match speed and close the distance.
- **Search:** Locate concealed or hidden prey.
- **Cut Off:** Predict the prey's route and intercept it.

The Game Master describes the opposing strategy before resolving the primary check.

### Step 3: Resolve the Primary Check

When the player characters are fleeing, a player makes the primary check against the opponent's relevant static rating.

When the player characters are pursuing, a player makes the primary check against the fleeing opponent's relevant static rating.

**Only one primary check changes Range during a Chase Round.**

This prevents the fleeing and pursuing sides from producing contradictory Range changes in the same exchange.

The opposing strategy determines the applicable opposition, modifiers, and narrative consequences.

### Step 4: Apply the Result

A successful fleeing check increases Range by one band.

A failed fleeing check decreases Range by one band.

A successful pursuing check decreases Range by one band.

A failed pursuing check increases Range by one band.

Apply any additional successes, Hidden status, penalties, or complications.

### Step 5: Resolve Other Actions

Characters not making the primary check may assist, attack, defend, or perform another appropriate supporting action.

Combat-related actions are limited as described later in this chapter.

### Step 6: Check for the End of the Chase

If Range reaches Engaged, the pursuers catch up.

If Range reaches Extreme, the prey escapes.

Otherwise, begin another Chase Round.

## Fleeing Actions

The fleeing party chooses Run, Hide, or Evade at the beginning of each Chase Round.

These actions represent the party's primary strategy for escaping.

|Fleeing Action|Strategy|Typical Pursuer Counter|
|---|---|---|
|**Run**|Gain distance through speed and endurance|Chase|
|**Hide**|Break contact through concealment or misdirection|Search|
|**Evade**|Prevent interception through unpredictable maneuvering|Cut Off|
The counters are guidelines rather than mandatory responses.

A pursuer may choose another strategy when the circumstances justify it.

### Run

Run represents an attempt to escape through raw speed, endurance, or sustained acceleration.

The fleeing character attempts to move faster than the pursuer and widen the gap.

#### Run Check

**On Foot or Riding:** Strength + Athletics

**Operating a Vehicle:** Dexterity + Vehicle

For a vehicle chase, the Game Master may allow Intelligence + Vehicle when route planning, navigation, or sustained technical control is more important than reflexes.

#### Opposition

Against an NPC pursuer:

- Use Physical Defense for a foot or mounted chase.
- Use an appropriate Vehicle Speed Rating for a vehicle chase.

Vehicle Speed is a relative performance rating, not an additional Attribute added to the player's dice pool.

When the pursuer has a significant Speed advantage, the Game Master may adjust the Difficulty or apply a modifier.

#### Run Results

|Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|Success|Increase Range by one band|
|Additional Successes|Gain +1 die on the character's next chase check|
|Failure|Decrease Range by one band|
|Critical Failure|Apply normal failure and –1 die on the next chase check; optional complication|
The additional-success bonus is awarded when the roll exceeds the required Difficulty by at least one success.

It is a single +1 die bonus, regardless of how many additional successes are rolled.

#### Examples of Run

|Movement Type|Examples|
|---|---|
|On Foot|Sprint through streets, dash across open ground, leap small obstacles without slowing|
|Riding Animal|Urge a horse into a gallop, follow a fast trail, clear fences and streams|
|Automobile|Accelerate along a highway, take the fastest route, exploit the vehicle's performance|
|Boat|Set full sail, use favorable currents, maintain maximum engine power|
|Aircraft|Use full throttle, afterburners, efficient headings, or rapid climbs and dives|
|Spacecraft|Maintain a sustained acceleration burn, use a favorable trajectory, exploit gravity assists|
##### Example: Running Through the Streets

A character flees from a police officer.

The starting Range is Short.

The character chooses Run and rolls Strength + Athletics against the officer's Physical Defense 3.

The player obtains 4 successes.

The action succeeds, increasing Range from Short to Medium.

Because the roll exceeds the Difficulty by one success, the character also gains +1 die on the next chase check.

### Hide

Hide represents an attempt to break line of sight or sensor contact and disappear from pursuit.

The fleeing character uses concealment, terrain, obstacles, crowds, darkness, or misdirection to avoid detection.

#### Hide Check

**On Foot or Riding:** Dexterity + Stealth

**Operating a Vehicle:** Dexterity + Vehicle

The Game Master may allow Intelligence + Vehicle when concealment depends primarily on navigation, sensor management, or knowledge of the environment.

#### Opposition

Against an NPC pursuer, use the pursuer's Mental Defense as the default Difficulty.

Modify the check when the environment makes hiding easier or harder.

Examples include:

- Crowded streets or dense forest: favorable conditions.
- Open fields or empty highways: unfavorable conditions.
- Fog, darkness, or heavy traffic: potential concealment.
- Radar, thermal imaging, tracking systems, or other sensors: potential detection advantages.

The Game Master should not apply both a concealment bonus and an equivalent opponent detection penalty for the same circumstance.

#### Hide Results

|Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|Success|Become Hidden and increase Range by one band|
|Additional Successes|Gain +1 die on the next chase check|
|Failure|Remain visible or detected; decrease Range by one band|
|Critical Failure|Apply normal failure and –1 die on the next chase check; optional complication|
#### Hidden

A Hidden fleeing party has temporarily broken direct contact with the pursuers.

While Hidden:

- The pursuers do not know the prey's precise location.
- The prey cannot normally be targeted by direct attacks without being located.
- The prey gains +1 die on its next Hide check.
- A successful Search action ends Hidden status.

Hidden does not automatically end the chase.

The prey must still reach Extreme Range or otherwise escape the encounter.

A fleeing character cannot make an attack during the same Chase Round in which they choose Hide.

Attacking while Hidden may reveal the character's position.

#### Examples of Hide

| Movement Type | Examples                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| On Foot       | Disappear into a crowd, enter a building, hide in vegetation, use rooftops or sewers            |
| Riding Animal | Enter dense forest, move behind hills, leave a trail, hide beneath tree cover                   |
| Automobile    | Enter a parking garage, turn into side streets, blend into traffic, hide in a warehouse         |
| Boat          | Enter fog, hide behind islands, slip into a secluded cove, blend among anchored vessels         |
| Aircraft      | Enter cloud cover, fly below radar, descend into a canyon, blend into air traffic               |
| Spacecraft    | Shut down engines, reduce sensor emissions, hide behind an asteroid, blend into orbital traffic |
##### Example: Hiding in Traffic

A character drives a stolen automobile through a crowded city.

The starting Range is Medium.

The character chooses Hide and turns into a busy parking structure.

The Game Master calls for Dexterity + Vehicle against the pursuing officer's Mental Defense 3.

The player rolls 3 successes.

The character becomes Hidden and increases Range from Medium to Long.

If the pursuers successfully Search during a later Chase Round, they may locate the vehicle and resume direct pursuit.

### Evade

Evade represents an attempt to avoid interception through skillful, unpredictable movement.

Rather than relying on pure speed or concealment, the fleeing character uses obstacles, sudden turns, rapid changes of direction, or defensive maneuvers.

#### Evade Check

**On Foot or Riding:** Dexterity + Athletics

**Operating a Vehicle:** Dexterity + Vehicle

#### Opposition

Against an NPC pursuer:

- Use Physical Defense for foot or mounted pursuits.
- Use Vehicle Handling Rating for vehicle pursuits.

The Game Master may modify the Difficulty to account for terrain, relative Handling, and maneuverability.

#### Evade Results

|Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|Success|Increase Range by one band|
|Additional Successes|Gain +1 die on the next chase check|
|Failure|Decrease Range by one band|
|Critical Failure|Apply normal failure and –1 die on the next chase check; optional complication|
#### Examples of Evade

|Movement Type|Examples|
|---|---|
|On Foot|Zigzag through crowds, leap fences, vault walls, duck through narrow alleys|
|Riding Animal|Weave between trees, jump streams, negotiate rocky or wooded terrain|
|Automobile|Swerve through traffic, drift around corners, brake suddenly, jump curbs|
|Boat|Weave among reefs, sandbars, docks, and anchored ships; tack unpredictably|
|Aircraft|Bank sharply, skim terrain, perform sudden climbs, dives, and rolls|
|Spacecraft|Change flight vectors, use lateral thrusters, maneuver through debris, force an overshoot|
##### Example: Evading a Patrol Boat

A smuggler's boat is pursued through a narrow coastal channel.

The starting Range is Close.

The smuggler chooses Evade, steering between rocks and shallow water.

The player rolls Dexterity + Vehicle against the patrol boat's Handling Rating 3.

The roll produces 4 successes.

The smuggler increases Range from Close to Short and gains +1 die on the next chase check.

## Pursuing Actions

When player characters pursue an opponent, they choose an action appropriate to the fleeing opponent's strategy.

The three primary pursuing actions are Chase, Search, and Cut Off.

### Chase

Chase represents a direct attempt to catch the fleeing opponent through speed and endurance.

The pursuer follows the prey's route and attempts to reduce the distance.

#### Chase Check

**On Foot or Riding:** Strength + Athletics

**Operating a Vehicle:** Dexterity + Vehicle

The Game Master may allow Intelligence + Vehicle when navigation or sustained control is more important than reflexes.

#### Opposition

Against an NPC prey:

- Use the prey's Physical Defense for a foot or mounted chase.
- Use the prey's Vehicle Speed Rating for a vehicle chase.

Relative Speed may justify additional modifiers.

#### Chase Results

|Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|Success|Decrease Range by one band|
|Additional Successes|Gain +1 die on the next chase check|
|Failure|Increase Range by one band|
|Critical Failure|Apply normal failure and –1 die on the next chase check; optional complication|

##### Example: Pursuing a Fleeing Thief

A character chases a thief through a marketplace.

The starting Range is Medium.

The character chooses Chase and rolls Strength + Athletics against the thief's Physical Defense 2.

The player obtains 3 successes.

Range decreases from Medium to Short.

The additional success grants +1 die on the next chase check.

### Search

Search represents an attempt to locate a fleeing opponent who has broken line of sight, concealed their position, or become Hidden.

The pursuer relies on observation, tracking, investigation, or technology.

#### Search Check

Choose the most appropriate combination:

- Acuity + Perception.
- Intelligence + Investigation.
- Acuity + Survival.
- Intelligence + Technology, when using sensors or specialized detection equipment.

#### Opposition

Against an NPC prey, use an appropriate Hide Rating.

**Recommended default:** Use the prey's Mental Defense as its Hide Rating unless its profile or the situation provides a more appropriate value.

The Game Master may modify the Difficulty based on the prey's Size, concealment, terrain, and available sensors.

#### Search Results

|Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|Success|Locate the prey, end Hidden status, and decrease Range by one band|
|Additional Successes|Gain +1 die on the next chase check|
|Failure|The prey remains Hidden; increase Range by one band|
|Critical Failure|Apply normal failure and –1 die on the next chase check; optional complication|
If the prey is not Hidden, Search may still be used to regain contact after a temporary loss of sight or sensor tracking.

##### Example: Searching for a Hidden Spacecraft

A patrol spacecraft loses sensor contact with a smuggler hiding among asteroids.

The pursuing pilot chooses Search.

The player rolls Intelligence + Technology against the smuggler's Hide Rating 3.

The roll produces 3 successes.

The patrol reacquires the smuggler, ends Hidden status, and decreases Range from Long to Medium.

### Cut Off

Cut Off represents an attempt to anticipate the prey's route and intercept it.

Instead of following directly behind, the pursuer takes a shortcut, predicts a turn, or maneuvers into a position that blocks the escape route.

#### Cut Off Check

Choose the most appropriate combination:

**On Foot or Riding:**

- Intelligence + Athletics, when planning and executing a shortcut.
- Acuity + Perception, when anticipating the prey's movement.

**Operating a Vehicle:**

- Intelligence + Vehicle, when plotting an interception route.
- Dexterity + Vehicle, when the interception depends on rapid maneuvering.

#### Opposition

Against an NPC prey:

- Use Physical Defense for a foot or mounted chase.
- Use Vehicle Handling Rating for a vehicle chase.

#### Cut Off Results

|Result|Effect|
|---|---|
|Success|Decrease Range by one band|
|Additional Successes|Gain +1 die on the next chase check|
|Failure|Increase Range by one band|
|Critical Failure|Apply normal failure and –1 die on the next chase check; optional complication|
##### Example: Cutting Off a Fleeing Automobile

A character pursues a criminal through a city.

The starting Range is Short.

The criminal turns toward a highway entrance.

The pursuing driver knows a shortcut and chooses Cut Off.

The player rolls Intelligence + Vehicle against the criminal's Vehicle Handling Rating 3.

The roll produces 3 successes.

The pursuer reaches the intersection first and decreases Range from Short to Close.

## Chase Action Summary

|Action|Primary Roll|Opposition|Success|Failure|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Run (Fleeing)|STR + Athletics or DEX + Vehicle|Physical Defense or Vehicle Speed|Range +1|Range –1|
|Hide (Fleeing)|DEX + Stealth or DEX + Vehicle|Mental Defense or appropriate detection rating|Hidden; Range +1|Not Hidden; Range –1|
|Evade (Fleeing)|DEX + Athletics or DEX + Vehicle|Physical Defense or Vehicle Handling|Range +1|Range –1|
|Chase (Pursuing)|STR + Athletics or DEX + Vehicle|Prey's Physical Defense or Vehicle Speed|Range –1|Range +1|
|Search (Pursuing)|ACT + Perception, INT + Investigation, ACT + Survival, or INT + Technology|Prey's Hide Rating|Locate prey; Range –1|Prey remains Hidden; Range +1|
|Cut Off (Pursuing)|INT + Athletics, ACT + Perception, INT + Vehicle, or DEX + Vehicle|Physical Defense or Vehicle Handling|Range –1|Range +1|
**Additional Successes:** If the roll exceeds the required successes, gain +1 die on the next chase check.

**Critical Failure:** Apply the normal failed Range change and –1 die on the next chase check. The Game Master may also introduce a complication.

Bonuses and penalties from chase results apply to the character's next primary chase check. They do not automatically apply to unrelated combat or social actions.

## Optional Failure and Complications

Chases are dangerous because participants move at high speed while navigating unpredictable environments.

The Game Master may introduce complications when a character fails a chase check.

These complications should reflect the movement type and circumstances.

Ordinary failure does not automatically cause a crash, fall, or collision.

### Example Complications

|Chase Type|Possible Complications|
|---|---|
|Foot|Become winded, stumble, pull a muscle, lose footing|
|Riding Animal|Mount becomes exhausted, stumbles, refuses to continue, or loses control|
|Automobile|Tires lose traction, engine overheats, vehicle fishtails, transmission strains|
|Boat|Engine overheats, sail tears, propeller cavitates, vessel takes on water|
|Aircraft|Engine temperature rises, aircraft approaches a stall, structural stress increases|
|Spacecraft|Thruster imbalance, overheating engines, excessive fuel use, reactor warning|
### Critical Failure

A Critical Failure may create a major complication.

Examples include:

- Falling Prone.
- Losing control of a mount.
- Striking an obstacle.
- Crashing a vehicle.
- Becoming entangled.
- Suffering collision damage.
- Damaging propulsion or steering systems.

When a complication causes injury or vehicle damage, resolve it using the appropriate rules in Chapters 5, 6, or 8.

A Critical Failure should not automatically destroy a vehicle or kill a character unless the danger clearly warrants that outcome.

## Chase and Ranged Attacks

Characters and opponents may attempt ranged attacks during a chase.

However, combat is more difficult while moving rapidly through a changing environment.

Unlike normal combat, each participant may take only **one combat-related Action per Chase Round**.

A character may make an Attack as their combat-related Action or use an appropriate Defense Reaction when attacked.

### Ranged Attack Penalties

Characters or opponents performing aggressive chase maneuvers suffer **–2 dice on Ranged Attack Rolls**.

This applies while:

- Running.
- Chasing.
- Evading.
- Cutting Off.

For an NPC opponent, reduce its static Ranged Attack Rating by 2 rather than rolling dice.

The minimum effective Attack Rating should follow the game's general rules for modified opponent ratings.

### Hiding and Attacking

A character choosing Hide cannot also attack during that Chase Round.

This represents the need to concentrate on concealment rather than revealing their position.

A Hidden character who attacks in a later Round may reveal their location.

### Defending During a Chase

A character targeted by an attack may use the normal Defense rules.

Appropriate cover, concealment, movement, and other circumstances may modify the Defense Roll.

Do not apply the same movement-related modifier twice.

### Chase Range and Weapon Range

Chase Range Bands describe the relative distance between the parties.

They do not automatically correspond to the fixed weapon ranges in Chapter 3 or Section 5.3.

The Game Master determines whether a weapon can reach its target based on the actual circumstances.

For example, Short Range in a foot chase may be within pistol range, while Short Range in a spacecraft chase may represent several kilometers.

## Multiple Characters in a Chase

Chases often involve several player characters.

The Game Master should choose a procedure that keeps all players involved without requiring excessive dice rolls.

## Foot Chases

### Option 1: Primary Character and Assistant

One character makes the primary chase check.

One other character may assist, describing how they contribute.

If the assistance is appropriate, the primary character gains **+1 die**.

The Game Master may rotate the primary character between Chase Rounds.

This is the recommended default because it keeps the chase fast and gives different characters opportunities to contribute.

### Option 2: Group Chase Check

Each participating character makes an appropriate chase check.

Compare the number of successful checks with the number of failed checks.

If successful checks outnumber failed checks, the group succeeds and Range changes in its favor.

Otherwise, the group fails and Range changes against it.

Ties count as failure unless the Game Master establishes a different rule before rolling.

This method is useful when every character's performance matters, such as a group fleeing together on foot.

## Vehicle Chases

### Separate Vehicles

If each character controls a separate mount or vehicle, the Game Master may use the Foot Chase group-check procedure.

Alternatively, the Game Master may track each vehicle separately when their routes or positions become significantly different.

### Shared Vehicle

When several characters occupy one vehicle:

- One character serves as the primary driver, rider, or pilot.
- That character makes the primary chase check.
- One other character may assist, granting +1 die when appropriate.
- Other passengers may perform supporting actions.

Supporting actions may include:

- Firing weapons.
- Repairing damage.
- Operating sensors.
- Navigating.
- Treating injuries.
- Preparing equipment.
- Assisting another passenger.

Each character remains subject to the limit of one combat-related Action per Chase Round.

## Ending a Chase

A chase ends when one of the following occurs.

### The Prey Escapes

Range reaches Extreme.

The fleeing party has successfully escaped the immediate pursuit.

### The Pursuers Catch Up

Range reaches Engaged.

The pursuit phase ends, and the parties resolve capture, combat, boarding, ramming, or other close interaction.

### The Prey Surrenders

The fleeing party stops resisting pursuit.

### The Pursuers Abandon the Chase

The pursuing party decides to stop.

### Circumstances End the Pursuit

The chase becomes impossible or irrelevant because of terrain, damage, environmental hazards, loss of transportation, or another event.

The Game Master narrates the conclusion based on the circumstances.

## Game Master Guidance

**Keep Chases Moving**

- A chase should feel urgent.
- Describe the environment, ask for the fleeing party's action, establish the pursuer's response, and resolve the primary check.
- Avoid slowing the encounter with unnecessary calculations.

**Make Terrain Matter**

- Different environments should encourage different strategies.
- Crowded city streets favor concealment and sudden turns.
- Open highways favor speed.
- Forests and mountain trails favor skilled maneuvering.
- Asteroid fields favor careful piloting and sensor management.

**Reward Creative Actions**

Players may attempt actions beyond Run, Hide, Evade, Chase, Search, and Cut Off.

Examples include:

- Knocking over obstacles.
- Creating distractions.
- Using shortcuts.
- Disabling a pursuing vehicle.
- Blocking a roadway.
- Changing transportation.
- Luring pursuers into hazards.

The Game Master should resolve these actions using appropriate Skill Checks and apply reasonable effects.

**Preserve Player-Facing Rolls**

- Players roll when their characters are fleeing or pursuing.
- NPC opponents provide static opposition ratings.
- The Game Master does not need to make a separate roll for each NPC.

**Use Complications Sparingly**

- Ordinary failures already change Range.
- Additional complications should make the chase more exciting, not punish every unsuccessful roll with an injury or crash.

**End the Chase Decisively**

- When Range reaches Engaged or Extreme, conclude the pursuit phase.
- Do not continue rolling unless a new pursuit begins or another clear challenge remains.

**Chases should be fast, flexible, and cinematic, allowing the same rules to support pursuits across any era or genre.**
