# Damage & Wounds

Combat is dangerous. Even a single successful attack can leave lasting injuries that affect a character long after the fighting has ended.

Rather than tracking large pools of Hit Points, _Journey Through the Ages_ uses a Wound-based damage system.

Every damaging attack can inflict a **Minor, Serious, Critical, or Lethal Wound**. As injuries accumulate, characters become less effective, suffer additional complications, and may eventually face death.

This system emphasizes the consequences of violence while encouraging tactical decision-making, teamwork, and medical treatment.

## Wound Severity

There are four levels of Wounds.

|Severity|Description|
|---|---|
|**Minor**|Painful but manageable injury. The character can generally continue acting.|
|**Serious**|Significant injury that may interfere with movement, combat, or other activities.|
|**Critical**|Severe injury that may disable a limb, incapacitate the character, or require urgent treatment.|
|**Lethal**|Life-threatening injury that immediately causes the Dying Condition.|

Wounds are recorded on the character's Injury Tracker.

A character's Wound capacity is determined by the following slots.

## Injury Tracker

|Wound Severity|Available Slots|
|---|---|
|Minor|Equal to Resilience|
|Serious|2|
|Critical|2|
|Lethal|1|

For example, a character with Resilience 3 has three Minor Wound slots, two Serious Wound slots, two Critical Wound slots, and one Lethal Wound slot.

Each Wound occupies one slot of the appropriate severity.

The number of successes on a Damage Roll or the shortfall on a Defense Roll determines the severity of the Wound, not the number of slots filled.

## Determining Damage

Damage is resolved differently depending on whether a player character attacks an opponent or defends against an incoming attack.

Both methods produce the same four Wound severities.

### Damage Against Opponents

When a player character successfully attacks an opponent, the player makes a **Damage Roll** to determine the severity of the injury.

The Damage dice pool is:

**Damage Dice = Weapon Damage Dice + Extra Attack Successes**

Where:

- **Weapon Damage Dice** are the damage dice provided by the weapon.
- **Extra Attack Successes** are the number of Attack successes exceeding the opponent's Defense Rating.

An Attack that exactly meets the opponent's Defense Rating hits but gains no extra Damage dice from Attack successes.

#### Making the Damage Roll

Roll the Damage dice pool.

Each die showing **5 or 6** counts as one Damage success.

Compare the number of Damage successes with the following table.

|Damage Successes|Wound Inflicted|
|---|---|
|0|No Wound|
|1|Minor Wound|
|2|Serious Wound|
|3|Critical Wound|
|4 or more|Lethal Wound|

A successful Attack followed by zero Damage successes is a glancing blow, ineffective impact, or attack that fails to cause a meaningful injury.

The Attack still hits, but it does not inflict a Wound.

A single Damage Roll normally inflicts **one Wound**, regardless of the number of Damage successes.

The severity of that Wound is determined by the final number of successes.

#### Push Your Luck

**Players cannot Push Your Luck on Damage Rolls.**

Push Your Luck may be used on the preceding Attack Roll if otherwise permitted.

Any additional Attack successes obtained through Push Your Luck are included when calculating the Damage dice pool.

However, the Damage Roll itself cannot be pushed or rerolled using that rule.

##### Example: Jojo Attacks a Mugger

Jojo strikes a mugger with a club.

His Attack exceeds the mugger's Defense Rating by 2 successes.

The club provides 1 Weapon Damage die.

Jojo's Damage dice pool is:

| Source                 | Dice  |
| ---------------------- | ----- |
| Club Damage            | +3    |
| Extra Attack Successes | +2    |
| **Total**              | **5** |

Jojo rolls:

**2, 3, 5, 5, 6**

The roll produces 3 Damage successes.

The mugger suffers a **Critical Wound**.

The attack strikes the mugger's left arm.

The Game Master records:

**Critical Wound — Left Arm (Disabled)**

The mugger also gains the **Disabled (Arm)** Condition, along with any additional applicable injury complications.

### Damage Against Player Characters

When an opponent attacks a player character, the player makes a **Defense Roll** rather than the Game Master making an Attack Roll.

The opponent's Attack Rating determines how difficult the attack is to avoid.

If the player rolls enough Defense successes, the character avoids the attack and suffers no Wound.

If the Defense fails, the severity of the resulting Wound is determined by the number of missing successes.

**Defense Shortfall = Opponent Attack Rating – Defense Successes**

|Defense Shortfall|Wound Inflicted|
|---|---|
|0 or fewer|No Wound|
|1|Minor Wound|
|2|Serious Wound|
|3|Critical Wound|
|4 or more|Lethal Wound|

A failed Defense normally inflicts one Wound.

The shortfall determines the Wound's severity.

The opponent's Attack Rating should reflect the effectiveness of the attack, including the weapon and other relevant circumstances.

#### Example: Defending Against a Shotgun

A gangster fires a shotgun at Suki.

The gangster's Ranged Attack Rating is 4.

Suki rolls her Defense dice pool and obtains 2 successes.

The shortfall is:

**4 – 2 = 2**

Suki suffers a **Serious Wound**.

The Game Master describes the injury and records it on Suki's Injury Tracker.

#### One Attack, One Damage Procedure

Do not use both a Defense Shortfall and a separate Damage Roll for the same ordinary attack.

Use:

- **Attack Roll followed by Damage Roll** when a player character attacks an opponent.
- **Defense Roll followed by Defense Shortfall** when an opponent attacks a player character.

A special weapon, hazard, or ability may establish a different procedure.

## Receiving Wounds

Whenever a character or opponent suffers a Wound, record it in an available slot of the appropriate severity.

For Serious and Critical Wounds, also record a brief description of the injury.

Examples include:

- Gunshot wound to the right leg.
- Broken left arm.
- Deep sword slash across the chest.
- Burned right hand.
- Shrapnel wound to the abdomen.

The description should identify the injury's location and any important details.

These descriptions help determine the effects of injuries, medical treatment, recovery, and lasting consequences.

### Minor Wounds

Minor Wounds represent bruises, cuts, scrapes, and other relatively manageable injuries.

### Serious Wounds

Serious Wounds represent substantial injuries that interfere with a character's activities.

Examples include:

- Deep cuts.
- Significant bruising or blunt-force trauma.
- Moderate burns.
- Painful sprains.
- Non-disabling fractures.
- Gunshot wounds that do not immediately threaten life.

### Critical Wounds

Critical Wounds represent severe injuries that may disable a limb, cause substantial blood loss, or otherwise threaten the character's ability to function.

Examples include:

- Major fractures.
- Severe burns.
- Deep penetrating wounds.
- Extensive tissue damage.
- Serious internal injuries.
- Injuries causing significant loss of mobility.

### Lethal Wounds

Lethal Wounds are immediately life-threatening.

Examples include:

- Severe gunshot wounds.
- Major internal bleeding.
- Extensive burns.
- Severe head trauma.
- Massive crushing injuries.
- Other potentially fatal trauma.

A character who suffers a Lethal Wound gains the **Dying Condition**.

## Upgrading Wounds

Characters and opponents have a limited number of Wound slots at each severity.

If no empty slot is available for an incoming Wound, its severity immediately increases by one level.

Continue increasing the severity until an available slot is found. The incoming Wound occupies that slot.

Previously recorded Wounds do not move.

### Wound Escalation

|Incoming Wound|If All Slots Are Full|
|---|---|
|Minor|Becomes Serious|
|Serious|Becomes Critical|
|Critical|Becomes Lethal|
|Lethal|See Lethal Overflow|

This escalation represents the cumulative effects of repeated injuries rather than a single exceptionally devastating blow.

#### Example: Accumulated Minor Wounds

A character has Resilience 2 and therefore has two Minor Wound slots.

Both slots are already filled.

The character suffers another Minor Wound.

Because no Minor slot is available, the new Wound becomes a Serious Wound.

The Serious Wound is recorded in an available Serious slot.

### Lethal Overflow

A character who suffers a Wound that must occupy a Lethal slot when that slot is already filled has exceeded their normal capacity to survive injury.

**Recommended rule:** The character dies unless a specific Edge, ability, or campaign rule prevents death.

The Game Master may allow a final narrative action when appropriate to the campaign.

This is an explicit addition to resolve a gap in the original rules.

## Injury Complications

Some injuries create problems beyond the Wound itself.

These effects are called **Complications**.

Complications may include:

- Bleeding.
- Disabled limbs.
- Embedded objects.
- Burns.
- Poisoning.
- Infection.
- Impaired movement.
- Loss of consciousness.

Some Complications impose defined Conditions from Chapter 9.

Others may be determined by the Game Master based on the injury.

A Condition remains in effect until removed according to its description, medical treatment, recovery, or another applicable rule.

### Wound Location and Condition Table

The following table provides default Conditions for Serious, Critical, and Lethal Wounds according to injury location.

|Location|Serious Wound|Critical Wound|Lethal Wound|
|---|---|---|---|
|Head|Dazed|Stunned|Dying + Unconscious|
|Upper Torso|Bleeding|Stunned + Bleeding|Dying|
|Lower Torso|Bleeding|Stunned + Bleeding|Dying|
|Arm|Dazed|Disabled Arm + Bleeding|Dying + Disabled Arm|
|Leg|Slowed + Bleeding|Disabled Leg + Bleeding|Dying + Disabled Leg|
|Groin|Dazed + Bleeding|Stunned + Bleeding|Dying|

The Game Master should use this table as the default rather than automatically assigning Disabled to every Serious limb Wound.

Minor Wounds normally do not impose an additional Condition unless a weapon, hazard, special ability, or unusual circumstance states otherwise.

### Additional Injury Complications

Certain damage types may cause additional Conditions.

Examples include:

- **Critical bullet wounds:** Bleeding.
- **Serious or Critical fire damage:** Burning.
- **Serious head wounds:** Dazed.
- **Critical head wounds:** Stunned.
- **Lethal Wounds:** Dying.

When multiple rules would impose the same Condition, apply that Condition once unless its description explicitly permits multiple instances.

The Game Master may substitute or add a Condition when the nature of the injury clearly justifies it.

For example, a severe crushing injury may disable a limb even when the ordinary location table would impose a less restrictive effect.

## How Wounds Affect Characters

Wound severity represents the seriousness of an injury.

|Severity|Immediate Effect|General Recovery Outlook|
|---|---|---|
|Minor|Painful but manageable; character can usually continue acting|Often short-lived|
|Serious|Significant impairment; may require treatment|May affect the character for minutes or longer|
|Critical|Potentially disabling or incapacitating|May require extended recovery|
|Lethal|Immediately causes Dying|Potentially fatal without intervention|

These descriptions provide narrative guidance.

The actual duration of a Wound depends on the campaign's selected healing model and any medical treatment received.

## Stabilizing a Dying Character

A character may attempt to stabilize another Dying character with a **Hard (3 successes) Intelligence + Medicine Skill Check**.

The attempt requires an Action and the ability to provide meaningful medical assistance.

On success, the Dying character is stabilized. Stabilization prevents immediate death from the current Lethal Wound but does not remove the Wound. The character must still recover with appropriate Rest and Healing.

### Timing

A Dying character may be stabilized before their next Turn.

If stabilization has not occurred by then, the character must resolve their survival using one of the options below.

The Game Master should establish the campaign's approach to Lethal Wounds before play.

## Survival Options

When a character suffers a Lethal Wound and is not stabilized in time, the Game Master may choose one of three approaches.

These options support different campaign tones.

### Option 1: Blaze of Glory

This option emphasizes heroic sacrifice.

The character may perform **one final heroic action** before dying.

The action automatically succeeds. It represents a final sacrifice or desperate effort and cannot be interrupted.

The Game Master determines the narrative limits of the action based on the circumstances. After completing the action, the character dies.

This option is particularly appropriate for cinematic adventures in which a character's final moments should have dramatic significance.

### Option 2: Fight for Survival

This option provides an uncertain chance of survival.

At the beginning of each of the Dying character's Turns, the player makes a:

**Resilience + Acuity Saving Throw Difficulty: Normal (2 successes)**

Push Your Luck cannot be used on these Saving Throws.

Each successful Saving Throw counts as one survival success.

Each failed Saving Throw counts as one survival failure.

Track the cumulative results.

| Result                     | Outcome                                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 3 successful Saving Throws | Character survives but remains Unconscious |
| 3 failed Saving Throws     | Character dies                             |

Continue making Saving Throws each Round until one of these outcomes occurs.

A character who receives successful stabilization before the third failure stops making survival Saving Throws.

#### Additional Wounds 

An additional wound taken during the fight for survival count as a number of automatic failures based on the severity of the Wound.  for level of damage received. 

| Severity           | # of Failures |
| ------------------ | ------------- |
| Minor              | 1             |
| Serious            | 2             |
| Critical or Lethal | 3             |

#### Example: Fight for Survival

A character suffers a Lethal Wound.

The player chooses Fight for Survival.

On the first Turn, the character succeeds on the Saving Throw.

On the second Turn, the character fails.

On the third Turn, the character succeeds.

The character has two successes and one failure.

On the fourth Turn, the character succeeds again.

The character reaches three successful Saving Throws and survives but remains Unconscious.

The Lethal Wound remains and must be treated.

### Option 3: Lasting Consequences

This option allows survival at a permanent cost.

The character remains Unconscious until medical treatment arrives.

After recovering, the character gains an appropriate permanent Hindrance selected by the Game Master.

The Hindrance should reflect the nature of the injury.

Possible examples include:

- Arm Injury.
- Leg Injury.
- Fragile Physique.
- Reduced mobility.
- Chronic pain.
- Another appropriate lasting impairment.

The Game Master should select a consequence consistent with the established Hindrance rules.

This option is especially suitable for campaigns emphasizing lasting consequences without frequent character death.

## Non-Lethal Damage

Characters may intentionally attack without lethal force when using unarmed strikes or suitable blunt weapons.

The attacker must declare the intention to use non-lethal force before making the Attack Roll. The Game Master determines whether the weapon and circumstances permit a non-lethal attack.

Non-lethal attacks use the normal Attack and Damage procedures. However, if a non-lethal attack would inflict a **Lethal Wound**, the target instead becomes **Unconscious**. The target does not gain the Dying Condition from that attack.

An Unconscious character does not make survival Saving Throws unless later subjected to lethal damage.

### Recording Non-Lethal Incapacitation

**Recommended clarification:** Record the result as **Unconscious (Non-Lethal)** rather than filling the Lethal Wound slot.

Any other Wounds already inflicted remain recorded.

The Game Master determines when the character regains consciousness, considering the injury, treatment, and circumstances.

Non-lethal attacks are not guaranteed to be harmless.

A character who is already severely injured may still be endangered by additional trauma.

## Random Injury Location (Optional)

The Game Master may choose the location of a Wound based on the attack and circumstances.

Alternatively, the group may use random injury locations.

When this optional rule is used, roll **2d6** after determining the Wound's severity.

### Random Injury Location Table

|2d6|Location|
|---|---|
|2|Head|
|3|Left Arm|
|4|Right Arm|
|5–7|Upper Torso|
|8–9|Lower Torso|
|10|Left Leg|
|11|Right Leg|
|12|Groin|

The result determines where the Wound occurs.

Apply the appropriate effects from the Wound Location and Condition Table.

The Game Master may substitute another location when the rolled result is inappropriate for the target's anatomy or the circumstances of the attack.

## Targeted Attacks

When a successful Attack specifically targets a body part using the Called Shot rules, use the declared location rather than rolling randomly.

### Example: Random Injury

A character suffers a Serious Wound from a pistol shot.

The group uses random injury locations. The player rolls 2d6 and obtains 10.

The Wound strikes the left leg. According to the Wound Location and Condition Table, the character gains **Slowed + Bleeding**.

The Game Master records the Serious Wound and its associated Conditions.

## Game Master Guidance

Damage and Wounds should make combat consequential without requiring excessive bookkeeping.

**Make Injuries Matter**

- A Wound should be more than a mark on the Injury Tracker.
- Describe injuries in ways that reflect the weapon, location, and severity.

**Apply Conditions Consistently**

- Use the Wound Location and Condition Table as the default.
- Consult Chapter 9 for the actual effects of Conditions.
- Avoid inventing additional penalties when an existing Condition already represents the injury.

**Respect the Difference Between Injury and Recovery**

- A character may recover from an immediate Condition before the underlying Wound fully heals.
- Likewise, a Wound may require continued treatment after the character becomes capable of acting again.

**Choose a Consistent Survival Tone**

- The three survival options create different expectations.
- Blaze of Glory favors heroic sacrifice.
- Fight for Survival emphasizes uncertainty and danger.
- Lasting Consequences emphasizes survival at a permanent cost.
- The Game Master should select the preferred approach before play whenever possible.

**Preserve Player-Facing Rolls**

- Players roll to attack opponents and determine damage.
- Players also roll to defend against incoming attacks.
- The Game Master establishes opponent ratings and describes the consequences.

**Encourage Medical Care**

- Characters who prepare medical supplies, develop the Medicine Skill, and protect injured companions should benefit from those decisions.
- Medical treatment should be an important part of surviving dangerous adventures.

**Damage & Wounds should reinforce the danger of combat, reward preparation and teamwork, and create lasting consequences without slowing the game.**
