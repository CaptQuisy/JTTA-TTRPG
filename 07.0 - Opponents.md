# Opponents

Every adventure presents characters with challenges to overcome. Some opponents can be reasoned with, others avoided, and some must be confronted directly.

Whether the characters face criminals, wild animals, soldiers, alien predators, robots, or supernatural creatures, opponents should be quick for the Game Master to create and easy to manage during play.

Unlike player characters, opponents do not require the complete character creation process. Instead, they use a simplified set of statistics representing their ability to resist attacks, threaten characters, and withstand injury.

This approach allows the Game Master to create memorable encounters across different historical periods and genres without managing extensive character sheets.

## Opponent Statistics

An opponent profile may include the following characteristics.

|Statistic|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Physical Defense|Resistance to physical attacks|
|Mental Defense|Resistance to mental and supernatural effects|
|Social Defense|Resistance to social influence|
|Close Combat Attack|Effectiveness of melee and natural attacks|
|Ranged Attack|Effectiveness of firearms, projectiles, and other ranged attacks|
|Size|Physical size and targeting modifiers|
|Speed|Movement capability|
|Weapons|Equipment and natural attacks|
|Wounds|Capacity to withstand injuries|
|Special Abilities|Unique traits and combat options|
Not every opponent needs every statistic.

For example, a wolf may not need Social Defense, while an ordinary unarmed civilian may not need Ranged Attack.

An omitted Attack Rating means the opponent does not ordinarily possess that attack. A Defense Rating listed as **N/A** means the corresponding type of influence is not normally applicable. It does not represent a Defense Rating of zero.

The Game Master may allow an unusual ability to affect such an opponent when the circumstances justify it.

### Opponent Defense Ratings

Opponents use three Defense Ratings.

These are static values. The Game Master does not roll dice to defend an opponent.

When a player character attempts an appropriate action against an opponent, the player rolls the relevant dice pool and compares the successes with the opponent's Defense Rating.

#### Physical Defense

Physical Defense represents the opponent's ability to avoid or withstand physical attacks.

It is used against:

- Close Combat attacks.
- Ranged attacks.
- Thrown weapons.
- Other physical attacks.

Physical Defense may reflect agility, combat training, natural armor, size, or other physical protection.

#### Mental Defense

Mental Defense represents the opponent's resistance to mental or supernatural influence.

It may be used against:

- Fear.
- Psionic attacks.
- Mental domination.
- Illusions that directly affect the mind.
- Other mental effects.

Mental Defense should be used only when the ability specifically targets the opponent's mind or mental resistance.

#### Social Defense

Social Defense represents the opponent's resistance to social influence.

It may be used against:

- Persuasion.
- Deception.
- Intimidation.
- Negotiation.
- Interrogation.
- Manipulation.

Social Defense does not make an opponent immune to conversation or roleplaying.

The Game Master should consider the opponent's motivations, circumstances, and willingness to cooperate.

#### Defense Rating Guidelines

|Defense Rating|Difficulty|Typical Opposition|
|---|---|---|
|1|Easy|Vulnerable or poorly prepared|
|2|Normal|Ordinary or lightly trained|
|3|Hard|Experienced or well protected|
|4|Extreme|Highly trained or exceptionally capable|
|5|Legendary|Elite or extraordinary|
|6+|Exceptional|Extremely powerful or unusual|
These ratings correspond to the number of successes required on the player's roll.

A higher rating does not necessarily mean an opponent is physically larger or stronger.

For example, a small, highly agile opponent may have greater Physical Defense than a large, slow creature.

### Opponent Attack Ratings

Opponents normally have two Attack Ratings.

**Close Combat Attack:** Used for punches, kicks, bites, claws, swords, clubs, and other attacks at close range.

**Ranged Attack:** Used for firearms, bows, thrown weapons, energy weapons, and other attacks at a distance.

Unlike player characters, opponents do not ordinarily make Attack Rolls.

Instead, the player makes a Defense Roll against the opponent's Attack Rating.

The Attack Rating represents the effectiveness of the incoming attack.

#### Attack Rating Guidelines

|Attack Rating|Threat Level|Description|
|---|---|---|
|2|Easy|Unskilled or poorly executed attack|
|3|Normal|Competent or trained attack|
|4|Hard|Dangerous attack by a skilled opponent|
|5|Extreme|Highly effective attack|
|6+|Exceptional|Extraordinary or exceptionally dangerous attack|

The Game Master may modify an opponent's Attack Rating when circumstances justify it.

For example, an opponent may gain +1 Close Combat Attack when supported by allies or suffer –1 Ranged Attack when firing under difficult conditions.

These modifiers change the target number of the player's Defense Roll.

#### Resolving Opponent Attacks

When an opponent attacks a player character:

1. The Game Master declares the attack and identifies the target.
2. The player describes the character's defensive response.
3. The player builds an appropriate Defense dice pool.
4. The player rolls the dice.
5. Compare the Defense successes with the opponent's Attack Rating.
6. If the Defense succeeds, the character avoids the attack.
7. If the Defense fails, determine the Wound severity using the Defense Shortfall rules.

**Defense Shortfall = Opponent Attack Rating – Defense Successes**

| Shortfall | Result         |
| --------- | -------------- |
| 0 or less | No Wound       |
| 1         | Minor Wound    |
| 2         | Serious Wound  |
| 3         | Critical Wound |
| 4 or more | Lethal Wound   |
##### Example: Street Thug Attack

A Street Thug attacks a character with a club.

The Thug has Close Combat Attack 3.

The player rolls Strength + Close Combat and obtains 2 successes.

The shortfall is:

**3 – 2 = 1**

The character suffers a Minor Wound.

### Opponent Size

Size represents the physical dimensions of an opponent.

Size may affect targeting, movement, available space, and interactions with the environment.

#### Opponent Size Table

|Size|Typical Height|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|Tiny|Under 2 ft|Rat, raven, house cat, monkey, drone, small robot|
|Small|2–4 ft|Child, medium dog, wolf, alien scout|
|Normal|4–8 ft|Human, zombie, large dog, average robot, motorcycle|
|Large|8–15 ft|Horse, bear, elephant, Xenomorph, small vehicle|
|Giant|15–40 ft|Giant, Tyrannosaurus, tank, helicopter|
|Huge|Over 40 ft|Sailing ship, blue whale, passenger jet, spacecraft, colossal alien|
These categories are approximate.

Some examples represent overall length or physical footprint rather than height.

The Game Master may assign a Size category based on the opponent's overall dimensions.

Use the Size modifiers in Section 5.3, _Ranged Combat_, when applicable.

Do not apply a Size modifier twice for the same attack.

### Opponent Speed

Speed determines how quickly an opponent can move.

Human-sized opponents normally use the same movement rules as player characters.

The Speed Rating provides a relative measure of movement capability.

#### Speed Ratings

|Rating|Category|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|0|Stationary|Immobile or not moving|
|1|Slow|Fast walker, heavily laden runner, camel, draft horse, cargo truck, armored vehicle, fishing boat, trawler, cargo plane, dirigible, cargo shuttle|
|2|Average|Running human, riding horse, family sedan, police cruiser, sailboat, patrol boat, light aircraft, helicopter, civilian shuttle|
|3|Fast|Olympic sprinter, racehorse, sports car, motorcycle, speedboat, fighter aircraft, military shuttle, light interceptor|
|4|Very Fast|Superhuman speedster, griffon, dire wolf, Formula racing car, hydrofoil, racing yacht, supersonic jet, interceptor spacecraft|
|5|Extreme|Teleporting speedster, legendary magical mount, high-speed train, experimental race vehicle, hypersonic aircraft, missile, military strike craft, courier vessel|
These ratings are relative to the type of movement and vehicle.

A Fast human is not necessarily faster than an Average aircraft.

For tactical encounters, the opponent's profile should also list its movement distance when it differs from the standard 30 feet per Move Action.

For example:

**Speed: Fast (40 ft)**

### Opponent Weapons

An opponent profile lists its commonly carried weapons and natural attacks.

Examples include:

- Club.
- Knife.
- Revolver.
- Military rifle.
- Claws.
- Bite.
- Energy weapon.
- Psionic blast.

Weapon entries may identify special effects, range limitations, Conditions, or other relevant characteristics.

For ordinary attacks, the opponent's weapon effectiveness is already represented by its Attack Rating. Do not automatically add a weapon's player-character damage dice to an opponent's Attack Rating.

The Game Master may assign different Attack Ratings to different weapons when appropriate.

Special attacks may use their own rules.

### Opponent Wounds

Opponents use the same four Wound severities as player characters.

- Minor.
- Serious.
- Critical.
- Lethal.

However, opponents do not need the same number of Wound slots as player characters.

Their Wound capacity reflects their role, resilience, and importance in the encounter.

#### Suggested Wound Profiles

|Opponent Type|Minor|Serious|Critical|Lethal|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Fragile|1|0|0|1|
|Minor|2|1|1|1|
|Standard|2–3|2|1|1|
|Elite|3|2|2|1|
|Major|4+|3+|2+|1+|
These are suggested profiles, not mandatory construction rules.

An opponent with no slots in a Wound category treats that category as full.

For example, a rat with one Minor slot and one Lethal slot has no Serious or Critical slots.

Once its Minor slot is full, another Minor Wound escalates through the unavailable Serious and Critical categories to Lethal.

### Defeating Opponents

An opponent is defeated when it dies, becomes incapacitated, surrenders, flees, or is otherwise removed from the encounter.

Not every defeated opponent must die. The Game Master should consider the opponent's nature, motivations, and circumstances.

Ordinary opponents may surrender or flee after suffering significant injuries.

Fanatical, mechanical, or supernatural opponents may continue fighting despite severe damage.

### Opponent Actions

Opponents act during the Opponent Phase of combat unless another rule changes the order.

They may move, attack, interact with the environment, and use Special Abilities.

The Game Master does not normally roll dice for opponent actions. Instead, players make the appropriate Defense Rolls, Saving Throws, or other checks.

#### Simplified Opponent Turn

For streamlined play, an ordinary opponent may:

- Move up to its listed movement distance.
- Make one Attack or use one appropriate Special Ability.
- Perform reasonable minor interactions.

This is a **recommended simplified procedure** rather than a complete three-Action-Point character turn.

The Game Master may use the full Action Point system for important opponents when greater tactical detail is needed.

Special Abilities may grant additional attacks, movement, or Reactions.

### Opponent Reactions

Opponents may have Reactions that improve their Defense, reposition them, or activate a Special Ability.

A Reaction that increases an opponent's Defense Rating changes the target number for the player's Attack Roll.

For example, an opponent with Physical Defense 3 that gains +2 Physical Defense against one attack has Physical Defense 5 for that attack.

The Game Master does not roll additional Defense dice.

## Special Abilities

Special Abilities distinguish one opponent from another.

Examples include:

- Natural armor.
- Poison or disease.
- Regeneration.
- Grappling attacks.
- Special movement.
- Psionic powers.
- Technomage abilities.
- Unique Reactions.
- Environmental adaptations.
- Leadership abilities.

Whenever possible, Special Abilities should reference existing rules rather than introduce a separate resolution system.

### Writing Special Abilities

A Special Ability should identify:

1. **Trigger:** When the ability can be used.
2. **Action Cost:** Whether it requires an Action, Reaction, or no additional Action.
3. **Target and Range:** Who or what it affects.
4. **Resolution:** Which roll, rating, or Difficulty applies.
5. **Effect:** What happens on success or failure.
6. **Duration:** How long the effect lasts.
7. **Frequency:** Whether it is limited per Turn, Round, or scene.

For example:

**Venomous Bite:** When the opponent inflicts a Wound with its Bite, the target makes a Resilience + Strength Saving Throw, Difficulty Normal (2). On failure, the target becomes Poisoned. The Condition lasts according to the venom's description.

This format gives the Game Master enough information to use the ability without inventing missing mechanics during combat.

### Opponents and Conditions

Opponents use fixed **Attack, Defense, and Skill Ratings** rather than rolling dice. These ratings remain unchanged when an Opponent is affected by a positive or negative Condition.

Instead, apply the Condition's modifier to the **player character's Attack, Defense, or Skill Check dice pool**, as appropriate. A Condition that benefits an Opponent imposes an equivalent penalty on the character's roll, while a Condition that hinders an Opponent grants an equivalent bonus.

This keeps Opponent Ratings fixed and ensures that all Condition modifiers are applied to the player's dice rolls.

**Example: Blinded Opponent**

A German soldier has a Physical Defense Rating of 3 and a Ranged Attack Rating of 4.

The soldier becomes **Blinded**, which normally imposes a −2 dice penalty on attacks.

- **When the soldier attacks:** His Ranged Attack Rating remains 4, but the defending player receives **+2 dice** on their Defense Roll.

- **When the soldier is attacked:** His Physical Defense Rating remains 3, but the attacking player receives the normal **+1 die** bonus for attacking a Blinded opponent.

The soldier's ratings never change. All modifiers are applied to the player's dice pools.

**Important:** Apply each Condition modifier only once. Do not modify an Opponent's rating and also adjust the player's dice pool for the same effect.
## Creating Opponents

Begin with the opponent's concept rather than its statistics.

Consider:

- What role does this opponent serve?
- How dangerous should it be?
- What makes it memorable?
- Does it fight alone or as part of a group?
- Does defeating it require a particular tactic?
- Is it intended to threaten, delay, negotiate with, or defeat the characters?

Once the concept is established, assign the statistics needed for that role.

### Step 1: Choose the Opponent's Role

Common roles include:

**Minor Opponent:** A disposable threat that is dangerous mainly in groups.

**Standard Opponent:** A competent adversary suitable for ordinary encounters.

**Elite Opponent:** A skilled or resilient adversary that can challenge several characters.

**Major Opponent:** An important villain, powerful creature, or other exceptional threat.

### Step 2: Assign Defense Ratings

Determine Physical, Mental, and Social Defense based on the opponent's capabilities.

Most ordinary opponents should have ratings between 1 and 3.

Ratings of 4 or higher should represent significant advantages or unusual abilities.

### Step 3: Assign Attack Ratings

Choose Close Combat and Ranged Attack Ratings appropriate to the opponent's weapons, training, and threat level.

Avoid making every opponent equally effective at every type of attack.

### Step 4: Choose Size and Speed

Use the Size and Speed tables as guidelines.

List movement in feet when it differs from the standard movement distance.

### Step 5: Assign Wounds

Choose an appropriate Wound profile.

Fragile opponents should be easy to defeat.

Important or exceptionally durable opponents may have additional Wound slots.

### Step 6: Add Special Abilities

Special Abilities should make the opponent distinctive.

As a general guideline:

|Opponent Type|Suggested Special Abilities|
|---|---|
|Ordinary|1|
|Trained or Dangerous|2|
|Major or Monstrous|3 or more|
An opponent does not need more Special Abilities merely because it has higher Defense Ratings.

A single memorable ability may be more effective than several minor bonuses.

## Example Opponents

The following opponents demonstrate how the streamlined format can represent historical, pulp, western, horror, modern, post-apocalyptic, and science-fiction threats.

The profiles retain the original ratings and Wound capacities. Where an original Special Ability omitted a Difficulty or action cost, the recommended clarification is included.

### Alien Drone

_A fast extraterrestrial combat organism or biomechanical scout._

|Statistic|Rating|
|---|---|
|Physical Defense|3|
|Mental Defense|2|
|Social Defense|N/A|
|Close Combat Attack|3 — Claws|
|Ranged Attack|4 — Energy Weapon|
|Size|Small|
|Speed|Fast (40 ft)|
|Wounds|Minor ×2, Serious ×2, Critical ×1, Lethal ×1|
**Special Abilities**

**Wall Climber:** The Drone moves across walls and ceilings without treating them as Difficult Terrain.

**Energy Burst:** Once per scene, the Drone attacks every target within Close range. Each target makes a Defense Roll against Ranged Attack Rating 3, reflecting the original –1 Attack penalty.

**Self-Destruct:** When the Drone suffers a Lethal Wound, characters within Close range must make a Dexterity + Athletics Saving Throw. Recommended Difficulty: Normal (2). On failure, they suffer a Minor Wound and become Prone. This is a special fixed-damage effect rather than the normal Saving Throw damage calculation.

## Game Master Guidance

**Make Opponents Distinctive**

- Opponents should behave differently, not merely have different numerical ratings.
- A Street Thug becomes more dangerous when fighting alongside allies.
- A Trained Soldier uses coordinated fire.
- A Vampire uses control and regeneration.
- A Werewolf relies on overwhelming physical aggression.
- A Security Robot uses targeting systems and mechanical resilience.
- These differences create memorable encounters without requiring complicated statistics.

**Use Special Abilities to Define Behavior**

- Ordinary opponents generally need only one defining ability.
- More dangerous opponents may benefit from two.
- Major villains and monsters may have three or more.
- Abilities should encourage recognizable tactics.

**Consider Encounter Size**

- A group of weaker opponents can threaten characters through numbers, positioning, and coordinated attacks.
- A single powerful opponent may need additional durability, movement, or special attacks to remain challenging.
- Avoid increasing every rating merely to make an encounter harder.

**Preserve Player-Facing Rolls**

- Players roll to attack opponents.
- Players roll to defend against opponent attacks.
- Players roll Saving Throws against hazards and special effects.
- The Game Master uses static ratings and established Difficulties.

**Use Wounds to Create Consequences**

- Opponents should react appropriately to injury.
- Ordinary humans may retreat or surrender.
- Fanatics may fight despite serious injuries.
- Animals may flee when badly hurt.
- Mechanical and supernatural opponents may continue until incapacitated or destroyed.

**Adapt Opponents Across Eras**

- The same basic statistics can represent many different adversaries.
- A Common Street Thug may be armed with a club in a medieval setting, a knife in the 1920s, or an improvised energy weapon in a science-fiction adventure.
- A Trained Soldier may carry a musket, bolt-action rifle, assault rifle, or futuristic firearm.
- Adjust weapons, equipment, and Special Abilities to reflect the setting while preserving the opponent's intended role.

**Opponents should be simple to create, quick to run, and distinctive enough to challenge characters across any era or genre.**
