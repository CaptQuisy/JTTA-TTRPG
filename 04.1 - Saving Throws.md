## Saving Throws

Not every danger comes from an opponent's attack. Explosions, collapsing structures, poison, disease, environmental hazards, fear, and other threats may endanger characters without involving ordinary combat.

A **Saving Throw** represents a character's attempt to resist, avoid, or lessen the effects of a harmful event.

Unlike a normal Skill Check, a Saving Throw is usually called for by the Game Master when a character is exposed to a threat. The character does not need to voluntarily initiate an action to attempt the Saving Throw.

Saving Throws use the same dice pool mechanics as other checks, but they may also reduce the severity of a hazard even when the character does not achieve enough successes to avoid it completely.

### When to Make a Saving Throw

The Game Master calls for a Saving Throw when a character must resist a danger that cannot be resolved through an ordinary Attack, Defense, or Skill Check.

Common situations include:

- Avoiding an explosion or collapsing structure.
- Resisting poison, disease, or toxic substances.
- Escaping falling debris.
- Enduring extreme heat, cold, or electrical shock.
- Resisting fear, panic, or supernatural influence.
- Overcoming mind control or compulsions.
- Surviving a fall or other environmental hazard.
- Avoiding injury during a vehicle collision.

Saving Throws may represent immediate reactions or resistance to effects that develop over time.

The Game Master determines when a Saving Throw is required and what happens if it fails.

### Making a Saving Throw

Saving Throws use the standard dice pool system.

**Saving Throw Dice Pool = Attributes or Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

The Game Master determines:

1. The appropriate Attribute combination or Attribute and Skill.
2. The Difficulty of the Saving Throw.
3. Any applicable modifiers.
4. The consequences of failure.

The player then:

1. Builds the appropriate dice pool.
2. Applies bonuses and penalties from Edges, Hindrances, equipment, Conditions, and circumstances.
3. Rolls the dice.
4. Counts each die showing **5 or 6** as one success.
5. Compares the successes with the required Difficulty.

If the character rolls successes equal to or greater than the Difficulty, the Saving Throw succeeds.

If the character rolls fewer successes than required, the Saving Throw fails. However, any successes may reduce the severity of the consequences.

#### Successful Saving Throw

A successful Saving Throw allows the character to avoid or resist the specified danger.

Unless the hazard states otherwise, a successful Saving Throw prevents the harmful effect.

For example:

- A character avoids injury from falling debris.
- A character resists the effects of a poison.
- A character overcomes a wave of supernatural fear.
- A character avoids being caught in an explosion.

Some hazards may still impose a reduced effect on a successful Saving Throw. Such exceptions should be established before the roll.

### Choosing Attributes

Most Saving Throws combine either:

- **One Attribute and one Skill**, or
- **One Physical Attribute and one Mental Attribute**.

Physical Attributes are Strength, Dexterity, and Resilience.

Mental Attributes are Intelligence, Acuity, and Presence.

The combination should reflect how the character attempts to resist the danger.

#### Common Saving Throws

|Threat|Saving Throw|
|---|---|
|Poison or Disease|Resilience + Strength|
|Explosion|Dexterity + Athletics|
|Electrical Shock|Resilience + Dexterity|
|Fear|Resilience + Acuity|
|Mind Control|Acuity + Presence|
|Temptation or Addiction|Resilience + Acuity|
|Falling Debris|Dexterity + Athletics|
These combinations are guidelines.

The Game Master may select a different combination when the situation warrants it.

For example, a character who anticipates a collapsing ceiling might use Acuity + Perception to recognize the danger, while a character already beneath the falling debris might use Dexterity + Athletics to escape.

The Game Master should apply similar rulings consistently.

#### Skills and Saving Throws

A Skill may be included when training directly improves the character's ability to resist the hazard.

Examples include:

- Athletics for avoiding physical hazards.
- Survival for enduring environmental threats.
- Perception for recognizing and reacting to danger.
- Vehicle for avoiding a collision while operating a vehicle.

When a Saving Throw uses two Attributes, do not add a Skill unless a specific ability or rule permits it.

### Saving Throw Difficulty

Saving Throws use the same Difficulty scale as Skill Checks.

|Difficulty|Successes Required|
|---|---|
|Easy|1|
|Normal|2|
|Hard|3|
|Extreme|4|
|Legendary|5|
|Impossible|6 or more|
The Game Master selects the Difficulty based on the severity of the threat and the circumstances.

**Easy (1)**

- A relatively manageable hazard.
- Examples include avoiding a small falling object or resisting a mild environmental discomfort.

**Normal (2)**

- A meaningful danger requiring a capable response.
- Examples include resisting moderate fear or avoiding an unstable section of flooring.

**Hard (3)**

- A dangerous hazard that may cause significant injury or impairment.
- Examples include escaping a substantial explosion or resisting a powerful toxin.

**Extreme (4)**

- A severe threat that could cause life-threatening injury.
- Examples include escaping a major structural collapse or surviving a devastating electrical discharge.

**Legendary (5)**

- An extraordinary danger that challenges even highly capable characters.
- Examples include resisting overwhelming supernatural influence or surviving an exceptionally destructive environmental event.

**Impossible (6+)**

- An extreme or extraordinary threat beyond normal human capabilities.
- Such Saving Throws should be rare and generally reserved for exceptional circumstances, unusual technology, or supernatural effects.

The Game Master may modify the Difficulty or dice pool to reflect equipment, preparation, environmental conditions, or special abilities.

### Saving Throws That Cause Damage

Some hazards inflict Wounds when a Saving Throw fails.

Examples include:

- Explosions.
- Falling from a height.
- Fire.
- Acid.
- Electrical discharge.
- Cave-ins.
- Vehicle collisions.
- Other dangerous environmental events.

For these hazards, the Saving Throw Difficulty represents the maximum severity of the resulting Wound.

Each success reduces the remaining severity of the hazard by one level.

### Determining Wound Severity

**Remaining Hazard Severity = Saving Throw Difficulty – Successes Rolled**

Compare the remaining value with the following table.

| Remaining Hazard Severity | Result         |
| ------------------------- | -------------- |
| 0 or less                 | No Wound       |
| 1                         | Minor Wound    |
| 2                         | Serious Wound  |
| 3                         | Critical Wound |
| 4 or more                 | Lethal Wound   |
|                           |                |
A character normally suffers **one Wound** from a failed Saving Throw.

The remaining Hazard Severity determines how serious that Wound is.

Record the Wound on the Injury Tracker according to Chapter 6, _Damage & Wounds_.

#### Maximum Wound Severity

The Difficulty of a damaging hazard establishes its maximum Wound severity when the character rolls no successes.

|Hazard Difficulty|Maximum Wound|
|---|---|
|Easy (1)|Minor|
|Normal (2)|Serious|
|Hard (3)|Critical|
|Extreme (4)|Lethal|
|Legendary (5)|Lethal|
|Impossible (6+)|Lethal|
Legendary and Impossible hazards may have additional consequences specified by the Game Master, but they do not introduce a Wound category beyond Lethal.

### Partial Success

Even when a Saving Throw fails, the character may reduce the consequences by rolling some successes.

For damaging hazards, every success reduces the remaining Hazard Severity by one.

This represents the character partially avoiding, resisting, or enduring the danger.

#### Example: Escaping an Explosion

A character is caught in an explosion requiring a **Hard Saving Throw (3 successes)**.

The character rolls Dexterity + Athletics.

The result determines the Wound suffered.

|Successes Rolled|Remaining Severity|Result|
|---|---|---|
|0|3|Critical Wound|
|1|2|Serious Wound|
|2|1|Minor Wound|
|3 or more|0 or less|No Wound|
If the character rolls 2 successes, the Saving Throw technically fails because it required 3 successes.

However, those 2 successes reduce the explosion's severity from 3 to 1. The character suffers only a Minor Wound.

The Game Master might describe the character diving behind a heavy table, avoiding most of the blast but suffering minor injuries from flying debris.

#### Example: Falling from a Height

A character falls from a dangerous height.

The Game Master assigns an Extreme Saving Throw (4 successes) using Dexterity + Athletics. The character rolls 2 successes.

**4 – 2 = 2**

The character suffers a Serious Wound. The Game Master describes the character landing awkwardly and injuring a leg.

The character records the Wound and applies any appropriate Condition.

### Saving Throws That Cause Conditions

Some hazards impose a Condition instead of causing direct damage.

Examples include:

- Poisoned.
- Burning.
- Bleeding.
- Frightened.
- Grappled.
- Stunned.
- Controlled.

A hazard may impose a Condition immediately when the Saving Throw fails.

Other hazards may permit additional Saving Throws after a specified period.

#### Resolving Conditions

When a Saving Throw is used to resist a Condition, the Game Master establishes:

1. The Condition being resisted.
2. The Saving Throw Difficulty.
3. Whether partial success reduces the effect.
4. The duration of the Condition.
5. Whether additional Saving Throws are permitted.

A successful Saving Throw normally prevents the Condition.

A failed Saving Throw imposes the Condition according to the hazard's rules.

#### Partial Success Against Conditions

The Game Master may allow partial success to reduce the severity or duration of a non-damaging effect.

Possible benefits include:

- Shortening the duration of a Condition.
- Reducing its mechanical penalty.
- Delaying the effect.
- Allowing the character to retain limited control.
- Preventing a secondary complication.

These benefits are not automatic.

The Game Master should establish the possible consequences before the roll whenever practical.

##### Example: Resisting Fear

A character encounters a terrifying supernatural apparition.

The Game Master calls for a Hard (3 successes) Resilience + Acuity Saving Throw. The character rolls 2 successes. The Saving Throw fails, but the character partially resists the fear.

The Game Master determines that the character gains the Frightened Condition for a shorter duration than would otherwise apply.

The character is frightened but remains capable of acting.

##### Example: Resisting Mind Control

A character is subjected to a mind-control effect.

The Game Master calls for an Extreme (4 successes) Acuity + Presence Saving Throw. The character rolls 4 successes. The Saving Throw succeeds.

The character resists the attempt and remains in control of their actions.

Had the character failed, the Controlled Condition or another effect specified by the ability would have applied.

### Repeated Saving Throws

Some hazards allow or require additional Saving Throws over time.

Examples include:

- Ongoing exposure to poison.
- Prolonged environmental hazards.
- Certain diseases.
- Continuing supernatural effects.
- Conditions that permit repeated resistance.

The hazard or Condition determines when another Saving Throw may be attempted.

Possible intervals include:

- At the end of the character's Turn.
- At the beginning of the next Round.
- After several minutes.
- After a period of rest.
- After receiving medical treatment.

Unless a rule specifies otherwise, a failed Saving Throw does not automatically grant another attempt.

The Game Master should avoid requiring repeated Saving Throws without a meaningful change in circumstances or an established ongoing threat.

#### Example: Poison Exposure

A character is exposed to a dangerous poison.

The Game Master calls for a Resilience + Strength Saving Throw. The poison's rules specify that a failed Saving Throw imposes the Poisoned Condition.

If the poison allows another Saving Throw after a period of time, the character may attempt to resist the effect again at that interval.

The Difficulty and duration are determined by the poison rather than by a universal Saving Throw rule.

#### Push Your Luck on Saving Throws

Players may use **Push Your Luck** when making Saving Throws unless the specific hazard or rule prohibits it.

After the initial roll, the player may reroll all dice showing 2, 3, or 4 once. Dice showing 1, 5, or 6 remain unchanged. If the reroll produces no additional successes, the character loses one original success, if available.

The final success total determines the outcome.

### Push Your Luck and Partial Success

When a Saving Throw determines Wound severity, use the final number of successes after Push Your Luck.

If the character gains additional successes, the remaining Hazard Severity decreases.

If the character loses a success because of Overextension, the remaining Hazard Severity increases.

##### Example: Pushing a Saving Throw

A character faces a Hard (3 successes) Saving Throw against an explosion.

The initial roll produces 1 success. The player chooses to Push Your Luck. After rerolling the eligible dice, the character gains 1 additional success.

The final total is 2 successes.

**3 – 2 = 1**

The character suffers a Minor Wound rather than the Serious Wound that would have resulted from the initial roll.

#### Critical Failure

The standard Critical Failure rules apply to Saving Throws.

A Critical Failure occurs when the final dice show:

- At least three 1s.
- More 1s than successes.

The Game Master may introduce an additional complication appropriate to the hazard.

Examples include:

- Falling into a worse position.
- Losing important equipment.
- Becoming trapped beneath debris.
- Exposing another character to danger.
- Suffering an additional Condition.

A Critical Failure does not automatically increase Wound severity unless the hazard or a specific rule states otherwise.

### Saving Throws and Character Defense

Saving Throws and Character Defense Rolls use similar mechanics but serve different purposes.

#### Character Defense

Use a Character Defense Roll when an opponent makes a direct attack against a player character.

Examples include:

- A gangster firing a pistol.
- A swordsman striking with a blade.
- A thug attempting to grapple a character.
- An enemy vehicle firing a mounted weapon.

Character Defense is described in Section 5.5.

#### Saving Throws

Use a Saving Throw when a character must resist a hazard or harmful effect that is not resolved as an ordinary direct attack.

Examples include:

- An explosion damaging everyone nearby.
- A building collapsing.
- Poison entering the bloodstream.
- A character falling from a height.
- A supernatural effect attempting to control a character's mind.

#### Avoiding Duplicate Rolls

Do not normally require both a Character Defense Roll and a Saving Throw to avoid the same damaging event.

Use the procedure most appropriate to the threat.

For example, a character defending against a direct gunshot uses Character Defense.

A character caught in the blast of an exploding fuel tank uses a Saving Throw.

A special attack may require both only when its rules explicitly establish separate effects.

### Game Master Guidance

**Use Saving Throws for Meaningful Danger**

- Call for a Saving Throw when failure creates a meaningful consequence.
- Avoid repeated rolls against trivial hazards or circumstances where success and failure have little impact.

**Choose Appropriate Attributes**

- The dice pool should reflect how the character resists the danger.
- Use the examples in this chapter as guidelines rather than rigid restrictions.

**Establish Consequences Before Rolling**

- Whenever practical, determine the Difficulty, possible Wound severity, Conditions, and opportunities for partial success before the player rolls.
- This makes the outcome easier to understand and keeps rulings consistent.

**Reward Preparation**

- Protective equipment, specialized training, planning, and environmental advantages should improve the character's chances.
- For example, a character wearing protective clothing may receive a bonus when resisting fire or hazardous chemicals.

**Make Every Success Matter**

- For damaging hazards, each success directly reduces Wound severity.
- For other hazards, partial success may reduce the duration, severity, or narrative consequences of the effect.
- This ensures that even a failed Saving Throw can represent meaningful resistance.

**Avoid Unnecessary Complexity**

- Do not require additional rolls when the result is obvious.
- If a hazard is harmless, resolve it narratively.
- If a threat is completely unavoidable, explain its consequences without requiring a meaningless roll.

**Saving Throws allow characters to resist extraordinary dangers using the same player-facing dice mechanics as the rest of the game, while ensuring that partial success can make the difference between a minor injury and a fatal one.**
