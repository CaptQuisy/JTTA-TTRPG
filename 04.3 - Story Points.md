## Story Points

Story Points represent moments when determination, luck, dramatic timing, or sheer force of will gives a character an unexpected advantage.

They allow players to influence important moments in the adventure while rewarding actions that make the story more exciting, memorable, and meaningful.

Depending on the genre or setting, Story Points may be called **Luck, Grit, Boons, Fortune, Fate**, or another name appropriate to the campaign.

Regardless of the name, their basic function remains the same.

**Spend 1 Story Point to add +1 die to a roll.**

### Starting Story Points

Characters begin play with **1 Story Point**.

A character may hold a maximum of **3 Story Points** at any time.

Unused Story Points carry over between sessions. They are not automatically lost or reset.

A character cannot receive additional Story Points while already at the maximum.

The Game Master may adjust the starting number or maximum to reflect the tone of the campaign.

### Spending Story Points

A character may spend **1 Story Point to add +1 die** to any of the following rolls:

- Skill Checks
- Attack Rolls
- Defense Rolls
- Saving Throws

The player must declare the expenditure before rolling the dice, unless the Game Master specifically permits otherwise.

Only **one Story Point may be spent on a single roll**.

The bonus die is added to the character's dice pool **before** the roll is made.

Story Points do not change the Difficulty, guarantee success, or eliminate the possibility of a Critical Failure.

#### Example: A Difficult Shot

A character attempts to shoot an enemy concealed behind cover.

The character normally rolls six dice.

The player spends 1 Story Point, increasing the dice pool to seven dice.

The player rolls all seven dice and counts each 5 or 6 as one success.

The additional die improves the character's chance of success but does not guarantee a hit.

### Story Points and Push Your Luck

Story Points may be used on rolls that can also benefit from Push Your Luck.

The Story Point bonus die becomes part of the original dice pool and follows the normal Push Your Luck rules.

For example, if the Story Point die shows a 2, 3, or 4, it may be rerolled when the player Pushes Their Luck.

If it shows a 1, 5, or 6, it is retained as normal.

Spending a Story Point does not grant an additional Push Your Luck attempt.

A character may still Push Their Luck only once per roll.

### Spending After a Roll

The standard rule requires Story Points to be spent before rolling.

The Game Master may occasionally permit a Story Point to be spent after seeing the dice results when an especially dramatic situation justifies it.

If permitted, the player rolls one additional die and adds any success to the existing result.

This exception must be declared before the Game Master applies the consequences of the roll.

Retroactive spending should remain exceptional rather than becoming the standard procedure.

### Awarding Story Points

Story Points are primarily awarded by the Game Master during play.

They reward actions and decisions that contribute to the adventure, develop characters, and create memorable moments.

A Story Point may be awarded when a player:

- **Acts Heroically:** Performs a particularly heroic, daring, or selfless action.
- **Advances the Story:** Makes a decision that strongly advances or connects with the plot.
- **Develops Their Character:** Brings their Background, personality, motivations, relationships, or history meaningfully into the story.
- **Accepts Complications:** Accepts a difficult situation or complication because it is appropriate for the character.
- **Demonstrates Creativity:** Develops an especially clever or creative solution to a problem.
- **Creates a Memorable Moment:** Contributes a scene that makes everyone at the table laugh, cheer, gasp, or otherwise react.

Story Points should not be awarded solely for succeeding.

A character who makes a courageous decision and fails spectacularly may be just as deserving of a Story Point as one who succeeds.

Likewise, an unsuccessful plan that creates an exciting new direction for the adventure may deserve recognition.

### Reward Decisions, Not Dice Results

Story Points should reward meaningful player choices rather than random success.

For example:

A soldier who risks their life to rescue a wounded companion may earn a Story Point regardless of whether the rescue succeeds.

A detective who follows a dangerous lead because of a personal obligation may earn a Story Point even if the investigation fails.

A criminal who refuses an easy profit because it conflicts with a personal code may earn a Story Point for remaining true to the character.

The Game Master should consider the player's intention and the significance of the decision.

### Fairness in Awards

Story Points should be available to all players regardless of their confidence, acting ability, or experience with roleplaying games.

Players should not be required to perform dramatic speeches or entertain the group to earn rewards.

A player who quietly describes a meaningful character decision deserves the same consideration as one who acts out an elaborate scene.

The Game Master should distribute awards fairly without requiring every character to receive the same number of points.

### Starting Each Session with Story Points

As an optional rule, the Game Master may award each character **1 Story Point at the beginning of every session**.

This point is added to the character's existing total, up to the maximum of 3 Story Points.

For example:

- A character with 0 Story Points begins the session with 1.
- A character with 1 Story Point begins with 2.
- A character with 2 Story Points begins with 3.
- A character with 3 Story Points remains at 3.

This option ensures that characters regularly receive opportunities to use Story Points, even when awards during play are infrequent.

The Game Master should decide whether to use this rule before the campaign begins.

### Player-Awarded Story Points (Optional)

The Game Master may allow players to award Story Points to one another. This encourages players to recognize memorable roleplaying, clever ideas, heroic actions, humor, and meaningful contributions from other members of the group.

Under this optional rule, each player may award **one Story Point to another player per session**. The award does not reduce the awarding player's own Story Point total.

The receiving character cannot exceed the normal maximum of 3 Story Points.

#### Guidelines for Player Awards

Players should award Story Points for contributions that improve the adventure or make another character memorable.

Examples include:

- A clever solution that helps the entire group.
- A selfless action that places a character at risk.
- An entertaining exchange that develops relationships.
- A decision that creates an unexpected story opportunity.
- A memorable moment of courage, loyalty, humor, or sacrifice.

Players cannot award Story Points to themselves.

The Game Master may limit or suspend player awards if they become automatic exchanges rather than recognition of meaningful contributions.

### Story Points and Genre

Story Points can reinforce the tone of a campaign simply by changing their name.

| Campaign Genre  | Suggested Name | Narrative Meaning                                    |
| --------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Gritty War      | Grit           | Determination, endurance, and courage under pressure |
| Pulp Adventure  | Fortune        | Remarkable luck and dramatic timing                  |
| Heroic Fantasy  | Boons          | Heroic destiny or extraordinary favor                |
| Modern Action   | Luck           | Fortunate opportunities and split-second advantages  |
| Science Fiction | Fate           | Extraordinary timing and moments of opportunity      |
These names are purely thematic.

The mechanics remain unchanged unless the Game Master deliberately introduces a campaign-specific variation.

#### Gritty and Realistic Campaigns

In realistic or survival-focused campaigns, Story Points may represent determination, experience, preparation, or a momentary advantage rather than supernatural intervention.

They should not allow characters to ignore established physical limitations or accomplish impossible feats.

#### Cinematic Campaigns

In pulp, action, or heroic campaigns, Story Points may be awarded more frequently to encourage daring actions and dramatic risks.

The Game Master may also choose a higher maximum if the campaign emphasizes exceptional heroism.

Such adjustments should be applied consistently to all characters.

### Game Master Guidance

Story Points should encourage players to participate actively in the adventure without overshadowing the game's Skill and Attribute system.

The Game Master should consider the following principles.

**Keep Awards Meaningful**

- Award Story Points for memorable decisions and important contributions rather than every successful action.

**Encourage Character Development**

- Reward players who incorporate their characters' Backgrounds, motivations, relationships, and personal values into the story.

**Recognize Failure**

- A failed action may still deserve a Story Point when the character takes a meaningful risk or remains true to their personality.

**Maintain Balance**

- The recommended maximum of 3 Story Points prevents characters from accumulating an excessive reserve of bonus dice.

**Preserve the Game's Tone**

- Story Points represent an additional opportunity for success, not immunity from consequences.
- In a realistic campaign, even an experienced character with good fortune remains vulnerable to danger.

**Story Points reward the decisions that make an adventure memorable while giving players a limited resource to improve their chances when the stakes are high.**
