# Journey Through the Ages
## QuickStart Rules

*Journey Through the Ages (JTTA)* is a universal tabletop roleplaying game designed for historical, contemporary, and fictional adventures. The system emphasizes believable characters, skill-based challenges, tactical combat, and meaningful consequences. Players roll six-sided dice to resolve their characters’ actions, attacks, and defenses, while the Game Master (GM) manages the narrative, opponents, and challenges without rolling dice. This guide introduces the essential rules for playing with pre-generated characters without reading the complete rulebook.

## 1. Characters

### Attributes
Attributes represent natural capabilities, rated from 1 (below average) to 4 (exceptional). A rating of 2 represents average human capability; 3 is above average.

| Attribute | Capability |
|---|---|
| Strength (STR) | Physical power |
| Dexterity (DEX) | Coordination, agility, balance, reflexes |
| Resilience (RES) | Endurance, toughness, resistance to injury |
| Intelligence (INT) | Reasoning, knowledge, memory |
| Acuity (ACT) | Awareness, intuition, alertness |
| Presence (PRS) | Confidence, leadership, social influence |

### Skills
Skills represent training and experience. Ratings range from 1 (Basic), 2 (Competent), 3 (Experienced), 4 (Expert), to 5 (Master).

| Skill | Default Attribute | Applications |
|---|---|---|
| Academics | INT | Education, history, culture |
| Athletics | STR / DEX | Running, climbing, swimming, lifting |
| Close Combat | STR | Melee weapons, unarmed combat, grappling |
| Crafting | DEX / INT | Making and repairing objects |
| Finesse | DEX | Delicate manipulation, sleight of hand |
| Influence | PRS / STR | Persuasion, negotiation, intimidation |
| Investigation | INT | Research, evidence, clues |
| Medicine | INT | Diagnosis, treatment, medical procedures |
| Perception | ACT | Awareness, detecting threats |
| Performance | PRS | Acting, music, storytelling |
| Ranged Combat | DEX | Firearms, bows, thrown weapons |
| Stealth | DEX | Concealment, quiet movement |
| Survival | ACT / INT | Tracking, navigation, finding resources |
| Technology | INT | Operating and repairing technical systems |
| Vehicle | DEX / INT | Operating and navigating vehicles |

The GM may select a different Attribute when the character’s approach warrants it. For example, climbing uses STR + Athletics, crossing a narrow ledge uses DEX + Athletics, and an exhausting swim may use RES + Athletics.

### Edges and Hindrances
Every character has at least one Edge and may have Hindrances. Edges provide special advantages through talent, training, or experience. Hindrances represent limitations, obligations, or personal flaws. Both may affect Skill Checks, Saving Throws, and Combat. Refer to the character sheet for specific effects.

## 2. Skill Checks

Roll when an action is challenging, its outcome uncertain, and failure meaningful. Routine actions generally succeed automatically; impossible actions require no roll.

**Dice Pool = Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

1. Describe the action and approach.
2. The GM selects the Attribute, Skill, and Difficulty.
3. Apply bonuses and penalties. Skill Checks use a minimum of two dice after modifiers.
4. Roll the dice. Each 5 or 6 counts as one success.
5. Meet or exceed the Difficulty to succeed.

| Difficulty | Successes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 1 | Climb a fence |
| Normal | 2 | Repair common equipment |
| Hard | 3 | Pick a quality lock |
| Extreme | 4 | Survive hazardous conditions |
| Legendary | 5 | Accomplish an extraordinary feat |

Difficulty measures the challenge itself; modifiers reflect factors affecting the character’s performance. Avoid counting the same circumstance twice. Edges, Hindrances, Conditions, equipment, and circumstances may add or remove dice. Equipment grants bonuses only when its rules or the GM authorize them. Combine distinct modifiers. The GM determines whether untrained attempts are possible.

**Example:** A soldier repairs a damaged field radio using INT 3 + Technology 2, with an approved toolkit bonus of +1 die. The GM assigns Hard (3). The six dice show 1, 2, 3, 5, 5, 6: three successes, so the repair succeeds.

### Opponents
Players roll against an Opponent’s relevant fixed Rating. The GM does not roll for Opponents. If no Rating exists, the GM assigns an appropriate Difficulty; do not directly substitute an Opponent’s Skill Level for a Rating.

### Push Your Luck
A player may Push Your Luck once per Skill Check, Attack Roll, Defense Roll, or Saving Throw, even if the initial roll succeeds. This must occur before consequences are resolved and requires at least one die showing 2–4.

- Retain all 1s, 5s, and 6s.
- Reroll every 2, 3, and 4 once; add new successes.
- If no new successes are rolled, remove one original success, if available (Overextension).
- Check for Critical Failure, then resolve the final result.

Damage Rolls cannot be pushed.

**Example:** A soldier needs three successes to cross a dangerous gap. The initial roll is 6, 5, 4, 4, 2, 1. Rerolling 4, 4, 2 produces 6, 3, 2. The soldier now has three successes and succeeds. Had the reroll produced no successes, the total would have fallen to one.

### Critical Failure
A Critical Failure occurs when the final dice contain at least three 1s and the number of 1s exceeds the final number of successes. This applies to ordinary rolls and pushed rolls. The GM determines an appropriate additional complication, such as lost time, damaged equipment, or alerting enemies. Overextension alone is not a Critical Failure.

## 3. Combat

### Rounds and Action Points
A Round lasts approximately six seconds. Normally, player characters act first in an order chosen by the players, followed by Opponents. Surprise or ambush may change this order. No Initiative roll is required.

Each character has 3 Action Points (AP). Actions may be repeated and performed in any order unless otherwise restricted. AP refresh at the end of the character’s Turn. Reactions spent before the next Turn reduce the AP available on that Turn. Unused AP never accumulate beyond three.

| Action | AP | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Move | 1 | Move up to 30 ft on normal terrain |
| Attack | 1 | Make a Close or Ranged Combat Attack |
| Aim | 1 | +1 die to next Ranged Attack |
| Assess | 1 | ACT + Perception, Normal (2); see below |
| Reload | 1* | Reload or prepare next shot |
| Interact | 1* | Operate equipment or manipulate objects |
| Recover | 1 | Attempt to remove or reduce a Condition |
| Stabilize Ally | 2 | Use Medicine to stop an adjacent ally’s Bleeding or Dying |

*Some weapons and interactions require additional AP.*

A successful Assess allows one relevant question, +1 die to the next Attack against an identified Opponent, or +1 die to your next Defense Roll. One Free Action per Round is allowed, such as speaking briefly, dropping an item, or drawing an accessible weapon.

| Movement | Distance per AP |
|---|---|
| Normal | 30 ft |
| Cautious, difficult terrain, swimming, climbing | 15 ft |
| Crawling | 10 ft |

### Attacking
**Attack Dice Pool = Attribute + Combat Skill + Modifiers**

Close Combat normally uses STR + Close Combat; Ranged Combat uses DEX + Ranged Combat. Martial Arts uses DEX + Close Combat with the appropriate Edge.

Declare the target and method, apply modifiers, and roll against the Opponent’s Defense Rating. If successes meet or exceed the Rating, make a separate Damage Roll. The second Attack on a Turn suffers −1 die; the third suffers −2 dice. These Rushed Attack penalties reset at the beginning of the next Turn.

### Close Combat
A successful surprise Close Combat Attack against an unaware target gains +2 dice when sneaking up on the target successfully. To Grapple, be within Close Combat range and make an unarmed Attack using STR + Close Combat or Athletics against the target’s Defense Rating. Success imposes Grappled instead of damage unless a special rule permits damage.

**Outnumber Bonus = floor(Attackers ÷ Defenders) − 1** (when attackers outnumber defenders).

### Ranged Combat
| Range | Distance |
|---|---|
| Close | 0–5 ft |
| Short | 6–30 ft |
| Medium | 31–120 ft |
| Long | 121–300 ft |
| Extreme | 301+ ft |

|---|---|
| Prone target / half cover | −1 Attack die each |
| Three-quarter cover | −2 Attack dice |
| Full cover | Attack unavailable (NA) |
| Higher ground | +1 Attack die |
| Charging in Close Combat | +1 Attack die; −1 Defense die until next Turn |
| Difficult terrain | −1 die to movement-related checks |
| Dim light | −1 Attack die |
| Darkness without aid | −3 Attack dice |

|---|---|---|

### Weapon Profiles
Range modifiers apply to Attack Rolls. Damage Dice apply only to Damage Rolls. A dash indicates a range at which the weapon cannot normally attack.

| Weapon role | Damage | Close | Short | Medium | Long | Extreme |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unarmed | 2 | 0 | — | — | — | — |
| Light Close Combat | 3 | 0 | −1 | — | — | — |
| Heavy Close Combat | 4 | 0 | — | — | — | — |
| Long Close Combat | 4 | 0 | 0 | −2 | — | — |
| Sidearm | 3–4 | −2 | 0 | −2 | −3 | — |
| Shotgun | 3–5 | −2 | 0 | −2 | −3 | — |
| Long Arm | 3–5 | −2 | 0 | 0 | −1 | −2 |
| Automatic Weapon | 4–5 | −2 | 0 | 0 | −1 | −2 |
| Support Weapon | 5–6 | −2 | 0 | 0 | −2 | −3 |
| Ranged Explosive | 5–6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | −2 | — |

### Full Automatic Fire
An automatic weapon may make up to three separate Attacks for 1 AP. Attack the same target repeatedly or divide attacks among targets within Short range of one another.

| Attack | Dice penalty |
|---|---|
| First | −1 |
| Second | −2 |
| Third | −3 |

Resolve each Attack and any resulting Damage Roll separately. The attacker may stop after any Attack. Reload after Full Automatic Fire.

### Suppressive Fire
Spend 2 AP with an automatic weapon and select an approximately 30-degree firing arc. Make one DEX + Ranged Combat Roll against Hard Difficulty (3). The GM determines which Opponents are exposed.

On success, exposed Opponents move at half speed, and characters attacked by them gain +1 Defense die. Suppression does not automatically inflict Wounds. A suppressed player character moves at half speed and suffers −1 die on their next Attack affected by suppression. On failure, no suppression penalties apply. AP and ammunition are spent regardless of success; reload before firing again.

### Grenades and Explosions
A hand-thrown grenade uses DEX + Athletics, has a maximum range of Medium, and provides 6 Damage Dice. A fragmentation grenade has Power Level 6 and a primary Blast Radius of 20 ft.

1. Select a location within range and make the Attack Roll with applicable modifiers. If placement fails, the GM determines where the grenade lands.
2. Identify everyone within the Blast Area at the actual point of detonation.
3. For Opponents, compare the Attack successes with each target’s Physical Defense Rating. For each successful hit, roll grenade Damage Dice plus excess Attack successes separately.
4. Player characters caught in the blast make a DEX + Athletics Saving Throw. The GM sets Difficulty based on explosive Power Level and circumstances. Subtract Saving Throw successes from Difficulty to determine Wound severity.

Placement and blast avoidance are separate steps. Do not apply direct-hit damage in addition to blast damage unless a specific weapon rule requires it. Each affected target normally receives only one damage resolution per explosion.

### Defending
When the Opponents take their Turns, the resolution shifts from player Attacks to player Defense Rolls. The GM declares the Opponent’s attack and target, but does not roll dice. The targeted player rolls to avoid or reduce the effects of the attack.

**Defense Dice Pool = Attribute + Skill + Modifiers**

When an Opponent attacks, the player rolls against its Attack Rating. Meeting or exceeding the Rating prevents a Wound. Otherwise, the shortfall determines Wound severity. Applicable armor and defensive bonuses add dice.

| Threat | Typical Defense pool |
|---|---|
| Melee | STR or DEX + Close Combat |
| Ranged | DEX + Athletics |
| Grapple | STR + Athletics |
| Thrown object | STR or DEX + Athletics |
| Being surrounded | DEX + Perception |

### Reactions
Declare Reactions outside your Turn when triggered, before the outcome is resolved.

| Reaction | AP | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Defend | 1 | +1 die against one attack |
| Interpose | 1 | Protect an ally within 10 ft; become the target |
| Opportunity Attack | 1 | Attack at −1 die when a valid opening occurs |
| Help | 1 | Grant an ally +1 to +3 dice; only one Help per roll |

## 4. Saving Throws
Saving Throws resist hazards that are not resolved through ordinary attacks or checks. The GM selects the dice pool, Difficulty, modifiers, and consequences. Use two Attributes or an Attribute and Skill; do not add a Skill to a two-Attribute pool unless permitted by a specific rule.

| Threat | Typical Saving Throw |
|---|---|
| Poison or disease | RES + STR |
| Explosion or falling debris | DEX + Athletics |
| Electrical shock | RES + DEX |
| Fear, temptation, addiction | RES + ACT |
| Mind control | ACT + PRS |

Use the standard Difficulty scale; Impossible requires six or more successes. Success normally avoids the danger, although specified hazards may impose reduced effects even on success. Failure may impose a Condition or Wound.

For damaging hazards, each success reduces severity by one:

**Remaining Hazard Severity = Saving Throw Difficulty − Successes Rolled**

Use the Wound Severity table below. A Hard (3) explosion Saving Throw with one success causes a Serious Wound (3 − 1 = 2). Some hazards allow additional Saving Throws after a specified period.

## 5. Damage and Wounds
JTTA tracks individual Wounds rather than Hit Points.

**Damage against Opponents:** After a successful Attack, roll Weapon Damage Dice plus Attack successes exceeding the Opponent’s Defense Rating. Each 5 or 6 is a Damage success. Do not Push Your Luck on Damage Rolls.

**Damage against player characters:** Subtract Defense successes from the Opponent’s Attack Rating. No separate Damage Roll is required.

| Damage successes, Defense shortfall, or remaining hazard severity | Wound | Slots |
|---|---|---|
| 0 or less | None | — |
| 1 | Minor | RES |
| 2 | Serious | 2 |
| 3 | Critical | 1 |
| 4 or more | Lethal | 1 |

### Understanding Wound Slots
Each slot represents one unhealed injury, not a Hit Point. Mark an empty slot at the severity inflicted. Minor Wounds are manageable injuries; Serious Wounds may impair actions; Critical Wounds may disable or incapacitate; Lethal Wounds threaten life and require immediate attention. A character with RES 3 normally has the following Injury Tracker:

| Wound level | Example slots |
|---|---|
| Minor | □ □ □ |
| Serious | □ □ |
| Critical | □ |
| Lethal | □ |

Edges, Hindrances, or special rules may modify these capacities. Marked slots remain occupied until the Wounds heal. For example, if all three Minor slots are occupied, another Minor Wound fills a Serious slot instead, escalating further if necessary.

Each damaging result normally fills one slot, not one slot per success. If no slot is available, escalate the Wound one level until a slot is available. Record the location and nature of Serious and Critical Wounds. A Lethal Wound causes Dying. The recommended rule is death if the Lethal slot is already filled, unless a specific ability or campaign rule prevents it; the GM may allow a final narrative action.

**Example:** An Opponent attacks with Rating 4. The player rolls two Defense successes and suffers one Serious Wound.

### Injury Complications
| Location | Serious | Critical | Lethal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head | Dazed | Stunned | Dying + Unconscious |
| Upper / lower torso | Bleeding | Stunned + Bleeding | Dying |
| Arm | Dazed | Disabled Arm + Bleeding | Dying + Disabled Arm |
| Leg | Slowed + Bleeding | Disabled Leg + Bleeding | Dying + Disabled Leg |
| Groin | Dazed + Bleeding | Stunned + Bleeding | Dying |

Minor Wounds normally impose no additional Condition. The GM may choose injury location or use the optional 2d6 table.

| 2d6 | Location |
|---|---|
| 2 | Head |
| 3 / 4 | Left / right arm |
| 5–7 | Upper torso |
| 8–9 | Lower torso |
| 10 / 11 | Left / right leg |
| 12 | Groin |

### Dying and Stabilization
Spend 2 AP and make a Hard (3) INT + Medicine Check to stabilize a Dying character with meaningful medical assistance. Success prevents immediate death but does not remove the Lethal Wound. If not stabilized before the next Turn, use the campaign’s selected survival option:

- **Blaze of Glory:** Perform one final heroic action that automatically succeeds, then die.
- **Fight for Survival:** At the beginning of each Turn, make a Normal (2) RES + ACT Saving Throw without Push Your Luck. Three successful throws mean survival while Unconscious; three failed throws mean death. Stabilization before the third failure ends these rolls. Additional Wounds cause automatic failures based on severity.
- **Lasting Consequences:** Remain Unconscious until medical treatment arrives; gain an appropriate permanent Hindrance after recovery.

### Non-Lethal Damage
Declare non-lethal intent before attacking with unarmed strikes or suitable blunt weapons. Resolve normally, but a result that would inflict a Lethal Wound instead causes Unconsciousness without Dying. Survival Saving Throws are not required unless later subjected to lethal damage.

## 6. Rest and Healing
The Epic Heroism recovery model supports cinematic adventures. A one-hour Short Rest removes all Minor Wounds. A full day of rest reduces each remaining Wound by one severity level. If the lower-severity slot is unavailable, the Wound remains unchanged until a slot becomes available. Natural recovery does not replace stabilization.

Medical treatment uses INT + Medicine. Serious Wounds require Normal (2); Critical and Lethal Wounds require Hard (3). Success reduces the selected Wound by one severity level; additional successes may reduce it further. Minor Wounds normally require no Medicine Check.

## 7. Conditions
Conditions alter capabilities, movement, or behavior. They may result from attacks, failed rolls, hazards, equipment, or abilities. Multiple different Conditions are cumulative unless otherwise stated. A Condition lasts until removed by its specified duration, recovery, treatment, or other applicable rule.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Battered | All Minor slots filled; further Minor Wounds escalate. No additional dice penalty. |
| Blinded | Fails sight-dependent checks; −2 Attack dice; attackers gain +1 die, or −1 Defense die against NPC attacks. |
| Bleeding | One Minor Wound at end of each Turn until stopped. |
| Burning | One Minor Wound at end of each Turn until extinguished. |
| Controlled | Cannot knowingly harm controller; controller gains +2 dice on social interactions; other restrictions depend on effect. |
| Dazed | −1 die to Skill Checks, Attacks, and Defenses. |
| Deafened | Fails hearing-dependent checks; cannot benefit from verbal warnings without alternative communication. |
| Disabled (Limb) | Arm: cannot use affected arm. Leg: half movement; both legs disabled causes Prone and prevents standing. |
| Distracted | −1 die to Skill Checks using INT, ACT, or PRS. |
| Dying | Becomes Prone, cannot stand; follow stabilization or survival rules. |
| Fatigued | −1 die to Skill Checks, Attacks, and Defenses. |
| Frightened | −1 die to Skill Checks and Attacks while threat is visible; cannot willingly approach threat. |
| Grappled | Movement 0; no movement bonuses; may attempt escape. |
| Invisible | Heavily Obscured; attacks against character suffer −2 dice; +2 Attack dice against targets unaware of position. |
| Paralyzed | Cannot move, speak, or take movement-dependent Actions/Reactions; fails applicable STR/DEX Saves; attackers gain +2 dice or −2 Defense dice against NPC attacks. |
| Poisoned | −1 die to Attacks and Skill Checks; specific poison may add effects. |
| Prone | Can only crawl; −1 Close Combat Attack die; −1 Defense die against Close Combat; +1 Defense die against Ranged attacks. |
| Restrained | Movement 0; −1 die to Attacks, Defenses, and DEX Saves. |
| Silenced | Cannot communicate verbally or perform speech-dependent actions. |
| Slowed | Half movement distance. |
| Stunned | Becomes Prone; cannot move or take movement-dependent Actions/Reactions; fails applicable STR/DEX Saves; −2 Defense dice against Close Combat. |
| Suffocating | Cannot speak; one Minor Wound at beginning of each Turn; normally holds breath for RES + 2 Rounds before suffocating. |
| Unconscious | Becomes Prone, drops held items, cannot act or react; fails applicable STR/DEX Saves; −3 Defense dice against Close Combat. |
