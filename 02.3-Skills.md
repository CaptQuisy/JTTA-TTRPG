## Skills

Skills represent a character's training, education, and practical experience. While Attributes describe natural talent, Skills reflect what a character has learned through study, practice, and real-world experience.

Skills determine how effectively characters perform specialized tasks, from climbing a mountain or negotiating with a criminal to treating a gunshot wound or piloting a spacecraft.

When a character attempts an action with an uncertain outcome and meaningful consequences, the player makes a Skill Check using the appropriate Attribute and Skill.

Every character begins with **Level 1 in all 15 Basic Skills**, representing the general knowledge and practical competence expected of an adventurer.

Characters can improve these Skills during character creation and through experience gained during play.

### Skill Ratings

Basic Skills are rated from **Level 1 to Level 5**.

|Skill Level|Description|
|---|---|
|**1 — Basic**|Familiarity with the activity and the ability to perform simple tasks.|
|**2 — Competent**|Reliable ability developed through practice or training.|
|**3 — Experienced**|Substantial experience and proficiency with challenging tasks.|
|**4 — Expert**|Advanced knowledge and skill recognized by other practitioners.|
|**5 — Master**|Exceptional expertise representing the highest levels of human achievement.|
A Skill Level reflects capability rather than professional status. A self-taught character may possess a high Skill Level, while a professional with limited experience may have a relatively low rating.

Skill Levels represent training and experience, not guaranteed success. Difficulties, circumstances, equipment, and natural ability all influence the outcome of a Skill Check.

### Beginning Skill Points

During character creation, players improve their starting Skills using two sources of Skill Points.

**Background Skill Points:** Each Background grants 3 Skill Points, which must be assigned to the Skills listed for that Background. No more than 2 Background Skill Points may be assigned to a single Skill.

**General Skill Points:** Every starting character receives 7 additional Skill Points, which may be assigned to any Basic Skill.

Under the standard method, each additional Skill Level costs 1 Skill Point.

Assign points in the following order:

1. Begin with Level 1 in all Basic Skills.
2. Select a Background and distribute its 3 Background Skill Points.
3. Distribute 7 general Skill Points among the Basic Skills.
4. Confirm that no Skill exceeds Level 5.

A Skill's final rating includes its starting Level 1, Background Skill Points, and general Skill Points.

**Example:**

A character with the Criminal Background begins with Close Combat Level 1.

The player assigns 2 Background Skill Points to Close Combat, increasing it to Level 3.

The player then spends 2 general Skill Points to increase Close Combat to Level 5.

The character's final Close Combat rating is Level 5, the maximum permitted rating.

The Game Master may grant additional starting Skill Points for campaigns featuring older, more experienced, or exceptionally capable characters.

### Using Attributes with Skills

Most Skills are associated with one or two Attributes, shown in parentheses after the Skill name.

These are the default Attributes for common applications of the Skill. The Game Master may select a different Attribute when the character's approach or circumstances justify it.

For example:

- **Athletics + Strength:** Climbing a steep cliff through physical effort.
- **Athletics + Dexterity:** Leaping between moving platforms.
- **Athletics + Resilience:** Completing an exhausting long-distance swim.
- **Influence + Presence:** Convincing a guard to allow passage.
- **Influence + Intelligence:** Presenting a carefully reasoned argument.
- **Influence + Strength:** Intimidating someone through a credible display of physical power.

The Skill represents training, while the Attribute represents the natural capability applied to the task.

A player should describe what their character is attempting and how they approach the challenge. The Game Master then determines the appropriate Attribute–Skill combination.

### Basic Skills

The following 15 Basic Skills represent abilities commonly encountered across historical periods, modern adventures, and science fiction settings.

Each Skill includes its default Attribute or Attributes, common applications, and guidance for distinguishing it from similar Skills.

#### Academics (INT)

Academics represents formal education, scholarly knowledge, research, and the understanding of established information.

It covers knowledge acquired through study, reading, instruction, and intellectual investigation.

**Typical Uses:**

- History and archaeology
- Law and government
- Philosophy and religion
- Politics and economics
- Literature and languages
- Mathematics
- Scientific principles
- Cultural and historical knowledge
- Academic research

**Specialties:** Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Geology, Sociology, Psychology, Astrophysics, and other scholarly fields.

Academics is used to recall or interpret established knowledge. Investigation is used to analyze evidence and uncover new information, while Technology governs practical work with machines and technical systems.

**Example:** Identifying the historical origin of an ancient inscription requires Intelligence + Academics.

#### Athletics (STR or DEX)

Athletics represents physical strength, endurance, and whole-body movement developed through exercise, training, or practical experience.

**Typical Uses:**

- Climbing
- Jumping
- Swimming
- Running
- Lifting and carrying
- Throwing objects for distance
- Breaking or forcing objects
- Overcoming physical obstacles
- Sustained physical exertion

Athletics governs physical effort rather than combat technique.

Throwing an object for distance or attempting an athletic feat uses Athletics. Attacking an opponent with a thrown weapon normally uses Ranged Combat.

Precise balance or controlled acrobatic movement may use Finesse when coordination matters more than strength or exertion.

**Example:** Climbing a rope while carrying heavy equipment requires Strength + Athletics.

#### Close Combat (STR)

Close Combat represents fighting at close range using unarmed techniques, handheld weapons, and grappling.

**Typical Uses:**

- Punching and kicking
- Wrestling
- Grappling 
- Escaping holds 
- Fighting with knives
- Fighting with clubs and improvised weapons
- Using swords, axes, and similar melee weapons
- Close-quarters fighting techniques

Close Combat determines a character's effectiveness when fighting an opponent within melee range.

Advanced martial arts, specialized combat techniques, or unusual fighting styles may be represented by appropriate Edges.

**Example:** A soldier attempting to strike an opponent with a combat knife uses Strength + Close Combat.

#### Crafting (DEX or INT)

Crafting represents the ability to create, repair, and modify physical objects through skilled workmanship.

**Typical Uses:**

- Blacksmithing
- Carpentry 
- Gunsmithing 
- Tailoring
- Leatherworking 
- Jewelry making 
- Shipbuilding
- Fabrication
- Construction and restoration 
- Working with specialized tools and materials

Crafting focuses on producing or restoring physical objects through practical workmanship.

Technology is normally used to diagnose, operate, and repair complex mechanical, electrical, or electronic systems.

Some activities may reasonably involve either Skill. The Game Master selects the Skill that best represents the task.

**Example:** Forging a replacement sword blade requires Dexterity or Intelligence + Crafting, depending on whether precision or technical knowledge is the primary challenge.

#### Finesse (DEX)

Finesse represents precise movement, manual dexterity, agility, and delicate physical control.

**Typical Uses:**

- Sleight of hand 
- Picking pockets
- Lockpicking
- Maintaining balance 
- Acrobatics requiring precision 
- Fine manipulation
- Forgery
- Handling delicate objects
- Escaping through narrow openings

Finesse is appropriate when careful coordination and precision are more important than physical strength.

Athletics is generally used for movement requiring substantial physical exertion, while Finesse governs delicate or highly controlled movement.

**Example:** Picking a mechanical lock without damaging it requires Dexterity + Finesse.

#### Influence (PRS or STR)

Influence represents the ability to persuade, deceive, intimidate, negotiate with, or inspire others.

**Typical Uses:**

- Persuasion
- Negotiation 
- Deception 
- Intimidation
- Leadership 
- Diplomacy
- Fast talking 
- Interrogation
- Inspiring cooperation

The Attribute depends on the character's approach.

Presence is normally used for persuasion, negotiation, and social confidence. 

Strength may apply when intimidation depends on a credible demonstration of physical power. Intelligence may be appropriate when persuasion relies primarily on logical reasoning.

Influence is used when the objective is to change another person's beliefs, decisions, or behavior.

Performance is used when the primary objective is to entertain, present, or captivate an audience.

**Example:** Convincing a reluctant official to provide confidential information requires Presence + Influence.

#### Investigation (INT)

Investigation represents the ability to gather, examine, interpret, and connect information to uncover facts or solve problems.

**Typical Uses:**

- Examining crime scenes
- Following clues
- Analyzing evidence
- Researching suspicious activities
- Decoding information
- Reconstructing events
- Identifying inconsistencies
- Solving mysteries

Investigation emphasizes analysis and interpretation.

Perception is used to notice an object, sound, or environmental detail. Investigation is used to determine its meaning or significance.

**Example:** Recognizing that several apparently unrelated burglaries were committed by the same person requires Intelligence + Investigation.

#### Medicine (INT)

Medicine represents knowledge and practical training in diagnosing, treating, and preventing illness and injury.

**Typical Uses:**

- First aid
- Surgery
- Trauma care 
- Disease diagnosis
- Treating infections
- Pharmaceuticals  
- Long-term patient care 
- Medical procedures 
- Cybernetic medicine in appropriate settings

Medicine governs medical treatment and recovery, subject to the equipment and medical knowledge available in the campaign's era.

A medieval physician and a modern trauma surgeon may possess the same Skill rating while having access to very different treatments.

**Example:** Stabilizing a wounded companion requires Intelligence + Medicine, following the applicable injury and healing rules.

#### Perception (ACT)

Perception represents awareness of people, objects, sounds, movement, and changes in the surrounding environment.

**Typical Uses:**

- Spotting hidden objects 
- Hearing distant sounds 
- Detecting ambushes 
- Reading body language 
- Noticing suspicious behavior
- Searching rooms
- Recognizing environmental changes
- Detecting concealed movement 

Perception determines whether a character notices information.

Investigation determines what that information means.

Perception may be used to recognize signs of deception, but successfully noticing suspicious behavior does not automatically reveal the complete truth.

**Example:** Noticing a concealed figure watching the party from a rooftop requires Acuity + Perception.

#### Performance (PRS)

Performance represents the ability to express ideas, emotions, and artistic talent before an audience.

**Typical Uses:**

- Acting
- Singing 
- Playing musical instruments
- Dancing 
- Storytelling 
- Comedy
- Public speaking  
- Dramatic presentation 
- Entertaining or distracting crowds

Performance determines how effectively a character presents or entertains.

Influence is used when the objective is to persuade an audience to make a decision or change its behavior.

A character may use Performance to hold a crowd's attention before making a separate Influence Check to persuade them.

**Example:** Delivering an engaging theatrical monologue requires Presence + Performance.

#### Ranged Combat (DEX)

Ranged Combat represents training and accuracy with weapons used to attack targets at a distance.

**Typical Uses:**

- Pistols
- Rifles 
- Shotguns 
- Bows 
- Crossbows
- Thrown knives
- Thrown spears
- Other ranged weapons

Ranged Combat governs weapon accuracy rather than general throwing ability.

Athletics may be used to throw an object for distance or to perform an athletic feat, but an attack made with a thrown weapon normally uses Ranged Combat.

Weapon availability and effectiveness depend on the campaign's historical period and technology.

**Example:** Firing a rifle at an enemy across a courtyard requires Dexterity + Ranged Combat.

#### Stealth (DEX)

Stealth represents the ability to avoid detection through careful movement, concealment, and awareness of the environment.

**Typical Uses:**

- Sneaking
- Hiding 
- Shadowing someone 
- Camouflage
- Silent movement
- Concealing one's presence
- Moving through guarded areas

Stealth is frequently opposed by an observer's Perception or an appropriate static Difficulty.

Equipment, lighting, terrain, noise, and the observer's alertness may affect the attempt.

**Example:** Moving quietly through a guarded warehouse requires Dexterity + Stealth.

#### Survival (ACT or INT)

Survival represents the ability to live, navigate, and function in dangerous or unfamiliar environments.

**Typical Uses:**

- Tracking 
- Hunting
- Foraging 
- Building shelter 
- Predicting weather
- Wilderness navigation 
- Finding safe routes
- Recognizing environmental hazards 
- Locating water and other resources 

Survival applies to deserts, oceans, forests, mountains, arctic regions, jungles, and alien environments.

Acuity is commonly used when detecting tracks, signs, or environmental changes. Intelligence may be used for planning, navigation, or applying survival knowledge.

**Example:** Following faint tracks through a forest requires Acuity + Survival.

#### Technology (INT)

Technology represents understanding, operating, diagnosing, repairing, and modifying mechanical, electrical, electronic, and other technical systems.

**Typical Uses:**

- Computers
- Electronics 
- Robotics 
- Mechanical repair 
- Security systems
- Communications equipment 
- Engines and machinery
- Technical diagnostics 
- Programming 
- Maintaining complex equipment 

Technology governs practical technical work rather than general academic knowledge.

Crafting is usually used to construct or restore physical objects through skilled workmanship, while Technology governs the operation and maintenance of functional technical systems.

The technological capabilities available to a character depend on the campaign setting.

**Example:** Diagnosing a malfunctioning spacecraft engine requires Intelligence + Technology.

#### Vehicle (DEX or INT)

Vehicle represents the ability to operate and control vehicles or mounts appropriate to the campaign's era.

**Typical Uses:**

- Riding animals 
- Wagons and carriages 
- Automobiles 
- Boats and ships 
- Aircraft 
- Spacecraft 
- Industrial vehicles
- Military vehicles

Dexterity is normally used when control, maneuvering, timing, and reflexes are most important.

Intelligence may be used for navigation, operating complex systems, or calculating difficult maneuvers.

A high Vehicle rating does not automatically mean a character is familiar with every type of vehicle.

The Game Master should consider the character's Background, training, and experience when determining whether unfamiliar controls or operating procedures introduce additional Difficulty.

**Example:** Maneuvering a speeding automobile through narrow streets requires Dexterity + Vehicle.

### Improving Skills Through Experience

After character creation, Skills are improved using Experience Points (XP) earned through character advancement.

**Starting Skill Points and advancement XP are separate resources.**

A character must purchase Skill Levels sequentially and may not exceed Level 5 unless a specific campaign rule permits it.

### Skills and the Game World

Skills are intentionally broad so the same character system can function across different eras and genres.

A medieval blacksmith and a futuristic fabricator may both use Crafting, but their tools, materials, and available techniques differ.

A horse rider and a spacecraft pilot may both use Vehicle, but their experience does not necessarily transfer directly between those activities.

The Game Master should consider the character's Background, training, available equipment, and familiarity with the task when establishing Difficulty and modifiers.

Characters should not be penalized simply for attempting an unfamiliar activity. However, specialized tasks may require appropriate knowledge, tools, or preparation.

The purpose of the Skill system is to encourage creative problem-solving while keeping character capabilities grounded in their experiences and the circumstances of the adventure.
